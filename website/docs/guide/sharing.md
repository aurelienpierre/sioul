# Sharing between your devices

## In short {#in-short}

Each of your devices keeps all of its data in its own files, and works without the others. What must travel between them goes through a folder that a sync app you already use carries (Nextcloud, Dropbox, Syncthing, Google Drive, OneDrive, or another), sealed on your device first: the folder and its server can read nothing, and change nothing without it showing. There is no server of ours, and nothing reaches the developer. Nothing that happens to that folder can take data away from your devices, and what a device cannot know, it says.

## How it works {#how-it-works}

- **Your devices hold your data; the folder only passes messages between them.** Each device keeps its own complete copy. The shared folder holds sealed changes, sent from one device to the others. A device that loses the folder, or finds it damaged, keeps everything it had.
- **One file per device, written by that device alone.** Each device writes its changes at the end of its own file in the folder, one sealed line per change, and never touches the files of the others. A sync app makes "conflicted copies" when two devices change the same file. Here that never happens, so it never has to choose for you.
- **Changes, not copies.** Each minute, Sioul compares your files with what they held at its last look, and sends only what changed, one entry at a time: a setting, a line of time, a dose, a sender, a note. Each device reads the new lines of the others and applies them. In the notes folder, Sioul looks again only at the folders that changed; a note another program rewrites in place, without saving it beside and renaming it, is seen within ten minutes.
- **The later change wins, entry by entry.** A setting changed on two devices keeps the later change. Time noted on each device stays on both. A note changed on two devices keeps both versions, one of them under a name that says so. Once all your devices run this version or a later one ([below](#devices-on-different-versions)), a money line, a time session or a pinned site changed on two devices stays one, and two lines alike made on one device stay two: each has an id of its own, given when it is made, or written into it once, when your devices take the newer form. A list you change on a page (words, days off, working hours, who may reach you, mail filters, meals and naps) changes by what you did there, over the list as it is at that moment: a word or a day off added on another device while the page was open stays.
- **What the sync app has to do: very little.** It must carry new files, and files that grow, sooner or later and in any order. It does not need to delete, rename or lock anything. That is why any sync app works. A slow one, such as one on Android that looks every half hour, makes changes come late, never wrong.
- **What a device cannot know, it says.** Each device says in the folder how it is: when it started, when it closed properly, whether it is in use, and when it last shared. Each knows how far it has read the others. A device that closed properly sent everything it marked; one in use is known while its news keeps coming. If something may have happened on a device whose news has not come (a dose marked on the phone while in use, which its sync app has not carried yet), Sioul says it does not know rather than guessing, and names that device. The dose is reminded with "check first", never as "not taken" ([Health](health.md#on-several-computers)).

### When the sync app is late {#when-the-sync-app-is-late}

Sync apps carry files at their own pace, and Sioul cannot hurry most of them. A computer's Nextcloud client sends a change within seconds; an app on Android may wait for its next scan, half an hour or more, and can leave some files behind for longer: on 6 October 2026, Murena's eDrive left a computer's changes on the server for hours while it scanned every few minutes. Until a change arrives, the other device does not know it: a do-not-disturb switch, a dose marked taken, whether the computer is still open. A device that cannot know says so (doses: "check before taking it"); it never guesses.

WebDAV is the one protocol for which Sioul takes matters into its own hands. When the shared folder sits on a Nextcloud server Sioul already has an account for (Murena's included), it also reads the other devices' files from the server itself, as a backup, and keeps whichever copy is newer: a phone or a tablet then follows within about a minute, whatever its sync app does. With Nextcloud, a sync app is even optional: Sioul can keep the folder in step itself, sending as well as fetching, so that a phone or a tablet needs no other app ([setting it up](#setting-it-up)). With any other sync app (Syncthing, Dropbox, Google Drive…), changes arrive when that app brings them. That is why Nextcloud is the first choice: [where to get one](compatibility.md#where-to-get-a-nextcloud).

### Sent at once {#sent-at-once}

A few seconds after you save anything that travels (a dose answered, a setting, a medicine, a project, a budget), Sioul exchanges, without waiting for the minute. And where the folder is on your Nextcloud, Sioul then sends this device's own files there itself, beside your sync app: your other devices have them within seconds, even when this device's sync app is late, and a server that lost them can be given them again (**Send everything again**, [below](#setting-it-up)).

- **Only this device's files**, never another's; each goes over what the server holds as Sioul last saw it, so it never undoes a newer copy; this device's records are never sent shorter than they were.
- **Your sync app goes on as before.** When it sends the same file again, nothing changes: Sioul finds it there as it is here and sends nothing twice. Sioul gives each file its date and checksum, so that Nextcloud's client recognises the same file without downloading it.
- **Small files, on a slow connection too.** Each change sends a small file (tens of kilobytes at most), so a phone's hotspot or mobile data carries it: a computer writing each minute sends about half a megabyte an hour. A large file, now and then, is given the time its size asks; one that does not go through is said in the panel (which file, how long it tried), and tried again later, never sooner: a minute, then two, five, a quarter of an hour, half an hour, up to an hour. On a metered connection, or one measured slow, large files go at most every ten minutes.
- **A text to send goes first.** A text written on a computer travels in a small file of its own, sent first and alone, so that it reaches your phone within its quarter of an hour even while a large file is still on its way.
- **One moment is left**: a sync app looking at the server in the second between a change here and Sioul's sending. Nextcloud's client then keeps your newer file beside, as a "conflicted copy" on that computer (never read; delete it when you like), and Sioul starts its file again with all it holds. Nothing is lost, and the other devices never take a dose as known wrongly: at most they say for a minute that they do not know.

### When something goes wrong in the folder {#when-something-goes-wrong-in-the-folder}

Nothing that happens in the folder can take your data away from you. Sioul treats every surprise as something it does not know:

- **A file cut, missing, damaged or out of order** (a sync app that put back an older copy, a disk's damage): what it holds is counted as not known, and said. It is never read as "taken out".
- **A settings file found empty or half written** on this device (a crash, a full disk) is never read as "everything deleted". It waits ten minutes, and nothing of it is taken out elsewhere meanwhile.
- **One of Sioul's own lists gone or empty** on a device that read it before (projects, client profiles, budgets, the bank's lines, movements, contracts, the papers' or the letters' list): never read as "everything taken out". It is written again from what your devices hold, nothing of it is taken out elsewhere, and the panel says so.
- **A folder suddenly empty, gone or unreadable** (a memory card not mounted, an access withdrawn): nothing of it is taken out on your other devices until you say so.
- **Many files deleted at once** from a folder still there: your other devices keep them two weeks, then they leave by themselves, unless you put them back ([Notes and papers](#notes-and-papers)).
- **An older copy put back by hand** (a backup restored with its old dates) does not undo your other devices' changes.
- **A device restored from a backup**, or one that lost its memory, carries on from where the folder is and catches up: what it marked after the backup comes back.
- **Before another device's change replaces or removes a file here**, the file as it was is kept on this device, to put back if needed ([below](#putting-back-an-older-version)).
- **The sharing folder gone, or its seal** (a disk not mounted, a sync app that moved or rebuilt its folder): nothing is written until it is back, and the panel says so. Sioul never makes the folder again, empty, beside the real one.
- **The folder sealed with another passphrase** than this device's (two devices that each set up the folder before the other's seal arrived): this device writes nothing there, and says so; start sharing again on it with the passphrase your other devices use, and they meet.
- **Two devices under one name** (a disk or a phone's data copied to another): each finds the other's lines in its own file, and the panel says so; stop sharing on one of them and start again there, which gives it a name of its own.
- **A list kept by another program in another encoding** (not text Sioul can read): left as it is, said, and nothing of it is taken out elsewhere.
- **A draft sent on one device while another changed it**, neither knowing yet: it stays sent everywhere, whichever came later; the change made meanwhile is kept among that device's earlier versions.

## What it protects, and what it cannot hide {#what-it-protects-and-what-it-cannot-hide}

- **Sealed on your device.** Every change is encrypted before it is written into the folder, with XChaCha20-Poly1305, a cipher that also detects any change made to what it sealed. Notes and papers are compressed, then sealed in pieces of 1 MiB.
- **One passphrase.** The key is made from a passphrase you choose, through Argon2id, which is deliberately slow and costly to try (64 MiB of memory and three passes for each guess). You type it once on each device, which keeps the key made from it in its keyring (on Android, behind its KeyStore); the passphrase itself is kept nowhere and sent nowhere. A wrong one is said at once. It needs at least 12 characters: a few words you will not forget.
- **What the folder and its server can see**: which device wrote (an identifier drawn at random, not your name nor the device's), when, and how much. For notes and papers, how big each sealed file is: a well-known document, such as a public form, might be recognised by its size. They can also tell when a change reuses a content already sealed there (a file put back as it was).
- **What they never see**: what the changes are. Not the names of things (addresses, file names, settings), not their content.
- **Tampering shows.** Each line is bound to the device that wrote it, its place in that device's file, and its time. A line changed, moved into another device's file, or put in another order does not open. Each piece of a sealed file is bound to its file and its place, so pieces cannot be swapped, cut or added. Whatever does not open is set aside as damaged and said. It never erases anything.
- **Someone who gets the folder but not the passphrase** (a hacked cloud account, a curious provider) can read nothing and forge nothing. They can delete or damage files: Sioul notices, says so, and your devices lose nothing ([above](#when-something-goes-wrong-in-the-folder)).
- **Someone who has the passphrase**, or an unlocked device of yours, can read and send changes like any of your devices. Even then, a change from the folder is written only where it belongs: never through a link, never outside the folder of what it changes (your notes folder for a note), never into Sioul's own state, never in plain into the sharing folder.
- **Passwords never travel.** Accounts arrive on another device without their passwords: each device asks once, and keeps them in its own keyring, or takes them from Bitwarden.
- **Not hidden yet**: the sizes and times above, and a small note each device leaves in the folder, unsealed: when it last exchanged, and how far it has read the others.

!!! warning "A lost passphrase cannot be found again"
    Nobody can recover it for you: it is never sent anywhere. If it is lost, stop sharing on every device and start again with a new folder. Nothing on your devices is lost.

## What travels, and how {#what-travels-and-how}

| What | How it reaches your other devices |
|---|---|
| Mail, contacts, events and tasks, with what Sioul writes in your tasks (steps, waits, links, kinds) | by their own servers, as with any program |
| Your notes folder: notes, their pictures and memos | by the folder's own sync; or, when no sync carries it (often on Android), through the sharing, sealed, once you switch **Notes** on |
| Your papers' files and your letters' scans, each in its folder | by that folder's own sync; or, when no sync carries it, through the sharing, sealed, once you switch **Papers** on |
| Sioul's own lists: projects, client profiles, budgets, the bank's lines, movements, contracts, the lists of your papers and letters | through the sharing, sealed (**Projects and money**); until you move them out of your notes folder, they are in it: its own sync carries them, or the sharing does, the papers' list with **Papers** and the letters' list and texts with **Notes** ([Where Sioul keeps its lists](#where-sioul-keeps-its-lists)) |
| What Sioul keeps on this device alone: your settings and accounts (without passwords), who may reach you, the ties between things, time spent, drafts, invoices, medicines and doses, lists kept on this device only, where the Porch was closed | through the sharing, sealed |

**Never shared**: what each device chooses to share, where things are on each device (each keeps its own folders), how text reads on this screen, how pages are laid out, this device's browser notices, caches, and your own OpenPGP keys (copy them by hand). Passwords stay in each device's keyring.

## Setting it up {#setting-it-up}

No cloud yet? Nextcloud is the one Sioul is tested with: [where to get a Nextcloud](compatibility.md#where-to-get-a-nextcloud).

On the first device:

1. Open **Settings ▸ Sharing**, and find **Between your devices**.
2. **Folder**: choose a folder inside the one your sync carries, such as a new folder `Sioul` in your Nextcloud folder.
3. **Passphrase**, then **Once more**: a few words you will not forget, at least 12 characters.
4. **Share**.

On the other device, choose the same folder (as your sync shows it there). Sioul sees that another device shares through it, and asks for the passphrase chosen there. Then **Share**.

With a Nextcloud account in Sioul (Murena's included), a device needs no sync app at all: choose **Sioul keeps it in step with your Nextcloud itself**, the account, and the folder's place among your files there (`Documents/Sioul`), then the passphrase: typed twice when the folder is new there, once when another device shares through it already. Sioul keeps its own copy of the folder on that device and sends and fetches the files itself; devices that use a sync app on the same folder share with it as before ([why](#when-the-sync-app-is-late)).

### A new device, through your Nextcloud's sign-in {#a-new-device-through-your-nextclouds-sign-in}

A phone, a tablet or a computer with nothing set up yet joins your other devices through your Nextcloud alone, Murena's included: no sync app, no account to add first, no password typed into Sioul.

1. Open Sioul on the new device. On its first start, the Porch offers **Join your other devices**: **Sign in to Murena**, **Sign in to another Nextcloud** (its address, such as `cloud.example.org`) or **I use a sync app's folder**. **Settings ▸ Sharing** offers the same, above the two ways.
2. Your Nextcloud's own sign-in page opens in your browser. Sign in there as you do on its site, its second step included, and grant access to "Sioul (Android)" (or the system it runs on). Come back to Sioul: it waits for that, twenty minutes at most; **Cancel** stops it.
3. Sioul looks for your sharing folder there: `Documents/Sioul`, `Sioul`, then the folders in `Documents`. It says where it found it; elsewhere, choose **Another place** and type its place. Found nowhere, every place looked at, you may start sharing there, as on a first device. When Sioul could not look through all of them (a slow server, an error), it says so, with **Look again**, and never offers a new sharing then.
4. Type the passphrase you chose on your other devices, once (twice for a new sharing), and choose **Join**.
5. A first exchange runs at once. Sioul then says who this device shares with now, and what came: your settings, your accounts, your hours. Your contacts and calendars on that Nextcloud use the sign-in, through your account for it come with your settings, or one made from the sign-in when there is none; decided once all your other devices' settings came, a little later on a slow first exchange, so that no second account is made beside yours. **Still to sign in on this device** lists the accounts whose passwords never travel: mail, mostly; give each its password in **Accounts**.

From then on, Sioul keeps its own copy of the folder in step with your Nextcloud itself, as with **Sioul keeps it in step with your Nextcloud itself**.

What the sign-in leaves: an app password of its own, made by your Nextcloud for Sioul, kept in this device's keyring, never in a file; your Nextcloud lists it among its devices and sessions (**Settings ▸ Security** there), where you can revoke it. Your own password never reaches Sioul. Sioul sends that app password to that server only, over HTTPS, and refuses a sign-in whose answer names another server. **Cancel** after the sign-in gives it back to the server; so does **Stop sharing**, unless your contacts and calendars use it.

On a computer that shares, **Add a device** (in the same panel) says what the new device needs: the server to sign in to and the folder's place there, or, for another sync app's folder, that same folder on the new device; then the passphrase, the one you chose, which Sioul never shows.

### A new phone in place of an old one {#a-new-phone-in-place-of-an-old-one}

A phone that shares beside another phone silent for a day or more, as this phone sees it, asks once: "Does this phone replace Moss, silent since Sat 10 Oct 21:40?" On a phone that just joined, that is a day after it joined; meanwhile the computer offers **Forget it now** (below). Said of a phone still in use, Yes would leave your doses without that phone's word: both could then remind you of the same dose. **Yes, it replaces it**: its sealed entry says so, and every device then counts the old phone as forgotten: it no longer holds your doses' reminders back, and a computer reminds a dose plainly again once the new phone has said it read its opening. Should the old phone ever share again, it counts again by itself. Nothing it said is lost: its last records, its texts and their pictures stay; its older records, which its last ones restate, and its versions since replaced leave the sharing folder, taken out by your other devices. **No, it is a second phone**: nothing changes.

On a computer, a phone silent for a day or more can be forgotten at once: **Forget it now**, on its card in **Settings ▸ Devices** ([Your devices](#your-devices)), says what it changes first. Without it, a phone that will never share again (lost, broken) holds the doses' reminders back for a week: each comes with "check first", a few minutes late. It also keeps your devices' older records in the sharing folder for a month: each device whose records it reads keeps them until it has read them, or until it has been silent a month (half a year for one on Sioul 0.0.6 or older). Back later, it reads from the oldest records kept and finds everything there. Forgotten on a device, it no longer holds that device's records back, and that device takes the old phone's own older records out of the folder; said replaced by a new phone, on every device at once.

### Removing a device's files {#removing-a-devices-files}

A device that left, that you forgot, or that a new phone replaces keeps its last records in the sharing folder: its last word on each thing, its texts with them. Under **Devices that no longer count**, each one still holding files there has **Remove its files…**; a device that still counts has **Forget it and remove its files…** on its card in **Settings ▸ Devices**. Before you press, Sioul says in plain words what goes, with how much room it frees (its records, the small files it kept there, the sealed copies of the notes and papers it was the last to change that no other device names), and what stays: what each of your devices already read of it stays on that device, and the pictures and videos of texts that another phone's log names stay in the folder. A device joining later gets none of its records, unless another device's records carry them, as the texts a new phone restored. Its few small files that your other devices may still need (what it last said of itself, how far it read) stay too, a few hundred bytes. Notes deleted many at once and still held keep their sealed copies, so **Put them back** still works. If it still counts, it is forgotten first, for your doses too; if it was heard from within the last day, Sioul first says it may be in use now, and your doses keep their doubt for it until it says anything newer. If it is still in use after all, it shares again by itself.


The first time, a copy of what this device had is kept aside, in case. Then the other device's settings come, and what this one alone had goes out: two devices set up apart end with the first one's settings and both of their lists.

From then on, changes are exchanged a few seconds after anything that travels is saved here, each minute, and when you choose **Refresh everything** or **Exchange now**. The panel says through which folder you share, with how many other devices, and when they were last heard from. Your devices themselves are listed in **Settings ▸ Devices**, which one line of the panel leads to ([Your devices](#your-devices)). Under **Devices that no longer count**, those that left, that you forgot or that a new phone replaces, while their files are still in the folder, each with **Remove its files…** ([below](#removing-a-devices-files)). Under them, this device's own: "This device: Sioul 0.0.3 (eff8661abcde).", as `sioul --version` says it. **Stop sharing** asks first, saying what stops and what stays, then ends it on this device: each device keeps its own files, and the others go on sharing among themselves.

On a Nextcloud (Murena's included), the panel also says whether Sioul reads the folder on the server itself, beside your sync app, and when it last did: **Also fetch them from the server**, on once the folder is found there under the same seal ([why](#when-the-sync-app-is-late)). If Sioul does not find it, give its place there (such as `Documents/Sioul`) and choose **Look there**. Below it, **Also send this device's changes to cloud.example.org directly**, on with it: Sioul sends this device's own files there itself, right after each change ([why](#sent-at-once)), and says when it last did.

**Send everything again**, while you share: each of this device's files the server lacks, or holds otherwise, is sent again, whatever was sent before; then the other devices' news is fetched. For a sync app that was late, or a server that lost files. It says what it did, such as "Sent 14 files to cloud.example.org at 10:42.", or why nothing went.

Your notes folder travels by its own sync, not by Sioul, unless you switch it on below. If it does not seem to be inside a synced folder, the panel says so: your other device would not see your notes (nor your projects, while Sioul's lists are still in that folder). Moved into one (and chosen again in the Notes page's ⚙), they travel too; or switch **Notes**, **Projects and money** and **Papers** on, and Sioul carries them, sealed. Below it, a line says where Sioul keeps its own lists on this device, and **Where Sioul keeps its lists** opens the window of their move ([below](#where-sioul-keeps-its-lists)).

## Your devices {#your-devices}

**Settings ▸ Devices** lists every device you share with, this one first. In a wide window their fields line up as a table; on a phone each device is a card. For each device:

- **Its name**: the one you give it with **Rename…**, such as "Work laptop", shown on each device your settings travel to. Without one, it has the name its system gives it: a computer's name, "The phone" with its model. Emptying the field puts that name back.
- **What it is**: a phone, a tablet or a computer. This device's own can be changed there, with **What this device is**, as in [Settings](settings.md#display).
- **Now**: "In use now.", "Closed since 14:02.", or "Silent since Tue 3 Nov 09:15: it may have stopped without closing." It also says a device you said is off, one silent for a week, one on an older Sioul, and one whose clock runs ahead.
- **Last opened** and **Last shared**: when Sioul was last opened there, and when it last read the other devices and wrote its own changes.
- **Sioul**: the version it runs, such as "0.0.3 (eff8661abcde)", with a sentence when it is older than this device's, so that you can update it when you like.
- **For**: three switches, **Work**, **Your admin** and **Leisure**, any of them together. With nothing ticked, the card says "Everything comes here". You can say it of any device from any device: it travels with your settings, so it reaches each device that shares the part **Settings and accounts**; on a device that does not, what is said there stays there, and the tab says so. Each device then brings forward only what it is for, at the hours for it, and under the devices a grid says what comes on each at each time; a cell of it can be changed ([What each device is for](hours.md#what-each-device-is-for)).
- **Shares**: the parts it shares ([What travels from this device](#what-travels-from-this-device)). Another device's parts cannot be switched from here: a part is switched on or off on the device itself, where its data lands. A device on an older Sioul does not say what it shares.

On the card of a device other than this one: **Count it again** for one you said is off; **Forget this device** for one silent for a week; on a computer, **Forget it now** for a phone silent for a day ([a new phone](#a-new-phone-in-place-of-an-old-one)); and **Forget it and remove its files…** ([Removing a device's files](#removing-a-devices-files)).

While this device does not share, the tab lists it alone. Its name and what it is for can still be said.

## Devices on different versions of Sioul {#devices-on-different-versions}

Your devices may run different versions of Sioul for a while, such as a phone updated after the computer. They go on sharing; what they send each other changes form only once all of them can read the new one.

- **A device on an older Sioul holds the newer form back.** This version of Sioul shares money lines, time sessions, pinned sites, routines, kinds of task, days off and lists of words in a newer form: one line or one session changed on two devices stays one, and two sites pinned, or two words added, on two devices at the same moment both stay. Until every device of the sharing runs this version or a later one, all of them keep sharing in the form the older one reads, and the panel says so: "One of your devices runs an older Sioul…", the device's own line in **Settings ▸ Devices** saying which. Meanwhile, a time session, your pinned sites or a list of words changed on two devices at the same moment keep only the later change, as before. Once that device is updated, your devices take the newer form by themselves, once: each writes into its own files an id for each money line, preset, split and time session that has none (the file as it was is kept among the earlier versions), and sends only what the older form had lost, such as a second line alike. A device retired without **Stop sharing** holds the newer form back until you choose **Forget this device** for it (offered once it has been silent for a week, or at once for a device known only by what it shared before), or for 180 days.
- **A device that needs a newer Sioul.** If your other devices share a part in a form newer than this device's Sioul reads, this device says so once in the status line ("This device needs a newer Sioul to share “Time”…") and, as long as it lasts, in **Settings ▸ Sharing**. It still reads what it can. What you change here in that part stays on this device, and what changes on the others waits here. Once you update Sioul on this device, both are applied, the later change winning: a change made here gives way to a later one made on the other device. If the device that shares the newer form stops sharing, you forget it, or it stays silent for 180 days, the part travels again between your other devices, and what waited here from that device is set aside. Should that part be Health, this device says meanwhile that it does not share its doses, after a restart too: your other devices then say they do not know a dose it may have answered, never that it was not taken.
- **Invoices want every device updated together.** Before you issue an invoice with a newer version, update every device that shares invoices: an older Sioul does not read newer invoices as they are. A file of your invoices that a newer Sioul wrote (an invoice, its payments, the journal of numbers) is read here where it can be, never written over, and `sioul invoices list` names it, so that you know to update Sioul on this device.

## What travels from this device {#what-travels-from-this-device}

Under **What travels from this device**, each part has its switch, says what it carries, and when it last sent and received a change:

| Part | What it carries |
|---|---|
| Settings and accounts | your settings and accounts (never their passwords), with what each agenda is; the ties between things; [your people](contacts.md#household), once every other device's Sioul knows them; where the Porch was closed, what you dismissed, read or did of its notices, mail you said is no payment, do-not-disturb's switch; your word on mail's topics; a [security key's](accounts.md#security-key) public part, never a private key nor its PIN |

| Messages from your phone | the messages your phone's notifications brought, for the apps you chose there (its texts first), a week of them, and those you dismissed or marked read: so that a computer's Porch shows them; off until you turn it on, on the phone and on each computer that should show them ([What reaches you](notifications.md#messages-on-your-computers)) |
| Texts | your phone's texts, the whole history with its multimedia messages' media, read on the phone and kept as an archive that only grows; the texts written on a computer for the phone to send, and what became of each; sealed, and sealed again on each device; off until you turn it on, on the phone and on each computer that should read or write them ([Texts](texts.md)) |
| Spam filter | what you said is spam or not on each device, and what each device's filter moved and flagged, so that every device learns from the same truth; the newest of the tables its training makes only while one of your devices does not learn (an older Sioul, a new device, one whose training is off), never over the table of a device that learns; the table of mail's topics that the same training makes; never the mail it learned from, nor its words ([Settings](settings.md#your-own-spam-filter)) |
| Health | medicines, prescriptions and the doses taken, and what each device did to remind them; your usual meals, naps and night, and the days you changed them |
| Time | time noted, the session running, the day's choices, where you stopped, working late or done for the day, how each day went, the time you keep around meetings with others and Sioul's own lines of them (such as who takes a child's event) |
| Drafts and invoices | mail being written, invoices made and their files, their payments, the day each was sent and the declarations you said made (each device writes its own, all read together), their drafts and the journal of their numbers |
| Projects and money | Sioul's own lists: projects and their mail routes, budgets, bank accounts and movements, contracts, your clients' profiles, and, once they have left your notes folder, the lists of your papers and letters with the letters' texts |
| Lists kept here | calendars and contacts kept on this device only |
| Notes | your notes folder: notes, their pictures, PDFs and memos, and scanned letters while Sioul's lists are still in it |
| Papers | your papers' files, with the papers' list while Sioul's lists are still in your notes folder; once they have left it, the papers' files and the letters' scans |

Each device chooses for itself: switching a part off on the phone changes nothing on the desktop. A part switched off sends nothing and takes nothing out on your other devices; switched on again, it joins them as a new device would: what they hold comes first, and what only this one holds goes out. A device that never chose shares what Sioul shared before parts had switches: everything but notes and papers, and projects and money if you had ticked them, or once its lists have moved; never the messages from your phone nor the texts, which wait for your choice on each device. Before **Notes** or **Papers** is switched on, the panel says how many files would travel, and how big they are in all.

## Notes and papers {#notes-and-papers}

Switched on, notes and papers travel through the same sealed folder, one file at a time:

- **Only what changed**: each file is compressed, sealed apart and sent once; a note changed sends that note alone, not the folder. Files over 64 MB stay on their device, and the panel says which. On a phone or a tablet whose sync app never deletes what was deleted elsewhere (Murena's eDrive), every sealed file it brought stays in the sharing folder there: count on room for your notes and papers twice over. On the server, Sioul takes out what it no longer needs itself, eDrive or not. A device where Sioul keeps the folder in step itself lets go of the sealed copies the server no longer has, apart from those it still needs. On such a device, its background brings the others' new notes and papers, so that they are there when you open Sioul, offline too; on mobile data or a slow connection, one large file at most every ten minutes. A recording or a scan of a megabyte or more travels once it has not changed for ten minutes, or an hour after it began to change: saved again within minutes, only its last save goes.
- **Left out**: what your sync app or your editor makes beside your files (hidden files, `~` and `.tmp` files, conflicted copies, Office's locks), names that are not text, and what the other parts carry.
- **Changed on two devices at once**: both versions are kept. The later one keeps the name; the other is kept beside it as "lease (conflict 2026-10-05 16.05).md", on every device, and the panel says so for a day. Keep the one you want, or merge them by hand. The notes page does the same when you save a note that changed on another device while it was open.
- **An older copy put back by hand** (a backup restored with its old dates) does not undo your other devices' changes: the newer version comes back, the older copy kept beside it, and the panel says so. To send an older version, use **Put back** below.
- **Taken out**: a note deleted on one device goes on the others ten minutes later (it may be on its way back), kept there among the earlier versions in case, as a copy: its sealed copy in the sharing folder then leaves it as soon as each of your devices has read that it was deleted. On Android that copy takes room (your notes and Sioul's own storage are apart), so a large deletion is kept a part at a time, over a few exchanges.
- **Many deleted at once**: if many files leave a folder at once (deleted in bulk, a folder moved within the notes), your other devices keep them two weeks. The Porch says it once, calmly: how many went, from which folders, when, and when they leave, with **Put them back** (each comes back where it was, dated as it was; a file written there since stays as it is) and **Take them out now** (those files alone). Nothing has to be pressed: two weeks after each went, they leave your other devices by themselves, unless they came back. **Settings ▸ Sharing** says it as long as it lasts, with the same buttons.
- **A folder suddenly empty, gone or unreadable** (a disk not mounted, an access withdrawn), or replaced by a link (Sioul never follows links in the notes folder): that is not a deletion. Nothing of it is taken out on your other devices, however long it lasts, and nothing is written into it; the panel says so, with **Take them out everywhere** for when you did it on purpose. Switching Notes off and on fills the folder again from your other devices.
- **Names Android takes for one**: two notes whose names differ only by case or by how an accent is written ("Lease.md" and "lease.md") are one file on Android: the second waits, and the panel says so; rename one.
- **Room**: nothing is written without room for it and some left over; a note that does not fit waits, said, and comes when there is room.
- **How long replaced versions stay**: a version replaced leaves the sharing folder, and its server, two weeks later (90 days while one of your devices runs Sioul 0.0.5 or older, which counts on it). A device away longer than two weeks catches up on the latest version of each file only: the versions made meanwhile are gone from the folder. What it held before is kept among its earlier versions there. A device you forgot, or that a new phone replaces, holds nothing back: its versions since replaced leave the folder two weeks later, taken out by a device of yours that shares Notes, Papers and Spam filter (and Texts, with a phone among your devices); its last records stay until you choose **Remove its files** ([below](#removing-a-devices-files)). What Sioul takes out of the server goes to the server's trash; on some servers (Murena's, by its users' reports) it counts against your space until the trash is emptied: in the server's web page, Files ▸ Deleted files, select them all, Delete permanently.
- **Not with a synced notes folder**: if a sync app already carries your notes folder on this device (Nextcloud, Dropbox, Syncthing), Sioul refuses to carry it too, and says why: two carriers would undo each other's changes. On Android, Sioul cannot read the sync app's settings: it asks you before Notes or Papers is switched on, and until you say, takes a notes folder in the same top folder as the sharing one (Documents…) as carried, with a button to say no sync app carries it. Your answer is for that notes folder: move the notes elsewhere and Sioul asks again. Move the notes to a folder no sync carries to let Sioul carry them, or leave them to that sync. Sioul does not recognise every sync app (Google Drive, Insync, rclone, MEGA…): leave Notes off where one carries your notes. Once Sioul's lists have moved, **Papers** is refused the same way while a sync app carries your papers' or your letters' folder (that app carries their files then), and a sync app carrying your notes folder speaks for your notes alone; Sioul's lists, which only Sioul's sharing carries, are never refused.
- **On Android**, notes and papers wait while Sioul lacks Android's access to all your files (without it, Sioul would see only the files it made).

## Where Sioul keeps its lists {#where-sioul-keeps-its-lists}

Your notes folder is yours. Sioul's own lists used to live at its root, with a `papers` folder and a `letters` folder beside your notes; they move out of it once, on each device, when you say so:

| What | Before the move | After it |
|---|---|---|
| Sioul's own lists: projects (`sioul-projects.toml`), client profiles (`sioul-clients.toml`), budgets (`sioul-budgets.toml`), the bank's lines (`sioul-bank.toml`), movements (`sioul-movements.toml`), contracts (`sioul-contracts.toml`), the papers' list (`sioul-papers.toml`) | the root of your notes folder | `records/` in Sioul's data folder (`~/.local/share/sioul/records/` on Linux), under the same names |
| The letters' list and each letter's text | `letters/` in your notes folder | `records/letters/` in Sioul's data folder |
| Your papers' files | `papers/` in your notes folder | the papers' folder: `Documents/Papers` unless you choose another (**Papers ▸ ⚙**) |
| Your letters' scans, filed by year, and the folder scans arrive in | `letters/` in your notes folder | the letters' folder: `Documents/Letters` unless you choose another (**Letters ▸ ⚙**) |
| Your notes | your notes folder | your notes folder, alone |

Sioul's lists stay plain text files that you may open and edit by hand, in their new place. A paper or a letter then names its file from its own folder (`passport.pdf`, `2026/…`). A project still names its notes by their path in your notes folder. On a phone, the papers' and letters' folders are by default beside the notes folder when it is on the phone's shared storage, else in Sioul's own storage.

- **What moves**: only what Sioul's lists name. The lists themselves; each paper the papers' list names under `papers/`; each letter's scan the letters' list names under `letters/`, and each letter's text. Anything else under `papers/` or `letters/` (a note of yours kept there, a scan not read yet, a file dropped there by hand) stays where it is, and the window lists it before the press. No note is ever moved or deleted. Scans still waiting in the old `letters/inbox` are read from there after the move too, as long as you have not chosen where scans arrive yourself.
- **When**: once every device you share with knows the new places, a window, "Sioul's lists get a place of their own", shows once by itself. It says what moves and where, lets you choose the papers' and the letters' folders, and nothing moves before **Move now**; **Later**, or closing it, leaves everything as it is. **Settings ▸ Sharing ▸ Where Sioul keeps its lists** opens it again at any time, below a line that says where they are on this device; a move begun and not finished (a failure, Sioul closed during it) also shows one line on the Porch that opens it. A device that stopped sharing, that you forgot or that a new phone replaced, or one silent for 180 days, is not waited for: forget a device only when it is gone for good.
- **The folders you choose** must be empty, or not exist yet: everything in them is shared by Sioul with your other devices, so a folder that already holds files is refused, with one sentence. Each needs its own, outside your notes folder and outside Sioul's own folders; the same folder reached by another path (a link, another case) counts as the same. Under each, the window says when a sync program is known to carry that folder: its files would then travel there too, readable on that program's server, not sealed by Sioul.
- **While a device runs an older Sioul**, nothing moves anywhere, since that device would go on writing the old files. The window names it: update it. If Sioul's sharing is set up on this device but cannot be read now (the system's keyring locked), nothing moves either, and the window says so.
- **When the notes folder is not there** (a drive not plugged in, a vault not opened), or looks empty, nothing moves: the window says why, and the Notes page asks for the folder. A device with no notes folder at all has nothing to move: its lists begin in Sioul's own folder. So does a new installation, or any device whose notes folder holds nothing of Sioul's yet (no list, no `papers` or `letters` folder) and whose own folder is still empty: once no device of the sharing runs an older Sioul, its lists start in Sioul's own folder, with no window and nothing to move.
- **How it moves**: Sioul copies each file, checks every copy against its original byte for byte, keeps a dated copy of everything moved in its data folder (`moved/<day>/`, a copy of its own, never a second name of the old file), names the papers' and letters' files from their new folders in the lists, looks once more for what came meanwhile, and only then reads from the new places. If anything goes wrong on the way, your lists stay where they were, and the window says why in one sentence, naming the file. A move stopped halfway (Sioul closed, the device off) has changed nothing either: **Move now** again finishes it, and takes back only what its own attempt copied, never a copy an earlier attempt made (if a file left your notes folder in between, its copy stays, and the status line says so).
- **The old files** stay in your notes folder until you take them out; Sioul never takes them out by itself. Once this device has moved, **Settings ▸ Sharing** shows one line, "Sioul's old files are still in your notes folder", with how many, and **Take them out**. The button stays greyed, naming the devices still to move, until every device that counts has moved. Each old file goes only when its copy is at the new place, reached through no link, with the same bytes at that moment (a list against its dated copy, since your lists change by use after the move); any other stays, and the line says which and why. The dated copy is kept thirty days after the take-out.
- **A change made in an old file after the move** (by hand, or by a sync from a device not moved yet) is not read: Sioul now reads its own folder. The Porch names the file once, merges nothing by itself, and the take-out leaves it in place: copy over what you need.
- **How they travel afterwards**: by Sioul's sharing alone. **Projects and money** then carries every list, the papers' and the letters' with the letters' texts too; if it is off on this device, the window offers to switch it on, ticked. The papers' files and the letters' scans travel in **Papers**, unless a sync app carries the papers' or the letters' folder: **Papers** then stays off on this device, one carrier for a file, never two. **Notes** then carries your notes alone; a folder named `papers` or `letters` at the top of your notes folder is never shared as notes again, since those names stand for Sioul's records.
- **Without Sioul's sharing**: if your lists reached your other devices through your notes folder's sync, they no longer do after the move. The window says so: set up the sharing on each device first, then move on each.
- **Move every device the same day.** Until all have moved, change these lists on one device only, as the window says, naming the devices still to move. A device whose lists travelled only through your notes folder's sync does not see the changes made on devices that have moved until it moves too; then it joins them as a part switched on does, what they hold coming first. Until two devices have both moved, a letter's scan made on one reaches the other only once both have; its card comes at once. A device that comes back on an older Sioul and takes a whole list out is never obeyed by a device that moved.
- **A task or an event made from a letter before the move** keeps its link to the scan's old place; opened, that link opens the moved scan.
- **Another papers' or letters' folder later**, chosen in that page's ⚙, goes through the same checks (empty, its own, outside your notes folder and Sioul's), then gets a copy of every file, each checked; the old folder stays as it was. An emptied row is refused.
- **What no guard brings back**: a change written to a list and not sent yet when that list is lost on its device.

## Putting back an older version {#putting-back-an-older-version}

Before a change from another device is written into a file here, or takes it out, Sioul keeps the file as it was, on this device only: the last 20 versions of each file, and all those of the last 30 days; of a file deleted since, those of the last 30 days; never more than a gigabyte in all, or a twentieth of the device's storage when that is less, the oldest going first. Your versions of events, tasks and contacts set aside when they changed here and on their server too are kept like a file's: the last 20, and all those of the last 30 days, whether the item is still on its server or not, within 64 MB of their own. They are never shared. A note or a paper whose version is still in the sharing folder is kept as a pointer to it, for 12 days, rather than copied (the folder keeps a replaced version two weeks); one no longer there is copied. A note another device deleted is kept as a copy, moved here, which costs no room; on Android, where it cannot be moved, it is copied, a part at a time over a few exchanges.

In **Settings ▸ Sharing**, **Show earlier versions** lists them: each part, its files (the one changed last first; type part of a name to find older ones), and under a file its versions, with when and how big. **Put back** first says what it would change, then puts that version back:

- a note, a paper, a draft goes back whole; the file as it was just before is kept in the list too, so putting back can be undone the same way;
- an event, a task or a contact changed here and on its server between two syncs: the server's version was kept, and yours is listed here, under **Your calendars and contacts**, named by its title, on Android too; put back, it goes to its server over the server's version;
- a file of settings or lists (your settings, the doses taken, time noted) goes back entry by entry: each setting or mark the version held is put back as it was, those taken out since come back, and those added since stay. Putting back never takes out, on any device, what was added after that version: a dose marked this morning stays marked.

The next exchange sends what was put back to your other devices, as a change you made now.

## On Android {#on-a-phone}

Sioul for Android shares the same way, through the folder your sync app keeps on the phone or the tablet: Murena's eDrive, Syncthing, FolderSync, Autosync, Nextcloud's own app, or any app that keeps a folder on the device in step with your cloud (Nextcloud, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive…). Sioul reads the folder; it asks only that new files, and files that grow, reach the other side some day.

- **No sync app at all**: a new phone joins through Murena's sign-in, or another Nextcloud's, from **Join your other devices** on its Porch ([a new device](#a-new-device-through-your-nextclouds-sign-in)); with your Nextcloud account in Sioul already, choose **Sioul keeps it in step with your Nextcloud itself** when you start sharing on the phone or the tablet. Sioul then sends and fetches the files itself, and the device needs neither eDrive nor another sync app for the sharing ([setting it up](#setting-it-up)).
- **Where**: some sync apps carry only a few folders. Murena's eDrive carries your cloud's **Documents** (with Pictures, Music…), not the rest of it: share through a folder inside Documents, such as `Documents/Sioul`. Sioul suggests one there when your synced folder has a Documents folder, and says so when the one chosen is outside it.
- **On the phone or the tablet**: Settings ▸ Sharing, **Allow access to files** (Android's switch), then **Choose…** the folder, your passphrase, **Share**.
- **Accounts** come without their passwords: each asks for its own once, typed or [from Bitwarden](accounts.md#an-account-from-your-other-device).
- **Your notes, projects and papers**: the phone keeps a notes folder of its own, which no sync carries. Switch **Notes**, **Projects and money** and **Papers** on in the same panel, on the phone or the tablet and on each device whose notes folder no sync carries: they then travel sealed with the rest, a note at a time. On a computer whose notes folder a sync app already carries, they stay off while Sioul's lists are still in that folder, and Notes stays off afterwards too: that computer's notes reach the phone only from a folder no sync carries, with Notes switched on there too. Once Sioul's lists have moved, **Projects and money** carries them on every device, and **Papers** carries the papers' and letters' files wherever no sync app carries their folders ([Where Sioul keeps its lists](#where-sioul-keeps-its-lists)).
- **The sync app's pace**: a sync app on Android often brings the cloud's changes only every half hour. Where the folder is found on your Nextcloud, Sioul reads your other devices' news there itself, each minute while it is open and as soon as you come back to it, and leaves the sync app to its own pace. A note or a paper changed on another device comes the same way: Sioul fetches it from there as soon as it learns of it, and writes it within a minute while it is open. Otherwise, when the app offers a way to be asked (Murena's eDrive does), Sioul asks it to look now: after you mark something, when you come back to Sioul, and every five minutes while it is open, so what your other devices marked comes within a minute. Without either, it comes at the app's pace, and the doses say what Sioul cannot know meanwhile. What the phone marks goes the other way at once where the folder is found on your Nextcloud: Sioul sends it there itself ([sent at once](#sent-at-once)).
- **With Sioul closed**: Sioul keeps your devices in step in the background, with one quiet notification. Every few minutes (every two while another of your devices runs Sioul, every fifteen while you sleep; at night a computer left on with nobody at it does not count, unless a dose is due and not marked) it reads your other devices' news on your Nextcloud itself, where the folder is found there, and asks the sync app nothing, so that the phone sleeps between two looks; otherwise it asks the sync app to look. It also reads what the sync app brings as soon as it is written: do-not-disturb turned on at your computer follows within a few minutes ([Do not disturb on every device](notifications.md#do-not-disturb-on-every-device)). It is on unless you turn off **Keep this phone in step in the background**, on this device's card in **Settings ▸ Devices**, under **Do not disturb on this phone**; turned off, changes come when you open Sioul.
- **Doses while Sioul is not on the screen**: each coming dose is given ahead to Android's alarm clock. At its time, Sioul first reads your other devices' news: on your Nextcloud itself where the folder is found there; else, or when that does not go through within a few seconds, from the sync app, given twenty seconds to bring it. Then it reminds you, or not if another device marked it taken, or with "check first" if it cannot know: offline, with no news read since the dose's time, that is the case even for a device last known closed.
- **Files kept online only**: if your sync app keeps files on the server until you open them (OneDrive, Google Drive, iCloud, Nextcloud's "virtual files"), set the sharing folder to stay **always on this device**.
- **What is never needed**: deletions (eDrive never deletes on one side what was deleted on the other: the old files Sioul clears stay, and are not read again), and nothing it keeps aside, such as conflicted copies, is read.

## Some things, one device at a time {#some-things-one-device-at-a-time}

- **Medicines** are reminded by the device you are using only, so that a dose is not reminded twice. A dose marked taken goes to the others at once.
- **The sites' gathered notification** comes on the device you are using.
- **Invoices** are numbered on one device only, so that a number is never given twice. Another device says where they are made, and offers **Make invoices on this device**. A device that numbers them and has not been heard from (its sync stopped, or late on Android) still holds them, until it is heard from, closes Sioul, or a day goes by without its news. A device whose sharing leaves **Drafts and invoices** off issues none: the others' numbers would not reach it. Nor does one whose sharing cannot read its key, such as when its keyring is locked: it could not tell which device numbers them. On a computer, the command line issues an invoice only while Sioul's window runs there and keeps the invoices, by the same rules as the window. See [Time and invoices](time.md#on-several-computers).

## Not there yet {#not-there-yet}

Sizes padded to steps, and the devices' notes sealed too, so that the folder tells even less; a database server instead of a folder, for those who would rather have one; drafts in your mail server's Drafts folder, for other mail programs to see; phones other than Android's. The format is plain and documented ([the design notes](../dev/database.md)), so that they can come.

## For technical readers {#for-technical-readers}

- **Records** are sealed with XChaCha20-Poly1305 (RustCrypto), a random 192-bit nonce each. The associated data binds each record to its device, its round, its place in the file and its clock: a record moved, reordered or copied into another device's file does not open.
- **The key**: Argon2id v1.3, 64 MiB, 3 passes, 1 lane, a 16-byte random salt, and a check value sealed under the key, all in the folder's `seal.toml`. A seal asking more than 1 GiB or 16 passes is refused, so that a tampered folder cannot make a device spend unbounded memory. Each device's keyring keeps the 256-bit key; the passphrase, at least 12 characters, is kept nowhere.
- **Notes and papers**: HKDF-SHA-256 derives a sealing key and a naming key from it. A file is gzip-compressed, then sealed in 1 MiB pieces, each bound to the file's name, its index and whether it is the last, so that pieces cannot be swapped, cut or added. Names are an HMAC-SHA-256 of the content's hash, so that equal files in two folders cannot be linked. A file must open whole and match its record before it replaces anything, and opening stops at 64 MiB, whatever the compressed size claims.
- **Order**: a hybrid logical clock, the later change winning entry by entry.
- **Writing** from the folder never goes through a link, outside the part's own folder, in plain into the sharing folder, or into Sioul's own state.
- **Sioul's lists, moved**: their records keep their names (`notes/sioul-cases.toml`, `notes/sioul-papers.toml`, `files/notes/letters/…`), each device mapping them to where it keeps them, so that devices before and after the move read one another; the letters' scans take a name of their own, `files/letters/…`, written only once every device knows it. `sioul move-lists --notes … --records … --papers … --letters … --kept … --state …` runs the move on folders you give, without the window or the sharing: a trial on a copy of your files (`--plan` lists what would go where and what stays; `--clean` then takes the old files out, each against its copy). It refuses any folder that is, holds or sits inside this device's own configuration, data, state, notes, papers or letters folder. See [the design notes](../dev/notes-folder.md#what-lives-where).
- **From a Nextcloud server**: WebDAV over HTTPS only, never redirected, with that account's password taken from the keyring for each request; a folder there is read only when its `seal.toml` is this folder's, byte for byte.
- **To a Nextcloud server**: this device's own files only, each a `PUT` with `If-Match` its ETag as last seen (`If-None-Match: *` where none was), `X-OC-Mtime` and `OC-Checksum: SHA1:…`; refused (`412`), the server's copy is looked at and taken as sent when it is the same (size and checksum, else its bytes). A records file is never sent shorter.
- **The build**: each device's version and commit, in its sealed entry and sealed in the first line of each batch of its records; the server never learns them.
- **Unsealed**: each device's small note (when it last exchanged, how far it read the others), sizes and times. Padding to fixed steps is not built.
- **Format**: [the design notes](../dev/database.md).
