# Calls: who may ring, and when {#calls-who-may-ring-and-when}

On an Android phone, Sioul can screen your calls. A call from someone who may not reach you now is declined plainly, as you would by hand, and your operator sends it to your voicemail: nothing rings, nothing shows meanwhile. Afterwards the Porch says who called, at a time they may reach you.

## Setting it up {#setting-it-up}

1. **Settings ▸ Calls** (on the phone) says what Sioul does with calls, what it never does, and what always rings.
2. **Let Sioul screen calls**: Android asks "Set Sioul as your default caller ID & spam app?". Say yes. It gives Sioul no other permission. To stop later, **Android's default apps** on the same page, then "None".
3. **Allow reading contacts**, if Android has not been asked yet: without it, Android rings your contacts' calls without asking Sioul, and Sioul screens the others only.
4. **Who may call you, and when**: Accounts ▸ Who may reach you ▸ Calls. Each row (your safe, neutral and restricted contacts, numbers not in your contacts, hidden numbers) rings at the times ticked for it: work, admin, leisure, meals, sleep, the pause. The others go to voicemail.
5. **The people who must always get through** (a carer, your doctor's office): put them on your do-not-disturb list, Settings ▸ Do not disturb.
6. **Where declined calls go**: **Check \*#67#** and **Check \*#61#** open the phone app with the code typed in; you press call. Most operators send declined calls to voicemail.

Your voicemail greeting can ask callers to send a text instead.

Android 9 lets no app screen calls, and Sioul cannot silence it either: that takes Android 10. Android's own do-not-disturb, set in its settings, can keep calls quiet there ([Do not disturb on every device](notifications.md#do-not-disturb-on-every-device)).

## What always rings {#what-always-rings}

- Emergency numbers, and emergency services calling back (in France from 0 800 112 112, or the emergency number itself).
- Every call for a day after you call an emergency number, if Android shows Sioul your outgoing call (this cannot be tried without calling one).
- A second call from the same number within 15 minutes, unless you blocked it.
- The people on your do-not-disturb list.
- Every call while **Let every call through** is on, but the numbers you blocked.
- Any call Sioul cannot decide in time.

Do-not-disturb is apart: while it is on (a pause, Free time, its switch, or your own), only starred contacts and a second call within 15 minutes ring, whatever Sioul lets through.

## Let every call through {#let-every-call-through}

When you expect a call. On the phone's notification "Sioul keeps your devices in step": **Let calls ring, 1 h**, or **Let calls ring** until you turn it off; **Screen calls again** ends it. In the window, the phone button in the status line, on any of your devices: a click lets every call ring for an hour; a right click, or a long press, chooses until when.

## Afterwards, on the Porch {#afterwards-on-the-porch}

"While you slept: a number not in your contacts called at 09:30." Each call waits until its caller may reach you, never counted, no badge. Under it: **Text back**, **Call back**, **Add to contacts**, **Block**, **Why?**, **Seen**. Sioul cannot see your voicemail, so it says: "They may have left a message." The phone's own call history lists every declined call too, with Sioul's name.

With Free, set your voicemail to mail you each message with its sound (your subscriber area: Messagerie vocale ▸ Notification, with the audio file): the line then says "They left a message (0:42)." with **Listen**. The mail stays in your mail.

## What Sioul never does {#what-sioul-never-does}

It never answers a call, never records one, never listens, and sends no number anywhere: no server, no lookup. Texts are not filtered: do-not-disturb keeps them quiet at the times you set, and Android's own blocked numbers stop a number's calls and texts for every app.
