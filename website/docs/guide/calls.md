# Calls: who may ring, and when {#calls-who-may-ring-and-when}

On an Android phone, Sioul can screen your calls. A call from someone who may not reach you now is declined plainly, as you would by hand, and your operator sends it to your voicemail: nothing rings, nothing shows meanwhile. Afterwards the Porch says who called, at a time they may reach you.

## Setting it up {#setting-it-up}

1. **Settings ▸ Devices**, this phone's card, under its **Details** (where each section opens when you press its name): its section **The call screening on this phone** says what Sioul does with calls, what it never does, and what always rings.
2. **Let Sioul screen calls**: in **What Android must allow** on the same card, the line **Caller ID & spam app** and its button **Allow in Android…**; Android asks "Set Sioul as your default caller ID & spam app?". Say yes. It gives Sioul no other permission. To stop later, choose "None" as caller ID & spam app in Android's own settings, among its default apps.
3. **Allow reading contacts**, the line **Contacts** there, while it says **Not given**: without it, Android rings your contacts' calls without asking Sioul, and Sioul screens the others only.
4. **Who may call you, and when**: the card's section **Calls**, which you can set from any of your devices ([Each phone its own calls](#calls-per-phone)). Until you change it, the table shows what the phone does today, the same on every phone.
5. **The people who must always get through** (a carer, your doctor's office): put them on your Always through list, in What reaches you ▸ Who is in which circle, or from their card, **How … reaches you** ([A person's sheet](notifications.md#a-persons-sheet)). Their calls ring at any time, sleep and pauses included, unless you blocked them. Under that list, the switch **Starred contacts are Always through, both ways** (off until you turn it on) keeps the list and the contacts you star on your phone in step ([Always through](notifications.md#always-through)).
6. **Where declined calls go**: in the section **The call screening on this phone**, **Check \*#67#** and **Check \*#61#** open the phone app with the code typed in; you press call. Most operators send declined calls to voicemail.

Your voicemail greeting can ask callers to send a text instead.

Android 9 lets no app screen calls, and Sioul cannot silence it either: that takes Android 10. Android's own do-not-disturb, set in its settings, can keep calls quiet there ([Do not disturb on every device](notifications.md#do-not-disturb-on-every-device)).

## What always rings {#what-always-rings}

- Emergency numbers, and emergency services calling back (in France from 0 800 112 112, or the emergency number itself).
- Every call for a day after you call an emergency number, if Android shows Sioul your outgoing call (this cannot be tried without calling one).
- A second call from the same number within 15 minutes, unless you blocked it, or the call screening keeps it out whatever else (an automated caller, a number that cannot be a caller's, a number your network could not verify), or the phone's own table has its switch **A second call within 15 minutes rings** off ([Each phone its own calls](#calls-per-phone)).
- Your Always through people, unless you blocked them.
- [Your people](notifications.md#by-person) (everyone you added in Contacts ▸ Your people), at every time unless an exception in What reaches you says otherwise, where Sioul screens calls; unless you blocked or restricted them, or your network says it could not verify their number.
- Any call Sioul cannot decide in time.

Do-not-disturb comes after: while one of Sioul's modes is on (a pause, Free time, its switch), the calls Sioul lets ring ring through it, your contacts' while this phone screens calls, and a second call within 15 minutes. Numbers nobody knows ring through it only where your contacts do and the call screening lets them ring, or while you expect a call. Your own do-not-disturb, set in Android, lets through whom you set there ([What each system does](notifications.md#what-each-system-does)).

## Each phone its own calls {#calls-per-phone}

A work phone and a phone of your own need not screen alike. In **Settings ▸ Devices**, each phone's card has, under its **Details**, a section **Calls**, which you can set from any of your devices:

- **Start from**: **Professional** (everyone else rings in your work hours; commercial callers never; outside work hours, everything goes to voicemail but Always through and your people, who ring as What reaches you says, and a second call within 15 minutes does not ring there either), **Personal** (your contacts ring in the day; everyone else goes to voicemail unless they call twice within 15 minutes), **Both** (work's rows in work hours, the personal ones outside), or **As Android does** (Sioul screens nothing on that phone; your blocked numbers still go to voicemail). Once you change a cell or a switch by hand, the profile reads **Your own**.
- **One small table**: who rings in your work hours, the rest of the day, the night, and while you are busy (an event marked busy, or a task marked busy while its timer runs: [Busy](notifications.md#busy)). Its rows: Always through, which rings at every moment; Your people, once someone is in Contacts ▸ Your people, shown as What reaches you sets it, the same on every phone (set in What reaches you, not here: a phone's own table keeps no row of theirs while a device of yours may still run an older Sioul, which would drop it); Safe; Your contacts; Restricted; Everyone else (numbers nobody knows); Hidden numbers. Each cell is **Rings** or **Voicemail**; while busy also **As usual**, the first value of every row: as the moment says, or as Do not disturb says when being busy turns it on. A cell set to Rings or Voicemail decides while you are busy, Do not disturb or not. **Give a group its row…** adds a group of your contacts (School, Clients…), which rings, except at night, until you change it; the **×** beside its name takes it off. On a phone the table is a list of rows: press one to see its four moments.
- **Switches**: commercial and automated callers never ring; a second call within 15 minutes rings; numbers you called in the last 14 days ring as your contacts do; under **More**, numbers that cannot be a caller's and numbers your network could not verify never ring. Each switch is one setting.

Until you change it, the table shows what the phone does today, as every phone does: where a moment of the day differs from another (your contacts ringing in leisure but not in Free time, say), the cell says **Varies**, and a line under the table says when it rings. A first change of a cell or a switch starts from it, for that phone only: a cell that said Varies rings from then on, which Sioul says, with **Undo**. Choosing a profile writes that profile's whole table instead, the groups' rows kept. **Screen as every phone does** takes the phone's own table away. Calls are set there alone: the presets of What reaches you leave them as they are. While you are Reachable, everyone you know rings. In a pause, under do-not-disturb, and at night or in Free time where their boxes turn do-not-disturb on, the table gives way to what every phone does, as What reaches you's circles say: the card says who rings then. A group's row decides for the contacts in that group, in place of Your contacts: someone in two such groups rings when either row rings, and someone you placed on Safe or Restricted follows that circle's row. A phone whose Sioul is older than the first that reads this table says so on its card, and screens as every phone does until it is updated; one whose Sioul does not know Your people yet shows no row of theirs and says that it follows it once updated. On a phone where Sioul does not screen calls, Android's do-not-disturb holds your people's calls as any contact's: its card says so, with the two ways out (the call screening turned on there, or that person put on Always through). Whatever the table says, emergency numbers ring, and every call for a day after you call one.

**Two SIMs**: Android does not tell a screening app which SIM a call comes on, so Sioul cannot screen by SIM. To keep one SIM quiet, turn it off in Android (on the phone, the card's **Android's SIM page** opens it).

## The call screening {#the-call-screening}

Calls from numbers nobody knows (in none of your address books, Sioul's or the phone's) and from hidden numbers are the call screening's, apart from do-not-disturb: neither do-not-disturb nor any state of yours switches it off. On the phone, Settings ▸ Devices ▸ this phone's card ▸ The call screening on this phone ▸ **Call screening**. Every phone follows these settings until it has a table of its own; from then on, that phone follows its table's rows Everyone else and Hidden numbers, and its switches ([Each phone its own calls](#calls-per-phone)):

- **Screen calls from numbers nobody knows**: on, at all hours as usual. Off, such numbers ring when your contacts may: while you are [Reachable](hours.md#work-now), at every hour.
- **When**: at all hours, or only outside your work and admin hours.
- **Numbers not in your contacts** and **Hidden numbers**: to your voicemail, ringing in your work and admin hours (a hospital's switchboard often calls from a hidden number), or ringing. Until you change one of the screening's settings, they follow what your Calls rows of numbers not in your contacts and hidden numbers said; the first setting you change fixes both, and no preset of What reaches you changes them.
- **Automated callers** (since 2023, a machine may show only a number from the roots ARCEP keeps for machines), **numbers that cannot be a caller's** (France's numbering plan forbids numbers beginning 08 9 as a caller ID) and **numbers your network could not verify**: to your voicemail at all hours, a second call too, even while you expect a call; or each as other numbers nobody knows.
- **Numbers you called in the last 14 days** ring as your contacts do, so that someone you called can call you back. The phone keeps the numbers you call for 14 days, and only while this is on.

Whatever the screening lets ring never rings where your contacts wait: under do-not-disturb, at night, in a pause or in Free time, numbers nobody knows go to your voicemail too, unless you expect a call. The people you know ring as their rows say.

## Expecting a call {#expecting-a-call}

<span id="let-every-call-through"></span>When you expect a call from a number you may not know: a hospital, a delivery, someone calling you back. Numbers nobody knows, hidden numbers and numbers you called lately then ring, even where your contacts wait; your blocked numbers, automated callers, numbers that cannot be a caller's and numbers your network could not verify stay out. The people you know still ring as their rows say: until 19 October 2026 this was **Let every call through**, which let every call ring, the people you know at night included; it no longer does. On Sioul's quiet notification on the phone, the one that says what now is for (its channel: "Devices in step"): **Expecting a call, 1 h**, or **Expecting a call** until you turn it off; **Screen calls again** ends it. In the window, on any of your devices: the card What reaches you now, behind the status line's button ([What reaches you](notifications.md#the-button-and-its-card)), with **Expecting a call, 1 h**, or **Screen calls again** while it lasts; on a phone, **Call screening settings…** there opens this phone's Calls in Settings ▸ Devices.

## Afterwards, on the Porch {#afterwards-on-the-porch}

"While you slept, a number not in your contacts called at 09:30." Each call waits until its caller's Calls row lets them reach you, or until your work and admin time for a row that never rings; never counted, no badge, never a notification. Beside it: **Text back**, since writing is easier than calling, and **Dismiss**; its **⋮** (or a long press, a right click, the Menu key) holds **Call back**, **Listen**, **Add to contacts**, **Block**, **How they reach you…** and **Why?**. When the same number called again and that call rang, the line says so: you may have spoken already. Sioul cannot see your voicemail, and the section's **?** says so once: a caller may have left a message. The phone's own call history lists every declined call too, with Sioul's name.

If your operator can mail you each message with its sound (Free can: your subscriber area, Messagerie vocale ▸ Notification, with the audio file), name the domain its mail comes from in Settings ▸ Words ▸ **Voicemail by mail** ([Settings](settings.md#words)): the line then says "They left a message (0:42)." with **Listen**. The mail stays in your mail.

### Calls you missed, and yours that did not reach them {#calls-missed}

Once you let Sioul read the phone's call history (Settings ▸ Devices ▸ this phone's card ▸ What Android must allow ▸ **Call log**), the Porch also lists, on each of your devices, the calls you missed and those of yours that did not reach them: "Noa Linden · Missed call at 09:30, also at 09:41", "Your call at 14:10 was not answered", "Your call at 14:10 lasted 0:40: their voicemail, perhaps". Each waits while its caller may reach you, never counted, and goes by itself once you spoke with them, either way. **Text back** (or **Text them**) comes first, then **Call back** (or **Call again**) in its **⋮**, and **Dismiss**. Sioul reads the history from the day you allow it, never the months before.

A missed call from someone in your address books also makes a task, "Call back Noa Linden", and a call of yours to them that did not reach them, "Call Noa Linden again": in your usual list, for that day, tied to their card and to the call, without a reminder. One task per person: another such call adds its time to it, "Missed call at 09:30, also at 09:41.". The task is done by itself once you spoke with them: a call of theirs you answered, or one of yours long enough; open it again and it stays yours to close. These are two rules that come with Sioul, on unless you turn them off, in Settings ▸ Rules ▸ **Presets**, under Calls ([Rules](rules.md#calls)): there you turn each on or off, choose which calls make a task (theirs you missed, yours that did not reach them, or both), and copy one to make a rule of your own (only for the people you keep as safe, say): once saved, yours runs in its place, and the preset turns off, which Sioul says with Undo. In the Porch's ⚙ ▸ Calls, **A call that reached their voicemail**: a call of yours shorter than 90 seconds (or 60, or 120) likely reached their voicemail, not them; a call of yours that long or longer, or any call of theirs you answered, is one in which you spoke.

### A call's card {#call-card}

Each call is a thing of its own in Sioul, as a message or a task is. Click a row of calls on the Porch (or choose **The call's card** in its **⋮**), or a call on a person's sheet: its card says with whom, their number, when, how long, which way, whether it was answered (or sent to voicemail by your phone's screening), on which phone, and what it is tied to: the person's card, and the task it made. A task made for a call lists that call among its ties too. A phone keeps its calls a month; after that, the card says the call is no longer kept. AI agents you allow read your calls the same way, each by its address, and never write one.

### On your computers {#on-your-computers}

The calls your phone declined also wait on the Porch of your computers, at the same times, once your devices share ([Sharing between your devices](sharing.md), the part **Calls**): "While you slept, a number not in your contacts called your phone at 09:30." **Dismiss** on one device takes the row away on all of them, with ten seconds to undo: a call has nothing to read, so Dismiss is how you forget it. On a computer, **Text back** and **Call back** go through the app your system has for phone links (KDE Connect hands them to your phone), else **Copy the number**; **Add to contacts** opens a new card in Contacts, the number filled in. To keep the calls off your computers' Porch: the Porch's ⚙ ▸ Calls ▸ **Calls your phones declined**. To keep the list off one computer entirely, switch the part **Calls** off there.

A person's calls of the last month, rang or declined, are on their sheet: Contacts ▸ their card ▸ **How … reaches you**, or **How they reach you…** on a call's line. Each device keeps a call a month, then takes it out.

### Several phones {#several-phones}

When more than one phone shares its calls with your devices (a work phone and a phone of your own, each with a call in the last month), every line of every list of calls says which phone the call came to: on the Porch, on the phone's home card and on a person's sheet, the lines of the phone you hold included. By its number when it is known: "While you slept, Noa called you on 01 99 00 12 34 at 09:30.", "Missed call at 09:30, on 01 99 00 12 34"; else by its name, "your phone (Moss)", or "this phone" on the phone itself. With a single phone, the lines say no more than before.

Each phone's number is on its card in **Settings ▸ Devices**, under **This phone's number**. Sioul fills it in on the phone itself when Android gives it with the permissions Sioul already holds for your texts (reading them, and your SIMs' names): it asks nothing more. Many SIMs carry no number; then nothing is filled in, and the lines name the phone. What Sioul filled in counts until you change it: **Change the number…** (**Give its number…** while none is known), from any of your devices, typed as you write it; it travels with your settings, sealed. Emptied, the one Android gives comes back, when it gives one. A phone with two SIMs that do not give one number gets none: Android does not tell Sioul which SIM a call came on, so its lines name it by its name.

## What Sioul never does {#what-sioul-never-does}

It never answers a call, never records one, never listens, and sends no number anywhere: no server, no lookup. The list of calls your phone screened travels only to your own devices, sealed, while the part **Calls** of the sharing is on; so does a phone's own number, with what each device says of itself and with your settings ([Several phones](#several-phones)). Texts are held as other apps' messages are, by who wrote, when Sioul has Android's notification access ([Other apps' notifications, on Android](notifications.md#other-apps-on-a-phone)); Android's own blocked numbers stop a number's calls and texts for every app.

## Why it works this way {#why-it-works-this-way}

- 78 % of autistic adults avoid the phone where they can, and 61 % avoid voicemail too (Doherty et al. 2022); people anxious about admin find it the hardest channel (Holkar, Evans & Langston 2018). So a call can wait for its time, and the Porch says afterwards who called, with **Text back**.
- Not knowing what comes weighs: intolerance of uncertainty and anxiety go together in autism (Jenkinson, Milne & Thompson 2020). So the rules are yours, and each call that did not ring is said.
- One phone is often both a work tool and a personal one: rules that differ by phone keep a work number from ringing at dinner without silencing your family. Each column is a moment you already set (your hours, the night, busy), so nothing new has to be kept in mind.
- No study tests call screening itself: this rests on what people say they prefer, and on a plausible mechanism.

More in [what the research says](../dev/research.md), finding 34, and in [Screening phone calls](../dev/research/call-screening.md).

## Compared with other apps {#compared-with-other-apps}

Some go further than Sioul can. Google's Call Screen, on Pixel phones, answers a caller with a voice; in France only when you tap "Screen call", and it saves the screening audio unless you change that. Apple's Live Voicemail writes out what a caller says on the iPhone, and iOS 26 can ask an unknown caller why they call before the phone rings. Android keeps answering with a voice to the system's own phone app, so Sioul sends a call to voicemail and never answers it.

Truecaller and CallApp name callers from directories fed by their users' address books, which Sioul never does: it sends no number anywhere. Exodus Privacy finds 30 trackers in Truecaller's store build, 24 in CallApp's and 3 in Hiya's; its reports find tracker code and do not prove that it runs.

As of October 2026, from each app's own documentation and Exodus Privacy's reports ([the research](../dev/research/call-screening.md), §4.4 and §5.2).
