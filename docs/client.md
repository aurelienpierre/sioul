# The full client: mail, contacts, calendars

Sioul becomes a complete client while keeping its rule: **only what is used most is in view; the rest is one right click, or one fold, away.** This page reviews what mail, address-book and calendar clients usually offer (Thunderbird, Apple Mail, Outlook, Gmail, Fastmail, K-9/Thunderbird for Android, Evolution, KOrganizer), sorts each feature into three tiers, and says how Sioul shows it.

- **In view**: on screen without asking, at most six actions at a time, in two groups (answering; filing away).
- **One step away**: the right-click menu (also the "…" button and the Menu key, for the keyboard), or a folded section that opens in place.
- **Settings**: beside what they change: a ⚙ at the end of each page's first row, each setting with a sentence on what it does, saved at once into the configuration file, its comments kept (`settings.rs`, `config::set_value`); how long text reads (font, size, line spacing) in the ⚙ of the pages where messages are read, Mail and the Porch, and behind "Aa" in Notes (one setting, shown where it is used).
- **Accounts**, in three tabs: *your accounts*, one card per address with each of its services (mail; calendars, tasks and contacts; Google), each with a switch (off, it keeps its settings and is neither synced nor shown; its calendars wait in their folder, `vdir::set_account_off`), and for mail its rank and your name and signature in view, while what it is for, whether it is protected against harassment, how far back and how often are folded under "Settings for this address" (made when unfolded: a card holding them all took half a second on a phone), its server and folders folded too; "What this server offers" asks the server (`sioul_sync::scout`: the provider's mail settings, calendars and contacts at the well-known addresses, a Nextcloud's version and its apps, Files, Notes, Talk, Deck, said as in Sioul or not yet), and adds the mail of an address whose calendars are here with their password; *add an account*; *encryption*. Who may reach you, and when, is not an Accounts tab any more: Settings ▸ What reaches you holds it, the four lists with it ([attention.md](attention.md), "The interface"). Sites are not accounts: they are `[[site]]`, made and changed on the Sites page.
- **Settings**, in tabs: display, hours, reminders and notifications, your folder and sharing, invoices.
- **One setting, one place**, by what owns or uses it: an object's own settings where the object is made (an address's area, history, pace and shield on its card in Accounts; a project's routes on its page; a bank account's budgets and rules on its card); a page's display and behaviour behind its ⚙ (Mail: conversations, pace, how messages read, the lists you left; Tasks: their hours, types, lists, the categories of work and of your own, GitHub; the Porch: the projects it shows, letters, its sorting); what belongs to no single page in Settings (language, colours, your folder, the hours, reminders, invoices, sharing); what reaches you, and when, is Settings ▸ What reaches you ([attention.md](attention.md)); what sets a phone up, Settings ▸ This phone. A test fails when a setting shows in two places (`settings::tests::each_page_has_its_own`).

## Calm first
A full client must not bring back the inbox that screams. These rules come before any feature; when a feature conflicts with one, the rule wins ([design.md](design.md), [research.md](research.md)).

1. **Nothing counts at you.** No unread numbers, no badges, no red. A folder with something new shows a small dot; how many is said in words when you open it.
2. **New mail does not interrupt outside its times.** It waits in the Porch; at the times your lists let it through, one quiet notification per batch says how many, in words, and who ([porch.md](porch.md), "Notifications"), and mail that waited is told once when its time begins; the codes and links you ask sites for notify at once, and what you send yourself comes in any hour. The client's folders are for when you choose to look.
3. **Undo, never "are you sure?".** Archiving, deleting, junking and moving happen at once, with a quiet "Undo" for ten seconds; the server is told only after that. No confirmation dialogs, nothing lost to a slip.
4. **Sending has a breath.** "Send" waits ten seconds with an "Undo" before the message leaves; drafts save as you type, so closing the window never loses anything.
5. **One thing at a time.** The list shows who, what and when, without previews; the reader shows one message; quotes, attachments and details stay folded.
6. **Plain words, no alarm.** "Move to the trash", "Junk", "Not now"; destructive actions are labelled plainly, never red; errors say what happened and what to do, in the status line, never in a pop-up.
7. **A short past by default.** A folder shows its last two weeks; "Earlier" opens the rest on request, so no list is endless. All the mail is kept all the same: after a first sync of two weeks, older mail comes a round at a time (300 messages, the newest first), each round's size asked first (`RFC822.SIZE`) so the disk keeps a reserve of 5 GB or a twentieth of it, whichever is more; when older mail waits for room, the status line says so once. Kept for searching, and for what will learn on this device what is spam and what matters.
8. **The same place for the same thing.** Writing, the message's actions, the folders: always in the same spot, all reachable from the keyboard.
9. **Calendars without pressure.** What comes, from today; nothing "overdue" in red; one silent reminder a quarter of an hour before (before its time to get there; yours to set, each event its own), one the working day before, and the event's own alarms ([reminders.md](reminders.md)).
10. **Contacts are names first.** Details fold until opened.

### Writing in Markdown
- **You write Markdown**; the message goes out as HTML (CommonMark with tables and strikethrough, `pulldown-cmark`) and as plain text (the Markdown itself, readable as is), in `multipart/alternative`.
- **Replies and forwards**: your text is converted first, then the quoted message is appended below it, already HTML (made safe, [porch.md](porch.md)) or text converted, in a `blockquote`, under "On …, X wrote:"; a forward carries the original headers and its attachments.
- **Threading**: `In-Reply-To` and `References`, so every client threads the answer.
- **Sending is deliberate**: a button or Ctrl+Enter, never automatic; then a copy goes to Sent (Gmail keeps its own).

### Reading what comes from outside
Whoever writes to you, and any server you use, chooses words Sioul shows. None of them loads anything, runs anything, or holds the window.
- **A message's text**: HTML made safe in Rust (`reading::safe_html`, ammonia): only text, structure and web or mail links; no picture, style, script, form or frame, so no tracking pixel fetches ([porch.md](porch.md#reading-a-message)). Plain text is escaped, its links made (`reading::linkify`).
- **Short words from outside**: a sender's name, a subject, a contact's or a list's name, an event's title from a shared calendar or an invitation, a folder's or a calendar's name from a server, a site's or a caller's name. Qt would draw them as HTML when they look like it, a picture in them fetched, and a button holding one crashed the window; every page draws them as plain text ([qt-quick.md](qt-quick.md#words-from-outside-as-plain-text)). They are kept as written: a sender's name becomes a contact's as it is (`pim::add_sender`), and only the drawing changes. `tools/check-plain-text.py` holds every page to it, and `tools/qml-test.sh tst_plaintext.qml` shows such words in Sioul's buttons, menus, tooltips, lists, dialog titles and a person's sheet while a stand-in web server notes what is asked of it: nothing is fetched, nothing crashes (the security review of 10 October 2026).
- **Never held**: a message is read on a thread of its own, the invitation it carries with it (`read_message`), and its page says "Opening the message…" meanwhile, so that a large or crafted one never holds the window, nor the reminders of doses with it. The work grows with the message, never with its square: the HTML's spacer blocks go in one pass (`reading::without_spacers`); the blocks of repeated headers, the attributions and the next line that is not blank are found by reading the lines once, from the end (`reading::parts_with`); and before ammonia's parser, which looks through every element still open at most tags, elements nested deeper than 512 levels are left out, their words kept, where web browsers stop too (`reading::shallow`). Before, 8,000 lines of "From: x" (64 KB) took 36 seconds, and 8,000 empty blocks nested in one another nearly 3; each now takes milliseconds (`reading::tests::hostile_html_reads_in_linear_time`, `hostile_plain_text_reads_in_linear_time`, each bounded at 2 seconds in release, 20 in debug).

### Writing to the server
Sioul was read-only. The client writes, and only when you act: flags with `UID STORE`, moves with `UID MOVE` (else copy, flag and `UID EXPUNGE`, RFC 4315; a moved message's file follows it here at once, below, "A move keeps the message here"), sending through SMTP submission (RFC 8314, TLS from the first byte or STARTTLS), and the sent copy with `APPEND`. Folders are found by their special use (RFC 6154: `\\Sent`, `\\Drafts`, `\\Junk`, `\\Trash`, `\\Archive`), else by name. Each folder is a Maildir++ subfolder; flags and deletions made elsewhere are brought back at each sync.

### Signing in to the servers
- **A password**, from the system keyring: IMAP `LOGIN`; SMTP `AUTH PLAIN` or `LOGIN` (mail-send); always over an encrypted connection. A password refused by Google's IMAP server is said as wanting an app password: Google takes no account password there.
- **An access token, for an account signed in with Google** (`auth = "google"`: Gmail and Google Workspace, [google.md](google.md#mail)): SASL XOAUTH2, `base64("user=" login "\x01auth=Bearer " token "\x01\x01")` (`crates/sioul-sync/src/sasl.rs`). IMAP: `AUTHENTICATE XOAUTH2` through async-imap's `Authenticator`, the token at the first continuation, an empty line at the next (Google's refusal, its reason as JSON). SMTP: `AUTH XOAUTH2` sent by Sioul itself with mail-send's `cmd`, since mail-send's own XOAUTH2 answers the refusal's challenge with the token again instead of the empty line Google's page asks for. A refused token is asked again fresh, once (`imap::sign_in_with_tokens`, `send::send`); refused again, the account asks to be signed in again.
- **Generic**: who gives the tokens is `sasl::OAuth`, Google only. Microsoft's accounts would be one more arm of it, with their own sign-in page and token endpoint; the IMAP and SMTP exchange stays the same. Not built.

### Writing, as built
- **Line breaks are kept**: a new line in the text is a new line in the mail, as in a chat; a blank line starts a paragraph.
- **The signature** (Accounts, "Name and signature…") is put below the text when the draft starts, after the usual "-- " line, so you see and can change what goes out. Your name there is the one recipients see.
- **What you answer stays out of the way**: the window says "Below your text: Jane's message of …, quoted"; the quote is added when the message leaves. A draft keeps its own copy of the message it answers, so archiving or deleting that message meanwhile changes nothing.
- **Drafts live on this device**, saved as you type, listed under "Drafts" while there are some. They are not copied to the server's Drafts folder.
- **Answering your own message** (from Sent) goes to whom it went to, as other clients do.

### Writing from other applications
A mail link (`mailto:`) and what another application shares become a draft whose writing window opens; nothing leaves before Send (`sioul_core::handed`, `crates/sioul-app/src/outside.rs`).
- **`mailto:`** (RFC 6068, `sioul_core::mailto`): the addresses, before "?" and in `to`, then `cc`, `bcc`, `subject` (MIME encoded words decoded), `body`, and `In-Reply-To`, which threads the answer with its message; each address once, in any case. Percent-decoding as UTF-8, a wrong byte replaced; "+" stays "+". Nothing else: no `attach` or `attachment`, which some programs take and which would let a web page name one of your files, no `from`.
- **Which address writes**: the one asked (an address of yours chosen in Android's share sheet), else the first by priority among those that send, as for **Write**; **From** changes it.
- **Android**: Sioul is in the share sheet, each address of yours too (Android 10 and later), and opens mail links; shared files are copied into Sioul as they come ([android.md](android.md#sharing)).
- **Linux**: the desktop file makes Sioul a handler of `x-scheme-handler/mailto` (`Exec=sioul-app %u`). Chosen as the mail program (KDE: System Settings ▸ Default Applications; GNOME: Settings ▸ Apps ▸ Default Apps; anywhere: `xdg-mime default com.aurelienpierre.Sioul.desktop x-scheme-handler/mailto`), a link clicked runs `sioul-app mailto:…`.
- **One Sioul at a time**: a Sioul started while one of the same profile is open hands it its links and ends; the draft opens in the first, and a plain start brings its window forward. Before, a second start opened a second Sioul on the same files. They speak over a socket only you reach (`$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/sioul-<hash of the state folder>.sock`; in a Flatpak, its own runtime folder), named after the profile, so that a demo or a test profile never reaches yours. A socket left by a Sioul that ended is replaced; a Sioul that does not answer within five seconds is passed over, and the new one opens. On Wayland, the second hands on the token its launcher gave it (`XDG_ACTIVATION_TOKEN`, xdg-activation-v1) with its links; the first gives it to Qt (`qputenv`, `cpp/application.cpp`) before the draft's window opens and asks to come forward, so that the compositor brings it to the front rather than leaving it behind the window you clicked in (KWin's focus stealing prevention). Not tried on KWin yet; on X11, nothing is handed on (`DESKTOP_STARTUP_ID`). The window's pictures and the demo (`SIOUL_GRAB`, `SIOUL_DEMO`), which run side by side on one made-up profile, neither hand over nor listen.
- **"Send by mail" in a file manager** (`xdg-email`) names the files in `attach=`: not taken, as above. Dropping them on the writing window attaches them.
- **macOS** would need the bundle to declare `mailto` (`CFBundleURLTypes` in `packaging/macos/Info.plist`) and an event filter, since macOS hands the address to the open program as an event (Qt's `QFileOpenEvent`), not as an argument; macOS keeps one program open by itself. Not done.
- **Windows** would need the installer to register Sioul for `mailto` (`HKCU\Software\Clients\Mail\Sioul` with its `Capabilities\URLAssociations`, and `RegisteredApplications`), chosen in Settings ▸ Apps ▸ Default apps, and a way for a second program to reach the first (a named pipe: the standard library has no local socket there). Not done.

### A calendar file opened
A calendar file (`.ics`) opened in Sioul (1 November 2026; `crates/sioul-app/src/calendarfile.rs`, `agenda::calendar_file`, `CalendarFileDialog.qml`):
- **Who opens it**: the desktop file declares `text/calendar` and `application/ics` beside `x-scheme-handler/mailto` (`Exec=sioul-app %u`; the Flatpak and the AppImage install the same file); Android, a `VIEW` filter on `ShareActivity` ([android.md](android.md#sharing)). `sioul-app <file or file:// address>` with a Sioul of the profile already open hands the path over its socket, as a `mailto:` link goes (`outside::hand_over`, a `"calendar"` list; a file alone goes without `"open"`). A Sioul older than this, which answers only `"open"`, ends the talk without a word (`HandOver::Older`, told apart from a frozen one, which does not answer at all): never a second window beside it (review of 4d81220, L7); the file waits in `handed/calendar/` for this version, the older window is brought forward, and a notice says to restart Sioul to add it (`outside::to_older`, `calendar-file-older`). With no Sioul open, this one opens with the file. The window reads the file, a megabyte and one byte at most, into `<data>/sioul/handed/calendar/`, then shows one file at a time.
- **What is read** (`agenda::calendar_file`, the invitation's own parser): each VEVENT of a calendar of METHOD PUBLISH, REQUEST or none, with the VTIMEZONEs its times name; one object per UID (a series and its overrides together; an event without a UID is given one); its clock as written (a TZID, UTC, whole days: `clock_of_text`), its UID, summary, place, description, organizer and alarms kept; METHOD taken out (RFC 4791 §4.1), the head's other components (a task, a journal) left out. A file over a megabyte, one that is no calendar, one without events, and an answer or a cancellation (REPLY, CANCEL, COUNTER…, which the invitation's own path takes from mail) each give one calm sentence. Nothing in it is followed: no address fetched, no attachment opened.
- **The dialog**: each event's title, its day and times on the zone shown, both times when its clock is another (`agenda::clock_line`), its place and its organizer, all drawn as plain text; **Agenda**, the calendars that take events and can be written, the one new events go to first (`pim::calendars`); **Category or tags**, those tasks already take offered, any other typed; **Add to the agenda** and **Cancel** (or Close, for a file that does not open).
- **What is written**: through the agenda's one door (`vdir::write_item`, `agenda::guard`), into the agenda chosen, `CATEGORIES` added to each event (one line, each name once, those it had kept). An event whose UID that agenda holds already is written over, after the dialog's line saying so, and keeps the categories it was given before; never doubled. A meeting with others already there is refused, as every writer is refused it. No answer goes to the organizer. Said on the status line ("Added to Personal."), the agenda's sync nudged.
- **Tests**: `agenda::tests::a_calendar_file_is_recorded_once_with_its_categories` (a published file with Paris's VTIMEZONE, a quarter of an hour, an organizer, a description on several lines, two empty alarms: read, recorded with its categories and clock, read back, recorded again in place), `a_calendar_file_that_does_not_open_says_why`, `calendarfile::tests::a_file_handed_is_read_a_megabyte_at_most`, `outside::tests::a_second_sioul_hands_its_link_to_the_first` (a calendar file handed over, an older Sioul not asked).

### Selection, moves and conversations
- **Several messages**: Ctrl+click adds or takes out, Shift+click takes everything from the last one clicked, Ctrl+A all, Escape none. On a touch screen, a long press opens the row's menu and **Select** begins a selection; a tap then chooses or leaves a message (as Ctrl+click), until none is chosen. A bar then marks them read, archives, deletes or moves them, under one "Undo"; on a phone it wraps over two lines.
- **Dragging** a message (or the selection it is in) onto any folder of any account, from a folder or from a search's results (below): the folder under the pointer is outlined, a folded address unfolds, and while a search is open the folders come back in its place for as long as the drag lasts, at the same width, so the list does not move. With a mouse or a touchpad; on a touch screen a drag scrolls the list, and **Move to…** is the way (a phone shows the folders and the list one at a time anyway).
- **With the keyboard**: the Menu key or Shift+F10 opens a row's menu at the row; **Move to…** there, or in the selection's bar, lists every address's folders, the one the messages are in left out when they share one.
- **In one connection**: the messages of one account move as one set per folder (`UID MOVE` with a UID set, else `UID COPY`, `\Deleted` and `UID EXPUNGE` of the same set, RFC 4315), at most 200 UIDs a command, whatever their number (`mailbox::act_many`); reading, flags, archiving and the trash on a selection likewise, each account's server told once. Before, each message opened its own connection: sorting two hundred messages was two hundred logins, and marking them read two hundred at once. Junk, Not junk and the review's Spam and Not spam still go one at a time, for the spam filter's label log.
- **Into another account**, the message is appended there with its flags (`mailbox::move_across`), and taken off the first only once the second server has it: if the copy fails, the original stays; if removing it fails, both remain. A message a search found on its server only is read whole from there first (`move_across_from_server`).
- **The Porch's rows** are not dragged: the Porch has no folders beside it and is about deciding one message at a time; an open message's ⋮ has **Move to…**.
- **Folders kept here or on the server only**: right click on a folder, "Keep a copy here". A folder left on the server only shows muted, its copy here removed and where its sync stopped forgotten, so keeping it again brings it back whole (`skip_folders` in the account). The inbox is always kept. "New folder…" (under "More folders") makes one on the server (`CREATE`); "Delete this empty folder" takes one of yours off the server (`DELETE`), only when the server says it holds no message (`STATUS MESSAGES`), never a folder with a purpose (inbox, Sent, Drafts, trash, junk, archive).
- **Conversations** (Mail ⚙, "By conversation"): messages tied by Message-ID, In-Reply-To and References (never by subject alone), the newest of the folder first with the size of the conversation; unfolded, the others, oldest first, your answers from Sent among them (`threads.rs`).
- **Real time**: the checkbox in Mail, and **Mail every minute** in the card What reaches you now behind the status line's button (the Porch's box until part 5 of "What reaches you"), fetches every folder of every address each minute, and opens the Porch, until unticked.

### Searching
**Why** (7 October 2026): a feature for when it is needed, which not everybody needs nor needs often, so nothing of it shows until asked; and its main use is sorting an inbox into folders by hand, hence dragging messages onto folders.

- **The quick search** stays as it was: one field at the top of a folder, by sender, recipient and subject, in that folder, every message kept.
- **More…**, beside it, opens the search by conditions in the folders' place; on a phone, a page of its own, then the results as a page, Back going from the results to the fields, then to the folder. Nothing of it shows until asked. One condition at first, "Anywhere", holding what the quick search held; **Add a condition** for another; with two or more, **Mail that meets all of them** or **any of them**. Each condition is a field, a test and a value, on two lines, in plain words (`MailSearch.qml`).
- **The conditions are the mail filters' own** (`rules::Condition`, `rules::Join`; "Filters", below): sender, recipients (To, Cc), subject, text, attachment (there is one, none, one named …), kind of attachment, the day it arrived (before, after, between), size, who the sender is to you, newsletter or list; **Anywhere** (sender, recipients, subject or text), which a filter takes too; and three of the search's own, added there for it (`rules::Field::SEARCH`), which no filter reads: **Address**, **Folder** (by purpose, every address's inbox or archive, or by name), **Message** (read, flagged, answered; "is not read" is unread). Text is compared as the filters compare it: case and accents aside. The same words describe a filter and a search, so that a search can become a filter: **Make it a filter…** opens the filters' editor with them (`newFilterFrom` in main.qml; "Filters", below).
- **The results** fill the usual list, the newest first, each row saying where it is ("Inbox · noa@example.com"); a message opens beside the list as in a folder, the column folding away as the folders do, so one goes back and forth between the results and a message. Above them a sentence says what is searched ("Mail from …@bank.example…, arrived after 3 June, with an attachment."), and a line what was found ("12 messages here. Looking on the servers for the mail kept only there…"). **Clear** returns to the folder. At most 500 rows, the newest; past them, the line says how many there are.
- **Where**: every folder of every address but the junk and the trash, unless a folder condition names them. The mail kept here first, at once, on every core, each message read as little as its conditions need: its headers for most; the whole of it only for its text, its attachments, or "Anywhere" when the headers do not hold the words (`mailsearch::Stored`).
- **Then the servers**, after a pause in typing (at once with Enter), for what this device does not hold: the folders left on the server, and those whose older mail still waits for room on the disk (`fetch::kept`: a folder fetched whole needs no server; one fetched since a day, only when the search reaches back before it, or within a day of it). One connection per address, side by side (`sioul_sync::search`): each folder examined, read-only; `UID SEARCH` with the same conditions (FROM, TO, CC, SUBJECT, BODY, LARGER and SMALLER, SEEN, FLAGGED, ANSWERED, HEADER for List-Id, List-Unsubscribe, Precedence and Reply-To, NOT and OR; "Anywhere" as FROM, TO, CC, SUBJECT or BODY, never TEXT, which would find words in the servers' own lines; `CHARSET UTF-8` and literals for words that are not ASCII, non-synchronizing where the server offers LITERAL+, RFC 7888); what is kept here left out by its UID; then the newest 200 fetched without marking them read (`BODY.PEEK[HEADER]`, `BODYSTRUCTURE`, `RFC822.SIZE`, `INTERNALDATE`) and checked here on their headers and parts, for what a server cannot say exactly (a whole address, an attachment's name or kind, who the sender is to you; with "any of them", one of those asks the folder's newest whole). Their text, the server matched. A key may find more than its condition, never less, since what it finds is checked here: so "is not" a whole address or subject is left to the check here (a server finds words inside longer ones: jean-paul@ for paul@, "Re: …"), and so is "without attachments" outside Gmail (a list's footer makes a message `multipart/mixed` without any). The day it arrived is its Date, never later than it reached the server, as here (`porch::sent`): after a day, SINCE; before one, SENTBEFORE or BEFORE (mail moved from another server keeps an old Date and a new day of arrival); each a day wider than asked, since each server reads days by its own clock. Attachments: Gmail's own search where the server is Gmail (`X-GM-RAW "has:attachment"`, `filename:`), elsewhere the headers most such messages wear (`Content-Type: multipart/mixed`, or `multipart/signed`), then the parts. Past the newest 200 of a folder, the line says how many more the server found, "up to": those are not checked yet. A server that refuses the words of a search is asked again without them, the days, sizes and marks only, and the newest checked here (`imap_key_without_words`): GreenMail, for one, reads FROM, TO and CC as whole addresses, where RFC 9051 finds parts of them, and answers BAD to a part of one ("@bank.example").
- **Merged without twins**: a message kept here is never shown again from its server (same address, folder, UIDVALIDITY and UID), nor its copy in another folder of the same address (same Message-ID: Gmail's labels), the folder you sort from kept first (the inbox, your folders, the archive, Sent; "All Mail" last).
- **Said plainly**: "Looking on the servers for the mail kept only there…", then "3 more on the servers." or "Nothing more on the servers."; a server that cannot be reached or refuses is named with why, the rest shown all the same. A row found there says "on the server"; opened, the message is brought into its folder here first, whole and checked as a sync brings it, still unread (`search::bring`), then reads as any. Moved, archived or deleted from the results, it is acted on by its UID, without being brought.
- **Sorting from the results** is the search's main use: choose (Ctrl+A for all), then drag onto a folder or **Move to…**; the results follow at once, under one "Undo" for ten seconds.
- **On a demo profile**, the servers are never asked.

### Filters
**Why** (7 October 2026): filters in a calm mail manager, not a dashboard, each field shown only once it needs setting; one flat list for every address, narrowed by address when wanted; carried by the settings to every device, and run on the inbox's unread mail only, so that the first device to fetch new mail does the work and no two devices race; a button runs them on mail already read.

Mail ▸ ⚙, under **Filters**. Code: `crates/sioul-core/src/rules.rs` (conditions, filters, what they do to one message), `crates/sioul-sync/src/filters.rs` (on the server: the mark, the acts, what waits), `crates/sioul-app/src/filters.rs` and `qml/MailFilters.qml` (the editor, "Try it", the run on the inboxes).
- **A filter** is a name if you give one, on or off, the addresses it runs on (every one, or some), conditions (all of them needed, or one enough), actions, and whether the filters after it are still asked once it acts.
- **Conditions**: a field, a test, a value. Fields: From, To, Cc, Reply-To, Subject, Text (an HTML message read as text), Anywhere (sender, recipients, subject or text), Attachment (there is one, none, one whose name contains), Kind of attachment (a PDF, a picture, a document, an archive, an invitation, a sound, a video, a text file), Sender (someone you know, safe, neutral, restricted, a stranger: who they are to you, [porch.md](porch.md)), Newsletter or list (it is one, none, its List-Id contains), Day it arrived (before, after, between), Day of the week, Time it arrived (before, from, between, across midnight too), Size (larger, smaller than "5 MB"). Tests: contains, does not contain, is, is not. Text is compared as the search compares it: case and accents aside, each run of spaces one space; "is" takes a whole address or a whole name, any recipient's. A negative is its positive turned around, but a message that cannot tell (its size unknown, its sender not judged) holds neither way. A condition that does not read (a date written "1er octobre") never holds, and the editor says why.
- **Actions**: move to a folder (named as you read it, "Banque", or as its server writes it, "INBOX.Banque"); archive (the address's archive found by its purpose, as the Archive button finds it: its Archive folder, Gmail's All Mail, else one made; so that one filter archives on every address whatever its server calls it); mark as spam (`$Junk` and the address's Junk folder, as the Junk button does, but with no line in the spam filter's label log: a filter is not your word on one message); flag; mark as read; move to the trash (never deleted for good); add a keyword (an IMAP keyword, which other mail programs show as a tag; never `$Junk`, `$NotJunk` or `$SioulFiltered`). The first filter that moves a message decides where it goes; reads, flags and keywords add up; a filter that stops the others ends the list there (`rules::plan`).
- **Stored in the configuration**, one flat list for every address, in its order, each condition and action an inline table on its own line (`config::set_filters`, comments kept):
  ```toml
  [[mail.filter]]
  name = "Bank"
  accounts = ["home"]          # absent: every address
  if = [
      { field = "from", test = "contains", value = "@bank.example" },
  ]
  then = [
      { do = "move", folder = "Banque" },
      { do = "read" },
  ]
  stop = true                  # the filters after it are not asked
  # match = "any"              # absent: every condition
  # enabled = false            # absent: on
  ```
  Shared with your other devices as the rest of the configuration: the list is one entry, and the last change of it wins ([database.md](database.md)). A filter that does not parse is left out alone, never the whole file. One holding a field, a test or an action this Sioul does not know (a newer one wrote it, on another device) does not run here and says so in the editor; when this device writes the list again, that filter is written back exactly as it was, as long as it was not changed here (`Filter::unknown`), so that an older phone does not damage a newer computer's filters.
- **When they run**: on each inbox's arrivals still unread, right after the fetch that brought them, on whichever device fetches first: the window (after the spam filter, on the watcher's thread), the phone's background step (`steps.rs`), `sioul watch`. Never on an account's first fetch (its last two weeks), never on mail already there: that is yours to ask (below). Never on what the Porch keeps from them: mail it sets aside (forged, blocked, a borrowed name, your provider's spam), hostile mail, what your spam filter keeps for review, and a code you asked for (`rules::untouched`). A sender nothing authenticates is read as nobody's: a From or Reply-To condition does not hold for it, and Sender reads "a stranger", as a project's route does not take it ([porch.md](porch.md)).
- **No race between devices**: before acting, a device marks each message `$SioulFiltered` (`rules::FILTERED`) on its server and acts only on what it marked. With CONDSTORE (RFC 7162), the mark is stored `UNCHANGEDSINCE` the mod-sequence read with its flags: of two devices fetching the same message in the same instant, one marks it, the other's store comes back in `[MODIFIED]`, and that one leaves the message alone. Without CONDSTORE, the flags are read, then the mark set: two devices would have to read within the same round trip. A message read meanwhile, marked already, renumbered or gone from the inbox is left alone; a device that fetches later sees the mark, or no message. An act that fails (no such folder on that address, the server refusing) takes the mark off again, and the message waits for the next fetches here, three tries ten minutes apart at least, two days at most (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/filters/waiting.toml`: the account and the UID, never a word of the message), or for another device's. While something waits, every fetch runs the filters, new mail or not (`filters::due`). The window says why a filter failed in its status line, `sioul watch` in its output, a phone's background step in its log.
- **Servers without keywords** (no `\*` in the inbox's PERMANENTFLAGS: Exchange, Outlook.com) cannot carry the mark. There each device acts on what it fetched while it is still unread on the server: a move, a flag or a read mark done twice leaves the message as once, and a message the first device moved is no longer in the inbox for the second. A filter whose only action is a keyword does nothing there, said once, and is not tried again.
- **Told or not**: what a filter would move out of the inbox, put in the junk or the trash, or mark read is held back from the new-mail notifications until this device's filters have run (`filters::untold`, read by `mailnote`); the rest is told at once, as before. Then a message they acted on is not told, nor one read meanwhile or gone from the inbox, nor one another device marked first and is acting on. A message they could not act on (no such folder, the server refusing, no password, a renumbered inbox) is told as any new mail is, by the same rules of what reaches you (`Done::to_tell`, `mailnote::unfiltered`), on every device that fetched it; a retry that works later does not tell it again (the notifications' ledger). A message another device marked waits here too and is looked at again at the next fetch, which that device's act brings at once (it wakes the inbox's watcher): its mark gone and the message still there unread, this device acts on it itself, and tells it if it cannot; still marked ten minutes on, neither read nor gone (that device stopped half-way: closed, out of battery), this device finishes it: it takes the mark off, only if nothing changed the message since its flags were read (`UNCHANGEDSINCE` with CONDSTORE; of two devices doing so at once, one wins), marks it again as its own and acts, as for a message just arrived (`Found::Stuck`). So no message stays marked in the inbox with no filter ever acting on it. If that act fails, the message is told and tried again like any other; if the first device comes back and acts meanwhile, its act changes the message, and this device leaves it. `sioul watch` tells codes alone, which no filter touches. The Porch reads the inbox: a message moved out of it leaves the Porch too. Flagged or given a keyword, it stays and is told as usual.
- **The editor**, calm: one line per filter, the rest when asked for. Each filter is one sentence ("From contains “@bank.example” → into “Banque”, marked read, and no other filter"), with its switch, and ▴ ▾ to ask it earlier or later; with several addresses, **Show the filters of** narrows the list to one, ▴ ▾ then moving a filter among those shown. A filter opens in place: its name, one condition and one action at first, **Add a condition**, **Add an action**; each value appears once its kind is chosen (words, a day, two days, a time, days of the week, a size, a kind of file, who the sender is); "every condition" or "one of them" once there are two; the addresses and "the filters below are not asked" folded under one line. Each change is saved at once, the sentence said again, and what keeps the filter from running said under it ("Choose the folder it moves messages into."). A folder only some of its addresses have is said ("This folder is only on …"). A deleted filter comes back with **Undo** for ten seconds. On a phone, the same in one column, the panel taking the whole screen.
- **Try it** counts what the filter being edited takes among the newest 2,000 messages of the inboxes here, read or not, judged as the Porch judges them, and names three ("Banque des Berges: Votre relevé").
- **Run them on the inboxes…** applies them to the messages of the inboxes here, read ones included. A dry run first says what would change ("In your inboxes now, 12 messages would change, read or not.", then each filter with its count); **Run them now** does it after ten seconds with **Undo**, each account in one connection, without the mark; what it moves leaves the lists meanwhile. Codes, mail set aside and the spam filter's review stay as they are (`filters::preview`, `mail::schedule_filters`).
- **A search made a filter**: **Make it a filter…**, in the search's column, adds a filter at the end of the list with the search's conditions and opens its editor in Mail ▸ ⚙, to choose what it does. The search's address conditions become the addresses the filter runs on; a folder other than the inbox, and read, flagged or answered mail, are left out, and said above the editor (`filters::new_from`).
- **Not done**: other folders than the inbox (no use seen yet); "keep out of the Porch" (moving to a folder does it); ordering by drag (▴ ▾ instead); forwarding, answering, running a program.

### Unsubscribing
A newsletter or a list's message says how to leave it in `List-Unsubscribe` (RFC 2369). The Reader shows **Unsubscribe** above such a message, in the place of the old "Aa" (how text reads is in the page's ⚙). Code: `crates/sioul-core/src/unsubscribe.rs` (what is offered, the record), `crates/sioul-sync/src/unsubscribe.rs` (the POST), `crates/sioul-app/src/mail.rs` (the undo, the sending).
- **One click, three ways**, the first that applies:
  1. **One click** (RFC 8058): `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`, an `https:` address, and a DKIM signature that passed (Sioul's own check at arrival, else the provider's) and covers both headers, signed by the domain in From or by the address's own. Sioul posts `List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` as a form: no cookie, no authorisation, nothing of yours, fifteen seconds at most; redirects followed only within the same site (none to another site); any 2xx is done. The signature is found by the results header's `header.d`, `header.s` and `header.b`; when several DKIM-Signature headers match, a header counts as covered only if all of them cover it. Exactly one `List-Unsubscribe` and at most one `List-Unsubscribe-Post`: a second one, added on the way, would be read first; then nothing is offered.
  2. **A message** (`mailto:`): one recipient, the subject and text the address asks for ("unsubscribe" where it gives none), never its `cc` or `bcc`; sent from the address the message came to, through the usual sending (`sioul_sync::send::send`), so a copy goes to Sent (Gmail files its own).
  3. **A web page** only: it opens in the browser at once, said plainly; Sioul cannot finish it there.
- **Never** for forged mail, a borrowed name, a blocked sender or hostile mail, nor when nothing proves the address is the list's own: the message must be verified (its From domain vouches for it), or a valid signature covering `List-Unsubscribe` must belong to the domain of the address it names (a mailing list's own signature).
- **Spam, a matter of proof** (4 November 2026): from a verified sender (`Trust::Verified`), the way out is offered whatever the spam verdict (your provider's, your filter's, a doubt) and in a junk folder too, the message staying there: such spam often comes through a sending service that keeps the rules, to an address someone gave without consent, and a `List-Unsubscribe-Post` marks such a compliant bulk sender, whose mail people junk rather than leave ([research/spam-filter.md](research/spam-filter.md), Q2 and Q3, "Gray mail"). The proof is of the sender, never of the mail's worth: a pass is almost no evidence that mail is wanted ([research.md](research.md), finding 35), so the verdict stays as it was. From an unverified sender, spam and mail in a junk folder are refused ("set aside as spam", `unsubscribe::in_junk`), and so is a doubt of your own filter's (since 3 November 2026; **Not spam** brings the button back, the message then out of the filter's reach, `porch::protected`): answering would tell whoever forged it that your address is read. A web address must be a name on the Internet: no IP address, no local name (`localhost`, `.lan`, `.local`, `.internal`), no "name@" before the host. The button then rests, dimmed; its tip, and a click, say why.
- **Ten seconds to undo**, as for sending: "Unsubscribing from Type & Pixels in ten seconds." with **Undo** in the status line; then "Unsubscribed from Type & Pixels.", or why not ("its server answered 404", "its server sent Sioul to another site", "did not answer within 15 seconds"). A second click while it waits asks nothing twice. The window closing does what waits, as for sending.
- **The message stays where it is**: unsubscribing is about the next ones. Archive or delete it as any other.
- **Kept on this device**: `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/unsubscribed.toml`, one entry per list (its List-Id, else the sender's address), with its name, the sender, the way and when. A list left shows **Unsubscribed**, resting, its tip saying when and how, and that blocking the sender is the answer if its mail keeps coming; a page opened is said, and can be opened again. The Mail page's ⚙ lists them under "Lists you left". Not shared with your other devices.
- **The demo profile** sends nothing: the click says so.

At the end of each full pass that went through, under the account's fetch lock, the old numbering's files are removed when the new numbering holds a copy of the same message (one to one, by Message-ID, else by a digest of the From, To, Date and Subject lines). The unmatched ones go only for a numbering noted here, once the folder is fetched whole and every UID the server lists for it (`UID SEARCH ALL`, asked once, only then) is here under the new numbering: a numbering left before this build, or seen first by an older Sioul, keeps what nothing pairs with. One line per removal in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/sync/<account>.removed.log` (when, the folder, the old and new UIDVALIDITY and UID; no subject, no address).

### The index behind the mail, and one address for a message
The unified mailbox's part 1 (1 November 2026):
- **One small index per folder** (`sioul_core::mailcache`), `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/sioul/mail-index/<account>/<folder>.tsv` ("INBOX.tsv" for the inbox, ".Sent.tsv", a folder held within another under `nested/` by a hash of its path), readable by you alone (0600, its folders 0700), never shared nor synced, removed with the address (`maildir::forget_account`). Its first line names its format and a stamp of the one column that depends on your settings (whether a message carries a one-time code: the words that find codes); another first line, and the file is made again from the messages: it is a cache, nothing is migrated, nothing is lost.
- **One line per message**, keyed by the part of its file name that never changes (when it was stored, its UIDVALIDITY and UID): when it arrived, its Date, Message-ID, sender's name and address, recipients, subject (300 characters at most), whether it comes from a list, its attachments' names, your provider's spam flag, the UID of the invitation it carries, its Authentication-Results, In-Reply-To and References as written (which servers are trusted is decided when read, so no stamp is needed for them), whether it carries a code, and, without a Message-ID, its digest. The folder stays the truth: its file names say what is there, read or not, answered, flagged; another mail program writing into the Maildir is seen as Sioul's own writes are.
- **Each message is parsed once, ever**: a name the index does not know is read whole once and its line added at the end, under the file's own lock (`filelock::with_lock`), so that the window, the phone's background step and `sioul mcp` never add a line twice. Lines whose files are gone stay until they make half the file; it is then written again beside and renamed. A stop half-way leaves at most a cut last line (no line end: never taken; its message read again), or, before a rename, the old file whole.
- **`MailIndex` reads these files** and keeps every copy of a Message-ID with its folder's purpose, the one that ranks first in front; it writes no file of its own. The older single file, `mail-index.tsv`, is removed once per device, when `mail-index/` is first made; this version neither reads nor writes it. An older Sioul still running (an older window, an older `sioul mcp`) finds it gone, reads every message's headers once and writes it again for itself; nothing new depends on it, and it goes once nothing has written it for two weeks.
- **The folders' lists read lines** (`mail::folder_cards`): a refresh of Mail parses no message, Gmail's archive leaves out the inbox's and Sent's copies by their lines' Message-IDs, and only the message you open is read whole. A line's card carries its code flag (`Card::code`), so that a list's DMARC failure with a code is judged as when the message was parsed.
- **One address for a message**: `mid:<Message-ID>`, or `sioul:mail/<account>/<digest>` for one without a Message-ID (16 hexadecimal digits of SHA-256 over its From, To, Date and Subject lines as the file holds them: `mailcache::digest`, the same function `renumbered::key_of` pairs old copies with). Links, ties, budget lines and agents read it; a tie, a task or a note made from such a message, mail a project takes as it arrives, and a money row write it.
- **Finding a message** (`sioul_core::mailfind::find`), for Rust, the command line and agents: the hinted file where it is now; the hinted account, folder and UID in the folder's current numbering (the sync state's UIDVALIDITY); the address in the hinted account, the copy that ranks first, the current numbering before an old one; the address in any account, ranked the same. Copies of one Message-ID that are different messages (their senders differ, and so does their Date or Subject: a list that rewrites the sender is the same message): the hint decides, else the copy that arrived first, and the answer says "Another message carries the same id." An older `file:` key (a money row's, a tie's) finds its message by its headers, else by the line its folder's index kept, else, once a renumbering removed it, through the removals' log. Answers: here, not here, on its way (part 3, below), gone, not reachable, kept (agents).
- **No copy here** (`find_or_ask`): each address's server is asked, read only, the way a search asks (`UID SEARCH HEADER Message-ID`, every folder it lists examined, each copy's own Message-ID compared whole), the copy that ranks first brought by search's own `bring` (unread stays unread) and found again. "Gone" is said only when every server answered and none holds it; a server that cannot be reached, and the demo profile, which asks none: "Sioul cannot reach your mail server now." A digest is never asked of a server. The window does not call it in this part (its opener comes with part 2, off its thread), nor does the phone's background step; a test holds that sioul-app never does.
- **An event's invitation by its UID** (`pim::invitation_of`, the event card's Answer…, asked on the window's thread): the organizer's newest message whose calendar names the event's UID, from the UID each folder's index read from the message's calendar part once (`MailIndex::invitations`), the copy that ranks first; no message is read there any more (before, the organizer's eight newest messages were).
- **A hinted file is kept inside your Maildirs** (`mailfind::inside`): written as it is, `..` folded without asking the system, it must start with one of the configured addresses' Maildirs (within the hinted account's own, and not inside another address's Maildir held within it), name a message file of one of its folders (`cur/` or `new/`, plain names, never `tmp/`), and, resolved by the system, the file and its folder must still be inside that Maildir; a link to a file or a folder elsewhere is refused. Anything else is not looked at, and the address alone counts.
- **Tests:** `mailcache::tests` (made, added to, compacted, made again on another format or stamp; two writers adding at once; a stop half-way, a cut digest never taken; a file another program wrote; a line read back as written; the older single index gone once, left alone after), `maildir::tests::forgetting_an_address_takes_its_indexes`, `mailfind::tests` (one test per row of the ids' table: a flag changed, a move here, a renumbering and an older `file:` key through the line and the log, Gmail's labels, a message to yourself, two addresses on one list, no Message-ID, one id from two senders, a move elsewhere through stand-in servers; every form of a hint outside the Maildirs; one digest, read by links and the index), `consent::tests::an_accounts_mail_can_be_kept_whatever_its_projects` (a digest address's account), `budget::tests::mail_without_an_id_is_keyed_by_its_address`, `mail::tests::a_folder_list_from_lines_equals_the_one_parsed`, `a_refresh_of_mail_parses_no_message`, `the_window_never_asks_the_servers_for_a_message`, `pim::tests::an_invitation_is_found_by_its_events_uid`, the MCP's `mail_is_read_by_its_address`, and against GreenMail `search::tests::greenmail_find_asks_the_servers`. Each fix was taken out once and its test seen failing (the swaps), then put back byte for byte.
- **Measured** on a stand-in Maildir of 20,000 invented messages over 20 folders: `mail::tests::measure_the_folders_on_a_large_stand_in` (ignored; run by hand).

### A move keeps the message here
The unified mailbox's part 3 (2 November 2026). Before, a message Sioul archived, junked, put in the trash or moved left this device: its file was removed, and the copy came back with the next round of the other folders (within five minutes on a computer; on a phone, only once the window was up again).
- **The server says where it went** (RFC 4315, UIDPLUS): a `UID MOVE` answers COPYUID untagged, before its expunges (RFC 6851 §4.3), a `UID COPY` in its OK, an `APPEND` into another address APPENDUID: the folder's UIDVALIDITY and the message's new UID. async-imap's `uid_mv`, `uid_copy` and `append` keep no code, so Sioul sends these commands itself and reads every answer to the tagged one (`mailbox::command`, `answers`; APPEND's literal written once the server says "+"), with imap-proto's reading of the code (`ResponseCode::CopyUid`, `AppendUid`). Trusted only from a server that lists UIDPLUS, and only one to one: both sets as long, no UID paired twice otherwise (GreenMail repeats members, "2,2:3", which agree), no 0, no set past 800 UIDs. A set of more than one message is never paired by the order of the server's text (imap-proto reads a range "4:2" as 2:4): once the move's commands are done, the destination's UIDs are read with their Message-IDs (`UID FETCH … BODY.PEEK[HEADER.FIELDS (MESSAGE-ID)]`) and each file is paired with the one destination UID carrying its Message-ID (`mailbox::confirmed`, review of 8b57a35, M1); one without a Message-ID, or whose Message-ID is carried twice in the set or at the destination, is not paired, and takes the path below of a server that did not say.
- **The file follows at once** (`mailbox::follow`): renamed into the new folder's `cur/` (`new/` without flags), its name's U part (`U<UIDVALIDITY>-<UID>`) made the new one, the rest kept (when it was stored, `.sioul`, its letters as the act left them: `$Junk`'s for Junk, `$NotJunk`'s for Not junk), so that every version reads it as before (`maildir::origin_of`, `unique_part`). One `rename`, under the account's fetch lock (the one a fetch holds for a whole folder), waited for 20 seconds at most; across disks (another address's Maildir elsewhere), written in the folder's `tmp/` first, its time kept. The next sync of that folder finds the UID kept (`fetch::to_fetch`) and fetches nothing twice; its reconcile finds it on the server. The folder's index reads the file once (a fresh line; its digest and address come from its bytes, unchanged); the old folder's line stays until that index is compacted, so that a hint to the old file still finds the message. Every move: Archive, Junk, Not junk, the trash, a move to a folder or a project's, a selection's (each file paired with its own UID), the spam filter's, a mail filter's (`filters::follow_here`), the CLI's; into another address (`move_across`: the file into that address's Maildir; `move_across_from_server`: the copy written there, checked as a sync writes mail).
- **The window follows too**: `mail_moved` ([[before, after], …]) keeps a message opened by a link or the opener open on its file now (`MailPage.followMoved`); one chosen in a folder's list leaves it with its file, as before. Links, the home card and agents name messages by their address, and find the new copy at once.
- **When the file cannot follow, it goes, as before**: the folder kept on the server only (no copy will come); another address's Maildir covering this one (`renumbered::shared_with_others`, only when a configuration file exists); no COPYUID; the lock not coming in time; the file removed meanwhile by a sync of its folder. A copy there already (Gmail's All Mail holds the inbox's mail; a sync of that folder fetched it first): the file goes, nothing renamed. One copy here, never two.
- **On its way** (`sioul_core::mailmoved`): before a move's command is sent, each message is noted (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/sync/moved.toml`, 0600, written beside and renamed under its lock: the account, the address, the folder, when; no older Sioul reads it). The note goes when the file follows, and when the server refuses the move (nothing moved); it stays when the answer does not come (the move may have been made), when the server did not say where, or when the file could not follow. While it stays and no copy is here, finding the message answers "on its way" (`mailfind::Found::OnItsWay`), never asking the servers: the reader says "On its way to Archive · work@example.org. It opens as soon as its copy is here." and asks again at each sync's publish, as for an unreachable server; agents are told the same. It goes when a fetch of that folder finds the copy there (by the folder's index, only when notes wait for it), with its address or folder forgotten, and after three days: long enough for a phone whose window opens once a day (its background step fetches the inboxes only), short enough that a message moved again elsewhere is not said on its way for long. Readers write nothing.
- **A stop half-way** leaves one copy here, never none for long and never two: stopped before the server's move, nothing changed; the server moved but the answer was lost or the file did not follow, the old file stays until its folder's next reconcile removes it (the server no longer holds that UID there) and the new folder's next fetch brings the copy, the note saying "on its way" meanwhile; renamed, nothing else is needed (the sync state moves past the UID at the next fetch); a note left behind a renamed file is never read while the copy is here, and goes at that folder's next fetch. A sync of the new folder during the move either waits for the rename or fetched the copy first (then the old file goes); a renamed file carries that folder's UIDVALIDITY and the UID its server gave there, so no sync removes it as unknown.
- **Tests**: `mailmoved::tests::a_move_is_on_its_way_until_its_copy_comes`, `maildir::tests::a_moved_file_takes_its_new_place`, `mailfind::tests::a_move_noted_is_on_its_way_until_its_copy_is_here`, `mailbox::tests::where_the_server_put_a_message_is_read_pair_by_pair`, `a_moved_message_follows_into_its_folder` (renamed once; a copy there already; no COPYUID; shared; a folder kept on the server only; the lock held too long; removed first), `a_move_refused_is_not_on_its_way`, `filters::tests::what_a_job_leaves_here`, `openmail::tests` (on its way), the MCP's `mail_is_read_by_its_address` (on its way), `tst_openmail.qml`; against GreenMail, `mailbox::tests::greenmail_a_move_keeps_the_message_here` (archive, junk, the trash, a move, a selection, into another address, the next syncs fetching nothing twice, another device seeing each message once, a server without UIDPLUS, a stop between the server's move and the file's, a move during a sync of its folder, a sync holding the lock too long) and `filters::tests::greenmail_filters_act_once_and_try_again` (a filter's moves). ### The mailbox
The unified mailbox's parts 4 and 5 (2 November 2026): every address's mail in one list, in time order, on a computer and a phone.
- **Where**: **All addresses** at the top of Mail's left column, chosen when Mail opens, on a computer and a phone (decisions 8 and 9; on a phone the folders button shows the addresses and their folders, made only while they show). New mail's notification of several messages opens it at its top, on both devices (decision 3; `openThing` "mailbox", `MailPage.openBoxTop`; on a phone `sioul-open:mailbox`, a notice an older Sioul posted, `porch`, opening the Porch as before).
- **Made** by `sioul_core::mailbox::page` from each folder's recent lines, kept in the window's process (`mailcache::recent`), off the window's thread with Mail's job (`mail::views`, coalesced): after each sync's report, each act and when the page shows; a hidden page asks for nothing (`open_mailbox("")`). Only a shielded address's mail from someone not let in is read, once per process, for the shield's reading (`shield::reading`), never written down.
- **What it lists** (§4.1): the inbox, your folders and the archive (received), Sent (sent), the junk (spam), Gmail's All Mail as the archive where there is no other; never drafts, the trash, what waits for its Undo, or blocked senders' mail.
- **Each message once** (decision 6): copies fold by address (`mid:`, else the digest), every kind and address kept; the copy that ranks first opens (the inbox, your folders, the archive, Sent, the junk, All Mail; the first to arrive). "You, to Jane" for a message you sent yourself and also received. What the row does acts on each address's first-ranked copy (`Row::keys`), never two copies of one address: Gmail's labels are one message, and deleting its All Mail copy would delete it everywhere. It reads as read, and flagged, as those copies are: a received one read, it is read (opening Gmail's inbox copy clears the dot while its All Mail copy's letters wait for that folder's next round). Where labels make one message (an address with All Mail), a copy waiting for its Undo, in the trash or in the junk is where the message is: that address's other copies are left out until their folders' next round (the trash is read for such an address alone).
- **Order** (decision 11): by when sent, never later than it arrived (`porch::sent`), newest first; days as the list's sections ("Today", "Yesterday", "Monday 26 October", the year when not this one; `mailbox-day`); the time on each row. Two weeks, then two more at each **Earlier messages** (decision 15), shown past the window's end.
- **A row** (§4.4): one line, the addresses' marks (a letter on their tint, never the same letter twice: in priority order, the first free one of the local part's initial, the domain's, then the local part's next letters; the whole address in the tooltip and to screen readers; none with one address), the dot, who, the subject (plain text, as every outside word), answered, an attachment, flagged, spam's quiet word or "forged", the time. No preview.
- **Chips** (§4.6): **All** (received and sent), **Unread**, **Sent**, **Spam**, one at a time. Kept on this device (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/mailbox-view.toml`: chip). The quick search looks through every address, everything kept; **More…** opens the search by conditions. No count anywhere (decision 13).
- **The filter** (4 November 2026; [areas.md](areas.md#a-pages-filter-by-area)): beside the chips on a computer, in the folders' sheet on a phone, the areas ticked (`AreaFilter.qml`: as the hours say, or as you ticked until they change) and each address's mark switched on or off (its outline alone when off). What shows is the mail of the addresses switched on whose area is ticked (`mailbox::Filter`, which the window's `mail::mailbox_filter` fills from this device's file); a row two addresses hold shows when one of them does. The window asks the list anew at each change (`tick_mail_areas`, `mail_areas_as_hours`, `switch_mail_address`, then `open_mailbox` fresh), and again when the time changes (`refresh_mode`).
- **Spam** (§5.2, 3): a Junk copy ("in the junk", or your filter's class when it moved it), one your filter flagged where it lies (its log by place: "probably spam", "maybe spam"), your provider's flag on a stranger's mail ("your provider: spam"; someone let in, in your books or on a list is no stranger); what you said wins (`$NotJunk`, the label log). Spam rows under Spam only, never a dot. Proven mail from someone you deal with (part 2 of 4 November 2026; [spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md#proven-mail-your-provider-called-spam)) is never spam on your provider's word: flagged, it stays in All, marked "Your provider marked it as spam."; in a Junk folder, unless the matrix's row says to do nothing, you said anything of it or it was brought back once, it shows in All, marked "Your provider put it in Spam.", as it does once brought back into the inbox. A Subject whose tag Sioul's check took off shows restored, with the first mark; in a folder's list too, whose rows read the index and so know of your provider's word only that tag (`view::provider_mark`).
- **Calm** (§4.7): the filter says what rests (no line per resting address since 4 November 2026; agents still have them said, `mailbox::addresses`). **What is not shown now** (5 November 2026, `mail::hidden_parts`): one line at the head of the list names it, on a computer and on a phone, each part with what brings it back: an area whose mail rests as your hours say, or that you left unticked (**Show it** ticks it with those ticked now); an address switched off in this list, a view of this list alone, its mail staying in its inbox (**Switch it on**); mail older than the weeks shown (**Show older…**). The list's end is never blank: "Mail older than two weeks:" with **Show older…**, or "Nothing older is kept here." The dot: unread, received, not spam, forged or hostile, and its cell shown now in the matrix (`Attention::mail_from`; ○ Later: no dot, decision 12). A shielded address's hostile mail: "Someone at example.org", its words hidden.
- **Spam and Not spam** (4 November 2026), offered and never asked, since your own word is the best label there is and spam detection must not become one more job ([research/spam-filter.md](research/spam-filter.md), Q3, "Labels, best first", and §2): on every row your filter flagged (probably spam, maybe spam, probably not spam), your provider's word on a stranger's, and every message in a junk folder: beside its quiet word on a computer (the mailbox's rows and a folder's), in the row's menu instead of Junk or Not junk (a long press on a phone), and in the reader. The act is the Porch's own (`mail::act`, `act_many` on each address's copy: Spam into the junk folder, or kept there marked; Not spam out of it into the inbox, or left where it lies with `$NotJunk`; the label logged by sync's `mailbox::act`). While its Undo is offered, what you said counts at once (`mailbox::World::waiting`, from `mail::waiting_words`): the mailbox's row is not hidden but changes kind (Spam: under Spam, "in the junk"; Not spam: received, its words gone), and in a folder a Not spam where it lies keeps its row, its flag gone (`folder_waiting`); a move in or out of the junk leaves its folder as any move does.
- **Pages** (§6): the rows' words are made for the rows the list holds or wants alone (`Ask::rows`, sixty at first, sixty more each time the list's end comes into view while `more` says the window holds more), after every message of the window was sorted. Each page of sixty crosses as its own JSON (`mail::page_texts`: keys relative to the folder they share, `prefix`; false flags, empty words and a single key left out; the marks as places in `addresses`), by the signal `mailboxPage`, and only when its text differs from the one last given at its place (`mail::changed_pages`; forgotten when the window asks `fresh`, its list empty: a new page, another chip, address, search or resting address). A new message at the top shifts every page held, which each cross again; **Earlier messages** brings its rows after those held, one page. The window holds them in one ListModel (`boxModel`, its rows changed in place, so the list keeps where it was) and, as plain rows for the menu, the selection and the opener, `boxRows`. Part 4's 500-row page went: the pages serve both devices.
- **The kept lines** (`mailcache::recent`): each folder's lines from the window's start on, kept in the process, taken again without listing the folder or reading its index while nothing says it changed: `cur/`'s and `new/`'s times (a flag rename, a new file, a removal all change their directory's), the index's length, time and inode (another process adding to it, a compaction, an index made again), and the stamp of the settings its lines depend on. A directory changed within two seconds of its listing is not trusted (its clock's tick may hide a second change: git's "racy" index), nor one dated ahead of the clock (a clock stepped back); times are compared as equal or not, never as older. A search reads every line, never kept.
- **The first index in steps** (§6): a folder looked at for the first time in the process has its newest 500 messages read at once (`FIRST_FILL`, by when they arrived), the others waiting; while some wait and the mailbox shows, the job looks again 150 ms after publishing, reading 500 more over every folder (`STEP_FILL`), until none waits or a look reads nothing (a file that does not read). The job waits while Sioul is away and runs when it is back (`backend::coalesced`); the background step never makes the mailbox. The links' world (`links::Loaded`, `MailIndex::build_ranked`) still reads every folder whole the first time it is made, as before.
- **What you do**: a click opens it through the window's one opener (by its address, its file a hint), beside the list on a computer, over the page on a phone (its reader made while it shows there); Back closes it, the list where it was. On a phone a long press opens the row's menu, its **Select** begins a selection a tap then adds to, and the bar acts on it. The selection (Ctrl, Shift), the bar, the row's menu, the reader's buttons and dragging onto a folder are a folder's, across addresses (`mail::act_many`, `move_many`, each server told once, one Undo); a row's keys travel as one list (`mail::rows_of`), counted as one message. Moved into a folder, only the copy of that folder's address moves (else the row's first): another address's copy stays where it is, never a second copy beside the first.
- **Its colour** (decision 10): Accounts' card of each address (with more than one), eight tints that read in both themes (`Theme.tints`), or as its rank gives it; `colour = 0..7` in its `[[account]]` of config.toml, carried by the sharing so every device draws the same; an older Sioul skips the key.
- **The phone** (§4.4, §6): two lines a row, the marks, the dot, who and the time on the first, the subject and its quiet marks on the second; twelve items a row at most (no layout, no handler of its own; the marks one text, `MailPage.marksText`), 400 for the whole page. When the mailbox shows on a phone, the other folders' round is asked at once (`bringFolders`: every mail watcher nudged, its round then bringing the other folders, as Sync now does but said nowhere; once a minute at most, decision 19).
- **Agents** read the same list (the unified mailbox's part 6): the `mailbox` tool, as data, under per-project consent, what is kept out left out before anything else inside the page's making (`mailbox::page_with`, its `keep_out` and `closed`/`blocked` counts), the resting lines said first from the same addresses (`mailbox::addresses`, which the window's `mail::mailbox_page` uses too) ([mcp.md](mcp.md#the-mailbox)).
- **Tests of the filter and of Spam and Not spam** (4 November 2026): `areafilter::tests` (the hours' ticks time by time; yours holding until the hours change, a day at most, settled for good; the button's words in both languages), `mailbox::tests::the_filter_shows_the_addresses_ticked_and_on`, `mail_ticks_what_the_hours_bring_and_nothing_at_night`, `spam_and_not_spam_change_the_row_at_once_and_after`, `unsubscribe::tests::unsubscribing_follows_proof_not_the_verdict` (verified or not × spam, maybe or ham × one click, a message or a page; in a junk folder), `mail::tests::mail_s_filter_is_kept_on_this_device`, `what_you_said_waiting_for_its_undo_shows_at_once`, `tst_areafilter.qml` (the button, the boxes, As the hours say, the "?", a phone's edge) and `tst_mailbox.qml` (the filter beside the chips, the switches, no resting line, Spam and Not spam on rows, menus and the reader, a folder's flagged row, the filter in a phone's folders' sheet within 412 points).

### Categories and duplicates
- **Categories** are vCard's `CATEGORIES`, what Nextcloud Contacts shows as groups. Every line of a card is read (several are merged; an escaped comma, `A\,B`, stays inside a name), each name once, compared trimmed, case and accents aside (`contacts::category_key`, as the sender lists compare them, [porch.md](porch.md)), kept as the card writes it. They are written back only when changed, then as one line where the first was; a card saved by what does not give its categories (an import, a sender added) keeps them as they are.
- **A person's sheet**, from any card or a message's sender (`PersonSheet.qml`, its words from `reaches.rs`): "How Camille reaches you": their list and why (their own choice, a category, or their address book), the list chosen for them, all their addresses and numbers, those added later too (the card's UID on that list, `contact:<UID>`; their addresses' and numbers' own entries taken out), Always through, and a sentence per channel of what reaches you from them at each time; an address or number of theirs that keeps a list of its own is said, and comes first ([attention.md](attention.md), "The interface"). It replaced the card's "Their list" and a message's "Their mail". The choices and the sentences are the lists' (`senders::person_json`), the sheet only shows them.
- **Phone numbers are compared, never rewritten** (`phones.rs`): each gets a key, its international form with its extension. Spaces, dots, dashes, slashes and parentheses aside; "00", or the country's own prefix for abroad ("011" in North America), read as "+"; a trunk 0 kept after a country code ("+33 (0)6…", "+33 06…") dropped, Italy's 0 kept; a national number with its trunk prefix given the code of the country chosen in the Contacts settings (`[contacts] region`), else of the system's locale (LC_ALL, LC_TELEPHONE, LANG), else of Sioul's language; extensions kept (`;ext=`, "ext.", "poste", "x", "#", ","); short codes ("3631"), numbers with letters, stars or hashes compared as written. The countries known: France with its overseas departments (Réunion and Mayotte, Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana: the same ten-digit plan, each its own code), Belgium, Switzerland, Luxembourg, Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland, Poland, Greece, the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, India, Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire. Elsewhere, numbers without their country are compared as written. libphonenumber knows every country, for megabytes of data: not for a comparison. E-mail addresses compare case aside.
- **On one card** (`duplicates::clean`): the same number or the same e-mail address written twice. The line kept is the one written internationally, else the one saying most of what it is for; it takes the kinds and the "use first" of the lines that go, and its value is never rewritten; an Apple label (`item1.X-ABLabel`) goes with its line, or to the kept line when that one has none. REV renewed, one write per card, read-only cards left out.
- **Two cards** (`duplicates::pairs`): the same name (case, accents, punctuation and the order of words aside: "DUPONT, Jean" is "Jean Dupont"), the same number or the same e-mail address. What more than four cards share says nothing of one person (a switchboard, an office). Read-only cards and cards waiting for their deletion are left out; pairs come one at a time, those sharing most first. "Not the same" is remembered on this device, by UID, in `~/.local/state/sioul/contacts-not-the-same.toml`.
- **Merging** (`duplicates::merge`): the card whose name is kept stays line for line; the other adds its numbers and addresses (then cleaned as on one card), postal addresses, web sites and chat addresses unless the same, its categories (one line), its notes after a blank line unless one holds the other, what the first lacks of one value (organisation, role, birthday, photo…), and another application's lines (`X-…`) unless the first holds the same (since 3 November 2026; never the other card's own Apple id, `X-ABUID`, `X-ABShowAs`, which goes with it); Apple's groups get free names; a vCard 4.0 line going into a 3.0 card is written as 3.0 (`as_vcard3`). The other card is deleted; the sync sends both changes (PUT with If-Match, DELETE).
- **Undo**: before any change, the cards as they were are written to `~/.local/state/sioul/contacts-undo/<time>-<clean|merge>.json`, kept thirty days. "Undo" writes them back: a card deleted comes back under its file name and the sync sends it again; a version changed meanwhile (a sync, an edit) is set aside beside the record first. Address books kept on this device only work the same, without the sync.

Recurrences are expanded for display (RFC 5545 §3.3.10); times are kept with their time zone and shown in the zone shown (`zone::current`: this device's own, found by `zone::system_of` from `TZ`, `/etc/localtime`'s link, then the zone Android said at its change while that word is fresh (six minutes, as the doses trust it: `zone::Recorded::fresh`), then jiff's own detection, then that record however old; looked at again each second since jiff keeps the system's for five minutes; or the one chosen in Settings ▸ Agenda ▸ Time zone, `agenda.time_zone`, kept on each device). An event on another clock says both its times in the lists, its card and the home card (`timeline::both_times`), once when they are the same (Berlin's seen from Paris); the Day and the Week draw it at its time here. Every timed event written from the form is on its own clock (`EventEdit::clock`, read by `agenda::clock_of_text` from its DTSTART, kept by an edit): "" the local clock with its zone and its VTIMEZONE, "UTC" or another zone with that zone's TZID and VTIMEZONE (`TZID=UTC`); never a floating time. A one-off event at its instant in UTC (`…Z`) names no clock: it is how programs write an instant (invitations, feeds, Sioul's own one-off events until 28 October 2026), seldom a time announced in UTC, so it shows on the zone shown alone and its form opens on local time; a series written in UTC repeats in UTC, its clock "UTC" (read through `agenda::utc_series`). A task's time block, written in UTC, is said here alone (`agenda::file_occurrences_as`). Without a clock chosen, every timed event, one-off or repeating, is on the local clock with its zone and its VTIMEZONE (28 October 2026: one-off events were written in UTC until then; appointments are given in local time, summer or winter): the zone is the system's by its name, else the one this device recorded (`zone.toml`, Android's word on a phone), else, with no name at all, a one-off event at its instant in UTC and a series on its offset's clock (`Etc/GMT-1`) (`agenda::time_lines`). Its VTIMEZONE starts in the year of the event's start on its clock (`agenda::ZoneWritten`): the phone's account manager (ical4j) gives a time before every part's start the last part's offset, and with this year's parts an appointment of an earlier summer read an hour late there. A move, a skip or an answer keeps each event's own form (`written_like`): one written in UTC elsewhere stays in UTC, its EXDATE and RECURRENCE-ID in its DTSTART's form, an UNTIL in UTC; a TZID is read on the clock calcard reads it on (`agenda::clock_of`: an IANA name, else a Windows or Outlook name by calcard's table, "Eastern Standard Time" as New York's, else a fixed offset), so that a drag moves such an event by what it was dragged; no file already written is rewritten. calcard expands a series from its start, so each stretch asked for is expanded from just before it (`agenda::from_window`): the series' start moved forward by whole periods of its rule, on its own clock, which gives the same occurrences from there on, for rules repeating by the second, minute, hour, day or week, without `COUNT` and without a change to "this and those after" (`RANGE`); an event repeating every hour for years still reaches today. The cap of 20,000 occurrences a file stays as a safety net. A changed occurrence is matched to its time in the series by its `RECURRENCE-ID` alone, as RFC 5545 says: calcard also asks both to have the same `SEQUENCE`, so a series edited since, or an occurrence changed by an application that counts its own changes, would show twice; each is read with the series' (`agenda::for_expansion`), the file left as it is. Leaving out one occurrence that was changed takes its changed copy out, and leaves its time in the series out (`EXDATE`). A series whose start is written in UTC (`DTSTART:…Z`, with `VALUE=DATE-TIME` or names in lower case too) is given to calcard as `TZID=UTC` first (`agenda::utc_series`): calcard 0.3.14 expands such a series an hour off when your zone is not UTC. A series with its zone whose UNTIL is in UTC, as RFC 5545 asks, is given to calcard with its UNTIL on the series' own clock (`agenda::until_on_its_clock`): calcard compares UNTIL on that clock, and left out the last time east of UTC (Paris). Both change the reading only, never what is written.

### Several agendas
Sioul holds several agendas side by side: yours for work and for yourself, one per child shared with another parent, another parent's and a partner's for when they are busy (built 4 November 2026, `sioul_core::agendas`; the research: [research/agendas.md](research/agendas.md)). People keep one agenda per relationship, to show each person what concerns them, and families keep several (Thayer et al. 2012, Neustaedter et al. 2009, research/agendas.md §2.3): several agendas are the usual case, not an edge one. An agenda is a calendar or a task list, named by its place: its account and its folder ("account/id", `agendas::place`), never its name.

- **What each agenda is**, on its line in Agenda ⚙ ▸ Your agendas, folded until opened (`AgendasSetup.qml`): whose it is (yours, a child's, another parent's, a partner's, or shared with you) and whose name; what it is for (work, your admin, leisure: the areas' switches, [areas.md](areas.md)); when a personal one reaches you (all day, or outside work hours); and what you may do with it (read and write, read only, or see only when it is busy). Sioul says each until you do, proposed from what it sees and corrected once, never asked in a wizard (research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 1, after Thayer et al. 2012) (`Agenda::of`): an agenda of an account for work alone is for work (the account's own "What this address is for", or the mail account's of the same address, when either says work alone; an address left unsaid makes nothing work here); one its server keeps read only is shared with you, for its availability only when its events carry no details (a place, notes, a guest or two titles: `agendas::carries_details`, read again when its folder changes; Nextcloud sends a confidential event to those who only read it as "Busy", its times alone, research/agendas.md §3.3), else read only; every other one is yours, for your admin and leisure, all day, read and write. Read and write is never offered where the server keeps an agenda read only (CalDAV's `current-user-privilege-set`; Google's calendars through Google's own CalDAV; Microsoft's calendars are not read yet).
- **Kept** in the configuration, `[agenda.calendars."<account>/<id>"]`, only what you said, a field each (`whose`, `name`, `area`, `reaches`, `access`; `person`, whose it is as a person of [persons.md](persons.md), by id, `name` beside it keeping their name for older versions), written in place, its comments kept (`agendas::set_field`); it travels with the settings, one entry per field ([database.md](database.md)), and a Sioul before reads around it. A word this Sioul does not know reads as unsaid. Kept in Sioul's settings, not in the calendars: another parent's or a partner's calendar is theirs to change, never Sioul's (research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 1).
- **Read only, for every writer**: an agenda kept read only, or for its availability, counts as one its server keeps read only (`vdir::Collection::read_only`, `vdir::calendar_read_only`): no view offers to change it, and the one rule of every writer (`agenda::guard`) refuses its events and its tasks alike, old or new. What its server sends still comes in, as it came (`vdir::store_item`, Google Tasks' pull too).
- **Availability only**: its events are read as busy times alone, whatever their files say (`agenda::file_occurrences_as`): titled by the agenda's name, without place, notes, guests, alarms or the time kept around them; one marked free (TRANSP:TRANSPARENT) is left out. Its switch **Show what its events say**, off by default, shows their titles alone, still read only (`titles = "shown"`, part 4, recommendation 14). Never written, never re-shared nor exported (Sioul exports no calendar), never given to agents: the MCP's tools leave its events out, counted among what is kept from agents (`Consent::keep_private`, `access::of`). It is consulted when choosing who takes a child's event, "Sam is busy then." in the question and on the card (`children::busy_then`), and never said as a notice: "Sam is away all week" is not Sioul's to say (§6, recommendation 14). The Day and the Week draw them as quiet blocks; their card says why nothing more is known. Contentious co-parents use technology to withhold and control (Ganong et al. 2012), and a shared calendar may set the expectation that each parent may watch the other's movements (Smyth et al. 2023, Relationships Australia Victoria 2024, research/agendas.md §2.2): what another household shares stays its busy times, never a view of where they are.
- **Not yours**: an agenda that is someone else's (a child's, another parent's, a partner's, shared) is shown and filtered as any, and holds none of your time: the plan, the day, what a day's events cost and the meals moved past events read your own agendas' events alone (`Occurrence::holds_time`), since what matters is whether you would be in two places (research/agendas.md §5.2). A child's event you take gets an event of its own in your agenda, its stand-in, which holds it ([Children's events](#childrens-events)).
- **When it reaches you**: a work agenda in work's hours alone: outside them its events are not shown unless you say, and their reminders wait while work rests ([reminders.md](reminders.md)), as recovery needs detachment from work (Sonnentag & Fritz 2007, [areas.md](areas.md); research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 2); a personal one all day, or outside work hours when you say so, its reminders coming all the same (a reminder of an appointment is never dropped). Work's hours, for the agendas, are the hours you set with their overrides ("Work until tonight", "Done for today"), time off, Free time and the pause; a meal inside them is still work's day (`agendas::Hours`). With no hours set, every agenda reaches you.
- **The filter**, one for every view (`AgendaFilter.qml`): the agenda's What comes, Day, Week and Month, and the Tasks page, where it lists the task lists ([tasks.md](tasks.md)), as calendar sets and filters work in Fantastical and TimeTree, without location, which would need tracking (research/agendas.md §3.3, §6, recommendation 17). "Agendas" opens the agendas grouped by whose they are (yours, each child by name, another parent, partners, shared), each group folded or open, a box for the group and one for each agenda in its colour. Unless you say, it shows what the hours admit; your word holds until work's hours change (`agendas::Override`, its `work` and `until`, kept in `agenda-view.toml`'s `[filter]`, this device's own), then the hours decide again; "As the hours say" gives them back at once, its tip saying until when your word holds. An agenda kept hidden on this device is left out before the hours look (`agendas::default_on`): the calendars an older Show hid, and any you keep hidden from its line in Agenda ⚙ ▸ Your agendas (**Keep hidden on this device**, **Show again**: `agenda::keep_hidden`); the filter lists it unticked, "kept hidden", and a tick shows it until work's hours change, as any word of yours. The home card follows the hours alone, a work agenda's events off it outside work hours, an agenda kept hidden never on it, no word of the filter's reaching it ([android.md](android.md#the-card-on-the-home-screen)). On a phone, the same list in a sheet from the bottom, the screen's width. What the filter leaves out is not among the days the agenda's job makes (`agenda::made`): it takes no lane, no column and no bar.
- **Sets** (part 4, recommendation 17, after Fantastical's sets and TimeTree's filters, §3): named sets of agendas ("Tom and Léa", "My week") at the head of the filter's list; **Keep these as a set…** names the agendas shown, with the times that bring it by themselves (work, your admin, leisure: Sioul's own, never a place, which would need tracking). Chosen by hand, a set shows its agendas and leaves out every other, as any word of yours, until work's hours change (`agenda::use_set`, two `agendas::turned`); brought by its time, it is what the filter shows then unless you say, the agendas outside it left out before the hours as an agenda kept hidden is (`agendas::set_of_time`, `FilterNow::out`). Kept in `[agenda.sets."<name>"]` (`agendas`, `times`), it travels with the settings (`agendas::set_set`).
- **Sharing your own availability**: built in part 5 ([Sharing your availability](#sharing-your-availability)).
- **Lanes**: in the Day and the Week, what overlaps stands side by side, each agenda in columns of its own, yours first (Sioul's own things, the steps and meals, with yours), then each child's, another parent's, partners' and shared ones (`timeline::lanes`), each in its colour; a run of one agenda alone keeps its columns. Overlaps then read as positions, the filter choosing what is laid over what (research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 18).

### Conflicts
A conflict is one person in two places: partners fought over overlaps neither had looked for (Thayer et al. 2012), and family plans break at the deviations, pick-ups and drop-offs (Davidoff et al. 2010), so a conflict follows the person, never a pair of calendars (research/agendas.md §5.2).
- **What takes time** (`overlaps::takes_time`): an event that is opaque (no TRANSP, or OPAQUE), not cancelled and not declined by you, as CalDAV counts busy time (RFC 4791 §7.10); a tentative one tentatively ("not confirmed yet"); a whole day only when its file says OPAQUE, as Outlook and Google make whole days free (research/agendas.md §5.1); nothing of a calendar its server says is transparent as a whole (`schedule-calendar-transp`, RFC 6638 §9.1, kept in its sync state).
- **Who is in two places** (`overlaps::conflicts`, `Occurrence::needs_you`): the events that need you are your agendas' (the work agenda's too, at any hour, though they do not reach you then: its busy time is where conflicts with a child's events arise, research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 2), the invitations you accepted, wherever they are, and the child events you take. Two of them at once conflict; closer than the time kept around them, they are tight, said so and never "at the same time" (research/agendas.md §5.1). A child's event nobody takes is nobody's conflict but a question, who takes it; an agenda for its availability only conflicts with nothing, and is consulted when choosing who takes an event (research/agendas.md §5.2).
- **The exceptions**, Agenda ⚙ ▸ Conflicts (`ConflictMatrix.qml`; `[agenda.overlaps]`, `"<a>|<b>" = true`, the two places sorted, an agenda with itself `"<a>|<a>"`): one box per pair of the agendas that take events, each with itself included, the rule said once above them. Unsaid, a box shows the rule: your agendas among themselves and each with itself ticked, every other pair not (children's among themselves, those for availability only, the rest). A ticked pair compares all its events, whoever they need (two children whose events share one car, research/agendas.md §5.2); one of yours unticked compares none. A box turned back to what it shows unsaid takes your word out (`Matrix::word_for`). On a phone, the same pairs as a list, grouped by agenda.
- **Shown where you look**: side by side in the Day and the Week, a thin neutral bracket joining the pair (the days' `conflicts`, `WeekPlanning.qml`); on the event's card, one sentence each: "At the same time as Léa’s “Swimming”.", "…, which you accepted.", "Tight with “Dentist”: 10 min between them, less than the 30 min kept after the first (its After)." (`pim::conflicts_known`). No badge, no red, no count, no list of conflicts: record-keeping between parents can encourage "petty account-keeping" (Relationships Australia Victoria 2024, research/agendas.md §2.2), and Sioul counts nothing (research/agendas.md §5.3).
- **Said once, when someone else's change made it** (`overlaps::Notices`, `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/conflicts.toml`, this device's own): a conflict that was not there at the last look, made by events that moved or came and may be another person's (an event of an agenda not yours; a meeting with others, which its organizer moves), is said once, as a notice of the Porch's kind, when its area is in view: an admin window, and a work matter in work's time alone, never in the evening ([areas.md](areas.md); research/agendas.md §5.3). Never one you made (your own event moved), one that came because you answered an invitation (nothing moved), nor what was there when this device first looked: a conflict you come upon while looking is not notified (research/agendas.md §5.3). Its answers (`answers` on each, in this order): ask the other parent to take it, when one of the two is your stand-in of a child's event ("ask": the other parent is asked as from its card, [Children's events](#childrens-events)), move your event (its form opens, where Sioul may write it), or keep both (the pair set aside). The Porch reads the notices from the window's `overlaps` property and answers through `answerConflict` (`pim::answer_conflict`); the home card says them on their day until answered.
- **Set aside**: "Keep both", and the Porch's "Don't mention it again", set the pair aside for good (`overlaps-set-aside.toml`, this device's own); an event of 24 hours or more set aside whole holds every pair it makes.

### Children's events
Who takes a child's event or task is said once, in the child's own event, and everything that goes with it moves to whoever takes it (built 4 November 2026, part 3; `sioul_core::holders`, `children.rs`). Mental labour is anticipating, deciding and monitoring, not only doing, and mothers carry the anticipating and the monitoring (Daminger 2019, research/agendas.md §2.1); handing over only the doing lifts little, so the holder takes the reminders, the time around it and the follow-up too (Rodsky 2019's *Fair Play*, §2.1).
- **Holders**, in Agenda ⚙ ▸ Your agendas, on a child's agenda: the other parent who may take its events, a person by id (`parent_person`, [persons.md](persons.md)), their name (`parent`, kept for older versions, and matched to a person when the key is unsaid) and the address their invitations go to (`parent_address`), and how they answer: by an invitation in their mail, or in their own Sioul, which reads the child's agenda (`parent_answers`, "mail" unless said); and who usually takes each kind (`[agenda.calendars."<place>".holders]`, "dentist" = "other"), proposed for each new event of that kind (research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 6). All of it travels with the settings, one entry per field.
- **The kinds**: health, dentist, school, activity, clothes, other, chosen on the event's card and written as a concept on the child's event, as a task's kind (`X-SIOUL-CONCEPT;VALUE=URI:tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/child-kind/dentist`; recommendation 6).
- **The holder line**, on the child's VEVENT or VTODO: `X-SIOUL-TAKEN-BY;CN=Sam;PARTSTAT=ACCEPTED;SINCE=20261103T181500Z:mailto:sam@example.org`, NEEDS-ACTION while proposed, DECLINED for "not this time" (iTIP's words), a second line with `PART=BACK` for who brings the child back (`holders::with_taken`, `taken`; recommendation 4, after OurFamilyWizard's driver field, §3.1). A repeating event's line holds the series, and a changed occurrence carries its own; one without any reads the series'. Never an ATTENDEE: an event with a guest is a meeting with others, which Sioul never writes, and a scheduling server would mail the guest on its own (RFC 6638 §7.1, §4.2). Sioul changes events line by line, so the line stays through every edit, sync and merge, as the unknown lines of [tasks.md](tasks.md) do; RFC 9073's PARTICIPANT, the standard equivalent, is not written yet (recommendation 4 waits for a phone's ical4j to be shown to keep it).
- **Asked once** (recommendation 9): an event or task that comes into a child's agenda without a holder is asked about once, under the Agenda's head line and on its card: "Tom: “Dentist”, Tuesday 10 November at 10:00. Who takes it?" with **Me**, the other parent's name, **Later**. Later asks once more at the next admin window, then never (`holders::Asked`, `holder-questions.toml`, this device's own). What was there at this device's first look is never asked, as a conflict you come upon is never notified (§5.3). Sioul proposes, the proposal shown first: the standing holder ("Usually Sam."); else who has the child that day, by the child's agenda's custody days, whole days titled "With Sam" (« Avec Sam »), as 2houses and OurFamilyWizard keep them (§3.1): "Tom is with Sam that day."; else nobody (`holders::propose`, `custody`). The card always says where it stands ("Sam takes it, since 3 November.", "Nobody takes it yet.") and offers the answers that fit, and who brings the child back when another than who takes it (`back-me`, `back-other`, `back-none`: the `PART=BACK` line, asked of the other parent as NEEDS-ACTION; no stand-in of its own yet for whoever brings the child back).
- **What taking it moves** (recommendation 8): you take it, and your stand-in comes into your own agenda with your margins, your reminder and your rides around it; the child's event itself never reminds anyone (`reminders`: an event of a child's agenda is skipped), since feeling solely responsible is what weighs (Ciciolla and Luthar 2019, §2.1) and in monocentric families the scheduler reminds everyone (Neustaedter et al. 2009, §2.3). Someone else takes it, or is asked: your Sioul shows it in the child's lane with their name, and nothing more. A child's event and your stand-in of it are one appointment, never a conflict (`overlaps::pairs`). If the one asked says not this time, the event is open again, said once to you (`Asking::Reopened`).
- **The stand-in** (recommendation 5, §4.4), in your agenda for that kind (`[agenda.stand_ins]`, else your personal agenda: the one new events go to when it is not for work, else your first that is not): title, times, place and status copied; `RELATED-TO;RELTYPE=PARENT:<child's UID>`, `URL:sioul:event/<child's UID>`, `X-SIOUL-LINK;LINKREL=latest-version;VALUE=URI:<the same>`; a description ending "Details: Tom’s agenda" and the address; its UID a name-based UUID (RFC 9562 §5.5, version 5) of the child's UID and the target agenda's place in Sioul's own namespace (`holders::NAMESPACE`: the version-5 UUID of `https://aurelienpierre.github.io/sioul/` in the URL namespace), its file named after it, its stamp the child event's: two devices make the same bytes, and a copy is found and never made twice. Where a server dropped its lines (Google's calendars), its UID still finds it: Sioul's index is the derived UIDs it computes at each look, which cannot go stale. In an agenda for work alone, a health, dentist or school event's stand-in says "Away", without its place, CLASS:CONFIDENTIAL (recommendation 16). `sioul:event/<child's UID>` opens the child's event.
- **It follows** (`holders::upkeep`, run with the plan, where rides follow their appointment): made where your line says you take it and none is there (another device of yours, an older answer), never for an event already over; the child's event moved, renamed or cancelled, the stand-in follows, your own lines kept; another is asked or takes it, or you said not this time, it is taken out; the child's event gone, or its line dropped by a server, nothing is written and nothing lost (the rides' rule, [tasks.md](tasks.md)). Each change is said once in the status line. What you do yourself (taking it, asking the other parent, not this time) is said with Undo, the holder line and your stand-in together; what follows a change from elsewhere is said plainly, since putting the stand-in back would contradict the child's event, which is the record.
- **The other parent's Sioul**, reading the child's agenda, makes their stand-in from the line in their own agenda: no mail (recommendation 12). A proposal reaches them as one question: "Alex proposes that you take Tom to “Dentist”, Tuesday 10 November at 10:00.", **Take it**, **Not this time**; it enters their agenda only when accepted, and the answer writes PARTSTAT and SINCE (recommendation 10, AppClose's model, §3.1).
- **The invitation**, when the other parent answers by mail (recommendation 13: their own Google or Outlook): answering **Sam** (or **Ask Sam to take it**) writes the line (NEEDS-ACTION) and drafts an iTIP REQUEST (RFC 5546 §3.2.2) in an iMIP message (RFC 6047) from the child agenda's account, to them, into Drafts: the child event's UID kept, you its ORGANIZER, them its ATTENDEE `ROLE=REQ-PARTICIPANT;PARTSTAT=NEEDS-ACTION;RSVP=TRUE`, its times, title and place, the details in DESCRIPTION, `URL:sioul:event/<UID>` (`holders::invitation`; `compose::Draft::calendar`, sent as a `text/calendar; method=REQUEST` part beside the text). It leaves only when you press Send. Once it left, the event changed, an update (SEQUENCE one more) is drafted; cancelled or taken back, a CANCEL; while it still waits in Drafts, nothing was sent: that draft is made to say the change, and a cancellation drafts nothing (`holder-invitations.toml`, this device's own, keeps what each said and its draft). Their REPLY, opened in the reader from the address it answers for, sets the holder line's PARTSTAT and SINCE to when they answered (`holders::replied`), and the reader says it: "Sam takes Tom to “Dentist”, Tuesday 10 November at 10:00." It is written only when the Porch would take the message as coming from them, not forged (`trust::judge_sender`) and authenticated with its account's sealers (`trust::authenticated`, `children::proven_sender`), since a From is easy to forge and a forged "Sam takes it" would leave the appointment with nobody; otherwise nothing changes, and the reader says that Sioul could not prove the answer comes from them and that the card's "Who takes it?" is still there. The child's stored event carries no ATTENDEE, so `SCHEDULE-AGENT=CLIENT` is not needed and not written: a scheduling server has no guest to mail. Built and tested on stand-ins only: never tried against Google, Outlook or Nextcloud; a Sioul before 4 November 2026 sends such a draft without its calendar part.
- **Acts, never views** (recommendation 10): SINCE says when it was taken, asked or declined, "Sam took it on 3 November", which answers "I never got it" (Ganong et al. 2012, §2.2); nothing records when something was read, which serves monitoring (TalkingParents' "viewed" times, §2.2).
- **Tasks** (recommendation 7): a child's task (a winter coat) is a VTODO in the child's list with the same line; it sits in its holder's plan and Next (`Task::elsewhere`, `holders::mark_tasks`, the plan's `open`), never in another's; nobody's yet, in nobody's, until someone takes it.
- **Follow-ups** (recommendation 6, *Fair Play*'s card): a held event of a kind with a rhythm, once over (within a month), gives a task in the child's list, held by the same parent, due a month before the rhythm comes round: "Book Tom’s next dentist appointment" six months on, "Book Tom’s next check-up" a year on (`holders::follow_up`; `[agenda.rhythms]` per kind, 0 none; `[agenda] follow_ups`, on unless said off; Agenda ⚙'s switch). Its UID derived from the child's, so it is made once. A child without a list you may write gets none.
- **Who does what** (part 4, recommendation 11): beside each child's agenda in the filter, **Who does what** opens its week (`WhoDoesWhat.qml`, `children::who_does_what`): one child or all, day by day, each event and task with who takes it ("You", "Sam", "Sam, asked", "Nobody yet"), open tasks without a date last; ◀ ▶ go a week back or on; **What I take** keeps yours, across children. A tap opens its card, where it is answered. No total per parent and no history of who did more: records invite "petty account-keeping" (Relationships Australia Victoria 2024, §2.2), and a tally of each one's part belongs to exchange relationships rather than close ones (Clark 1984, §2.2); each row says who, and when, and that is the tracking. On a phone, the same list in a sheet.
- **Tests**: `holders::tests` (the line written, read and kept through an edit and a sync; a series and a changed occurrence; the proposal's order; kinds; the derived UIDs, RFC 9562's own example; the stand-in made once, found by its UID alone; a work stand-in without a title for health; following a move and a cancellation, zones too; the iTIP REQUEST's lines, an update, a CANCEL, a REPLY setting PARTSTAT and SINCE, and the REPLYs that set nothing; the follow-up task; asked once; the upkeep; a child's task planned by its holder alone), `compose::tests::an_invitation_goes_out_as_its_calendar_part`; in the window, `children::tests::a_child_s_event_taken_then_asked_of_the_other_parent` (its line, the stand-in made once and taken out, said with what Undo puts back, the week's who does what, Later, no address of yours) and `agenda::tests::a_set_chosen_shows_its_agendas_alone`; QML, `tst_cards.qml` (a child's event's card: its line, the proposal marked, an answer) and `tst_agendafilter.qml` (sets chosen, kept, the who-does-what button beside a child's agenda alone).

### Agendas on their servers
Agendas are made, renamed, recoloured, removed and shared from one place, Agenda ⚙ ▸ Your agendas (built 4 November 2026, part 5; `sioul_sync::calendars`, `crates/sioul-app/src/servers.rs`, `AgendasSetup.qml`), on the server that holds them, so that what you keep where is controlled from where you set what each agenda is.
- **New agenda…**: the account (a CalDAV account, a Google account, or this computer), a name, a colour among eight, events, tasks or both, and in the same form whose it is, whose name, its area and when it reaches you, so that the agenda arrives with its role (`servers::make`, `agendas::set_field`). It is made here at once and on its server by the next sync, as every collection made here: MKCALENDAR with its `supported-calendar-component-set` (RFC 4791 §5.3.1), its name and its colour (`dav::create_pending`); an address book keeps its extended MKCOL (RFC 5689), unchanged. On Google, `calendars.insert` (it makes a secondary calendar, the account its owner) and then `calendarList.patch` for its colour (`colorRgbFormat=true`, a background and a foreground colour), events only: a Google calendar holds no task (its task lists are Google Tasks'), so "both" makes an events calendar there.
- **Rename and colour**, on each line of yours: written here (`vdir::rename_collection`, `vdir::recolour_collection`, the vdir `color` file in `#rrggbb`), said with Undo, sent by the next sync: one PROPPATCH of `displayname` and `calendar-color` (Apple's iCal namespace, as Nextcloud and most servers keep it) on CalDAV; `calendars.patch` (its `summary`) and `calendarList.patch` on Google. A rename or a colour not sent yet is kept when the server's listing comes first (`dav::register`). Agenda ⚙ ▸ Calendars renames too, Google's calendars included now; Google's address books and task lists stay greyed there.
- **Removing is safe** (`vdir::remove_collection`): one sentence says what goes, "Remove “Swimming”, 42 events, from cloud.example.org?", with what the server keeps: Nextcloud keeps a deleted calendar in its trash bin, 30 days by default (Nextcloud user manual, Calendar app, "Trash bin", read 4 November 2026); Google "deletes a secondary calendar" (`calendars.delete`, read 4 November 2026) and keeps no copy (said as final; Google's own help on it not checked); another server may keep it or not. Before anything goes, a dated copy of the whole agenda, one VCALENDAR with its time zones and every event and task (`vdir::collection_as_ics`), is written to `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/removed-agendas/<account>-<id>-<date>.ics`, and the status line says where. The removal waits for the next sync (`state.removed`, DELETE on the calendar whatever it holds; on Google `calendars.delete`); the agenda leaves every view at once. No Undo: the copy is the way back. A Google account's main calendar is never removed (Google deletes secondary calendars only); a calendar on this computer goes at once, its copy kept the same way.
- **Someone else's is never removed**: an agenda that is not yours, or one its server keeps read only, offers **Stop showing it** instead, "It stays its owner's" (`vdir::stop_showing`, `state.leave`): on Nextcloud a DELETE of a calendar shared with you takes its share away from you alone (`Calendar::delete` when `isShared()`, apps/dav, read 4 November 2026); on Google `calendarList.delete`, the calendar left in its owner's account; elsewhere a sharee's DELETE, and refused, it stays hidden here. Only when its server says it is someone else's (below); otherwise it is kept hidden on this device, as **Keep hidden on this device** does.
- **Whose it is comes from its server, never from its role** (`State::owned`, read at each listing): `DAV:owner` (RFC 3744 §5.1) or Nextcloud's `oc:owner-principal` against your `current-user-principal` (RFC 5397), CalendarServer's `shared`/`shared-owner` resource types otherwise, and on Google the calendar list's `accessRole` "owner"; made here, yours. A child's agenda on your own server is yours to remove (its copy, its question); only one its server says is someone else's is left there by **Stop showing…** (its DELETE); whose it is unknown, it is hidden on this device alone, nothing sent; and the sync asks it again before any DELETE of a whole calendar, showing it again when the answer does not allow it.
- **Stable places**: a Google calendar's folder now takes its name from the calendar's id (its CalDAV address ends in `/events/` for every calendar), and the listing keeps each existing folder's id by its address (`dav::keep_ids`), so what you said of an agenda stays with it.

**Sharing, per agenda** (`calendars::Sharing`), read when its line opens, asked of the server at once in a thread of its own (`servers::sharing`, the `agendaShared` signal), never by the sync. Nothing is shared unless you ask.
- **The line says who sees it**: "Only you.", "Sam can read it.", "Sam can change it.", "Sam sees when you are busy." (Google), "Anyone with the link can read it."; on a child's or another parent's agenda, the other parent is named so ("Sam, the other parent, can read it."). A person or a group is added, read or read and change, and taken out with ×, said with Undo, which shares again as before. A read-only public link is turned on or off; on, the link itself shows under its switch, in a field that selects, with **Copy** beside it (`LinkLine.qml`), which puts it on the clipboard (`copy_text`, through Qt's `QClipboard`, read back) and then says "Copied." a moment; where the clipboard refused it, the link is selected, to copy by hand. Turned on, it says once what anyone with it sees.
- **Nextcloud** (sabre/dav's sharing, Nextcloud's apps/dav): a server says it shares by its DAV header, `oc-resource-sharing` from its sharing plugin, `calendarserver-sharing` and `oc-calendar-publishing` from its publishing plugin (`DAV/Sharing/Plugin.php`, `CalDAV/Publishing/PublishPlugin.php`, read 4 November 2026); CalendarServer's own sharing note names `calendarserver-sharing` (caldav-sharing §5.1, read 4 November 2026). The shares are read from `oc:invite` (each `oc:user` with its `d:href`, `oc:common-name` and `oc:access`, `oc:read` or `oc:read-write`) and changed by a POST of `oc:share` with `oc:set` (a `d:href` `principal:principals/users/<name>` or `…/groups/<name>`, `oc:read-write` for change) or `oc:remove` (sabre.io, CalDAV sharing, read 4 November 2026). The link: a POST of `cs:publish-calendar` or `cs:unpublish-calendar`, read back as `cs:publish-url`, and given as its feed, that address with `?export`, which any agenda app subscribes to, as Nextcloud Calendar's own "Copy subscription link" gives it (`PublishCalendar.vue`, read 5 November 2026; `calendars::subscription`): the bare address is a CalDAV collection, which a browser and most agenda apps cannot open. Users and groups of the same server alone: for someone elsewhere, the line says that the link is the way. What they see: a private event never, neither by a share nor by the link (`Calendar.php` and `PublicCalendar.php` skip `CLASSIFICATION_PRIVATE` when shared, read 4 November 2026), and a confidential one as "Busy", its time alone, to those who only read it and on the link (`CalendarObject.php`, read 4 November 2026). CalendarServer's pubsub note (caldav-pubsubdiscovery) is about push notifications, not publishing: the link follows Nextcloud's PublishPlugin.
- **Google**: the calendar's ACL (`acl.list`, `acl.insert`, `acl.delete`; Calendar API v3, acl, read 4 November 2026): `reader` to read, `writer` to change, `freeBusyReader` to see when you are busy; scope `user` with an address, `default` for anyone (the link, `https://calendar.google.com/calendar/ical/<id>/public/basic.ics`). The owner's own rule is left out of the line. Those who read it see a private event without its details. Sharing needs the **Google Calendar API** turned on in the Google project of the key; a key made without it says so, and what to turn on (`calendars::api_off`).
- **Another CalDAV server**: sharing is found by the same DAV header; one that says neither feature has the line "This server does not share from Sioul."
- **On this computer**: the line says that Sioul's own sharing carries it to your devices.

### Sharing your availability
Agenda ⚙ ▸ **Your availability**, below Your agendas (`AvailabilitySetup.qml`; `sioul_core::availability`, `servers`, `mirrors`; [research.md](research.md), findings 53 and 66; [research/availability-sharing.md](research/availability-sharing.md)), holds what you give of your time, and to whom: your busy times, never what fills them. Nothing is shared until you press Share.
- **An availability** is the agendas of yours that count as busy, each with one word at most ("Work" rather than "Busy", for a partner who reads a bare "busy" as secrecy), and a name of your own ("For Sam"), shown to you alone. Its blocks are the events of those agendas that take time (not cancelled, free or declined, never a whole day), widened by the margins you keep around them, since getting there is your time and no server knows it. An event Sioul knows as health (a child's health or dentist event, your stand-in of one, an errand's block) always says "Busy", whatever the agenda's word. Blocks of one word that touch are made one, so that nothing says how many things fill a time (`availability::blocks`). "New availability…" starts with no agenda ticked.
- **Several shares**, one per audience: a person, a group, anyone with a link, a booking tool. "Share with…" asks only who. Sioul then names the way it will use and why, in one sentence, best first, the others one press away (`availability::choose`):
  - its server's own way where one fits, which needs no Sioul running and never goes stale: on Google, your calendars shared busy only with a Google user when the availability is all in that account and has no word, or the calendar's public free/busy for a link (Google then shows the events' own times, without your margins, and the line says so); Fastmail's own free/busy link of one calendar, which you switch on there and paste here; a booking page on your own server (Nextcloud Appointments, Google's appointment schedules), which Sioul opens and whose link you paste back; for a booking tool, that page first, then the tool reading your calendars on their server itself (cal.com over CalDAV, Calendly over Google: nothing to share from Sioul), then a mirror's link, which cal.com reads as an ICS feed;
  - elsewhere a **mirror**: a calendar of plain blocks Sioul keeps in an account, one per availability and account, shared as often as needed: with a user or a group of a Nextcloud, with someone on another Nextcloud by their cloud id (`user@instance`, Nextcloud 32 and later), or by its read-only link. A mirror is named after you ("Alex, busy times"), and its description says only a day ("From Sioul. As of 5 November."), never a time, which would tell when your devices run. Your agendas lists it as kept for its availability ("Alex, busy times · kept for “For Sam”"), with no role to choose: pressed, it opens Your availability. It is left out of the conflicts' matrix, of the calendars to rename and of the choice of a calendar for new events.
  - A person by a name alone is a user of your Nextcloud; by `name@host` of one of your Nextclouds, that user; by another address, a Nextcloud's federation first, then Google (Google first for Google's own mail and your Google account's domain), then a mirror's link to give them.
- **The wider the audience, the coarser the view**, by its first audience, never as a setting: a person or a group, exact and sixty days ahead; a link, blocks rounded outward to the quarter hour (gaps under it merged) and two weeks ahead; a booking tool, the quarter hour, sixty days and a last block "Not known yet" from there on, so that the tool offers nothing Sioul cannot vouch for. Two audiences that need different views are two availabilities: sharing one with an audience of another view makes an availability of its own, with the same agendas and words, named after both ("For Sam · Anyone with a link").
- **Each share's line** says who sees what ("Sam, on cloud.example.org, sees these busy times.", "Anyone with the link sees the next 14 days."), its link with Copy, Open for a booking page, and who keeps it up to date: "Your server keeps it up to date." for a way of its own; for a mirror, "Your devices keep it up to date: the phone in the background, a computer while Sioul runs.", or "Kept up to date while Sioul is open on one of your devices.", with when it was last brought up to date once that is more than an hour ago. A mirror not brought up to date for a day is said once, at the next admin window, never as an alert. **Stop** takes that share back on its server, then here, with Undo; the last share of an availability takes it and its mirrors with it, a dated copy of each kept as for any removal.
- **Who keeps a mirror**: every device of yours that has the account, from the freshest view only (`availability::may_write`). A device writes a mirror only when every source agenda was known current here after the freshest pull any other device last wrote it from; each device notes what it wrote from in its sealed entry ([database.md](database.md#devices), `mirrors`), never on the server. So a device opened after days away writes nothing over newer blocks until its own pulls are newer. On a computer, after each calendar sync that brought events and at the plan's run; on the phone, inside the background steps it already runs, at most every quarter of an hour while you are awake and every hour while you sleep: one token check per calendar that counts (`dav::check_named`, RFC 6578), a pull and a rewrite only when one changed ([android.md](android.md#in-the-background)). Two devices with the same view write the same bytes (the window in whole days, each block's UID derived from its time and word).
- **What each Nextcloud shows without being asked**: its users see your busy times when they invite you; Agenda ⚙ says so for each, with **Send my working hours to cloud.example.org** (off at first; your working hours from Settings ▸ Hours, written as Nextcloud's own settings page writes them, `availability::working_hours`, and taken back when switched off, never written empty). Sioul marks each mirror, and each agenda of someone else's availability, transparent there (`schedule-calendar-transp`), so that neither counts as your own busy time when others invite you.
- **Kept** in `[[agenda.availabilities]]` (`id`, `name`, `agendas`, `words`, `rounding`, `horizon`, `closing`, `[[…mirrors]]` and `[[…shares]]`) and `[agenda] send_hours`, travelling with the settings, the list as one entry (the later list winning, as for the lists edited on one page); the settings' format says 2 (`config::FORMAT`). What this device asked of the servers is its own (`state/availability.toml`). The single share of before (`[agenda.availability]`) is read once at the start and becomes one availability: named "For Sam" or "By link", its calendar the mirror as it is (same folder, same name), its share as it was made; said once at the next admin window (`config::migrate_availability`). A Sioul of before on another device then reads no share and keeps nothing; it shows the mirror among its agendas until it is updated.
- **Agents never** make, remove or share an agenda, nor share your availability: the MCP has no tool for them; these are your own presses in the window.

**Tests**, against stand-ins only (never a real server or account): `availability::tests` (blocks with margins and rounding, health says Busy, the horizon and the closing block, two devices' bytes, the freshest view, the way per audience and server, working hours as Nextcloud writes them, a mirror's day), `agendas::tests::availabilities_are_written_one_by_one_and_keep_what_this_sioul_does_not_know`, `agendas::tests::a_mirror_is_marked_and_never_compared_by_the_rule`, `config::tests::the_single_share_of_before_becomes_one_availability`, `calendars::tests::nextcloud_shares_and_publishes_a_calendar`, `calendars::tests::availability_marks_calendars_and_writes_working_hours_on_a_nextcloud`, `calendars::tests::google_s_public_free_busy_goes_on_and_off_and_never_touches_a_public_calendar`, `calendars::tests::google_makes_shares_and_deletes_a_calendar`, `dav::tests::a_check_of_named_calendars_reads_nothing_while_nothing_changed`, `devices::tests::a_device_says_what_it_last_wrote_a_mirror_from`, `servers::tests::availability_by_link_names_its_mirror`, and the QML tests `tst_availabilitysetup.qml` and `tst_agendasetup.qml`. Untested against the real servers: [compatibility.md](compatibility.md#sharing-and-agendas-on-their-servers).

## Storage and sync
- **Contacts and calendars**: one `.vcf` or `.ics` file per item, one folder per address book or calendar (vdir, as pimsync and khal use), with the server's ETags kept apart in the state folder; changes go back with `If-Match`, so nobody's edit is overwritten silently. When both sides changed one item, the server's version is kept and yours is set aside among this device's earlier versions (Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing ▸ Show earlier versions, under "Your calendars and contacts", named by the item's title), said in the status line, to put back from the window, on a phone too; put back, it goes to its server over the server's version (`history::ACCOUNTS`, [database.md](database.md#earlier-versions)), through the agenda's one rule (`vdir::write_item`): never into a calendar you only read, said before and after; onto a meeting with others, only your own lines of that version come back (your time around it, its costs and your reminder), its time, title and guests staying as its organizer has them now (`agenda::with_own_lines_of`; review of 36002ea, can-wait 6). Until 10 October 2026 it went to `~/.local/state/sioul/dav/conflicts`, which no file manager reaches on a phone.
- **Discovery**: the address you give, else `.well-known/carddav` and `/caldav` (RFC 6764) on your domain, then a few providers' known places (Murena, Fastmail, iCloud, Posteo, mailbox.org) and port 2080 of your mail server for cPanel hosts; then the principal and its home sets. Redirections are followed within the same domain only (`caldav.example.org` from `example.org`), since each request carries your password: a domain whose calendars live with another provider needs that server's address given. The principal and the home sets a server names keep to that domain too.
- **Your password goes to your server only**: once an account's homes are known, each request goes only to their servers, by origin (scheme, host and port, `dav::origin`). An address book, a calendar or an item a server's answer names on another server is never asked, with or without your password: that one is refused, the others sync, and the sync says so ("the server named an address on another server (…); Sioul did not go there, and sent no password", `SyncError::Elsewhere`). A hostile or broken server could otherwise have sent your password wherever it named. The folder of files that sharing uses on a Nextcloud keeps to the server its calendars were found on, for the same reason (`remote::files_root`). Tested against two stand-in servers, one naming the other, which notes every request it gets: it gets none (`dav::tests::the_login_stays_on_the_account_s_server`, `remote::tests::the_files_stay_on_the_account_s_server`).
- **Google**: signed in on Google's page with your own key (OAuth, no password), calendars and contacts over its CalDAV and CardDAV, tasks over Google Tasks; what Google does not keep shows greyed ([google.md](google.md)).
- **Moving** a task to another list or a contact to another address book, across accounts too: written there, then taken out here; when the place would not keep something it uses (Google), Sioul says what before moving.
- **Sync**: what changed here goes first (PUT with `If-Match`, new items with `If-None-Match: *`, DELETE), then what changed there, with the sync token when the server keeps one (RFC 6578), else by comparing ETags; changed items come back in batches (multiget) of 50 at most, half as many after a slow or cut answer, twice as many again after quick ones. An invitation answered on two devices meets its UID on the server at the second: the item there takes its place (said when its answer differs). Each wait for the network is bounded by Sioul's own layer on `ureq`'s transport (`sioul_sync::stalls`, the one place built on `ureq::unversioned`, which follows no semver: written for ureq 3.4). Every fifteen minutes in the background, and at once after a change in the window.
- **An event moved to another calendar** (21 October 2026): its editor says its calendar and moves it to another writable one of any account, this device's own to a server's above all, so that an event filed there by mistake reaches your other devices (`pim::move_event_file`, `vdir::move_item`): the move noted first (`state/dav/moves.toml`, under its lock, `.moves.toml.lock`: one move or finishing at a time, in a program and between programs, since 22 October 2026, when CI's Windows job saw another test finish a move half made), the event written there, taken out here, the note gone; a step cut short (a crash, Android stopping Sioul) is finished before the next move and before each sync (`vdir::finish_moves`): there and still here, taken out here; not there, kept here. Never twice, never lost (`vdir::tests::a_move_cut_at_each_step_ends_once`); an item made another in its own place (a plan's task become its event, one collection, one name) is never taken out (`an_item_made_another_in_its_own_place_stays`). The sync sends the new item first and deletes the old copy on its server only once the new one is there (`state/dav/moved.toml`, `vdir::deletion_waits_for`; `dav::tests::a_moved_event_is_deleted_there_only_once_its_new_copy_is`): a refused PUT leaves the old copy on every other device, and the new item is never replaced by the old one's UID. A meeting with others (a guest who is not you, or an organizer who is not you beside a guest) never moves: its server would cancel it for them, then invite them again; its editor says why (`pim::with_others`). Another device that changed the old item meanwhile gets a 412 for its deletion: the old item comes back beside the new one, doubled, never lost (review of 22 October 2026, F9). **New events go into** (`agenda.calendar`, Settings ▸ Agenda, `agenda::default_calendar`): there was no such setting before, a new event going into the first calendar that takes events, which stays the first value. Which calendars stay on one device: this device's own (`local`) while your sharing does not carry them (none set up, or its part "lists" off here); their events' editor says "Only on this computer" (`pim::here_only`). A server's calendar is on each device that has its account.
- **Every collection at once** (the phone's calendars of 21 October 2026: only the first calendar was made while its first pull lasted): every calendar and address book the server lists is made here right after the listing, its folder, name, colour and state, before any is pulled (`dav::register`), so that each is listed, can be written to and is reached at once, empty until pulled. Then they are pulled in turns, a slice of twenty seconds each (`dav::SLICE`), a batch at least, until all are whole: a big calendar never keeps the others waiting, and a pull cut short (Android stopping Sioul) goes on from its last batch. The calendars go whole before the address books, which never hold them back. An error in one collection, a lasting one too (a calendar shared with you that refuses your login), ends that collection alone (`Report::problems`); only a home refused ends the account. The account's status says how many were found and how many synced whole: "Contacts and calendars synced at 09:30: 3 of 3 found.", or "1 of the 3 calendars and address books found synced." with the first problem.
- **Editing keeps what Sioul does not show**: cards and events are changed line by line, so a photo, an alarm, a guest list or another application's fields come back exactly as they were. An event's time and repeat rule are rewritten only when you changed them: an event in another time zone keeps its zone, a rule the form cannot show ("every other Monday and Wednesday") stays whole.
- **One model of time, one read** (27 October 2026): the agenda's days are made from one model of what has a time (`sioul_core::timeline`), each event and each time block of a task an item that says what its writers let change it (nothing in a calendar you only read, nothing of a meeting with others, no drag of a whole day), which the window offers and nothing more. Within the next four weeks the events are the plan's one read of the calendars (`work::PlanShown`: from today's midnight, what waits for "Undo" left out), as long as nothing was written since it was read, by Sioul, another program or the sync (`timeline::Stamp`: this process's count of writes and the calendars' folder times); before today, beyond those weeks and after a write, the agenda's own job reads them (`agenda::show_agenda`), off the window's thread, with the tasks alone when no plan is made yet (never a read of everything, which writes the mail's index). It writes nothing. It makes the days once after the first plan, so that the agenda's first opening shows them (no extra read: the plan's), then when the agenda page asks; the Porch's overlaps of the next two weeks at every run, whether the page has asked or not (review of 1880bce, M2 and L18). Its `sioul-perf` line gives its run and its read of the calendars. The contacts' job no longer reads the calendars, and the overlaps come from the same read. The day and the week (part 2, 28 October 2026) are made from the same read with the plan's days, Health's meals, naps and night (pure reads, today's meals where the plan moved them, another day's by `work::held_from`, the plan's own rule) and the doses ahead (`Health::read`); today's and tomorrow's appointments come from one read for the head line. A heavy week (fifteen events a day, four doses) is about 102 KB of JSON and is made in 2 ms once warm (17 ms the first time: Fluent's messages read once, `agenda::said` keeps one translator). ## PGP (RFC 9580, PGP/MIME RFC 3156)
- **Sequoia** (`sequoia-openpgp`, LGPL-2.0-or-later), with its pure-Rust cryptography, so it builds the same on Windows.
- **Your keys** live in Sioul's store; their passphrase in the system keyring, so nothing is asked at each message. Keys come in by import (a file, or GnuPG's export), or are made by Sioul.
- **Others' keys**: from their messages (Autocrypt headers, attached keys), from their contact card, from the Web Key Directory of their domain and keys.openpgp.org.
- **Reading**: an encrypted message is decrypted on opening; a signature is checked and said next to the sender's shield (signed by whom, valid or not).
- **Writing**: two small toggles, sign and encrypt; encrypt is offered only when every recipient's key is known, and says whose key is missing otherwise.
- **In view**: the two toggles and the reader's line. **One step**: key details, fingerprints, import and export, in Accounts under "Encryption".

### As built
- **Sending**: PGP/MIME (RFC 3156). Signed alone: `multipart/signed`, the text parts in quoted-printable so trailing spaces (the "-- " line) and long lines arrive as signed. Encrypted: `multipart/encrypted`, signed inside, to every recipient's key and yours (to read it again in Sent). The subject stays readable, as in most clients today. An encrypted message names every key it was encrypted to, the Bcc recipients' too, and anyone can look a key up on a key server: so every recipient can see that a key was added for someone, and which. With "Encrypt" on and recipients in Bcc, the writing window says so before Send (`DraftProtection::bcc_seen`); a message of its own for each Bcc recipient is for a later round.
- **Reading**: PGP/MIME encrypted or signed, and inline PGP; a decrypted message lives only in memory, its attachments decrypted again when opened. The line under the sender says "Encrypted · Signed by …", "Signed with a key Sioul does not know", or "The signature does not match the text", in warm colours, never red. Answering an encrypted message switches "Encrypt" on when every key is known; its copy beside the draft stays encrypted, opened only at Send (below, "Encrypted messages you answer").
- **Keys**: "Make a key for …" in Accounts makes a Curve25519 key valid three years, with a random passphrase the keyring keeps, and keeps the revocation certificate Sequoia makes with it beside it: `~/.local/share/sioul/pgp/own/<fingerprint>.rev` (`$XDG_DATA_HOME/sioul/pgp/own/`, the data folder's `pgp/own/` on Windows and macOS), readable by you alone as the key is, never shared between devices, moved aside with the key to `pgp/removed/` when the key is removed (`pgp::revocation_path`). As GnuPG writes its own (`openpgp-revocs.d`), the file says what it is, then the certificate armored with a colon before its first dashes, so that it cannot be imported by mistake. "Save the revocation certificate", beside the key, copies it to your downloads, to keep somewhere safe apart from the device; `sioul pgp make` prints its path after the fingerprint. Revoking is not built: once it is, the certificate is what revokes a key that is lost. A key imported or on a security key has no certificate here: its own tools made it; "Import a key…" takes GnuPG's export (`gpg --export-secret-keys --armor you@example.org`), asks its passphrase once and keeps it in the keyring; "Save the public key" writes it to your downloads. Others' keys come from their messages (Autocrypt, only when the message is not forged), from a file, or from "Look for their keys" in the writing window (their domain's Web Key Directory, then keys.openpgp.org: only on request, since it tells a server to whom you write). Your outgoing mail carries your key in an Autocrypt header.
- **Which key is whose** (review of 19 October 2026, F1 and F2): a user ID counts only when Sequoia's standard policy sees it bound to its key and not revoked (`pgp::bound`): one written into a certificate without its key's signature names nothing. A certificate revoked as a whole serves nothing (`pgp::usable`, `pgp::names`; review of 19 October 2026, N3): Sequoia's test of a key's own revocation misses the certificate's, so a certificate revoked as compromised used to keep its live subkeys, and the newest-key rule below picked it. It is never chosen for an address, never encrypted to (`pgp::encrypt` passes it over whoever chose it), never takes a seal or a copy of your own, never signs; it still opens what was sent to it before. It is still kept when it comes (a header, a lookup), so that its revocation reaches the copy kept and stops it; the writing window says your own was revoked, and what to do: make a new key, or read your new security key (`pgp-self-copy-revoked`). A certificate revoked by its designated revoker (a Revocation Key, GnuPG's `addrevoker`) counts as revoked once that revocation checks against the revoker's certificate kept here, yours or someone's (`Keys::usable`; R2): Sequoia says such a certificate "could be" revoked and leaves the check to whoever holds the revoker's key; a revocation that does not check is passed over, since anyone can attach one, and with the revoker's certificate not kept here nothing checks it. A signature by such a key reads "Signed with a key that was revoked" (`pgp-signed-revoked`). A key that came from elsewhere keeps only the user ID of the address it came for (`pgp::for_its_address`): from an Autocrypt header, the one its `addr` names; from a lookup, the one asked for. An Autocrypt header carrying one of your certificates is passed over (`learn_autocrypt_at`), your security key's too on a device that knows it from the sharing alone (your own mail read on the phone: `pgp::yours_at`), and a lookup that brings one of yours again adds to it only what your own primary key binds (a renewal's signatures, a subkey), never another source's user IDs (`store_from_elsewhere_at`). Your addresses are those of your accounts and those your security keys name (`Keys::is_own_address`), never what a certificate's user IDs claim. When two keys of someone else's claim one address (a key made anew, the old one kept), the one whose newest live encryption key is the most recent serves, then the one whose primary key is, then the fingerprint's order: never the order the folder lists them in (`Keys::for_address`).
- **What others' keys serve** (review of 19 October 2026, N2): beside each key in `pgp/others/` a small file says the addresses it was learned for, the only ones it serves (`<fingerprint>.toml`, format 2, travelling with it in the senders' part): an Autocrypt header's address or a lookup's, or, for a key you import from a file, every address bound to it, since you chose the file. A key that comes again for another address serves both; its file keeps only those addresses' user IDs (`pgp::store_others`), so another address it names never serves, nor shows in Accounts. Keys kept before, or written through the sharing by an older Sioul, have no such file: each device writes it once for them, as it judges them, saying those addresses were judged rather than learned (`judged`, for a later rule to look at again; `pgp::keep_others_for_their_addresses`, marked in `state/pgp-others.toml`, run with the look below), and judges at each reading one that has none (an older Sioul's write arriving). A key is judged by every user ID it carries, bound or not, revoked or not (`pgp::judged`), since a Sioul before 1f9acc0 took a key on any of them: a header's key had to name its sender in some user ID, bound by nothing if it liked. It serves its one address only when every user ID names that same address and it is bound; else none. So a key naming several addresses serves none, since which one it came for was never recorded and a key's owner can bind any address to it (Mallory's key naming Jane's address served it, the newest-key rule choosing it every time); and so does a key whose one bound address is not the one it came for: Mallory's key binding Jane's address and carrying Mallory's own in a user ID bound by nothing, which Mallory's own header made pass (review of 19 October 2026, R1: taken by its one bound address, it served Jane's, and the first rule of a18e58f would have written that down on every device). It stays on the disk, never deleted, and serves again as soon as it comes for an address: its owner's next message (Autocrypt) or **Look for their keys**; meanwhile the writing window says the key is missing, and its signatures read as a key Sioul does not know. Written so, a key an older Sioul later merges with every user ID of a header still serves only what its file says. A lookup that brings one of your own certificates adds only what your own key binds: a subkey bound by nothing, or by another key, is left out (`only_bound_subkeys`). Tested: `pgp::tests::an_autocrypt_header_never_makes_another_address_yours`, `a_senders_key_naming_janes_address_never_becomes_janes`, `a_key_kept_before_serves_no_address_it_was_not_learned_for`, `a_key_kept_before_bound_to_one_address_it_did_not_come_for_serves_none`, `others_keys_say_what_they_serve_and_an_older_write_adds_none`, `a_revoked_certificate_never_serves`, `a_designated_revokers_revocation_is_honoured_once_it_checks`, `a_lookup_bringing_yours_adds_only_what_your_key_signed`.
- **Tested** both ways with GnuPG 2.4: Sioul's signed and encrypted message decrypts in GnuPG with "Good signature"; GnuPG's encrypted, signed and tampered messages read as such in Sioul.

### Encrypted messages you answer
The copy of the message a draft answers or forwards, kept beside it (`drafts/<id>.eml`, `Draft::keep_original`) so that the answer can quote it once the message is archived or deleted, stays as it came: encrypted when it came so. The draft says it was kept so (`Draft::copy_as_received`). Until 19 October 2026 Sioul wrote the decrypted text there (`crypto::protect_answer`): it then sat in clear on the disk, the sharing carried it to every device (sealed in the sharing folder, in clear on each device) and kept its earlier versions there. What you type in an answer is kept as any draft is, in clear on each of your devices and sealed in the sharing: encrypting a draft's own words would make each draft ask for your key, and a phone could not open them.
- **At Send** the copy is opened in memory only (`send::build_draft`): with the session key kept since you opened the message (until Sioul closes), with your key here, or with your security key in the same access that signs: one PIN. A key whose touch setting asks for each use asks two touches, one to open and one to sign. A message encrypted to another security key than the one that signs is opened first with that key and its PIN, its session key then kept, and the answer is signed after (`securitykey::open_quote_now`). The quote goes in the encrypted answer, to the recipients and to you; nothing of it is written anywhere.
- **Not encrypted**, an answer goes without its quote, and a forward does not go: an encrypted message's text never leaves in clear. The writing window says so under the boxes as soon as "Encrypt" is unticked (`DraftProtection::sealed_quote`); Send refuses such a forward at once (`securitykey::forward_refused`), never at the end of the ten seconds.
- **Where the copy does not open** the writing window says so under the text, and its copy stays encrypted: on a device that cannot use your security key (a phone with neither NFC nor a USB host: `securitykey::usable_here`, `crypto::Answered::Elsewhere`; a phone with either opens it at Send, as a computer does) an answer sent from there goes encrypted without the quote, and a forward only from a device that opens it; when no key of yours known on the device opens it (encrypted to a key you no longer have, to one kept on another device alone, or to others alone), the window says that, sent from there, the answer goes without its quote, and that it cannot be forwarded from there; Send refuses such a forward at once with the same words (`crypto::Answered::Elsewhere`, `NoKey`). The window never says the message "came encrypted": a copy the look sealed (below) may be of one that came in clear.
- **Copies written in clear before** (`sioul_core::draftcopies`, `crypto::seal_clear_drafts`) are encrypted again to your own certificates (your security key's, as your computer read it, and your keys here; an encryption key of yours that expired, not revoked, still takes them: you open them with it), on every device that holds them: beside the drafts, among the sharing's earlier versions (`share/history/drafts/`) and in what was kept before sharing began (`share/before-sharing-<day>/`). In doubt, a copy is sealed, judged by where it comes from, never by its shape alone (review of 19 October 2026, N7). One kept as it came (its draft says so) is passed over. Any other was written before 19 October: the message itself when it came in clear, its decrypted text when it came encrypted. It is sealed unless it is, byte for byte, a message found in your mail on that device under its Message-ID or the one its draft answers (`Draft::in_reply_to`), every message of those IDs there having come in clear (the mail index keeps every file of an ID: `MailIndex::paths`): a copy of a message found nowhere, or of one that another message shares its ID with (anyone who knows an ID can send one), is sealed, whatever mailer wrote it. (Until 19 October a copy whose head was not one Sioul rebuilt was taken for a message as it came; but a decrypted entity with an empty head, or none, rebuilt so has exactly that shape.) A sealed copy keeps outside only the outer headers its message came with; everything after the `MIME-Version: 1.0` line Sioul added goes inside as it was: protected headers (the subject its sender encrypted), or an entity's first line when it had no head, which would else read as a header (`pgp::seal_message`). What a look decides is kept with the file as it read it (the stamp of the bytes read, from their descriptor; for a copy it sealed, the new file as written), never with what the path names afterwards: a version an exchange puts in place meanwhile is looked at again (N6). A copy that only quotes an armoured block is not taken for sealed (`pgp::is_encrypted`: the whole message, or its whole first text part, one block). The draft's own file, its text and its choices, is never touched, and a copy is replaced whole, never missing: no draft is lost, and a draft edited on two devices meanwhile keeps both versions, as any draft does. An encrypted copy whose draft went (sent while the look replaced it) is taken out a day later.
- **When the look runs** (it first writes, once, what others' keys kept before serve: above, "What others' keys serve"): at the window's start, in its own thread once the environment is set, before each of the window's exchanges, before it takes the hand that a dose's alarm waits for (`BUSY`), and at a phone's background step, after its exchange; never on a dose's alarm, at "Taken" nor at a waking, where Android gives seconds (review of 19 October 2026, F9). Its verdicts are kept on the disk (`state/draftcopies.json`, never shared: each file's path, its device's and inode's numbers, its size and its time), so that a process started for a moment reads only what changed, and your keys are read only when a copy is to be sealed. Measured in a fresh process, "Taken"'s exchange with 150 copies of 100 KB pending in the history and 300 others' keys took as long as with the keys alone (9 to 26 ms; 6 to 17 ms with nothing kept): the copies cost it nothing (`share::tests::measure_the_alarm_path_with_a_look_pending`). A device with no certificate of yours that takes a seal writes nothing and looks again later; Accounts ▸ Encryption says in one quiet line how many copies wait, with nothing to press. The log says counts only. Tested: `draftcopies::tests`, `share::drafts_tests::clear_copies_are_encrypted_on_every_device_and_none_stays`, `send::tests::an_encrypted_message_answered_opens_at_send_with_the_key_that_signs`, `send::tests::a_key_here_opens_the_copy_and_a_stranger_s_does_not`, `share::drafts_tests::a_crash_s_leftovers_among_the_drafts_go_an_hour_later`, `filelock::tests::a_read_only_copy_is_overwritten_too`, `draftcopies::tests::a_decrypted_copy_without_headers_of_its_own_is_sealed`, `a_copy_replaced_while_the_look_reads_the_mail_is_looked_at_again`, `a_copy_replaced_as_soon_as_the_look_lets_go_is_looked_at_again`, `crypto::tests::the_look_leaves_no_clear_copy_and_says_counts_only`, `the_look_is_off_the_doses_path`, `a_draft_opened_without_the_key_says_so_and_stays_encrypted`, `securitykey::tests::a_forward_that_cannot_go_is_refused_at_send`, `pgp::tests::a_sealed_copy_keeps_the_protected_headers_inside`.

#### What a disk keeps
A copy Sioul encrypts again itself is written beside the old one, under a hidden name the sharing passes over, and onto the disk (`filelock::Prepared`); then, under the sharing's exchange lock, it is renamed over the old one if that is still the file that was read (`Prepared::place`), so that its path always holds the old copy or the new one; then, the lock let go, the old file, held open across the rename and now without a name, is overwritten with zeros and flushed before it goes (`Placed::finish`; a copy that is yours but read-only is made writable first). The lock is taken a short hold at a time, a fifth of a second at most, for the renames alone (and the removal of an orphan copy, above), so that an exchange waiting for it (a dose's) never waits longer for it; and since the look runs neither on a dose's alarm nor while the window's exchange holds the hand a dose's alarm waits for, nothing else of it delays a dose. An exchange reads these files only under that lock and by their names, so it never holds a copy being replaced: it never reads, seals or sends the zeros. Every other reader (Send, the writing window, the sharing's own reads and copies as a last guard) reads through `filelock::read_whole` or `copy_whole`: the file read through its descriptor, then checked to still have a name and to be the one its path names, else read again, eight times at most; what the sharing records of a file (its size, its time, its hash) is that of the bytes so read. On 19 October 2026 the final pass caught a plain read getting the zeros (`filelock::tests::a_file_replaced_between_its_opening_and_its_reading_is_read_again` makes it happen; `share::drafts_tests::no_copy_is_replaced_while_an_exchange_holds_the_lock`, `exchanges_running_while_copies_are_replaced_never_send_zeros`). What a crash leaves beside a file (its hidden temporaries) is swept once it is an hour old, at the sharing's daily tidy, in the folders that travel whole too. That is as far as a program can go, and it covers the copy Sioul replaces itself. On ext4 or XFS on a hard disk, the zeros land where the text was. The earlier copies the sharing replaced, on each device it reached (another device's encrypted copy written in place of a clear one, by a plain rename), the versions pruned before, the temporaries of a version received or put back, and an old copy whose overwrite a crash cut short, are freed as any deleted file is, never overwritten: the disk's encryption is what covers them. A filesystem that writes each change elsewhere (Btrfs, Fedora's default; ZFS; APFS on a Mac; F2FS on many phones), and any SSD or flash memory, which moves its blocks on its own, keep the old blocks until they are reused; snapshots (Btrfs's, Time Machine's) and backups keep them as long as they last. Only the disk's own encryption makes those leftovers unreadable to someone who holds the disk: LUKS on Linux, FileVault on a Mac, BitLocker on Windows; Android encrypts a phone's storage itself. On Windows nothing is overwritten: the copy is replaced, its old blocks freed, and no reader there can meet zeros. The sharing folder never held the text in clear, but it and its server keep the old copies, sealed with the sharing's key, until those rounds go (a device's round goes once it has written a megabyte past it and every device read past it) and the server forgets them (its versions, its trash bin, a sync app's own versioning, backups of the folder): anyone holding the sharing's passphrase could open them meanwhile.

### Your keys on a security key
An OpenPGP card (a YubiKey, a Nitrokey: OpenPGP card specification 3.4) holds the private keys and never gives them out. Sioul hands it a digest to sign or a wrapped session key to open, after its PIN, and the key may wait for a touch. Sioul keeps what is public, above all the certificate, which a card does not hold.
- **Where a key's private part is** (`pgp::Place`): here (`pgp/own`, its passphrase in the keyring), or on a security key, listed in `pgp/cards.toml` with the fingerprints of its two keys, the certificate they belong to, the addresses it signs for, and where and when its certificate came; no secret. A security key read on this device that serves an address signs for it alone, and Autocrypt announces its certificate; a key kept here for the same address only opens older mail (`Keys::own_for`, `Keys::card_for`).
- **On every device** (19 October 2026): what a computer read of the key and its certificate, public parts only, travel sealed to your other devices (`data/pgp/cards/`, one file per key: `securitykey::share_cards`); never `pgp/own`, never a private key, a PIN, a session key or an opened message. Written only by a computer that read the key itself: after every change of `cards.toml`, each time its certificate is kept again (a renewal, a new subkey), and at an exchange when the shared file lacks something the computer holds (F5: the file is merged, which only adds, so it settles), merged with the one shared already so that a renewal made on one computer is never undone by another. A key is shared for the first time once, by a mark kept on that computer and never shared (`state/security-keys.toml`, `securitykey::share_cards_at_exchange`): a file another computer took out when it stopped the key is never written again by the next exchange; only reading the key again, or keeping its certificate again, writes it again (F4). **Stop using this security key** stops it on that device: it goes from `cards.toml` there and from the sharing (`securitykey::forget_in`), and a list kept there, never shared (`pgp/stopped.toml`), makes that device pass over what another computer shares of it again (`Keys::load_at`); reading the key there again takes it off the list. The other computers that read it keep using it and may share it again (a renewal there), and the words say so: stop it there too (`seckey-stopped`, `seckey-stopped-shared`, which also says how to take it back on a computer: read it there). A key another device read is not offered to stop on a phone (review of 19 October 2026, N5): a phone with neither NFC nor a USB host cannot read a security key, so nothing there could take it back, and stopping it there would only stop the phone encrypting your own copies (a phone with either reads it again, held against it or plugged in, and may stop it); stopped on a computer that read it, it leaves the phone too (`securitykey::may_stop`; pressed anyway, the phone says to stop it on a computer that read it). A device that receives the file counts that certificate as yours only when its primary key is the one the key's record names and its signing and decryption keys are bound to it, alive, as Sequoia's policy sees them (`securitykey::vouched`, through `securitykey::check` with the slots' fingerprints: F3); anything else is passed over. A device signs only with a key it read itself (F18): a key shared from another computer encrypts to you, gives Autocrypt your certificate and the Reader its words, and signs nowhere else; on a computer that did not read it, Accounts says "Read on another computer of yours. To sign with it here, plug it in and read it here.", and the writing window keeps "Sign" off and says so (`seckey-sign-read-here`). Accounts never offers "Make a key for" an address your security key serves, read here or elsewhere (N4): on a computer that did not read it, it says so under the keys, with **Use a security key** (`crypto::offers`, `pgp-read-card-here`); on a phone, nothing. The same offer stands on a computer that keeps a key of its own for that address: that key signs there meanwhile while Autocrypt hands out your security key's certificate, which a recipient cannot match to the signature until the key is read there. Autocrypt is not switched to the key kept here instead: correspondents would take whichever certificate came last, from this computer or another, and mail encrypted to the key kept here opens on this computer alone. "Renew for two years" is offered only for a key read on that device: renewing looks for the key among those read there. So your sent copy opens on each of your computers (and on a phone with NFC, the key held against it: below, "On a phone"); a phone encrypts to you as well as to the recipients and hands your key by Autocrypt; a phone with neither NFC nor a USB host says "Encrypted to your security key: open it on a computer for now", with no button, and its writing window keeps "Sign" off and says why. For your own addresses only your own certificate is used (`Keys::for_address`, `Keys::self_cert_for`): never one someone sent claiming them (an Autocrypt header, a lookup). Whenever a message goes encrypted without a copy for you, the writing window says so before you send (`crypto::self_copy_line`): which part of your certificate cannot take it (its encryption subkey expired, with its date, or revoked: `securitykey::expired_parts`, `revoked_encryption`) and what can renew it (N8): your security key's certificate, **Renew for two years** in Accounts on a computer (on a phone, "on one of your computers"); a key kept here, only the program that made it, or a new key ("Make a new key in Accounts ▸ Encryption, or extend this one with the program that made it": Accounts cannot renew it). A certificate revoked as a whole is said so; with none of yours known here for that address, that is said. Tested: `pgp::tests::your_security_keys_certificate_travels_and_only_it_is_yours`, `a_renewal_reaches_every_device_and_is_never_undone`, `a_renewal_lost_to_an_older_write_comes_back_at_the_next_exchange`, `a_card_stopped_on_one_computer_never_comes_back_to_it`, `a_card_read_on_another_computer_signs_only_there`, `a_shared_certificate_counts_only_with_the_cards_keys_bound`, `send::tests::the_phone_encrypts_to_its_recipients_and_to_you`, `share::tests::an_older_sioul_keeps_your_security_keys_waiting`, `crypto::tests::the_writing_window_says_what_your_key_can_do_here`, `the_self_copy_line_says_what_can_renew_the_key`, `a_shared_security_key_is_read_here_never_doubled_by_a_new_key`, `securitykey::tests::a_shared_key_is_stopped_only_where_it_can_be_read_again`.
- **The card's traits** (`securitykey::Readers`, `securitykey::Card`): one key is opened for one operation (what it says, its PIN in mode 81 or 82, a signature or a deciphering), then closed, which resets it, so that no other program finds it unlocked. `CardSigner` and `CardDecryptor` are Sequoia's own `Signer` and `Decryptor`: the key is opened when Sequoia asks, never before, and each signature is verified with the certificate's key before it is used. The glue follows `sequoia-keystore-openpgp-card`: RSA's DigestInfo built by `openpgp-card` before the card pads and signs it (`signature_for_hash`), EdDSA as r ‖ s, ECDH's shared secret unwrapped by Sequoia. The tests run it against `SoftCard`, a software key behind the same traits.
- **Signing at Send**: building and sending are two steps (`send::build_draft`, `send::send_built`). A message your security key signs is built and signed in the writing window when you press Send (its PIN, maybe a touch), before the ten seconds of "Undo" (`Work::SendSigned`); "Undo" throws the signed copy away and opens the draft again. A key kept here still signs at the end of the ten seconds. An encrypted answer to a message encrypted to your security key is built the same way, the message opened in the same access, with the same PIN (above, "Encrypted messages you answer"); unsigned, the band only opens it, and offers no "Send unsigned".
- **Opening**: nothing touches the key because a message is shown. The Reader says "Encrypted for your security key" and offers **Open with your security key** (on a phone with neither NFC nor a USB host, the line above); the session key then stays in memory (`pgp::SessionKeys`, by the SHA-256 of the encrypted bytes), so the message, its attachments and the copy kept with an answer (the same bytes) open again without the key until Sioul closes.
- **The PIN** is typed in Sioul's own field (`qml/SecurityKeyBand.qml`), emptied at once, and held in Rust as Sequoia's `Password` (encrypted in memory, wiped when dropped): 15 minutes after its last use (`securitykey::PinMemory`), forgotten when its key's reader goes, on **Forget the PIN now**, and when Sioul closes; never written anywhere, never logged. On a phone it is never held: typed for each use, dropped when the visit is done (below). A key that asks its PIN for each signature gets it each time. A key with one PIN try left, as it says at the moment of use (the others spent elsewhere), is never given a PIN typed without a word (review of 23 October 2026, F1): `CardAccess::run` refuses it (`CardError::LastTry`), nothing sent, and the band says "one try left: type it again only if you are sure" (on a phone, "enter it on a computer, or…"); the PIN typed next in that band is sent (`confirm_last_try`), once; a PIN held, which the key took before, goes as before. A PIN typed waits a minute for its key at most, then is forgotten and asked again (`PIN_WAITS_AT_MOST`, F2). The PIN typed reaches Rust as a `String`, taken into Sequoia's protected memory and the plain copy overwritten (`take_pin`, F3); QML's text and Qt's `QString` cannot be overwritten (the field is emptied at once), nor the VERIFY command `openpgp-card` builds in a plain vector (its issue #73). Sioul asks only the PIN: never the Admin PIN, never to change or unblock one, never to load or make keys.
- **Touch**: each key's touch setting (its user interaction flag, DOs D6 to D8: on, fixed, cached) is read without the PIN when the key is read, and kept with it. When the key is asked to sign or open and its setting asks for a touch, the band says so at that moment: "Touch your security key now, and hold your finger on it for a second or two: it waits about 15 seconds." A YubiKey's light says little, and a brief tap is often not taken. A setting that does not read is said "If your key asks for a touch…": a wrong guess costs nothing (`Touch::words`, `smartcard::touch_of`). A touch that never came is said as such, with **Try again**.
- **The certificate** is looked up only when you ask (at setup, "Look for it", and "Look for a newer version"): the address written on the key (HTTPS only), the Web Key Directory of your addresses' domains, then keys.openpgp.org by fingerprint; or **Import from GnuPG**; or a file (**Import a file…**). `securitykey::check` keeps it only when it is the key's: each key on the card is in it, not revoked, allowed to do what its slot does, and its public part is the card's (a fingerprint on a card is only data written to it). An expired certificate is said part by part, never as "the key" when one part alone expired: "Its encryption subkey (B81A…BF8C) expired on 8 April 2025: nobody can encrypt to you with it.", the same for a signing or an authentication subkey and for the primary key (`securitykey::parts`, `expired_parts`, `expiry_words`), where it is added or imported and under the key in Accounts; when its subkeys alone expired, **Renew for two years** is offered right there, with the commands by hand. A month ahead, it is said that it expires soon. An expired encryption subkey still opens what was sent to it: Sequoia's standard policy allows it.
- **GnuPG's steps as buttons** (`sioul_sync::securitykey::Gnupg`), each run on its press only, with your own GnuPG and its home as GnuPG finds it; in a Flatpak, on a phone, or where `gpg` is not found, the button says so and what to do instead:
  - **Import from GnuPG** runs `gpg --batch --armor --export <fingerprint>` and keeps the certificate when `securitykey::check` says it is the key's, its signatures' numbers read as GnuPG 2.4.4 may write them, one octet too long ([compatibility.md](compatibility.md), "Read as GnuPG writes them"). GnuPG holding no such key, it says so, with **Look for it** and **Import a file…** beside.
  - **Renew for two years**, shown when the certificate expired or expires within a month and GnuPG holds the key's secret part or knows the security key that does (`gpg --list-secret-keys`): `gpg --quick-set-expire <fingerprint> 2y`, then the same with each subkey named (`gpg --quick-set-expire <fingerprint> 2y <subkey> <subkey>`, as GnuPG lists them, expired ones included: GnuPG's `"*"` sets only the subkeys not yet expired, and a subkey expired since long stayed so); GnuPG's own pinentry asks for the security key's PIN (and the key for a touch); Sioul never sees the PIN. The certificate is then read again, part by part: renewed, each part is said with its new date; a part still expired is said, and the certificate is not kept as renewed. At the end, done or not, Sioul asks GnuPG to let the key go (`gpgconf --kill scdaemon`, `Gnupg::release`): GnuPG's smart card daemon holds a key for itself after any use, and Sioul could not read it next. This is Sioul cleaning up after its own GnuPG run, never a change to GnuPG's settings; gpg starts the daemon again when it needs the key. "Import from GnuPG" exports the public certificate only, which needs no key: nothing to let go. Sioul reads GnuPG's status as it goes (`--status-fd`): while GnuPG's pinentry is open, the setup says to type the PIN there, then to touch the key; once it closes (on Linux, its process gone), or when no PIN was asked, it says to touch the key now and hold the finger a second or two, until GnuPG answers. A key that waited in vain (GnuPG's timeout, code 62, whatever the language of its words) is said plainly, with **Try again**. Meanwhile Sioul's own looks at the keys wait (`securitykey::may_look`): a key opened by Sioul between two of GnuPG's commands, and reset when closed, would undo the PIN GnuPG gave it. The renewed certificate is then exported, checked and kept, and **Send it to keys.openpgp.org** offered, since others see the new date only once they have the key again. GnuPG not knowing the security key yet, it says to run `gpg --card-status` once. GnuPG has five minutes before it is said as not answering.
  - **Send it to keys.openpgp.org**: first one sentence says what goes public (the public key, findable by its fingerprint; each address only once its link is opened; the private key stays on the security key), with **Send it** and **Not now**. Sent, over HTTPS only, to keys.openpgp.org's own interface (`/vks/v1/upload`, then `/vks/v1/request-verify` for each address not published yet, the mail in your language); Sioul says to which addresses a link went, or that each is published already, or that the certificate it holds names no address (one from keys.openpgp.org itself names none until confirmed: **Import from GnuPG** first). Never by itself.
- **By hand**: under each key's buttons, and under the setup's, the commands that do the same in a terminal (`gpg --export --armor <fingerprint> > key.asc`; expired or expiring, the two `gpg --quick-set-expire`), each in a read-only field that selects, with **Copy** (`qml/CommandText.qml`). Every command the window shows is offered so: the smart card service's (no service), YubiKey Manager's and GnuPG's for a locked key, `gpgconf --kill scdaemon` in a Flatpak, and the packages that install an antivirus or the scans' reader. Sioul writes a command in backticks in its words; words from elsewhere (a server's, GnuPG's, a file's name from a message) never become one.
- **Sioul's looks at the keys**: none runs by itself but two, each while it waits for something: whether the key whose PIN it holds is still plugged in (the readers' names, every second and a half, no key opened), and, while a band waits for a key to be plugged in, the keys opened once the readers change. Both wait while Sioul's own GnuPG works with a key. Bitwarden's unlock with a security key goes through the browser's WebAuthn, never the OpenPGP card.
- **GnuPG** keeps the key for itself after any use, unless `pcsc-shared` is in `scdaemon.conf`. Every step that reads the key and meets it held says so, with **Let GnuPG release it**, rather than waiting: adding the key, reading it again for a certificate when it is not known yet, waiting for it to be plugged in, signing and opening. The lasting way is said with it, the line to copy: `pcsc-shared`, added to `scdaemon.conf` in GnuPG's folder (`~/.gnupg`), lets GnuPG and Sioul share the key. Sioul connects in shared mode, says that another program holds the key, most likely GnuPG, and offers **Let GnuPG release it**, which runs `gpgconf --kill scdaemon` on that click, as Sioul also does at the end of its own GnuPG renewal (above).
- **Errors in words** (`securitykey::CardError`): no smart card service (with the command of the system, to copy), no key plugged in (Sioul goes on as soon as it comes), a key held by another program, a wrong PIN with the tries left and a plain warning before the last, a locked key (what unlocks it, elsewhere), a touch missed, the key pulled out, another key than the one known, a signature that does not match.

- **On a phone, held against it (NFC)** (23 October 2026): the same steps, in one tap. The band asks for the PIN first, from what the phone knows of the key (`pgp/cards.toml`, the shared record: `securitykey::pin_from`), never from a read of its own; then "Hold your security key flat against the back of the phone…" and, once it is held, in that one tap, Sioul reads it, checks it is the key wanted, gives its PIN and signs or opens ("Keep holding it…"), then says "Done: you can take the key away." (`seckey-done-lift`). Its touch setting asks nothing more: over NFC the tap is the touch, for every setting (on, fixed, cached), and a key that refuses all the same is said so, with the USB-C port as the way (`seckey-nfc-touch`). Another key held is said so at once (`CardError::NotYours`) rather than waited for; a key moved away before it finished is said so, and at Send nothing was sent (`seckey-moved-away-sign`); NFC switched off offers **Open NFC settings** (`Remedy::NfcSettings`), and a phone without NFC says so, on a phone that takes no key plugged in (else below). A key locked after three wrong PINs is unblocked on a computer: Sioul never asks the Admin PIN. The PIN is never kept on the phone (`keeps_pin`): typed in the band's field each time, with the screen kept from captures and from the recent apps' picture while a key is wanted (`FLAG_SECURE`). At the setup, a key your other device made known, its certificate shared, is kept at once when the phone reads it (`shared_certificate`): that is how the phone comes to sign (F18 holds: it signs only with a key read there). Sioul reads keys only while one of its bands asks for one and it is in front: other apps (OpenKeychain, Yubico Authenticator) and the key's Yubico OTP tap work as before.
- **On a phone, plugged into its USB-C port** (23 October 2026): the same visit, PIN first, never kept; the key plugged in comes before one held. Its touch is asked as on a computer (the band's touch line, whatever the setting: `PhoneReaders::by_cable`); the waiting line says "hold it, or plug it into the USB-C port" (`seckey-hold`), "plug it in" on a phone without NFC (`seckey-plug-phone`), and with NFC off, "plug it in, or switch NFC on", with **Open NFC settings** (`seckey-plug-nfc-off`; `Readers::reach_note`): a phone without NFC, or with NFC off, still takes a key plugged in. Unplugged before it finished: "The key was unplugged before it finished…", at Send "Nothing was sent" (`seckey-unplugged-sign`); done: "Done: you can unplug the key." Android's permission is asked once for each plugging, while a band asks for the key (the key plugged in before or after): refused, "Android did not let Sioul use the key. Unplug it, plug it in again, and choose Allow." (`CardError::UsbRefused`), asked again only once it was unplugged; another app holding the key's smart card interface, "Another app holds your security key, Yubico Authenticator or OpenKeychain perhaps…" (`CardError::UsbBusy`). Yubico OTP stays on: the key's keyboard is never claimed, so a touch while nothing waits types its code as before; while the key waits or works no text field keeps the focus (the band takes it), and a code the key typed into the PIN's field (32 to 64 letters of modhex, then Enter) is never given to the key as its PIN, which would cost a try: the band says so and asks again (`typed_by_the_key`, `seckey-pin-otp`; on a computer too). Tested: `securitykey::phone::tests::a_key_plugged_into_the_phone_signs_and_opens_its_touch_asked`, `how_a_phone_reaches_a_key_is_said`; the app's `a_phone_says_hold_or_plug_and_what_went_wrong`, `a_code_the_key_typed_is_not_taken_for_its_pin`. Tested: `securitykey::tests::a_phone_asks_the_pin_first_and_never_keeps_it`, `a_phone_says_hold_and_what_went_wrong`, `a_phone_says_to_hold_the_key_where_a_computer_says_to_plug_it`, `a_key_known_from_another_device_is_kept_when_held_to_the_phone`; in sioul-core, `another_key_held_to_the_phone_is_said_not_waited_for`.

#### As built
- **The transport** (`sioul_sync::securitykey::smartcard`): `openpgp-card` 0.7 and `card-backend-pcsc` 0.5 over the system's PC/SC, pinned; each reader connected by Sioul itself in shared mode (`card-backend-pcsc` would skip a key GnuPG holds without saying so: a sharing violation is said as such), one transaction per operation, the key disconnected after with `ResetCard`. The card's status words in words: 63Cx a wrong PIN with x tries left, 6983 locked, 6600 a touch missed, and 6982 too when the PIN was just taken by a key that asks for a touch (to be confirmed on a YubiKey); a card gone mid-way, removed. `sioul pgp cards` says what each key plugged in says, without its PIN.
- **Logs**: `openpgp-card` traces whole APDUs, a VERIFY's PIN included, and a card's answers. Sioul installs no logger, no crate it builds with installs one, and each program caps the level at "info" as it starts (`securitykey::cap_logging`), never raising it; a test holds all three.
- **Packaging**: Linux builds need pcsc-lite's development files (CI: `libpcsclite-dev`); the AppImage links pcsc-lite 2.5's client in, built in its job, since Ubuntu 22.04's speaks only protocol 4.4, which the daemons of Debian 13 and Ubuntu 25.10 refuse; the Flatpak has `--socket=pcsc`.
- **Tested without hardware** (7 October 2026): everything above but the transport itself, against the software key; the transport's mapping of status words in a unit test; the window's states pictured on a demo profile (`SIOUL_GRAB_STEPS=security-key`), whose own software key (PIN 123456) never reaches the smart card service. A test reading a real key without its PIN (`a_real_key_reads_without_its_pin`) is ignored in every run: it is run by hand, with a key plugged in.
- **GnuPG's steps, tested** (8 October 2026) in a GnuPG home made for the test, its agent never starting the smart card daemon, never `~/.gnupg`, never a card, never the network (`gnupg_exports_and_renews_in_a_throwaway_home`): the software key's certificate imported there, exported and checked against the key; a key GnuPG does not hold, and a fingerprint that is none, said so; a key of its own made without a passphrase, valid a year, renewed for two, its subkey too. keys.openpgp.org against a stand-in server (`keys_openpgp_gets_the_certificate_and_mails_each_address`): the certificate as it is, then a link asked for the address not published yet, in the language given; nothing asked when all are; a refusal said with the server's words; never over plain HTTP. The commands' fields: `tools/qml-test.sh tst_takes.qml` (words, then each command apart; a lone backtick, or a line not Sioul's own, stays words). Not tried yet with a real security key and GnuPG's pinentry.
- **Tried with a real key** (8 October 2026): a YubiKey 5 (OpenPGP application 3.4, Ed25519 and Cv25519, a touch asked for each use), on the native build: the setup, an expired key and its expired subkeys renewed through GnuPG, mail signed and encrypted at Send, and encrypted mail opened. GnuPG's `'*'` leaves expired subkeys as they are, so Renew names each one.
- **Limits** (review of 23 October 2026, F4): Sioul gives the PIN only to a key that answers with the identity and the fingerprints of the one it knows, but a counterfeit card made to copy them (they are public), held within centimetres of the phone or plugged in, would receive the PIN; it gets no private key, and the PIN is useless without the real key. A reader under someone else's control on a computer is the same risk. Nothing on the wire tells a real key from such a copy (the OpenPGP card has no attestation Sioul could check).
- **The phone's transport** (`sioul_sync::securitykey::phone`, `sioul_sync::securitykey::ocard`; `crates/sioul-app/src/nfc.rs`, `android/main.cpp`, `SecurityKeyLink.java`): Android has no smart card service, so `openpgp-card` gets Sioul's own backend, whose APDUs go through Android's `IsoDep`; the card is read, given its PIN and used by the same code as a computer's (`ocard::OpenCard`). Reader mode (`NfcAdapter.enableReaderMode`: NFC-A and B, no NDEF read, a presence check every 50 ms) is on only while a band asks for the key, ahead of other apps, and off after; no tag filter in the manifest, so holding the key never opens Sioul, and NFC is not required to install it. Each exchange is at most what the phone's controller carries (`getMaxTransceiveLength`); extended length only where both the key and the phone take it, else short APDUs, chained, their long answers read with GET RESPONSE (`phone::limit`); five seconds an exchange; one visit makes at most 512 exchanges (`phone::MOST_EXCHANGES`, F5), so that a card that answers "more bytes wait" (61xx) without end is let go, said as no OpenPGP card. At the end of each use the PIN's checks are undone (VERIFY with P1 = FF, OpenPGP card specification 3.4.1, §7.2.2), then the key is let go: Android hands it to no other app until it has left the field (`NfcAdapter.ignore`). Nothing of an APDU is logged, in Rust, the bridge or Java; each command's and answer's copies are wiped. Tested without hardware: an OpenPGP card emulated APDU by APDU over the software key (`securitykey::phone::tests`: Ed25519 and RSA signing, RSA and Curve25519 opening; a usual kind of key, Ed25519 and Cv25519, end to end: a message Sioul encrypts opened through PSO:DECIPHER's ECDH cipher DO (A6 { 7F49 { 86 point } }, the ephemeral point's 32 bytes without OpenPGP's 0x40, the shared secret back), a signature made through PSO:CDS verified by Sequoia (`an_ed25519_and_cv25519_key_opens_and_signs_end_to_end`; X25519 lent to the stand-in by x25519-dalek, in tests only), short and extended APDUs, chaining, GET RESPONSE, every touch setting and a refusal, a key moved away mid-way, NFC off, no NFC, a bank card, the phone's frame, nothing logged), and the Java and the bridge read from their sources (`android/jvm-checks/SecurityKeyCheck.java`). Plugged in (`SecurityKeyLink.java`, `Ccid.java`): while a band asks for the key, the USB devices plugged in are looked at, and those heard plugged in (a receiver registered in code, not exported from Android 13, and before it reached only by senders holding Sioul's signature-level permission, the system and Sioul itself (F6); a key unplugged believed only once Android no longer lists it, Android's answer heard only for a key Sioul asked for; Sioul in the background, the key is let go and the receiver unregistered, unless Android's own dialog came in front, and back in front the key is looked for again (F8); no USB filter in the manifest, so plugging the key never opens Sioul, and USB host is not required to install it); of a key, its smart card interface (class 0x0B) alone is claimed, never its keyboard (Yubico OTP) nor FIDO. Android's permission is asked by `UsbManager.requestPermission`, its answer through a `PendingIntent` for Sioul's package alone, mutable from Android 12. The APDUs go as CCID messages over the two bulk endpoints (`PC_to_RDR_IccPowerOn`, `XfrBlock` with sequence numbers, `IccPowerOff`): packets of the endpoint's size, a zero-length packet after a message that fills its last one, answers read across transfers, a key waiting for a touch, by time extensions or silent, read through for 25 seconds (a YubiKey waits 15), a second per transfer, a key unplugged meanwhile said at once (F9); short APDUs, chained by `openpgp-card`. Unplugged mid-way, a key gone (`CardError::Removed`); when the band no longer asks, the key's PIN checks are undone, it is powered off, its interface released and the connection closed: other apps have it again. Each message's buffer is wiped once sent or read. Tested on the JVM: `android/jvm-checks/CcidCheck.java` (the messages' bytes, packets, a key waiting for a touch, an answer to another message, a reader's failure, a key unplugged, buffers wiped) and the USB parts of `SecurityKeyCheck.java`. Not yet tried on a phone with a real key, by NFC or USB-C.

## Antivirus
- **A program is never opened from mail**: an attachment that runs (`.exe`, `.js`, `.lnk`, `.desktop`, `.terminal`, `.iso`…) can only be saved, and opening it stays your own act, outside Sioul. Files opened or saved from mail carry the system's mark that they came from the Internet (Windows' Mark of the Web, macOS' quarantine), so Office opens them in Protected View and macOS checks them.
- **An attachment is checked before it opens or is saved, when an antivirus is there** ([porch.md](porch.md)). Without one, nothing is refused: Sioul says the file will not be checked, asks before opening or saving it ("Open it unchecked"), and gives the command that installs one on this system, to copy.
- **Each system's own antivirus, nothing shipped**:
  - **Windows**: the Antimalware Scan Interface (AMSI), which hands the file to the antivirus Windows already runs (Defender by default).
  - **Linux and macOS**: ClamAV when the system has it (its packages; Homebrew on a Mac), as macOS always had it. Nothing of ClamAV ships with Sioul: ClamAV is GPL-2.0-only, and a separate program beside Sioul would be allowed, but the packages stay simpler and every system keeps its antivirus current itself. A Flatpak does not reach the system's ClamAV: there, Sioul asks.
- **On a phone, no antivirus, and none claimed.** Android gives an app no way to have another app scan a file (nothing like AMSI), the antivirus apps made for it offer none to call, and ClamAV does not run there. So a phone never scans and never asks: the Reader says that attachments are not checked there, and so does the line after each opening or save. What protects instead:
  - **Sioul's own refusals, as on a computer**: mail set aside keeps its attachments closed, hostile mail shows nothing until you choose to read it, and programs, scripts and installers never open; on a phone Android's installers join them (`.apk`, `.apks`, `.apkm`, `.xapk`; `links::is_phone_program`). Each can still be saved, your own act.
  - **The type from the name, never from the message**: the app is asked to open what the name says (`compose::mime_of`, else Android's own table for the extension), and never as Android's installer, whatever either says.
  - **Android's sandbox**: the attachment goes to the app you choose by a `content://` address lent to read only, for that one file, never to write, never kept for later; that app reaches nothing else of Sioul's. Sioul cannot install anything: it does not hold `REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES`.
  - **Why no question each time**: on a computer, the question points at an antivirus you can install. A phone has none Sioul could call, so the question would come at every opening with the same answer, and a question that always comes is soon answered without being read. The words say instead, in the Reader and after each opening, that the file was not checked.
- **Opening on a phone**: in the app you choose, Android's own "Open with" when no app is set for the type, else the one you set: a PDF in your PDF reader, a picture in the gallery. Sioul's own PDF view exists on computers only (Qt PDF has no Android version), and a computer opens attachments with the desktop's application too, so the two behave alike. When no app opens the type, the line says so, and **Save…** keeps the file with yours.
- **Saving on a phone**: **Save…** asks Android where (its own document picker: Downloads, a folder, a cloud's app), since a phone's Downloads folder is not Sioul's to write; the file is copied there, and leaves Sioul's cache, saved or not.
- **Too big is not clean.** ClamAV stops at its limits (a file's size, what an archive holds, how deep it nests; `MaxFileSize`, `MaxScanSize`, `MaxFiles`, `MaxRecursion`) and, unless told otherwise, calls the file clean without reading the rest (checked with ClamAV 1.4.6: a file over `--max-filesize` comes out `OK`). Its scanner is always run with `--alert-exceeds-max=yes`, which names such a file `Heuristics.Limits.Exceeded…`; Sioul reads that as "not scanned: too big", in calm words, and asks before opening or saving, as without an antivirus. Its daemon has no such option for one scan: its own configuration must say `AlertExceedsMax yes` (the file `clamdscan` reads: `/etc/clamd.d/scan.conf` on Fedora, `/etc/clamav/clamd.conf` on Debian and Arch, Homebrew's under `etc/clamav/`). Without that line, Sioul does not ask the daemon and runs the scanner, slower (seconds, as it loads the signatures) but true. AMSI takes at most 4 GB: a larger file is too big there too.
- One interface in Sioul (`scan(file) → clean | threat | too big | unavailable`), one implementation per system; `install_hint()` gives the command (`sudo dnf install clamav clamav-update && sudo freshclam` on Fedora, apt, pacman, zypper elsewhere, `brew install clamav` on a Mac).

### As built
- `sioul-sync/src/antivirus.rs`: ClamAV's daemon when its configuration alerts on its limits (`daemon_alerts`), then its scanner with the system's signatures, then with Sioul's own in `~/.local/share/sioul/clamav`, which Sioul refreshes with the system's `freshclam` once a day when the system keeps none. On Windows, AMSI. When none answers: the question, with the command.
- **Tested** on Linux without ClamAV (the question comes, with Fedora's command). The AMSI code is written to Microsoft's documented sequence and is checked by the Windows build of the workflow below; it has not run on Windows yet.
- **On Android** (`crates/sioul-app/src/attachments.rs`): `checked_attachment` takes the phone's way there, the computer's unchanged: no scan (`attachments::check`), the installers kept closed (`attachments::kept_closed`), the words. `antivirus::scan` answers "unavailable" on Android without trying, and `install_hint` says to open the file on a computer that has one. Java (`android/package/src/com/aurelienpierre/sioul/Attachments.java`) hands the file through Qt's FileProvider (`<package>.qtprovider`), whose folders are `android/package/res/xml/qtprovider_paths.xml`, which replaces Qt's: Qt's four, and the attachments' folder of the cache, `sioul/attachments/`, alone. Until 8 October 2026 nothing opened on a phone: since Android 7 a `file://` address handed to another app throws `FileUriExposedException`, and Qt 6.11's own `openUrl`, which turns a file into an address of that provider, found no folder of it holding Sioul's cache and gave up without a word. One FileProvider only: when there are two, Qt hands every file it opens (papers, notes, letters) to the one that is not its own. The address is built as FileProvider builds it (`Uri.encode` part by part) and read back by it. **Save…** is `AttachmentSave.java`, an activity never shown: `ACTION_CREATE_DOCUMENT`, the copy on a thread of its own, the cache's copy taken out, then Rust told (`AttachmentSave.nativeSaved`, `sioul_attachment_saved`), which says it in the status line.
- **Tested on a computer** (8 October 2026): the address, the type, what an app is handed (ACTION_VIEW, read only, no other grant) and what Android is asked to save, the provider's folders and the manifest (`android/jvm-checks/AttachmentsCheck.java`); the phone's decision and its words (`attachments::tests`); the Reader's words and buttons in English and French, at a computer's size and at a phone's (`tools/demo/run.sh OUT en attachments phone`, the step list "attachments", nothing opened).

## Windows, macOS, then Android
- **Folders**: the system's own (`directories`): `%APPDATA%\\Sioul` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/Sioul` on macOS, XDG on Linux.
- **Maildir**: `:` is forbidden in Windows file names, so the info separator is `!` there, as mbsync does.
- **Passwords**: the keyring crate's Windows Credential Manager and macOS Keychain backends.
- **Notifications**: Windows toasts and macOS notifications (`notify-rust`).
- **Icons**: the Breeze icons Sioul uses (LGPL-3.0) ship inside it, since only Linux desktops have them.
- **DNS** for the sender checks: the system's resolver on each (hickory reads Windows' own settings).
- **Building**: Qt 6 from Qt's installer on Windows (MSVC) and macOS; a CI build on the three systems for each change.
- **Packages**: Flatpak on Linux, an installer on Windows (MSIX or Inno Setup, `windeployqt`), a disk image on macOS.
- **Android**: the same window, with Qt for Android ([architecture.md](architecture.md), [android.md](android.md)).

### As built
- **Folders**: the XDG variables when set, on every system (tests set them); else XDG on Linux, `%APPDATA%` and `%LOCALAPPDATA%` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support` on macOS (`directories`). Downloads and the cache follow the same rule.
- **Maildir**: `!` before the flags on Windows; `:` and `!` are both read everywhere, so a Maildir copied across reads the same.
- **Keyring**: the Secret Service on Linux, the Credential Manager on Windows, the Keychain on macOS (the keyring crate's backend per system).
- **Notifications**: the "copy" button only where the desktop offers buttons (Linux); elsewhere the code is in the text.
- **Icons**: the Breeze icons Sioul uses ship inside the window, light and dark (`tools/bundle-icons.py`, 1.4 MB, Breeze's LGPL-3.0 licence alongside), one folder per size; the desktop's own theme still comes first on Linux. The light or the dark set follows the window's colours, as they change ([qt-quick.md](qt-quick.md#icons-fonts-and-resources)). They are SVG: each package carries Qt's SVG icon engine and image plugin (`iconengines`, `imageformats`), which its build checks, and the CI reads every name the window uses from the bundle alone on the three systems (`crates/sioul-app/tests/icons.rs`). The AppImage of 0.0.5 and before lacks the icon engine, without which Qt reads no SVG icon of a theme, Breeze's on the desktop as much as Sioul's own.
- **Symbols**: the few Sioul writes (→ ▸ ▾ ✓ ⚙) ship as Sioul Symbols, cut from DejaVu Sans (`tools/make-symbols-font.py`, 15 KB, DejaVu's licence alongside), first in line after the text's own font, so that Qt does not look for them through all the system's fonts.
- **Style**: Qt Quick's Basic style everywhere, the tips Qt makes itself included (`QT_QUICK_CONTROLS_STYLE`, unless set): the system's style (KDE's Breeze, Android's Material) is not loaded for them.
- **OpenPGP**: Sequoia with its pure-Rust cryptography, the same on the three systems.
- **Building**: `.github/workflows/build.yml` tests the core, sync and command line, and builds the window, on Linux, Windows and macOS; on Linux it also runs rustdoc on the five crates, warnings as errors. It runs on each push to `main` that touches code (the docs and the website are skipped), and by hand (Actions, "Build on three systems"), where a Windows folder and a macOS .dmg can be asked for.
- **Packages**: `.github/workflows/release.yml` builds four from a version tag and publishes them as a GitHub release (0.0.1, a pre-release, on 4 October 2026): a Windows installer (Inno Setup, `packaging/windows/sioul.iss`, after `windeployqt`), one macOS .dmg for Apple silicon and Intel (the two builds joined by lipo, signed ad hoc, not notarised), an AppImage, and a Flatpak bundle (`packaging/flatpak/`). None is signed yet: Windows and macOS say so the first time.
- **Android**: an experiment, built: the window as a library that Qt for Android packs into an APK, by `.github/workflows/android.yml` at each change that touches the code; installed by hand, in no store, not ready ([android.md](android.md)).
