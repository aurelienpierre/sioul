# The Porch: the first slice

The Porch is where new mail waits, checked and sorted, until you look. It is the first slice because it removes the main source of distress, the inbox that screams, before anything else is built.

## Lanes, in the order they are decided
Order matters: what protects you most comes first.

| Lane | When | Shown |
|---|---|---|
| **Set aside** | blocked, forged (DMARC fail), borrowing a name, or a stranger's mail your provider calls spam, never proven mail from someone you deal with (below, "Spam: never for people you know") | in the window, at the bottom, with the reason; never deleted; spam has **Not spam**, which puts it back in its lane for good |
| **Caught by your own spam filter** | a stranger's mail Sioul's own filter flagged, or moved into the Junk folder, as you chose for its verdict (below) | in the window, folded, no count, never notified; **Spam** and **Not spam** on each, and for all |
| **Right now** | a one-time code, a password, a password reset, a sign-in link or an address to confirm: what you just asked a site for, from its automatic address (no-reply…) too, and sent with a newsletter's headers too | at once, whatever the time: the one exception to the windows |
| **A project** | a route of a project matches ([notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md#routes)) | in the window, under the project's title |
| **From yourself** | mail from one of your own addresses to another (a file sent from your phone), verified | in its own section, in any hour; never screened, never taken for spam |
| **Filed** | a newsletter or mailing list (`List-Id`, `List-Unsubscribe`, `Precedence: bulk`), or an automatic address (no-reply…) | in the window, folded |
| **Screener** | a stranger (in none of your address books, on no list, not let in), or a sender only a domain on a list names | in the window; you let them in, or not |
| **People** | someone you know (in your address books, let in, or on a list by their own address), outside any project | in the window |
| **Less important accounts** | any mail of an account you ranked below the others (social networks, notifications read sometimes, never answered), unless a project takes it | in the window, folded near the bottom |

These messages come from an explicit request of yours, a moment ago: they are expected, and needed at once. A forged one is set aside before (DMARC fail, a borrowed brand's name, spam); one from a sender that is only **not verified** comes right now too, with a warning to use it only if you just asked that site for it, because fake codes are a phishing trick. A message "from yourself" that is not verified gets no pass: a forged own address is a classic trick.

Many sites send their codes and links through the same services as their newsletters, with the same headers (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Id`, `Precedence: bulk`, a service's own markers). Those headers never decide that a message is not a code: codes are decided before newsletters, and the headers only narrow where a code is looked for ("Detection", below). A message the detector missed used to land in Filed as a newsletter because of them: what was missed was the wording, not the order.

Each lane is a section of the Porch (below, "Sections"); its "?" says once what it holds and how mail lands there, sentence by sentence, and, pressed, adds the Porch's own settings that change it (the senders you let in, the words of automatic senders) and where the others are: an address's rank and shield on its card in Accounts, a project's routes on its page in Projects, who is safe, neutral, restricted, a stranger or blocked, and when each comes, in Settings ▸ What reaches you ([attention.md](attention.md)). Nothing is said under a section's title. The Porch's ⚙ holds what is the Porch's alone: the projects that have a section here, one per line (the others' mail stays on their page in Projects), and every lane but the projects', empty or not, in the order mail is sorted; and how a message opened here reads (font, size, line spacing: the same settings as in Mail's ⚙ and behind "Aa" in Notes). The paper letters' settings are with the letters (below, "Paper letters").

## Notices
A notice is Sioul's record of one new thing: a message, a text, a call your phone declined, another app's notification from your phone, a site's notification, a paper letter, money due (`sioul_core::notices`). The Porch lists notices, never the data behind them, so that one press settles a new thing on every device: a notification left unanswered still costs attention ([research.md](research.md), finding 2). "Notification" stays the system's word: a system notification is one way a notice may reach you.
- **Made by its source**: mail by the Porch's own sorting (`view::porch`), the others by theirs (`calls::view`, `phonemsgs::view`, `texts::notices`, `notices::view` for the sites, the money and the letters). Its id is made from the source alone, the same on every device: `mail:<account>:<digest of its Message-ID>` (without one, the digest of its `sioul:mail/` address), `text:<phone>:<thread>:<its row>`, `call:<phone>:<the call>`, `phone:<phone>:<the message>` (one notification can bring several messages: each is its own notice, so that one that comes later is new again), `site:<site>:<when>-<digest of its words>`, `letter:<the letter>`, `money:<the item>`.
- **What it holds**: its kind, its address (what opening it opens), when it happened, who, a short what, its area (its account's or its site's), its project, and its section (for mail, the Porch's own decision; for the others, their own).
- **Its state**: New until a word is said of it, with when and on which device. **Dismiss** takes it off the Porch of every device and touches nothing else. **Mark read** reads its item too, each kind its own way, where its item has a read state: a message gets `\Seen` here at once and on its server (the client's own path); a text is read on the Texts page once its ten seconds are over, since that mark cannot be taken back; another app's notification is marked read on every device's Porch. A call has no read state: Dismiss is its forget. A site's news is read by opening the site. **Done**: an action settled it, said by what settled it; an item filed or gone needs no word, since its source gives no notice of it any more. A source changes notices too: a message read elsewhere (`\Seen` on its server, another client) is Read; an item that is gone (moved out of the inbox, filed by a project's rule, deleted) gives no notice any more.
- **At once**, with ten seconds to undo: the row goes, and a line at the foot of the list says what was done, with **Undo**, which takes the press back (and unreads a message read), as anything moved is said in one sentence with time to undo (finding 9, trust and transparency).
- **The words travel** in each device's own log, a line per press (below, "The notices' words"; [database.md](database.md), "The notices' words"); the newest word on a notice wins, whichever device said it.
- **Kept** a week after it was said once its source no longer holds the notice: a message dismissed while it stays in your inbox stays dismissed. Another device's words three days more, for a device that stopped sharing. A notice its source never gives again goes with it, after the source's own time (the phone's messages: a week).
- **System notifications follow notices**, so that none keeps asking for attention already given (finding 2): new mail's notification is taken away where it shows once each message it told is dismissed, read or done on any device, or read or gone here: a Linux desktop's looks every 30 seconds while it shows (`notify::mail_until`); a phone's at the window's minute and at each background step (`notices::withdraw_if_settled`, MailNotes.java). On Windows and macOS it stays as the system keeps it.

### The notices' words
Each device writes its own log, `notices/<device>.jsonl` in the state folder, its name in the sharing; nothing but appending, taking a press back within its ten seconds, and forgetting:
- **A line per press**: `{"at": 1793000000000, "ids": ["mail:home:6f1c…"], "state": "dismissed"}`; `state` is `dismissed`, `read` or `done`. New is never written: it is the absence of a word.
- **Read together**: every device's log, the newest line on an id winning; at the same time, Done over Read over Dismissed. A word said here stands over what the source says unless the source goes further (a message dismissed here and read elsewhere is Read).
- **The calls' and the phone messages' Seen logs** (`calls/seen/`, `phone-messages/seen/`) are the same lines without a word: each is read as a Dismiss, by the call's or the message's own id. A word on a call or a phone message is written there too, with its `state`, which an older Sioul passes over and reads as Seen.

## Sections
Every section of the Porch is drawn alike, one simple and consistent layout (finding 10) (`PorchSection.qml`): a one-line header with its title, a "?" that says its help once (a tooltip on a computer; pressed, or on a phone, a popup with the settings that change it), and a chevron that folds it; its folding is kept on this device. A section with nothing to show is not there at all. Each notice is a row (`NoticeRow.qml`), who and what first to make looking cheaper (finding 4): who, what and when on one line on a computer, on two on a phone or when the list narrows beside a message open in the Reader; an amount right-aligned in tabular figures; its kind's main action (Open, Reply, Text back before Call back, See the scan, Open in Budgets); **Mark read** and **Dismiss**, quiet, beside it on a computer and in its menu on a phone (a long press); what else may be done, and something new tied to it, in its menu (⋮, a right click, the Menu key or Shift+F10). Times and days are said as Mail's rows say them. Nothing is counted, no summary of what came and no number beside a title, since counts and badges nag (findings 2 and 23).

In this order:
1. **Right now**: codes, whatever the time;
2. each **project**, then each shielded public address;
3. **From yourself**;
4. **From people you know**;
5. **From your phone**: the calls your phones declined, the texts, the other apps' notifications (below);
6. **Sites**: what your sites notified while you were away, a row per site ([sites.md](sites.md));
7. **Money**: the week's payments, as Budgets reads them from your bank's file, and what its watch found; none while you sleep;
8. **Paper letters**, while one waits; none while you sleep;
9. **Filed**, folded;
10. **Someone new**, the screener;
11. **Less important accounts**, folded;
12. **Caught by your own spam filter**, folded;
13. **Set aside**, folded; and **Hostile**, folded, when an address is shielded.

A folded section opens by itself when it holds mail of an account ranked above; hostile mail never does. The message open in the Reader stays in its section while you read it, though opening it read it.

## A public address, shielded
A contact address brings work, and also insults. An account with `shield = true` has its mail read before you see it (`shield.rs`):
- **its tone**: calm, rude (swearing, contempt), or hostile (insults aimed at you, harassment, threats), from word lists in French and English matched on whole words, case and accents aside; an insult near "you" weighs more, and shouting adds to it;
- **its topic**: work (a job, a mission, a quote), a question, the press, thanks, a donation, other.

Hostile mail goes to a folded "Hostile" lane that shows neither the sender's name nor the subject; opening one asks first (forward it to someone you trust, block, delete, or read anyway). The rest of the address's mail has its own lane, work first, with its topic and, when rude, a mark; the address can have its own fetching pace (`fetch_minutes`).

With `shield_ai = true` and a key for Anthropic's API (typed in the settings, kept in the system keyring), the subject and text of each new message are sent once to Claude Haiku, which says the tone, the topic and one neutral line on what it asks; the answer is kept under the Message-ID and a digest of who sent it and what it says (`shield::ai_key`: a message borrowing a calm one's Message-ID is read again), in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/shield/<account>.toml`, and takes precedence over the word lists. An answer not in the form asked is not kept; the message is marked there (`malformed`, by the same key, how many times) and sent once more at most, at the next round, then never (`AiCache::wants`, `TIMES`), so that a message the model will not answer does not go to Anthropic at every round, nor hold one of the twenty places of each. The Porch, `sioul shield` and the MCP server read it the same way (`shield::reading`). Off by default: the text leaves the device. `sioul shield [--account <id>] [--read]` shows what the shield makes of each message.

Before the sections, when there is something, a few cards: where you stopped, until "Done" (`StoppedCard.qml`, [tasks.md](tasks.md)); today's two events at once, margins counted, with "Open “…”" for each event and "Don't mention it again" ([client.md](client.md), Calendars); today's doses not marked yet, from their time on, reminded or not, with "Taken" ("Taken…", asking when, past their half hour) and the doubt under each when another device may know, whatever the hours (doses are not mail; asleep, as "Doses during sleep" says); the doses due while Sioul was closed, or while it ran but their reminder could not be shown (each under its own question), with "Taken…" and "Not taken" and the doubt under each when another device may know more ([health.md](health.md), "On the Porch").

**Account priority**: above, average (the default) or below, in the Accounts page or with `sioul account priority <id> <above|average|below>`. Mail of the accounts ranked above comes first in every lane, and a folded lane opens by itself when it holds some. Verified codes come at once whatever the account's rank.

## Right now: the exception
- **When it shows**: as soon as the message arrives. Each account's inbox stays open with IMAP IDLE (RFC 2177), renewed every five minutes, so a code reaches you within seconds of reaching your provider.
- **How it shows**: one desktop notification, no sound (none while you sleep: the card waits on the Porch), with the kind of secret, the sender and its trust, the code itself with a button to copy it and **Open the message** beside it, which opens the code's message in Mail's reader and brings the window back (`backend::code_actions`; the unified mailbox's part 0), where the desktop's notifications take buttons (Linux; on Windows and macOS the code is in the text), and how long it stays valid when the message says. In the window, its row heads the Porch, in Right now: who sent it, what it is with the code itself and how long it lasts, its trust in its shield, with **Copy**. On a phone, the card alone: no notification for a code there yet ([android.md](android.md)); the card on the home screen shows it too, with its site, until it expires (android.md, "The card on the home screen").
- **What it does not do**: open the rest of the Porch.
- **Afterwards**: the code is hidden once expired, and the message goes to its lane. Expired means the validity the message states ("valid 10 minutes", "for 2 days"), else what its kind usually lasts: a code 30 minutes, a sign-in link an hour, a password reset two hours, an address to confirm a day, a temporary password a week (it lasts until you have used it).
- **Detection**: in French and English, of codes (4–8 digits, two groups of three, `123-456`, or a prefixed code like `G-482913`; letters and digits right after their words, `K7Q-2M9`, `F7G2K`; two-factor, validation and activation codes too; "your Picshare code: …", "123456 is your Picshare code", "votre code Picshare : …"; "enter the code below", "le code suivant", in a message about signing in or checking who you are), passwords (temporary, initial, "your login details"), password resets ("choose a new password"), sign-in links ("magic link", "confirm your login"; or a message about signing in, in its subject or first lines, whose link or button expires within two days or works once: "Sign in to Readwell… This link expires in 15 minutes") and addresses or accounts to confirm ("confirm your email", "activez votre compte"). Years, prices, phone numbers, dates and postcodes are excluded, and so are a shop's code ("promo", "discount", "coupon"…) and the codes that are no secret ("your zip code", "votre code client"). Web addresses are left out of what is read (a tracking link's digits are no code, and its length pushed codes out of reach), with the invisible characters that pad a newsletter's first line; a text version that only says "view this email in your browser" is read with the HTML version after it (`crates/sioul-core/src/codes.rs`, `text::readable`, `card.rs`).
- **With a newsletter's headers** (`List-Unsubscribe`, `List-Id`, `Precedence: bulk` or `list`): read the same, but only the subject and the first 1,000 characters of the text, where a site puts what you asked for; and a password, a reset, a link or an address to confirm named only below the subject counts beside a line that answers a request ("if you did not ask for this, ignore this message", "change it after you sign in"), or beside a link said to expire within two days or to work once. A code needs no such line: it is the code beside its words. A newsletter's article about passwords, resets and sign-in links stays a newsletter (`codes::detect_message`; tests: `crates/sioul-core/tests/porch.rs`, fixtures in `tests/fixtures/porch-bulk/`).

## Notifications: new mail at the times it may come
The Porch keeps mail until you look; you are also told when mail your lists let through now arrives, so that you need not come and look. Batched notifications helped attention and mood where none at all made people more anxious (Fitz et al. 2019): what helps is predictability, not silence. Code: `crates/sioul-core/src/mailnote.rs` (what is told, the words, the ledger), `crates/sioul-app/src/mailnote.rs` (batches, the device that tells, the platform's notification).
- **When**: after a fetch, the inbox's new mail whose row lets it come now (the Mail rows of the one matrix, [attention.md](attention.md): `Attention::mail`, `decide`) makes one quiet notification for the batch, never one per message: how many, in words, and the first three senders with their subjects. "Two e-mails" / "Murena, Your invoice · Alice, Dinner on Friday". Arrivals ten seconds apart or less, every account together, are one batch (a minute at most): Sioul starting makes one notification, not one per address. **Open** (the desktop's button where it takes buttons, and a click on the notice itself; a tap on a phone) opens the message when the batch is one message (by its address, through the window's one opener: [client.md](client.md), "One opener for a message"), else the mailbox at its top on a computer ([client.md](client.md), "The mailbox"; decision 3 of the unified mailbox), the Porch on a phone until the phone's mailbox (part 5). The Porch itself does not change: it decides, by project and lane, in your admin hours; the mailbox records every message in time order. A code card's **Open** opens its message the same way.
- **What waited**: mail that came while it could not (outside its row's times, while you sleep, in a pause, Free time included, in a slot of time for you, as its row says, [attention.md](attention.md); while the Porch rests after a pause) waits, never lost. When a time begins in which some of it may come (work starts, you wake, a pause ends), one notification: "The Porch opens: three e-mails wait for you.", the first senders below. Once.
- **Never told**: mail set aside (forged, spam, a borrowed name), what Sioul's own spam filter flagged or moved (the review queue: on any device, the phone's background step and home card included), the blocked, hostile mail, codes (they keep their own notification, above), what you send yourself, the "Less important accounts" lane, newsletters and mailing lists in the Filed lane unless **Include newsletters** (an automatic sender there, a bill from no-reply, is told), mail read elsewhere before it came here (the server says it is read); and nothing while its row says shown, not told (◑: your safe senders' mail while you sleep, pause or take Free time, as usual) or not at all ([attention.md](attention.md)).
- **Quiet**: no sound (on Linux the server is asked for none; on a phone, the "New mail" channel at low importance: no sound, no banner); your platform's own notification settings apply, its do-not-disturb included (normal urgency, where codes and reminders pass it). A batch with someone Always through in it goes out at critical urgency on a computer, and on a phone on its own channel, "New mail from people always let through", with a sound: it passes do-not-disturb (Q10). On a phone's lock screen, the count alone, never who.
- **Once**: each message by its account and Message-ID (else its file's name), told or waiting, on this device (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/mail-notified.toml`, hashed: no address, no Message-ID; forgotten after 30 days). Mail fetched on an address's first fetch (its last two weeks) is never told.
- **Taken away** where it shows once each message it told is dismissed, read or done on any device, or read or gone here (above, "Notices").
- **One device tells**: your phone and your computer fetch the same mail, and the sharing does not carry what each told (`porch.toml` holds each account's "Done for now" mark, not each message). So only the device you used last tells: the "notices" lease, as for the sites' gathered notification ([database.md](database.md), "Leases"); the other marks the mail told without a word. Sharing off, each device tells its own.
- **The settings**: Settings ▸ What reaches you ▸ By person ▸ Mail: **Tell me of new mail** (on: `[reminders] mail`) and **Include newsletters** (off: `mail_newsletters`).
- **On a phone**: while Sioul runs, as above; while it is away, as Android lets it fetch ([android.md](android.md), "New mail").

## Working hours, and quiet time
- **Working hours**: the seven days of the week, each on or off, from a start to an end (Settings, at the bottom of the left column; `[[window]]` with `day`, `start`, `end` in the configuration). Older windows written as a start and a length still open the Porch, but only hours with an end bring quiet time.
- **Outside them**, the Porch says when it opens next, and nothing else of the mail: no counts, no names. Today's doses still show: they are not mail ([health.md](health.md), "On the Porch"). "Show all mail now" stays possible, quietly, and Reachable opens it ([do-not-disturb.md](do-not-disturb.md#the-three-states)).
- **Inside them**: the sections, in their order (above, "Sections").
- **With no hours configured**, the Porch is always open and nothing is ever quiet.
- **Quiet time**: whenever it is not work's time: leisure (the evening, days without hours, time off from a day to another with a word, and once the day is closed, "Done for today", [tasks.md](tasks.md), which asks how the day felt first, every answer optional, [reviews.md](reviews.md)), admin hours, a meal, sleep ([areas.md](areas.md)). Work rests until it comes back: the Porch shows the codes and links you ask sites for, what you send yourself, and the mail your lists let through now (below); work addresses fold on the Mail page without their dots; work sites keep their notifications for later, chats and dating sites stay unless marked as work; budgets show only what fits now; Projects shows only yours, Time waits behind "Show anyway"; Tasks shows only what fits now. One sentence says until when (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/quiet.toml` keeps the overrides).
- **Sleep**: from winding down to waking, and naps (Health). Nothing notifies but doses, the codes you asked for and your Always through people, as usual ([areas.md](areas.md), "Sleep"); what their rows let show still shows on the Porch if you open Sioul.
- **Closing is offered, never pressed**: once the day's last hours of work or admin are over, the status line offers **Close the work day**, with one quiet notice, once; in the evening, **Close the whole day**; nothing during sleep ([reviews.md](reviews.md)). On a phone the offer takes the place of the sentence below while it stands.
- **The way back is never suggested**: the sentence in the status line opens a menu: work half an hour to four hours more, back to the usual hours, or, the day it was closed, back to today's plan. Detachment from work in the evening is what recovery needs most, and the mere expectation of work mail undoes it (Sonnentag & Fritz 2015; Becker et al. 2021).

## Who may reach you, and when
Everyone is in one of five states (`sioul_core::reach::Who`), the same for mail, calls and messages from other apps:
- **Strangers**: in none of your address books (no address or number of theirs on a card Sioul reads, synced or on this device only) and on no list.
- **Blocked** (`blocked-senders.txt`): never, on any channel: mail set aside for good, never shown, never counted, never notified; calls refused.
- For the people in your address books, three levels:
  - **Safe** (`safe-senders.txt`): friends, chosen colleagues, chosen family. Only you put someone there.
  - **Neutral** (`neutral-senders.txt`): anyone in your address books, unless you chose otherwise. Naming someone here keeps them neutral inside a domain or a category on another list.
  - **Restricted** (`restricted-senders.txt`): those you would rather hear from only at chosen times (a demanding client, someone whose mail weighs).

**What changed for strangers** (October 2026): everyone no list named used to be neutral. Now only the people in your address books are, and those you let in from the screener; everyone else is a stranger, with a row of their own on each channel. Mail is unchanged: its strangers' row starts as its neutral row stood. Calls send strangers to voicemail at every time until their row is changed. Mail from a contact, or from an address with an entry of its own, no longer waits in the screener: the screener holds strangers, and senders a domain alone names.

**The lists**, four files beside the configuration, edited in Settings ▸ What reaches you ▸ By person; each line one of:
- an address, or a pattern with `*`: `*@example.org` for everyone there, `*@*.example.org` for its subdomains, `news*@shop.example`; `@example.org` too;
- a phone number, `tel:+33199001234`, however written (compared by its international form, `phones::key`; one written without its country is read as the Contacts settings' country), or a prefix, `tel:+33899*`; in the list editors, a number alone;
- a contact card, `contact:<its UID>`: all its addresses and numbers, now and later (the card's chooser writes it);
- a category of your contacts, `category:Friends`.

**From the person to the group** (`porch::Senders::judge`, `judge_number`): the address's or the number's own entry; then its card's (`contact:`; a card with no line of its own whose every address one list holds stands on that list: how "Their mail" chose for a person before cards were on the lists, so their numbers follow); then the card's categories; then patterns and prefixes, the more precise first; then neutral, when you let them in from the screener (`known-senders.txt`) or a card holds the address or the number; else a stranger. At one level, blocked comes before restricted before neutral before safe: in two categories, one blocked and one safe, a person is blocked. So a friend in a category marked safe can be demoted to neutral alone, and a colleague in Friends stays safe in a domain marked restricted. A message known by its sender's name only (another app's chat) is matched to a card with that very name, case and spaces aside (`judge_name`); between two cards of one name, the stricter; no card has it, the caller decides. An app where people choose their own name (Discord, Telegram) can only make its sender stricter by that name: a name there keeps Restricted or Blocked, and is otherwise a stranger (`appnotes::named`).

**When each list comes** is the one matrix of what reaches you ([attention.md](attention.md)), drawn above the lists in Settings ▸ What reaches you ▸ By person, the channel chosen at its top: **Mail**, **Calls**, **Messages** (texts and chats from other apps, on a phone). Rows: Always through, Safe, Neutral, Restricted, Strangers, for calls **Hidden numbers** (a call that shows no number: someone who hides theirs, a hospital's switchboard; research, [call-screening.md](research/call-screening.md) 7.2), for messages **Groups**; Blocked, a fixed row of never. Columns: the seven times ([areas.md](areas.md); the pause and Free time, [pauses.md](pauses.md)) and the two layers, time for you and do-not-disturb. Each cell a level, from at once to not at all; the usual values, and why, are attention.md's §3. Calls' are the research's (call-screening.md, 8.2), hidden numbers ringing in working and admin hours, when hospitals call that way; what rings whatever the matrix says (emergency numbers, a second call within minutes) is the calls' own ([android.md](android.md)).

The Porch shows a message when `Attention::mail` says so (at once, or shown, not told); the codes and links you just asked a site for, and what you send yourself, show whatever the time; the blocked never. A message waits, never lost, until a time its row lets it come. The matrix is `[attention]` in the configuration, shared with your other devices with the settings ([attention.md](attention.md), §7).

**Who wrote, and to which address** (mail): your safe senders' mail, and your Always through people's, comes to any of your addresses; the others' (neutral, restricted, strangers) only to an address for what now is for, unless the two never meet in your week: then their row alone decides, so that no mail waits for good ([areas.md](areas.md), "Mail").

**People on a list**: under the lists, everyone placed on one by their card (`contact:<UID>` lines), by name, with a choice: As their categories say, Safe, Neutral, Restricted, Blocked. A card no address book on this device holds is said as such, and stays on its list.

**Your contacts' categories**: under them, one row per category your contacts use (and each one a list names), with a choice: no list, Safe, Neutral, Restricted, Blocked. Everyone whose card is in that category, on a server or on this device only, takes it, each of their addresses and numbers. Nothing goes on a list by itself, family and friends included. Category names are compared without case or accents.

- **A person's sheet** (`PersonSheet.qml`, `reaches::person`), from a message (⋮, "How they reach you…") or a contact's card ("How … reaches you"): what decides now, in a sentence ("Safe, as the category Friends says", "A stranger: in none of your address books, on no list"); their list; Always through; and what each channel does with them at each time, from their rows. ## Blocked senders and borrowed names
- **Blocking** an address, a number, a card, or everyone at a domain, sets their mail aside for good: never shown, never counted, never read as a payment, never notified; their calls are refused. Nothing is deleted. The list is `~/.config/sioul/blocked-senders.txt` (above); the window blocks from a message or a contact and unblocks from Settings ▸ What reaches you ▸ By person or a person's sheet, the command line has `sioul block` and `sioul unblock` (an address, a pattern, or `tel:` and a number). Unblocking takes the entry out; when a card, a category or a domain would still block them, they are written neutral.
- **A name borrowed from a brand or a public service** ("lnfos PrimeVlDEO" sending from an unrelated domain), or **from one of your own domains** (a name made only of "yourdomain" and words like "support" or "mail admin", from an address outside that domain: the fake "your account will be deleted"), is set aside with the reason. Shared providers such as gmail.com are nobody's own domain, and your own name in full is not your domain. A signature proves the domain, not the name shown before it: names are compared by their skeleton, as Unicode's confusable detection does (UTS #39 §4), so lowercase l for capital I, 0 for O, rn for m and Cyrillic letters do not hide the brand. A brand's words count anywhere in a name ("Crédit Agricole Nord de France", "PayPal Kundendienst"), except for a brand whose name is also an everyday word or a place (`brands.everyday` of the packs: Amazon, Apple, Outlook, UPS; in France, Orange, La Poste, Impots, Free Mobile, Société Générale): its name counts only when it stands alone, or beside the words of a service (`brands.service_words` of the language packs: "support", "service", "security", "billing", "customer", "delivery", "client", "espace", "suivi"…, and small words such as "your", "votre", "of", "de"), a number, or a label of the brand's own domains ("Amazon.com"). So "Apple Support", "Infos La Poste" and "Service client Orange" borrow a name, while "Orange County Library", "Apple Pie Bakery", "Café de la Poste" and "Jean Orange" do not (`lookalike::borrows`; `names` for the others). Settings ▸ Words lists both, yours to change. Someone you know keeps the name they use: a sender let in, safe, or named for themselves, when their mail is authenticated (above, "Forged mail is judged before and apart"; `crates/sioul-core/src/lookalike.rs`).

## Spam: never for people you know
Spam is said of a stranger's mail only, whoever says it: your provider (its `X-Spam-*` headers, below in "Trust") or Sioul's own filter, trained on your mail ([spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md)). `porch::triage`, in order:
1. **Blocked, forged, or a stranger borrowing a name**: set aside, as above, whatever any filter says.
2. **Protected from every spam verdict**: mail from anyone but a stranger (safe, neutral, restricted: in your address books, on a list, let in from the screener); a code or a password you asked for; mail a project's routes take (a conversation a project pulls in leaves the review queue for the project); your own verified mail; a message you said is not spam, here, on another device or in another mail client (`spam::labels::said_ham`: its `$NotJunk`, or every device's label log). Mail nothing authenticates (SPF and DKIM failed, no DMARC or ARC vouching) has a stranger for its sender whatever its address, and no protection.
3. **Your provider's verdict**: a stranger's mail it flags is set aside; never on its word alone proven mail from someone you deal with (verified by Sioul's own check, its domain one you wrote to, keep in your books, your Sites or your contracts, kept mail from or said Not spam to: [spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md#proven-mail-your-provider-called-spam)), which takes its own lane, marked "Your provider marked it as spam." (`porch::provider_sets_aside`, the one condition).
4. **Hostile mail** to a shielded address: its own lane, whatever the filter says.
5. **Sioul's own filter**, once it has a table (Mail ▸ ⚙, "Your own spam filter"), says how likely a stranger's message is spam: **probably spam** from the spam threshold (95 % unless you change it), **maybe spam** from the unsure threshold (50 %) up to it, **probably not spam** below. What is done with each is yours, the matrix ([spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md), "Two thresholds, and what is done with each verdict"): **Move to spam** (into the account's Junk folder on the server as it arrives, by every device that fetches it, `crates/sioul-app/src/spam.rs`), **Flag only** (it stays where it is), **Do nothing** (its lane, as if unjudged). Until you choose: probable spam and doubts flagged, the rest nothing.

**The order of words**, written once here and in [spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md#proven-mail-your-provider-called-spam): **your own word > blocked > forged > vouched > your filter > your provider**. Vouched mail is proven mail from someone you deal with: no filter moves it into a Junk folder by itself (your own filter may flag it), and the matrix's row "Proven mail your provider called spam" brings it back from your provider's Junk folder as it arrives, with Undo, unless you chose "Mark only" or "Do nothing".

What the filter flags or moves goes to **Caught by your own spam filter** (`Lane::Review`, the review queue in the code): folded, no count beside it, never on the phone's home card, never notified, opened when you want. Nothing there asks to be confirmed: the training learns from what the filter caught as it is until you say otherwise, a maybe spam not until you say ([spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md), "The labels"), and **Not spam** is there for a mistake. Each message has its quiet word ("probably spam", "maybe spam"), never why nor how sure (by design: "your own filter: probably spam" is the whole reason), and **Spam** and **Not spam**; **Spam for all** and **Not spam for all** above them; ten seconds to undo each. "Done" never closes the queue: flagged mail stays two weeks after it, moved mail as long as it sits in the Junk folder unreviewed, here or after a move by another device (found by its Message-ID in the account's Junk folders, through every device's log of what the filter moved). Said on any device, or marked junk or not junk in another mail client, it leaves the queue everywhere. The lanes' "?" say the matrix as it is.

## Trust: Sioul checks every message
- **When a message is fetched**, Sioul checks it itself (Stalwart's `mail-auth`, through the system's DNS):
  - **SPF**, on the server that handed the message to your provider: the first hop from a public address at the top of the `Received` chain. Every hop below it could be written by anyone. Walking down from the top, your provider's own hops are passed (no `from`, a private or local address, the delivery into the mailbox, and Microsoft 365's hops between its own servers, by the names they write after `by`: `*.prod.outlook.com`, `*.prod.exchangelabs.com`, `*.outlook.office365.com`, `*.prod.protection.outlook.com`, whose addresses are public); the next line is where the message came in (`trust::boundary`). Its address is read in brackets (`[203.0.113.5]`, Postfix, Exim, Gmail, sendmail), or in parentheses alone right after the first word, as Microsoft 365 writes it (`from mail.sender.example (203.0.113.25) by BN8NAM12FT034.mail.protection.outlook.com`, its Internet frontend) and qmail (`from unknown (HELO mail.sender.example) (203.0.113.5)`; `(ident@203.0.113.5)`); a HELO name in brackets or after `helo=` is the sender's, never the address. A line written another way gives no entry at all, and no SPF check, rather than an address from the sender's own lines below it.
  - **DKIM**, each signature against the key its domain publishes. Checking at arrival matters: keys rotate, and some signatures expire within days.
  - **Your provider's subject tag** (part 2 of 4 November 2026; `sioul_sync::verify`): a provider's filter that writes its mark at the start of the Subject (`***SPAM***`, `[SPAM]`, `{Spam?}`, `(Indésirable)`, `SPAM:`) breaks every signature that covers the Subject. When a signature fails on a message a filter of your provider's marked (`X-Spam-Flag: YES`, `X-Spam-Status: Yes`, `X-Spam: Yes`, rspamd's action; `trust::filter_marked`), Sioul takes the leading tags off one at a time and checks again: a tag is found by its shape, never by a list of words (text between stars, brackets, braces or parentheses, or one word before a colon, with its spaces, three at most; `trust::subject_tag_ends`), the rest of the message kept byte for byte, the Subject's own folding and spaces too, so both relaxed and simple canonicalisation see what the sender signed. Only a candidate whose signature verifies is taken; the stamp says so, `dkim=pass (after your provider's subject tag)`, and **DMARC is judged again** from that result and written in the stamp: SPF failing on a forwarded message, the tag, and a good signature once it is off make DMARC pass, so the sender is verified, not forged under `p=reject`. Otherwise the checks fail as they should. An encoded Subject (`=?UTF-8?…?=`) whose tag the provider wrote before it is restored the same way, the tag being plain text before the encoded words; a tag written inside the encoded words is not found, and the signature fails as before. With SpamAssassin's `report_safe` 1 or 2 (the original attached to a report your provider wrote), the attached original is checked whole instead, and the stamp says so ("checked on the original your provider attached"). **What you see is what was proven**: the window and the Porch show the restored Subject, never the tag, with one quiet mark, "Your provider marked it as spam." (`trust::restored_tags`, read from this installation's own stamp only). Mail stored before is checked again once on each device, at the first start after this change, in the background (`verify::recheck_tagged_once`, `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/verify/subject-tags.toml`, a folder marked done once the DNS answered): only messages whose stamp failed a signature, a filter marked, the Subject tagged; their stamp written again in place, the file's name and time kept, only when a signature verifies; their index lines read again; those in a Junk folder brought back as on arrival ([spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md#proven-mail-your-provider-called-spam)).
  - **DMARC**, with the domain's own policy. A failure is a forgery when the domain asks receivers to act on it (quarantine, reject). With `p=none`, on a mailing list someone answers for (it rewrites what it relays; since 3 November 2026 only when someone answers for it: an ARC seal that passed, of your provider's or your own domain's, placed before your provider received the message (`trust::arc_voucher`, below), or a signature of the list's own domain that passed: List-Id's, List-Post's or Sender's, `trust::list_excused`, since anyone can write list headers; the reason line names who answered, "lists.example.org signed it", "provider.example sealed its way here", since anyone with a domain can sign a "list" naming itself), or for mail that never came from outside (your provider's own notices, a message to yourself), the sender is only "not verified". A code is never excused by list headers: no list relays your codes, so a code failing DMARC under such a policy is forged, and set aside, whatever its `List-Id` says.
  - **ARC**, for forwarded mail, and the **reverse DNS** of the sending server.
- **The results go on top of the stored message**, as an `Authentication-Results` header (RFC 8601) under a name unique to this installation and under `.invalid`, so no sender can write results that pass for Sioul's. The Porch trusts them first, then the provider's. Mail fetched before is checked once, in the background (`sioul verify`; `--again` checks everything again).
- **Verified**: DMARC pass for the domain the From line shows, else a valid DKIM signature of that same domain (a signature of another domain says nothing of the sender). "The same" is DMARC's relaxed alignment (`trust::aligned`): one organisational domain, the name registered under its public suffix, read from the Public Suffix List that the `psl` crate carries, so `mail.example.org` and `shop.example.org` are one sender, while `victim.github.io` and `attacker.github.io`, or `co.uk` and a name under it, are not. Sioul's own DMARC check at arrival (`mail-auth`) finds the organisational domain by DMARC's DNS tree walk instead, as the newer DMARC standard does. A DMARC failure always wins over any pass, and a results line that cannot be read to its end (an unclosed quote or comment) proves nothing. **Forged**: DMARC fail under a policy that says so. **Not verified**: everything else, with the reason.
- **Not authenticated**: SPF and DKIM both failed, and neither DMARC nor ARC vouches for it. An ARC chain counts when the seal placed before your provider received the message is your provider's (mail forwarded inside it) or one of your own domains' (`trust::arc_voucher`): anyone can seal a chain of their own, and Gmail and Microsoft seal every message as it arrives, a chain that passes on its own (Sioul's own check, mail-auth, says arc=pass for a lone seal: `verify::tests::a_lone_arrival_seal_passes_and_vouches_for_nothing`). So the newest seal, when it is your provider's, answers for nothing: the one below it does, and with none below, none does (review of 01ca9df, 3 November 2026; `trust::tests::arc_answers_by_the_seal_placed_before_your_provider`). Nothing proves it comes from the address it shows, so its sender is a stranger for every rule, whoever the address is: no People lane (the screener, where letting them in would change nothing), a stranger's times, no protection from spam, no project route that names a sender (one by the subject, the text or an attachment still takes it), and no list changed from it; "Its sender could not be verified (SPF and DKIM failed): treated as someone you do not know" says why. A blocked address stays blocked: keeping someone out asks for no proof. A code keeps its lane and its warning. "none" (nothing published to check) and a softfail are not failures (`trust::authenticated`).
- **Hovering the shield** says each check, its result and the domain it was for, and who checked: Sioul at arrival, or the provider.
- **Spam verdicts**: SpamAssassin's `X-Spam-Flag` and `X-Spam-Status`; rspamd's `X-Spamd-Result`, `X-Spam` and `X-Rspamd-Score`; only those your provider wrote, above the `Received` line where the message came in from the Internet (`trust::read_spam_verdict`). Reading down from the top, the provider's own lines are passed: the delivery into the mailbox (LMTP), filters and servers on private or local addresses, lines without `from`. The first line from a public address, or one written another way, ends what is the provider's. Below it a sender writes what it likes, "X-Spam-Flag: NO" too; a filter that writes its verdict at the bottom of the headers, or just below that line, cannot be told from the sender, and is not read. Mail that never came from outside is read above its lowest `Received`; mail with none (copied in) has no verdict.
- **What you say of spam is kept**, for the spam filter to learn from and for every device to know: "Junk", "Not junk", "Spam", "Not spam", and blocking a sender from one of their messages, each add a line to this device's own label log, `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/spam/labels/<device>.jsonl` (`spam::labels`), which travels sealed to your other devices, each reading every device's: when, the account, the folder, the message's UIDVALIDITY, UID and Message-ID, spam or ham, and which act; never a word of the message. The keywords `$Junk` and `$NotJunk`, set by Sioul or another mail client, are kept at each sync in the message's file name, as Dovecot keeps keywords (a letter each, named in the folder's `dovecot-keywords`).

## Paper letters
The envelope stays outside: a scan, a phone photo or a PDF dropped in a folder (by you, a scanner, or someone who opens the post for you) is read on this device, understood by rules, and waits for the window as a notice, like mail. Opening post is part of admin anxiety (Money and Mental Health 2018), and a date written "within two months" is remembered by no one: Sioul says it as a date. Code: `crates/sioul-core/src/letters.rs` (reading, keeping, filing), `crates/sioul-sync/src/ocr.rs` (the text), `crates/sioul-app/src/letters.rs`, the Porch's rows `crates/sioul-app/src/notices.rs` and `qml/PorchPage.qml`.
- **Where scans arrive**: the letters' own settings, with the letters (unset: `letters/inbox` in the notes folder). PDF, PNG, JPEG, TIFF, WebP. A file still being written (less than twenty seconds old) is read the next minute. Each is read once (by its name, size and time).
- **On the Porch, only while a letter waits**: each letter a row of the section Paper letters, who sent it, what it is, when it came, and what it says under it (asks €86.40; by Sunday 15 November); **See the scan**, its main action, opens it as a letter opens until the Letters page does; its menu holds "A task for that date", "Into the agenda" for an appointment, its task once made, and "Done, filed"; **Dismiss** takes the row away on every device and leaves the letter waiting. No place line, no count, no badge. The New menu holds **A paper letter (scan)…** (not on a phone): a file chooser for PDF and pictures, several at once, each copied into the folder under a free name, "scan (2).pdf", the chosen file left where it was, read at a coming minute (`letters::add`, the result on the status line). Whether this computer reads scans is asked off the window's thread every ten minutes (`ocr::reads_here`: Tesseract and `pdftotext` start).
- **A phone reads none**: `letters::tick` returns at once on Android, and the phone's Porch shows the letters a computer read (with the notes part of the sharing). Before, a phone holding unread scans in its copy of the folder said "no program here reads them" with a computer's install hint.
- **The text**: a PDF's own text when it has one (Poppler's `pdftotext`), else its pages at 300 dpi (`pdftoppm`) read by Tesseract, in French and English when those are installed; photos and images by Tesseract directly. Both are your system's programs, optional as the antivirus is: without them the scans wait, unread, and the Porch says the command that installs them (Fedora: `sudo dnf install tesseract tesseract-langpack-fra poppler-utils`). Nothing leaves this computer.
- **What the rules find**, French first, English too:
  - *who*: a body that writes to everyone found in the letterhead (CAF, Assurance Maladie, Finances publiques, Urssaf, France Travail, MDPH, préfecture, tribunal, commissaire de justice, hospital, mairie, banks, EDF…), else the letterhead's first line;
  - *what*, the gravest first: a formal notice ("mise en demeure"), a tax notice, a decision with its ways of appeal, a reminder, an appointment ("convocation"), a bill ("avis des sommes à payer", "titre exécutoire", "facture"), an acknowledgment, an attestation, a contract;
  - *how much*: the amount on the line that asks for it ("Montant à payer", "somme de");
  - *by when*: a date after "avant le", "au plus tard le", "date limite", "échéance", "jusqu'au", "no later than", "by"; else a delay ("dans un délai de deux mois à compter de la notification", "sous huitaine", "sous quinzaine", "within 30 days"), counted from the day the scan came (the notification) or from the letter's own date when it says so; the words that set it shown as the letter writes them;
  - its own date ("Lyon, le 28 septembre 2026"), an appointment's day and hour ("le jeudi 12 novembre 2026 à 14 h 15"), "recommandée avec accusé de réception", your number with them ("Numéro allocataire", "Référence"), and the project whose name it carries.
- **What it says**, in the window only: who and what, then a sentence each (asks €86.40; by Sunday 15 November ("avant le 15 novembre 2026"); sent registered; dated; your number). "See the scan"; "A task for that date" (in your usual list, due that day, tied to the scan and the project: "Pay the water bill €86.40", "The tax office's decision: contest it or not"); "Into the agenda" for an appointment, an hour long; a project; "Done, filed": the scan moves to `letters/<year>/<day> <sender> <kind>.pdf`, its text kept beside the list (`letters/<id>.txt`). Making the task files the scan first, so the task's tie holds; a letter whose task is in a list here already gets none made again, whatever a row said before the plan was made again (review of 21436bf, M1).
- **Not built**: the AI reading a letter line by line, the "is this genuine?" checks (the office's domain, its IBAN), a mail intake address for a helper.
