# Your data on several devices

Built: sharing through a folder your sync carries (Nextcloud, Dropbox, Syncthing), or that Sioul keeps in step with a Nextcloud itself (below), set up in Settings ▸ Between your devices, part by part on each device. Why plain files and a log, rather than a database server, git or whole-folder archives: below. Code: `crates/sioul-sync/src/share.rs` (the log, the parts), `blobs.rs` (notes and papers sealed apart), `history.rs` (earlier versions), `remote.rs` (the folder read on its server too, and this device's files sent there), `trigger.rs` (an exchange as soon as a shared file is saved), `crates/sioul-app/src/share.rs` and `qml/SharePanel.qml` (the window).

## What travels how
- **By their servers**: mail (IMAP), contacts, events and tasks (CardDAV, CalDAV, Google), with what Sioul writes in each task (kinds, projects, links, steps, waits: RFC 9253).
- **By their own folder, or by the log**: the notes folder: projects and their routes (`sioul-projects.toml`, or `sioul-cases.toml` under its first name), budgets and bank accounts (`sioul-budgets.toml`), the bank's movements (`sioul-bank.toml`), contracts (`sioul-contracts.toml`), papers (`sioul-papers.toml`, `papers/`), notes, their pictures, PDFs and memos. A synced folder carries them itself; a folder no sync carries (a phone's: eDrive syncs only its own folders) sends them through the log, sealed, when its parts are switched on (below). Never both on one device: a file carried twice, by the sync and by the log, would undo changes. The Settings page says when the notes folder seems outside every synced folder (Nextcloud's own list of folders, `~/Nextcloud`, `~/Dropbox`, `~/Sync`…) and notes are not shared through the log.
- **By the log**: what only this computer keeps, and the notes folder when asked, in parts:

Passwords and tokens stay in each device's keyring: an account arriving from another device asks for its password once.

## Parts
Each device chooses what it shares, part by part (Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing ▸ What travels from this device): `[parts]` in its `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/share/here.toml`, never shared. A device that never chose shares what was shared before parts had switches: everything but notes and papers (the spam filter's table, which came later, included; never the phone's messages nor the texts, which came later still: each device shares them once you turn them on there), and projects and money as `share_projects` said (a setting every device followed then; the first time sharing is on with this version, what it said becomes this device's own choice, and the old key is still read until then). A part switched off takes nothing out elsewhere and sends nothing; the others' changes for it are not kept waiting (switched on again, it joins as a new device joins, below: "Stores that leave, arrive or move", and reads them then). Notes and papers are refused on a device whose notes folder a sync app already carries, with the sentence why: the two carriers would undo each other's changes. Known carriers: on a computer, a folder inside one named like a sync's (`~/Nextcloud`, `~/Dropbox`, `~/Sync`…), Nextcloud's own list of folders, Syncthing's (its `config.xml`) and Dropbox's wherever it is (its `info.json`); on a phone, a notes folder in the same top folder of its storage as the sharing folder (Documents for eDrive), whatever name the storage goes by (`/sdcard`, `/storage/emulated/0`). Other sync apps (Google Drive, Insync, rclone, MEGA) are not recognised: leave Notes off where they carry the notes folder. Before Notes or Papers is switched on, the panel says how many files and how many bytes would travel. On Android, notes and papers wait while Sioul lacks "All files access" (without it a folder lists only what Sioul made, and the rest would seem gone): said. Each part shows what it carries and when it last sent and received a change.

## Notes and papers in the vault
- **One file at a time**: each file of the notes folder (or of `papers/`) is a record, `files/notes/<path>` → its content's hash (SHA-256), its size, its time, and the content it replaced where it changed (`b`, its base). The content itself goes apart, once: compressed (gzip), sealed in pieces of 1 MiB (XChaCha20-Poly1305, each piece bound to its file, its place and whether it is the last, so a piece swapped, cut or moved does not open; nothing may follow the last), under `<folder>/blobs/<name>`, the name a keyed hash of the content (HMAC-SHA-256), which tells the server nothing, not even that two files are the same. The keys for sealing and for naming are made from the sharing key (HKDF-SHA-256, one for each use, for this format: `sioul-blob 1`); the records' key itself seals no file. Written once, under a hidden name of its own then renamed. A change sends one file; a file in two places, or back as it was, is sealed once. A name may hold `#` ("Facture #123.pdf").
- **Only what changed**: a file whose size and time did not change since it was last read, read more than a moment after it changed, is not read again; one whose time changed is read through for its hash, and sent only when its content changed. A file changed less than two seconds ago (maybe still being written) is read at the next exchange. Files are read and written a piece at a time, never held whole in memory; a sealed file opened inflates 64 KiB at a time and stops past 64 MB, whatever it says. Over 64 MB a file stays here, said (`share-too-big`). A name that is not text stays here, said (`share-not-text`). Left out: what sync apps, editors and systems make (hidden files, `~` and `.tmp` files, conflicted copies of Nextcloud, Dropbox, Syncthing, ownCloud, Seafile, Office's `~$` locks), links, tools' folders (`node_modules`, `target`), a folder shared through (it holds a seal), and what the other parts carry (the manifests, `papers/` in the notes, whatever their case). What Sioul left half written in a crash (its hidden `.sioul.tmp` files) is cleaned an hour later.
- **Coming in**: nothing is written without room for it, for the copy kept of the file it replaces and some left (a quarter of a GB, or a hundredth of the disk: `disk::kept_free`): else it waits, said (`share-no-room`). The content is opened beside its file (a hidden name), checked against its hash; just before it takes the file's place, the file is looked at again: changed since the exchange looked (an editor saving), nothing is written over it, and the next exchange sends that change first, both kept. Then the file as it was is kept (Earlier versions), the content takes its place, dated as where it was made (never after this computer's clock), and what the next look compares with is what was written: a change made after it is a change made here, sent. A file taken out takes its folders with it when they are left empty. Never written: through a link in the notes folder (a link to elsewhere would carry the write out of it), over a link, into the sharing folder (it would travel in plain) or Sioul's own state (`share-refused`); nor a name this device's storage would take for another file's, by case or by how an accent is written (`share-name-clash`: a phone's, a memory card's, Windows', a Mac's); nor, on Windows, a name Windows keeps for itself (`aux.md`). Not there yet (a slow sync app, an empty placeholder of a file kept on demand): it waits, said after a day (`share-missing`). Damaged (it does not open whole, or opens to another content): said (`share-damaged`), the file here stays as it is, that copy is not opened again until another copy comes (another size, time, or place on the disk), and a later version comes as ever. A failure here (a full disk, a folder that cannot be made) is tried again after a minute, then ten, then an hour, then six: not at every exchange.
- **Changed on two devices**: a file here that is neither what the incoming change replaced nor a content the others' records named for it (their versions and bases, kept while a change waits) changed here too. It is copied beside first, "lease (conflict 2026-10-05 16.05).md", the same words in every language, dated by when its version was made, so that every device holding that version names it alike and one copy travels (the notes editor names the version it saves over a changed note the same way); then the later version takes the name. Said for a day (`share-conflict`). A file taken out on one device and changed on another: what changed is kept the same way (`share-conflict-gone`). A device joining with files of its own that differ is the same case: its versions are kept beside.
- **An older copy put back by hand**: a file whose time went back past the version last seen here (a backup restored with its old dates) is not a change: that version comes back from the folder, the older copy kept beside, said (`share-older-copy`). To send an older version on purpose: Put back (Earlier versions), or give the file today's date.
- **Taken out**: a file deleted here is taken out elsewhere once it has been gone ten minutes (a file being replaced, a disk away a moment), a record taken out with its base; meanwhile another device's change to it still comes. Many at once (more than ten, or three and more past a fifth of the folder), or a folder that lists empty while it held files at the last look, or one that cannot be read whole (one name that errs is enough), or the notes folder gone: nothing of them is taken out elsewhere and nothing is written into them; said (`share-vanished`), until you say "Take them out everywhere" (`share::confirm_gone`); or switch the part off and on to fill the folder again from the others.
- **Old contents go**: a sealed file no record points to any more is removed by the device that wrote it, 90 days after the last record that did (the spam filter's tables sooner: below); never one a record read in the last 90 days names, nor one whose file was made fresh lately (a device using a content sealed long ago makes it fresh). Every ten minutes, each device looks for the sealed files of its own records and seals again any gone or not of the size it sealed, while its file still holds that content. With eDrive, which never deletes a file deleted elsewhere, the phone keeps every sealed file its sync app brought.
- **Its own memory**: what an exchange remembers of notes and papers (their entries, their files as last read, the changes waiting), and of the logs of lines (calls, the phone's messages, texts), is kept in `share/files.json`, apart from `share/memory.json`, which what the doses and claims ask reads alone. That file lost or unreadable, notes and papers join again as a new device's would: nothing goes out as new. It is written only when what it holds changes: an idle exchange writes nothing of it (`share::tests::an_idle_exchange_writes_nothing_with_ten_thousand_texts`, half an hour idle with ten thousand texts). A log's lines are kept once, as its entries, not again in its file's state: a log is read at every exchange instead (ten thousand texts, 5.5 MB, where 9.7 MB were). The times that change on their own (when the sealed files were last looked for, the history last tidied) are kept in `memory.json`; a sealed file still used is said so once a day, not at every exchange. Until 8 October 2026 that file was rewritten whole at every exchange (on the owner's computer, 25 MB every 12 to 50 seconds, with 9,833 texts). The window gives back to the system what an exchange held a moment (glibc's `malloc_trim`).

## The spam filter's table and label logs
The table a training makes on a computer ([spam-filter.md](spam-filter.md)), about two megabytes, replaced at each training that does no worse, read by every device, the phone's background step included. One store, `files/spam/table.bin` (`share::SPAM_TABLE`), in its own part, **Spam filter**, on by default.
- **Sealed apart, as notes are** (`Shape::Files`): the record says its content's hash, size and time; the content goes once, as a sealed file in `blobs/`. Whole in a record (`Shape::Whole`), its two megabytes, some 3.6 MB sealed in a line, would go again in every round its computer starts, every device would read them again in each, eDrive would keep each round for good, and with Sioul keeping the folder itself the round being written goes up whole at each change: every minute, while a computer writes.
- **In every exchange**, unlike notes and papers: a single file sealed apart is Sioul's own, quick to read, in its private folders (a phone needs no "All files access" for it), so a dose's alarm, a button and the phone's background step carry it too. A device without a table yet has none, not an unknown one: the first one is written.
- **Two tables in the folder**: the computer that sealed them takes its own older tables out of the folder at once, past the newest two (`share::KEPT_TABLES`, `prune_tables`), not 90 days on: a training a week left a dozen there, two megabytes each. Never another device's file. Every device reads the newest record of a file only, an older Sioul too, so a table taken out is never asked for; an earlier version of the table kept by reference on another device can no longer be put back from the folder. Which tables are its own is in this computer's `share/files.json`, never shared: those sealed before 9 October 2026 go by the 90-day rule. A phone synced by eDrive still keeps each table eDrive brought it, since eDrive never deletes a file deleted elsewhere (`share::tests::only_the_two_newest_spam_tables_stay_in_the_folder`).
- **The one it replaces is kept beside**, as `table.prev.bin`, when this Sioul can use it (`spam::table::keep_previous`); a table this Sioul refuses (another version's tokenizer or header features, damaged) is written all the same, and the one before stays in use until a table it reads comes (`Table::cached`). The Earlier versions below keep it too, by reference.
- **Never** the language model, the corpus nor the outside material beside it: no store names them (`share::tests::the_spam_table_travels_alone_in_every_exchange`).
- One computer trains, by hand: two computers training is not supported. Should both train, the later table wins everywhere, as for any file; a table trained on each before they exchange keeps both, the other as "table (conflict …).bin" beside it, which nothing reads: a safety, nothing more.
- **The label logs travel with it**, in the same part, as folders of lines (`share::SPAM_LABELS`, `share::SPAM_MOVED`, `Shape::Lines`): each device writes its own file (`<device>.jsonl`, its name in the sharing), a line per act, never rewritten; every device reads them all, in every exchange, quick ones included. So a message said not spam on the phone is never flagged again on the computer, and the training there learns from it (`share::tests::a_label_on_one_device_reaches_the_others`).

## The calls' logs
The calls your phones screened ([android.md](android.md#calls), [porch.md](porch.md), "Calls declined"), in their own part, **Calls**, on by default: a computer that others see can take your lists without taking who called.

- **Two folders of lines** (`share::CALLS_LOG`, `share::CALLS_SEEN`, `Shape::Lines`): each phone's own log, `state/calls/log/<device>.jsonl`, a line per call, declined or let ring (never a blocked caller's); each device's own Seen, `state/calls/seen/<device>.jsonl`, a line per press. Each file has one writer; every device reads them all, in every exchange, quick ones included. A line at a time, not a month at a time: a month sent whole would go again at each call, or leave a sealed copy at each change in the folder, which eDrive never deletes.
- **A month**: each device takes its own lines out after 31 days (`calls::trim_own`, once a day); the sharing takes them out everywhere. Another device's lines 45 days old are taken out by any device (`calls::trim_others`): a phone gone for good never takes its own out; while it shares, it took them out long before.
- **Remembered beside**: a store of lines names each entry by the line itself, so the calls' entries are kept in `files.json` with the notes', not in `memory.json`, which every exchange writes and the doses' knowing reads (`share::calls_store`); `files.json` lost, they join again rather than going out again as new.
- **No earlier versions, no first copy**: never kept in `history/` nor copied into `before-sharing-<day>/`: a log has nothing to put back, and a call leaves every device after its month.
- **An older Sioul** knows neither folder (`known_part`): their records wait in its memory, written nowhere, taken out nowhere; updated, the stores join and the calls come in (`share::tests::an_older_sioul_keeps_the_calls_waiting`).
- **Tested** (`cargo test --release -p sioul-sync share`): a phone's call reaching the desk and the desk's Seen reaching the phone, sealed in the folder, remembered beside `memory.json`, no versions, `files.json` lost (`a_phone_s_calls_reach_the_desk_and_seen_comes_back`); several phones (`several_phones_each_keep_their_own_log`); an older Sioul; the part switched off (`the_calls_part_switched_off_receives_nothing_and_keeps_nothing_waiting`); a line taken out by its phone a month on, gone from the desk's copy (`a_trimmed_line_leaves_every_device`).

## The phone's messages
The messages your phone's notifications brought, for the apps you chose on the phone ([android.md](android.md#messages-on-your-computers), [porch.md](porch.md#from-your-phone)), in their own part, **Messages from your phone** (`phone-messages`), **off until you turn it on**, on each device: on the phone to send them, on a computer to show them there. A notification's words leave the phone this way only, sealed, and only for the apps you chose.

- **Two folders of lines**, as the calls' (`share::PHONE_MESSAGES_LOG`, `share::PHONE_MESSAGES_SEEN`, `Shape::Lines`): each phone's own log, `state/phone-messages/log/<device>.jsonl`, a line per message (`sioul_core::phonemsgs::Line`: when, the app, the kind, the conversation's key and a group's title, who as the app named them and their number's key, the row the phone judged them by, the words cut at 1,000 characters or none, "a picture", "a code", when the phone let it through); each device's own Seen, `state/phone-messages/seen/<device>.jsonl`, a line per press. One writer per file; every device that has the part on reads them all, in every exchange.
- **Never in a line**: a code or an approval (the line says a code came), a copy Android redacted, a picture (said), a blocked sender's messages, a conversation held for good, a notification its app marks secret, ongoing ones, Sioul's own, any button or tap ([android.md](android.md#messages-on-your-computers)).
- **Padded**: each line is padded with spaces inside its JSON to a multiple of 256 bytes before it is sealed (`phonemsgs::padded`), so that the server learns no message's length; it still sees which phone wrote, when, and roughly how much.
- **A week**: each phone takes its own lines out after 7 days (`phonemsgs::trim_own`, once a day), and the sharing takes them out everywhere; another device's lines 10 days old are taken out by any device (`phonemsgs::trim_others`): a phone gone for good never takes its own out.
- **Remembered beside**, **no earlier versions, no first copy**, as the calls' logs (`share::calls_store` covers both folders).
- **On a computer, in plain**: the lines a computer received sit in its state folder unsealed, as the calls' do, readable by whoever reads your files there; the key that opens the folder's copies is on every device. That is the price of seeing them there, and the reason they are kept a week.
- **An older Sioul** knows neither folder: their records wait, written nowhere, taken out nowhere.
- **Tested** (`cargo test --release -p sioul-sync messages`): a phone's message reaching the desk sealed and padded, the desk's Seen reaching the phone, remembered beside `memory.json`, no versions, `files.json` lost (`a_phone_s_messages_reach_the_desk_sealed_and_seen_comes_back`); off by default, off on the phone sending nothing, off on the desk receiving nothing and keeping nothing waiting, then on (`the_phone_messages_part_is_off_until_turned_on_and_off_receives_nothing`); a line taken out by its phone a week on, gone from the desk's copy (`a_trimmed_line_of_phone_messages_leaves_every_device`); an older Sioul (`an_older_sioul_keeps_the_phone_s_messages_waiting`). What makes a line, the padding, the trims and the Porch's lines: `cargo test --release -p sioul-core phonemsgs`.

## Texts
Your phone's texts, read there for your computers and kept there as an archive, and the texts written on a computer for the phone to send ([texts.md](texts.md)), in their own part, **Texts** (`texts`), off until you turn it on, on each device.

- **Three folders of lines** in the data folder, one writer per file, every line sealed at rest by its writer (`textseal`, a key made from the sharing key) and padded inside to a multiple of 256 bytes, then sealed again in the folder as every record is (`share::TEXTS_LOG`, `TEXTS_SEND`, `TEXTS_OUTCOME`, `Shape::Lines`): each phone's texts, the whole history, every column of the provider's rows, and a line per text deleted on the phone; each computer's texts to send, a random key each; each phone's outcomes.
- **The media** of multimedia messages (pictures, sounds, videos, contact cards, other files) go through `blobs/` as sealed, content-addressed blobs, never inside a line; each computer keeps them sealed in `data/texts/media/<hash>`. A part over the size chosen on the phone (10 MB by default) stays there.
- **Paced**: the phone's import runs after the exchange, never inside it, 500 texts and about 24 MB of media a step; the doses, do-not-disturb and calls go first (`share::tests::a_first_import_goes_in_batches_and_never_holds_back_the_doses`, and `doses_are_never_known_wrongly_whatever_carries_them` with a texts import beside it).
- **Kept**: the texts always, an archive that only grows (`share::tests::the_texts_archive_only_grows_and_a_deletion_is_marked_everywhere`); a device takes its own requests and outcomes out after 31 days, and another's 14 days later (`texts::trim_own`, `trim_others`).
- **Remembered beside**, **no earlier versions, no first copy**, as the calls' logs (`share::calls_store` covers `data/texts/`). `files.json` lost, the requests are read again, and the phone's ledger never sends one twice (`share::tests::a_text_written_on_the_desk_is_sent_once_by_the_phone_and_its_outcome_comes_back`).
- **Passed over**: the records of the first build of the texts, never released, which kept them in `state/texts/`, are known and written nowhere, never kept waiting (`share::RETIRED`); that build's reader starts again from the first text.
- **Never shared**: the phone's ledger, its mark, the reader's marks and measures and Java's results stay on the phone (the state folder's `texts/`); each computer's choice of the phone that sends and the phone's largest media size (`texts.toml`), and the clock's measure (`texts/clock.json`), stay on their device.
- **Tested** (`cargo test --release -p sioul-sync text`): a text written on the desk sent once by the phone, its outcome back, sealed in the folder and at rest, `files.json` lost; the part off until turned on; the archive growing, a deletion marked on every device.

## Earlier versions
Before another device's change is written into a file here (any shared file: settings, lists, notes, papers), or takes it out, the file as it was is kept in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/share/history/<part>/<file>/<time>`, on this device only, never shared: by reference when its content is sealed in the sharing folder already (a note or a paper a record named: a few bytes, `<time>.sealed`, kept 60 days, since the folder keeps that content 90 days after its last use), else copied, when the disk has room for it (refused otherwise, and the change waits). Of each file, the last 20 versions are kept, and all those of the last 30 days; of a file no longer here, those of the last 30 days; never more than a GB in all (or a twentieth of the disk if less), the oldest going first. Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing ▸ Show earlier versions lists them, part, then file (found by part of its name; a hundred shown, the others said), then versions. "Put back" says first what it would change, then puts it back, off the window's thread: a note, a paper, a draft whole (the file as it is then kept too); a file of entries (settings, lists, the doses' record, sessions) entry by entry: each entry the version held goes back to what it was, those taken out since come back, and those added since stay. Putting back never takes out, on any device, what was added after that version. What changed is dated now, and the next exchange sends it as a change made here. Notes and papers are kept this way rather than in `before-sharing-<day>/` (they may be large).

## The log
- **Each computer has a name**: a UUID made once (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/share/here.toml`), made again when the configuration was copied to another machine (a hash of `/etc/machine-id` tells).
- **Changes are found, not announced**: Sioul writes its files as before. Each minute (and on "Refresh everything"), the shared files are compared with what they held at the last look, entry by entry; a file whose size and time did not change is not read again. Each change becomes a record: the entry, its new value or "taken out", a clock.
- **The clock** is hybrid logical: the time in milliseconds, made strictly increasing on each computer and never behind a record already read. A change is dated by its file's time, so a change made here before another computer's, but found after, still loses to it.
- **Merging**: for each entry, the record with the later clock wins (ties broken by the computers' names). A setting changed on each computer keeps the later; two sessions noted apart both stay; a sender let in on one computer and blocked later on another ends blocked, and let in nowhere. A money line, split or preset, and a time session, has an id (given when it is made, or written into it when the device takes format 2): changed on two devices it stays one, the later change winning whole, and two alike stay two (format 2, below).
- **Joining**: on the first exchange, a copy of every shared file goes to `share/before-sharing-<day>/` (notes and papers: their earlier versions instead); the others' records come first; then what this computer alone had goes out. Two computers set up apart end with the first one's settings and the sum of both lists.
- **Prudence**: a settings file missing or unreadable (half written, broken by hand) is never read as emptied: nothing of it is taken out elsewhere, and what comes for it waits, kept, until it can be written. A settings file that held entries and is found gone or of no bytes at all (a crash, a full disk, a file written in place) waits ten minutes before its entries count as taken out: never everything, everywhere, at once (`share-emptied`); a list emptied on purpose, or a file gone from a folder of files (a draft sent), is taken out at once. A file over 16 MB stays here.
- **Stores that leave, arrive or move**: a store no longer shared here (a part switched off, a notes folder unset) takes nothing out elsewhere; a store new here (a part switched on, a store a new version adds) or moved (another notes folder) joins as a new computer joins: its files are rebuilt from all the records first, then only what they alone hold goes out, so an old copy here never beats the others' newer values and an empty new folder is filled. A store this version no longer has is left aside when a device on an older Sioul still sends it: the watch's (`data/watch/`, `state/watch-offers.json`), taken out on 8 October 2026 ([health.md](health.md), "Your watch"); its changes are neither written nor kept waiting, and the rest of the exchange goes on (`RETIRED` in `crates/sioul-sync/src/share.rs`).
- **The build that wrote them**: the first line of each batch a computer appends (an exchange's changes, a new round's opening) carries, sealed in its change, the build that wrote it (`build`, `sioul_core::build::DESCRIBED`: "0.0.3 (eff8661abcde)", [building.md](building.md#which-build)); the lines after it, until the next that says one, are that build's. Sealed: the server never learns it. An older Sioul passes over the field, and its lines, which say none, read as ever (`share::tests::each_batch_of_records_says_its_build`).
- **Records** go to `<folder>/<computer>-<round>.jsonl`, one line each, appended. Each computer writes its own file only, so the sync never makes conflicted copies. Each reads the others' from where it stopped (round and byte), whole lines only, each line on its own: a line that is not text or does not open (a disk's damage) is one broken line, said, and the next ones are read. **Rounds**: a computer's file past 32 KiB (`share::SEGMENT`) is continued in a new round of its own, nothing restated: a server sent the file whole at each change (WebDAV has no append) is sent a small one. Past 1 MiB appended since its last full round (`ROUND_SIZE`), a computer opens a full round, restating every entry it holds the last word on (on a computer, a megabyte or two); while its connection is metered or measured slow (`share::set_frugal`), the full round waits, within 4 MiB appended. A computer's first round, holding all it had when it joined, counts as full. An old round goes once every computer heard from in the last 180 days has read past it, and only one before the last full round: a device joining reads from the oldest kept, and finds every entry there (`share::tests::a_change_sends_a_small_file_and_a_late_joiner_reads_everything`, `a_full_round_waits_while_the_connection_is_metered`). Until 8 October 2026 a round was its opening plus a megabyte of growth: 2.5 MB on the owner's computer, sent whole at each change. Entries taken out are remembered 90 days. Read a piece at a time (8 MiB), never a file whole: a line longer than any record (32 MiB; the longest, a file whole of 16 MiB, is about 30) is one broken line, said, and the next ones are read; a computer's notes, the seal and the claims are never read past 64 KiB (a larger one is none); the end of this computer's own file is read back from its end only, to mend a line left half written.
- **A file put back older**: a sync app may put back an older copy of a computer's file (after a conflict, or keeping old content when only the version changed). Its writer notices its own file shorter than it left it and starts a new round with all it holds (`share-own-cut`); a reader finding a file shorter than what it read, or a record whose number does not follow the last one, says so (`share-other-cut`, `share-other-gap`) and counts what that computer wrote after as not known (doses: a doubt) until its next round. A line left half written is cut before the next one is appended.
- **A memory restored or lost**: a computer whose own records in the folder go further than its memory (restored from a backup, its state lost, sharing stopped and started again) goes on numbering after the folder's last line, never again from the start, and reads every file again from all the records, its own too, so what it marked after the backup comes back (`share-own-ahead`).
- **Notes to each other**: `<folder>/<computer>.toml` says when each computer last exchanged and how far it read the others; written when that changes or a quarter of an hour after the last time.
- **One exchange at a time** on a computer, whoever runs it (the window, a dose's alarm, a second Sioul, the command line): a lock on `share/exchange.lock`. A whole exchange finding another running tries again for a second, then does nothing (`share-busy`); a quick one waits for it. The second covers a program starting elsewhere in Sioul at that moment: it holds a copy of every file open then until it runs (a Mac's for milliseconds), the lock's among them. Hence, too, the host name is asked once per process (`lease::host_name`): on a Mac, which has no `/etc/hostname`, it once started `hostname` at every claim, three times a minute, each start holding the lock's file for a moment (found on 6 October 2026, by the tests on macOS).
- **Quick exchanges**: a dose's alarm, the "Taken" button, the time notification's buttons and Sioul closing exchange without notes and papers, which stay as they are and wait for the next whole exchange (where each store was read is kept: nothing joins again). A quick exchange waiting tells the one running to hurry: its work on notes and papers stops where it is, and goes on at the next. A button pressed is applied here first, then sent: nothing slow can lose it.
- **Files written by the sharing** go under a hidden name of their own (`.<name>.<process>-<n>.sioul.tmp`), made new, then renamed: two exchanges never write into one. This computer's name and sharing (`here.toml`) and its memory reach the disk before they take their name.

## The format of what travels
- **Two formats.** Format 1 is how Sioul 0.0.4 and every earlier version write their records. Format 2 (`share::FORMAT`) names each money line, cover, mail rule, split and preset, and each time session, by an id of its own, and divides lists that format 1 sends whole (pinned sites, routines, task kinds, days off, the words you added to a list or took away, the lighter days) element by element: two devices adding to one list in the same exchange keep both. A device writes format 2 only once every device of the sharing reads it; until then it writes format 1, which an older Sioul reads. Each file's two ways of dividing are its rules and their `next` (`share::Rules`).
- **Ids.** Sioul gives an id when a money line, a split, a preset or a session is made (`sioul_core::ids`: 128 random bits, shaped as a UUID; pinned sites, routines and task kinds already had theirs). What an older Sioul made has none: a device taking format 2 writes one into each such element of its files, once (below). An element still without one afterwards (written by hand) is named by an id derived from the key format 1 named it by (SHA-256 of its list and that key, shaped as a UUID): a session by its start, task and project, a money line by what it holds; elements format 1 named alike stay one entry, as in format 1.
- **Each part says its format.** Each record carries, sealed in its change, the format of its part (`f`; absent: 1): settings, time, and projects and money are in format 2, the other parts in format 1 (`share::part_format`). An older Sioul passes over the field.
- **Who holds format 2 back** (`share::format_holders`). Each device's sealed entry says the format its Sioul reads (`format`, below, "Devices"); an older Sioul's says none. A device writes format 2 once another device at least counts (alone, it writes format 1: one joining later may run an older Sioul) and none holds it back. Every device known here by a sealed fact counts: its entry (the latest seen here, kept, `Memory::seen`: an older copy put back never lowers it), its records in the folder, or its records read here before (`Memory::read_n`, `heard_at`), whatever the folder shows now; but one whose sealed entry says it left (Stop sharing), one you forgot (Forget this device, `share::forget_device`) until it says anything newer, and one not heard of for 180 days. A file alone (plain notes, an entry that does not read) with no record is a stray file, not a device. Heard of is judged from sealed facts only: its entry's times and the clock of its last record read here (`Memory::heard_at`; a memory from before it was kept learns it, at this version's first exchange, from each device's last record in the folder), never its notes in the folder, which are plain. A device that counts holds format 2 back when its entry does not read here, or it has none (an older Sioul, a device known by its records alone), or says an older format, or when what it wrote up to its entry is not all read here. A hostile folder can hold the switch back longer; it cannot open it past a device this one has read, even by hiding all its files. Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing lists every device that holds it back: one the doses do not count (known to the sharing alone) has a line of its own there, with Forget this device; one they count is offered it once silent for a week, as any device, since forgetting a device in use could make a dose it took look not taken. A device that meets a record in format 2 takes it from its next exchange: another device writes it already. A device joining takes it at once when every other says it reads it.
- **The switch, once on each device** (`switch_format`, at the start of an exchange):
  1. every file format 2 divides otherwise must read whole; one that does not (half written, broken by hand), or of no bytes while it held entries, keeps the device on format 1, said (`share-format-wait`), and the switch is tried again at the next exchange;
  2. what changed in those files since the last look goes out first, in format 1 and as format 1 names it (`flush`): a removal or a corrected session made just before reaches the devices still on format 1;
  3. each element of a list format 2 names by ids is given its id in the file, through the file's lock, the file as it was kept among the earlier versions first (`give_ids`). The element that stands for a format-1 entry (what was last sent or received under it) takes the id derived from that entry, as every device holding it derives it; another (a second session at one start, task and project, a second line alike, a preset an older Sioul never carried) takes a random id, and goes out as new. Of several elements format 1 named alike, the one standing for the entry is the first when the entry's last word came from another device (format 1 wrote it there) and the last when from this one (format 1 sent that one). A preset's new id is put where splits and lines named it by its place (`#3`);
  4. the memory is said in format 2's names, each renamed entry's last clock kept as its floor (`Floor`), with, for a list format 1 sent whole, the elements it held. An element standing for its entry but not holding exactly what was sent goes out again under its id.

  The memory then says format 2. Stopped half way, the next exchange does it again from the files and the memory as they are: ids already written stay.
- **Format 1 read after the switch** (a device not switched yet, during the minutes its sync takes to carry the news; a device's own old records read again when a store joins, a record is rebuilt or an older copy of a round file comes back; an older Sioul back) is translated (`translate`). A record at or below its entry's floor is passed over: nothing format 1 already said comes back. An element's change takes its id: its own, else for a session the id of the session of that start, task and project here (a device on format 1 writes a month back without the id the switch gave it here: the id is kept, and the session sent again from here under it when that id is its own rather than the one derived, so that a device joining later holds it once), else the id derived from its format-1 key. A value without its id takes the one it goes under, written into it: what the switch wrote stays. From an older Sioul (its sealed entry says an older format), a line's removal naming a version no device holds under that id any more (a copy changed since) is passed over; from a device whose entry says it reads format 2, and which still writes format 1 for the minutes it takes to see every other's entry, the later word wins, a removal too. The versions of one element in one reading (an older Sioul changes a line by adding the new one and taking the old one out) make one change: the latest held, at the latest clock. A list sent whole sets what it holds, and takes out, of what the previous whole list held, what it no longer holds: never an element added since in format 2.
- **What an older Sioul does meanwhile** (0.0.4 and earlier, such as a phone not updated yet): it reads and writes format 1 as before, since no device writes format 2 while it counts. The fixes of format 2 wait until it is updated: until then, one session, the pinned sites or one list of words changed on two devices at the same moment keep only the later change, and two lines alike that it made become one. Lines, splits, presets and sessions made meanwhile by a newer Sioul already carry their ids, so that two alike stay two; an older Sioul keeps a line's id, and writes a month of sessions back without theirs, which costs nothing (format 1 names sessions by their start, task and project). Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing says that a device runs an older Sioul and holds the newer form back (`share-format-held`), on that device's own line. Retired without Stop sharing, it holds format 2 back until you choose Forget this device (offered once it has been silent for a week, or at once for a device the doses do not count), or for 180 days.
- **An older Sioul back after the switch** (silent 180 days, forgotten, or put back by hand) cannot be taught: it reads format 2 wrongly on its own screen (a line changed elsewhere added a second time there, a list's change failing to write there). What it sends stays format 1 and is translated by the others with the floors above: a version of a line it takes out that the others changed since is passed over, and its whole lists take out only what their previous whole list held. Its other changes are taken as changes, the later word winning. The panel says so (`share-format-older`) while it counts; updating it there mends it.
- **A newer format met** (records of a part in a format above this Sioul's: `share-newer`): they wait (`Memory::waiting`), never written into the files here; nothing of that part goes out from here: what changes here in it stays in its files, with when it was found (`Memory::held`). The records of formats this Sioul reads, of that part too, are still read. It is said once in the status line, and in Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing as long as it lasts, with the build that wrote it. Once this Sioul reads that format (updated), the waiting records are written and what changed here goes out at the time it was found, so that a later change of the newer device still wins over it, and an earlier one loses. Once the devices that wrote it no longer count (`present`, by sealed facts: left, forgotten, silent 180 days, such as a test build tried once), the part is released: what waited of it in that format is dropped, what changed here goes out, and those records read again are passed over (`Memory::released`). Nothing else releases it: an entry saying an older format (its records ahead of its new entry, or an older copy put back) does not. For the doses' part, while it is held, the newer device's dose records are not counted as heard (`share::heard`), this device's exchanges claim no export (`Outcome::looked` 0), its entry says its doses are not shared (`doses` false) and it never closes its entry as if all its answers went out: the hold is read from the memory on the disk (`share::holds`), the same after a restart, in any process. The others then doubt a dose this device may have answered, never take it as not taken (docs/health.md, "Knowing").
- **What stays as it was**: lists edited whole on one page, or whose order counts, still travel whole, the later winning: working hours and office hours (a row per day), mail filters (their order decides), the hours' categories, the helps of a pause, the times of the gathered reminders, the projects hidden from the Porch, the attention's rows, the meals and naps (named by their place, `meal:1`). Ties (`links.toml`), the mail said to be no payment and the tasks set aside today are named by what they hold, as before. Putting back an earlier version (above, "Earlier versions") puts back each element by its name, and takes nothing out that was added since.
- **Tested** (`cargo test -p sioul-sync format_tests` and `switch_tests`, the fixtures in `crates/sioul-sync/tests/fixtures/format-1/`, made by Sioul 0.0.4 before format 2 was written): two lines alike made on one device stay two through two devices and a third joining, and two made by 0.0.4 reach the other device once the switch gives the second its id (`two_identical_lines_made_on_one_device_stay_two`); one session changed on two devices at once stays one in format 2, one 0.0.4 noted without an id too (the switch writes it its id), and doubles in format 1 (`one_session_changed_on_two_devices_stays_one`, `a_session_without_id_changed_on_two_devices_stays_one`); two sites pinned and two words added at once on two devices all stay, a word taken away on one while another is added on the other both hold (`sites_and_words_changed_on_two_devices_at_once_all_hold`); a sharing written by 0.0.4 read while the phone still runs it, then both switching, nothing doubled or lost on the desk, the phone or a device joining afterwards (`an_older_sharing_is_read_then_upgraded_without_doubling_or_losing`), the phone's 0.0.4 records arriving after the switch (`an_older_device_s_write_after_the_switch_doubles_nothing`); a newer format read, never written over, said once (`a_newer_format_is_read_never_written_over_and_said_once`); format 2 waiting for every device (`format_2_waits_for_every_device`); lists divided element by element within tables (`format_2_divides_lists_element_by_element_and_writes_them_back`); and the review of 9 October (R1 to R14, L1 to L4), each finding a test of what now holds: a memory put back from before the switch, a whole list from a device not switched yet, a word taken away or a session corrected just before the switch, an older Sioul taking out a stale copy, a hostile folder, a held doses' part, held changes against a newer device's later ones, a part released once its writers are gone, a device joining after a translation, records read again after the switch (an older copy of a round, the doses' record repaired, notes switched on), an earlier month put back, a companion that left, a device forgotten, an older device said only while it counts, a file that does not read, and the switch under eDrive (size only, never deleting) with one device editing, then both, the phone updated at once, at minute 20 or at 45; and the second review (S1 to S9, `share_switch_more_tests.rs`): a device known by its notes alone, or read before this version and silent since, holding nothing back; a newer device's records ahead of its entry keeping the part held; a format-1 change of one session leaving its twin; a split naming a preset by its place ending the same everywhere; a session corrected in the mixed window keeping its id, then moved to another project on one device and corrected on the other, staying one; a hostile folder hiding every file of a device keeping the switch shut; a switch stopped half way; a later removal from a device that reads format 2 taken everywhere; 360 simulated upgrades under eDrive (40 seeds, three lags, the phone updated at once, at minute 20 or 45) and 150 with a third device joining mid-switch, all converging.

## Sent as soon as it is saved
When a file a sharing part carries is saved here, whatever saved it (a dose answered, a setting, `health.toml`, a project, a budget, a draft), an exchange runs a few seconds later, then this device's files go to the server (Sioul keeping the folder itself, or beside the sync app: "Sent to the server too", below). One mechanism, in `crates/sioul-sync/src/trigger.rs` and the window's `share.rs`:
- **Seen, not announced**: every two seconds the window looks at the shared files' sizes and times (`trigger::Stamps`, a few hundred files: the notes and papers folders left out, their thousands of files read by the minute's exchange as before; hidden files left out: locks, files half written). A change, by whatever wrote it (a page, the command line, another program), is a save. Whatever was written while an exchange ran (the exchange itself, what runs before it, the minute's other work) is taken in after it, never taken for a save: a change it did not carry goes at the next one. Nothing is looked at while an exchange runs.
- **One worker**: every exchange of the window runs on one thread (`share::worker`), when it is due (`trigger::Soon`): asked (a button, a dose answered, a switch, do-not-disturb, the minute's tick: `share::exchange`, as before), at once; saves seen, two seconds after the last, five after the first at most, so that a page saving a field at each key sends once, and soon; but never less than 50 seconds after the last exchange began (`trigger::MIN_GAP_MS`), and the minute's scheduled exchange is left out when one began less than 50 seconds ago (`Soon::tick`, `share::exchange_tick`): a file Sioul writes each minute (a session's time, a plan) costs one exchange a minute, not two, and a quiet minute only the scheduled one. A save is thus carried within the minute, within seconds when the last exchange is older. What is asked or saved while one runs makes the next one; before, an exchange asked while another ran was dropped until the minute's tick. Never on the window's thread.
- **Then sent**: after each exchange, what it wrote goes to the server (Sioul keeping the folder itself, or beside the sync app when the backup found the folder there), then a phone's sync app is asked to look (`CARRIERS`): after, not before, so that it finds the server as the files here.
- **On a phone**, the window's process looks only while Sioul is on the screen. The processes that write in the background exchange themselves, then send, as before (`share::exchange_here`: a dose marked from its notification, an alarm, the time notification's buttons, the background step): a button pressed sends off its thread (Android waits eight seconds for its answer); an alarm and the background step wait for it.
- **Tested**: the pause and its bound (`trigger::tests::the_trigger_waits_for_a_pause_and_never_long`); a quiet minute running the scheduled exchange alone, a file saved each minute costing about one a minute whenever in the minute, never two closer than 50 seconds (`a_quiet_minute_runs_the_scheduled_exchange_alone`); a save seen in any shared file, never in a note, a lock or a file half written, and what the exchange wrote never taken for one (`trigger::tests::saves_are_seen_in_the_shared_files`).

## What the sync app must do
Very little, so that any sync app carries the folder: Nextcloud and ownCloud, Murena's eDrive, Syncthing, Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud Drive, or another; or none, Sioul keeping it in step with a Nextcloud itself (below, "Kept in step by Sioul itself"). The sharing asks only that **new files and files that grow are copied some day**, in any order, maybe late, maybe never deleted, maybe with a conflicted copy beside them.
- **Nothing is rewritten in place at the same size**: the records only grow; the sealed notes and papers (`blobs/<name>`) are new files, written again only when gone or cut (or made fresh, now and then, when a content sealed long ago is used again); the few files rewritten (the notes to each other, the claims, the devices' entries) change size at each writing, one step past the last, over a turn of several hundred writings (`share::pad_for`). Some sync apps tell a change by its size alone: eDrive brings a file changed on the server only when its size differs from the phone's copy (its `FileDiffUtils.getActionForFileDiff`), others compare size and time.
- **Half-written files never travel**: files are written under a name starting with a dot (which sync apps leave out), then renamed.
- **Conflicted copies, temporary files, files of other names** are never read: only `<id>-<round>.jsonl`, `<id>.toml`, `leases/<part>/<id>.lease`, `devices/<id>.device`, `blobs/<name>` and `seal.toml` count. Two devices sealing the same content may both write its `blobs/<name>`: either copy opens to it.
- **Deletions are not needed**: eDrive never deletes a file deleted elsewhere; an old round left behind is never read again, an old sealed file never opened again.
- **Files kept on demand** (OneDrive's, iCloud's and Google Drive's online-only files, Nextcloud's virtual files): a file not downloaded yet reads as empty or not at all, which is a file not here yet; the doses say what is not known meanwhile. Keep the sharing folder "always on this device" in those apps.
- **Timing is the sync app's**: the desktop's apps bring a change within seconds; a phone's often look every half hour. On a phone, Sioul asks the sync app to look now when it offers a way (`CARRIERS` in `crates/sioul-app/src/share.rs`: eDrive's "force scan" today; Syncthing, FolderSync and others can be added the same way), after writing, when Sioul comes back on the screen, and every five minutes while it is shown. Without one, changes come at the sync app's own pace, and the doses say what is not known until then: nothing depends on it but how soon. Where the folder lives on a Nextcloud one of your accounts reaches, Sioul also reads the others' files there itself, and sends this device's own (below, "Fetched from the server too", "Sent to the server too").
- **Tested against each kind of carrier**: a simulator copies the folder between a phone and a server as each kind does (every change both ways; size only; late and out of order; older copies put back with conflicted copies; files on demand); doses are never taken as known when they are not, and every dose comes in the end (`doses_are_never_known_wrongly_whatever_carries_them`); notes travel file by file whatever carries them, a change and a deletion both ways, nothing of them readable in the folder (`notes_travel_file_by_file_whatever_carries_them`).

## Fetched from the server too
When the sharing folder lives on a Nextcloud server (Murena's, ownCloud's) that one of your contacts-and-calendars accounts reaches, Sioul also reads the other devices' files there, over WebDAV, beside the sync app: a backup; and sends this device's own files there too (below, "Sent to the server too"). The synced folder stays the way; a sync app late or stuck (eDrive, 6 October 2026: a computer's records an hour and a half late on the phone, its entry among the devices never) then delays nothing. Code: `crates/sioul-sync/src/remote.rs`; the window's side in `crates/sioul-app/src/share.rs` (`fetch_from_server`).
- **Found**: on the accounts whose server address holds `/remote.php/` (Google's never), in each one's files, `…/remote.php/dav/files/<user>/`, the user as its principal names it (`current-user-principal`). Where in them: the place given by hand (Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing), alone; else the folder's path from a phone's storage (eDrive carries `/storage/emulated/0/` as the cloud's root: `Documents/Sioul`), from the Nextcloud app's own folder, from a folder the Nextcloud client carries (`localPath` → `targetPath`, `nextcloud.cfg`), then its last one, two and three names. Looked for again every six hours while not found, at once when asked.
- **Confirmed** only where the server's `seal.toml` is the folder's, byte for byte: a folder sealed otherwise is never read. The seal is read again when its ETag changes there, and every six hours; another one, or none, and nothing more is fetched.
- **When** (`remote::due`), before an exchange: the first pull as soon as the folder is found, whatever the hour: until then this device knows the others only from the stale folder, and cannot tell whether one is in use (6 October 2026, 23:30: found on the phone at night, then never pulled, the desktop in use taken for closed since 17:39). Then once a minute on a computer, on a phone with Sioul on its screen, or while another device is in use (as read here, what was fetched included); otherwise at the phone's background step's pace, five minutes, fifteen at night: a device opened at night is noticed within those fifteen. A dose's alarm, a waking and the background step asking for news pull twenty seconds after the last, while the sync app is given its twenty seconds; a background step that asks the sync app nothing pulls at its pace. Never on the window's thread; one at a time. Each look and each pull is said in a phone's log (`adb logcat -s sioul`: "backup: 23:52:10 pulled from murena.io: 14 listed, 3 fetched, 2468 bytes", or why not), never a password, a name or a content.
- **What**: `PROPFIND` (`Depth: 1`) on the folder, `devices/`, `leases/` and each part's claims; a folder whose ETag did not change is not looked through again for ten minutes (a Nextcloud folder's ETag changes with anything below it). Of the other devices' files, only those newer than both the synced folder's copy and what was fetched before come down: records (`<id>-<n>.jsonl`) when the server's is longer, from the end of the longer copy here, its last whole line asked again (`Range`) and checked to be the same there, else the whole file; notes to each other, entries and claims whole, when their ETag changed, kept only when they differ from the synced folder's copy. Sealed notes and papers (`blobs/`) stay the sync app's. Never this device's own files.
- **Where**: `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/share/remote/`, the folder's paths mirrored; what the backup knows in `share/remote.toml` (the place found, the account by its id, ETags and sizes, when, why not; your switch and the place you gave), never shared. Never the password: it comes from the keyring for each look, as the calendars' sync has it, and goes to the server only, over TLS (plain HTTP only to a test's own stand-in).
- **Read**: an exchange attaches what was fetched when its `seal.toml` is the folder's (`remote::attach`); then, for each other device's file, the newer copy is read: records the longer (they only grow, and each line starts at the same place in both, so where each device was read stays true), notes to each other the later `at`, an entry by its times (each writing sets one to its device's clock: a start, a close, an exchange), a claim the later `renewed`. The synced folder's copy wins a tie. The doses' knowing reads the same (`devices::all`, `lease::claims`, `share::others`), so `doubts_now` sees what the server holds.
- **Never half**: a file goes into the cache under a hidden name, then is renamed, once all of it came (its length said, or chunked to its end); records keep whole lines only, a line cut on its way coming again next time. A records' file cut on its way is kept up to its last whole line: a slow phone's next look goes on from there.
- **Gone**: what the synced folder caught up with leaves the cache (records as long there, entries and notes the same there). Records longer than the synced folder's stay until it catches up, even switched off: read past its end, a records' file gone would read as cut, and the doses would doubt for hours. Switched off, the entries and notes fetched go at once, and nothing is asked of the server. Sharing stopped, all of it goes.
- **Failures**: a server's error, a timeout (ten seconds for each wait, thirty for a look), a refused password end that look, said; the exchange runs as without it.
- **Said** in Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing: "Also fetched directly from cloud.example.org, last at 22:41.", or why not (not found, sealed otherwise, a refused password, no such account), with the switch (on unless switched off here) and a field for the folder's place there.
- **Costs**: while another device is in use, a minute: three to six listings of a few kilobytes, its new lines, its entry and its claims (some hundred bytes each); nothing changed, one listing.
- **Tested** (`cargo test --release -p sioul-sync remote`), against a stand-in WebDAV server in the tests (a folder on the disk served as a Nextcloud's, faults on demand): a stale phone folder caught up (do-not-disturb, the desktop's entry, `doubts_now` as with a fresh folder; nothing written into the synced folder, nothing but `PROPFIND` and `GET` asked, never this device's files); a fresh folder unchanged, byte for byte against the same device without it; a file cut on its way never read as whole, each line read once; a seal that differs refused, and a seal changed here dropping what was fetched; server errors and timeouts leaving the exchange as without it; a fetched copy never winning over a newer synced one; doses never known wrongly with a sync app that brings nothing down; found at night, the phone's folder stale, the first pull at once, then the pace of who is in use (`found_at_night_the_first_pull_comes_at_once`, `the_pace_of_pulls`); where the folder is looked for.

## Sent to the server too
Where the backup found the sharing folder on a Nextcloud (above, "Fetched from the server too"), this device's own files go there too, sent by Sioul itself over WebDAV beside the sync app, both ways then: the others' fetched, its own sent. The sync app stays the way; Sioul no longer waits for it to carry what this device wrote (a computer's records an hour and a half late on the phone, 6 October 2026), and a server that lost files is given them again ("Send everything again"). Code: `crates/sioul-sync/src/remote.rs` (`send`, `Sender`), the window's side in `crates/sioul-app/src/share.rs` (`send_to_server`, `send_again`).
- **Only this device's own files**: its records (`<id>-<n>.jsonl`), its notes to the others (`<id>.toml`), its claims (`leases/<part>/<id>.lease`), its entry (`devices/<id>.device`), in that order; then the sealed files it made itself (`share::own_sealed`: their names and the size it sealed each at, from its memory), whole only: one being written, or of another size, waits. Never another device's file, never another device's sealed file the sync app brought, never `seal.toml`.
- **Over what is there as it was seen**: `If-Match` the ETag it had after sending, or `If-None-Match: *` where none was there. Each `PUT` says the file's time here (`X-OC-Mtime`) and its SHA-1 (`OC-Checksum`), which Nextcloud keeps and shows to clients.
- **Records only grow**: a records file is never sent shorter than what went, nor over a longer one there. One a sync app cut here (an older copy brought down) stays as it is there, remembered at the server's size: what is appended here later, while still shorter, never goes over it either; the exchange opens a new round (`share-own-cut`).
- **Its texts to send first, on a lane of their own** (`remote::send_urgent`, its own lock and `share/sending-urgent.toml`): this device's texts to send are copied into the folder as a small file of their own, `texts/send/<id>.jsonl` (`remote::place_texts`, the lines sealed already, written only where the part "Texts" is shared), and sent before the rest, and right after a text is written, never behind a large file going up on a slow line. The others' pull brings that file (`texts/send/`, up to 4 MiB), the sync app carries it too, and a phone's decision reads it beside the records' copy, each request by its key, once (Texts, below). Sioul keeping the folder itself sends it the same way.
- **Given the time its size asks**: each file a minute, and as long more as it takes at 16 KiB a second (`remote::FLOOR`: a 2.5 MB file, 60 s and 156 s more; a phone's 3G uplink is faster); cut when nothing moved for half a minute; the wait for the server's answer, while the last bytes drain from the system's buffers, within that time alone. A send is not cut at thirty seconds as a pull is. Until 8 October 2026 a file had a flat minute: the owner's records, 2.5 MB on a phone's hotspot, never went, tried again from zero every minute.
- **A file that does not go** (too slow, cut on its way): said in detail (`Failure`: which file by kind, how, how long, how many bytes were handed to the network, of how many), in `sending.toml`, in the log and in the panel ("This device's records did not go at 19:24: 2.5 MB did not go through in 216 s: slower than Sioul waits for. Tried again at 19:25."); held back, never tried before its backoff (a minute, two, five, a quarter of an hour, half an hour, an hour), the records after it with it (records go in order), the other files still going. The server unreachable, a certificate or a password refused: nothing is tried before the backoff ("Send everything again" apart).
- **Paced on a metered or slow connection**: a file over 256 KiB goes at most every ten minutes when NetworkManager says the connection is metered (or guesses so), or when the last large file went slower than 64 KiB a second (`Sending::slow`; a phone's hotspot may not say it is metered); the full rounds wait meanwhile (above, "The log").
- **When**: right after each exchange of the window (each minute, and a few seconds after a save, above, "Sent as soon as it is saved"); after an exchange from a phone's background, before its sync app is asked to look; at the window's close (a computer waits five seconds for it); when sharing stops (its entry saying it left). Only what changed here since it went (its size and time): with nothing new, not a request. Every six hours, and the first time, the server's folders are listed (the folder, `devices/`, each part's claims, `blobs/`) and each file compared with what is there: one lost there goes again.
- **The sync app sending the same file after**: found the same there, nothing more. Its upload gives the file a new ETag (Nextcloud gives one at every `PUT`, the same bytes included); the next send of a file changed here meets `412`, looks at the server's copy (`PROPFIND`: size and checksum), and when it is the file here (the sync app sent it first) takes it as sent, with no second upload: a sealed file by its size (its name is its content's), records by their size and their checksum when the server keeps one, the rest by their checksum, else read there and compared. Otherwise it goes over the copy there, `If-Match` its new ETag.
- **What the sync apps do with it** (read in their sources, 8 October 2026: eDrive `main` of 31 August 2026, the Nextcloud desktop client 34.0.4, Nextcloud server 33.0.8):
  - **Nextcloud's server** keeps the `X-OC-Mtime` given and the `OC-Checksum` as sent (a `PUT` without one clears it; a plain `PUT`'s checksum is never checked against the bytes), shows it in `PROPFIND` (`oc:checksums`), and gives a new ETag at every `PUT`.
  - **The Nextcloud desktop client**, finding a file changed here since its last sync and a new ETag there, takes it for a conflict, then compares: the same size and a checksum it can compute (SHA-1 among them) and the same hash, it takes the server's as its own, nothing downloaded; else it downloads and compares the bytes: the same, nothing more. Only when the bytes differ does it rename the file here "(conflicted copy …)", which it never sends up, and put the server's copy in its place. With Sioul sending right after each write, the server holds the file here when the client looks.
  - **Murena's eDrive** sends a file whose time here is later than the one it kept, `If-Match` the ETag it knows in Documents (a "media" folder there), blindly elsewhere, with `X-OC-Mtime` and no checksum; refused (`412`), it holds that file back an hour, then two, then five, and after a fourth refusal leaves it until it restarts, never making a conflicted copy. It brings a file down when the ETag there changed and the size differs from the phone's; the same size, it keeps the new ETag. It looks at the server first, then the phone; a folder whose time did not change (a file appended in place) is not looked at on the phone side, and a download planned then goes ahead over the phone's newer file. So after Sioul's send its upload of the same file is a second copy of the same bytes (harmless), and Sioul asks it to look only after sending.
  - **What is left**: the server one version behind the file here (Sioul's send of a write a moment before landed, the next write's not yet) at the very moment the sync app looks. The Nextcloud client then puts the server's copy back here and keeps the newer one beside as a conflicted copy; eDrive may bring the older copy down over an appended file. Never anything lost: the device's own records cut here start a new round with all it holds (`share-own-cut`), its entry and claims are written again from its own memory at the next exchange, the server's copy never shrinks (no `PUT` over a longer copy, every `PUT` over what was seen), and the others read the server's copy, which only grows: a doubt at most, never a dose known wrongly. The conflicted copies stay on that computer, never read ("Conflicted copies… are never read"), to delete by hand. Sending right after each write keeps that moment short.
- **Switched** in Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing: "Also send this device's changes to cloud.example.org directly", on by default wherever the backup is on (`remote.toml`: `send`, never shared), with where it stands ("Sent directly to cloud.example.org, last at 10:41.", or why not). Off, nothing is sent; with the backup off, neither.
- **"Send everything again"**, a button in the same place, sharing on: each of this device's files compared with the server's copy, whatever was sent before, and sent where it is missing there or not the same; then the others' newer files fetched, and an exchange. It says what it did: "Sent 14 files to cloud.example.org at 10:42.", "Nothing needed sending: cloud.example.org held this device's 14 files already, checked at 10:42.", or why not (the folder not found on a server, sending switched off, the server's error). Sioul keeping the folder itself, the same: what it remembers of sending its files is forgotten (its sealed files apart, which tell its own from the others'), and the server's folders looked through again (`remote::again_mirror`).
- **Where**: what was sent, when, and why not, in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/share/sending.toml`, apart from the backup's `remote.toml`, so that a pull and a send never write over each other's; never shared, never a password. One send at a time on a device, whichever process runs it (a lock beside the memory). Said in a phone's log ("backup: 10:42:03 sent to murena.io: 2 sent, 0 there already, 0 records longer there left as they are, 1840 bytes", and, after a listing, what differed there by kind and size: "records 70708 here, 71234 there"), never a name or a content.
- **Taken out there**: this device's records past their time (removed here once every device read past them), never its last round, and nothing while none of its records is here; the sealed files it swept; each only as it was sent (`If-Match`).
- **Costs**: this device's current records' file when it changes (32 KiB at most), its entry and its claims each minute while Sioul runs (some hundred bytes each), now and then a full round (a megabyte or two, once per megabyte appended). Measured on the stand-in server for a computer holding 8,000 entries and writing each minute, its claim renewed each minute: about 520 KB an hour (`bytes_an_hour_of_a_computer_writing_each_minute`); the same computer before 8 October 2026 sent its 2.5 MB file each minute, 150 MB an hour, had it gone. With eDrive, the phone's own files go up twice (Sioul's, then eDrive's same bytes).
- **Tested** (`cargo test --release -p sioul-sync remote`), against the stand-in server, which now gives a new ETag at each `PUT`, keeps `OC-Checksum` and honours `X-OC-Mtime`, and two sync apps simulated as their sources read: only this device's files sent, never twice when the sync app sent them before or after, records never sent shorter, switched off not a request (`beside_a_sync_app_only_this_devices_files_go_up_and_never_twice`); sealed files whole and only this device's, taken out once swept (`sealed_files_go_up_whole_and_only_this_devices`); the server losing this device's files, sent again and said, a second time nothing sent, a server's error said (`send_everything_again_after_the_server_lost_this_devices_files`); doses never known wrongly, every dose arriving and the records there never shorter, with eDrive sending too and looking at the worst moments (it brought an older copy down over the phone's newer one, refused its own uploads: `doses_are_never_known_wrongly_with_edrive_sending_too`), and with the Nextcloud client on the computer sending too (conflicted copies made there, rounds started again: `doses_are_never_known_wrongly_with_the_nextcloud_client_sending_too`). On a throttled uplink (the stand-in server reading slowly): a large records file cut at a flat budget, said in detail and held, not tried before its backoff, then given the time its size asks and going whole (`a_large_file_goes_through_a_slow_uplink`); the server failing, nothing tried before the backoff, the count starting again once it answers (`a_failed_send_waits_for_its_backoff`); a text written while a large records file crawls up reaching the phone's pull within seconds (`a_text_to_send_reaches_the_phone_while_the_records_crawl`); the bytes an hour (above).

## Kept in step by Sioul itself
With a Nextcloud (Murena's, ownCloud's) that one of your contacts-and-calendars accounts reaches, no sync app is needed: Sioul keeps its own copy of a folder of your files there and sends and fetches the files itself, over WebDAV. Chosen when sharing starts (Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing: "Sioul keeps it in step with your Nextcloud itself", the account, the folder's place there). The folder on the server is the same as a sync app's: a device with a sync app on it shares with one without, each in its own way. Code: `crates/sioul-sync/src/remote.rs` (`step`, `open`, `create`, `begin`), the window's side in `crates/sioul-app/src/share.rs` (`start_on_server`, `mirror_step`).
- **Its copy**: `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/share/mirror/`, the sharing folder as the exchange knows it (`here.toml` names it); what was fetched and sent in `share/remote.toml` (`mode = "mirror"`), never shared.
- **Starting**: the folder looked for in the account's files (`Documents/Sioul` by default). Sealed already (another device shares through it): its `seal.toml` comes into the copy, and the passphrase is the one chosen there (checked against it). Not there: each missing folder is made (`MKCOL`), the seal made here and sent only if none is there yet (`If-None-Match: *`): two devices starting at once never seal it twice ("Another device started sharing in that folder at the same moment").
- **A step**: the server's folder looked through as the backup does (`PROPFIND`, a folder whose ETag did not change skipped for ten minutes), the other devices' newer files brought into the copy (records from where the copy ends, the rest whole); then this device's own files sent: those changed here since they went up (size and time), or changed or gone there (their ETag in the listing): its records first, then its notes to the others, its claims, its entry (it says how far the records went), its sealed files last; then the others' sealed notes, papers and spam filter's tables brought (`blobs/`, whole or not at all). So no large sealed file on a slow line, going up or coming down, holds back the records, the doses' answers among them (7 October 2026: before, a sealed file failing to go up stopped the step before the records, and one slow to come down left no time for them in the step's thirty seconds). A record naming a sealed file not there yet is read as a sync app's late copy is: the others wait for it. A sealed file that fails to come down is looked for again at the next step, not ten minutes later.
- **Never another device's file**: each device sends only its own (`<id>-<n>.jsonl`, `<id>.toml`, `devices/<id>.device`, `leases/<part>/<id>.lease`, the sealed files it made). Each goes over what is there as it was seen: `If-Match` its ETag, or `If-None-Match: *` when none was there. Refused (`412`: changed there meanwhile), sent again over what is there now, once looked at: it is this device's own. A sealed file goes only where none is (one there is the same: its name is its content's). Refused again, a server's error, a timeout: the step stops, said; nothing here is lost, and it all goes at the next step.
- **Taken out there**: this device's records past their time (removed here once every device read past them, `remove_old_rounds`), never its last round, and nothing while none of its records is here; the sealed files it swept (`blobs::sweep`), by their ETag.
- **Its copy lost** (none of its records here): its own files come back from the server first, so that it goes on after its last record rather than numbering them again over what the others read.
- **When**: before each exchange, the server looked through (at most every twenty seconds, unless news is asked for: a dose's alarm, a waking); after it, what it wrote sent at once, its entry with it; where the sync app would be asked to look (a dose marked from a notification, Sioul back on a phone's screen), what changed sent; at the window's close (a computer waits five seconds for it) and when sharing stops, a last sending. A phone in the background: at each of its steps, in place of the sync app's twenty seconds. One step at a time on a device, whichever process runs it (a lock beside the memory).
- **Said** in Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing: "Kept in step with cloud.example.org by Sioul itself: Documents/Sioul.", when last in step, or why not.
- **Costs**: a step with nothing new: one listing (a few kilobytes). This device's records go whole when they changed (up to a megabyte before a new round starts): on a phone, which writes few, little; on a computer that writes each minute, more, on its own connection.
- **Tested** (`cargo test --release -p sioul-sync remote`), two devices with no sync app at all, through the stand-in server alone: do-not-disturb and a dose's answer both ways, each device knowing how the other is; a device beside its sync app and one without sharing one folder, both ways; a device never writing another's file; a refused (`412`), failed (`500`) or cut upload losing nothing and going at the next step; its copy lost, a device going on after its last record; sealed files travelling whole, never sent back, taken out once swept; a sealed file failing or slow, going up or coming down, never holding back the records (`a_slow_or_failing_sealed_file_never_holds_back_the_records`); nothing sent until it is set up.

## Knowing what the others wrote
For the doses (docs/health.md, "Knowing"), a device must know whether it has read everything another wrote, and whether that one may still hold an answer not sent. Its entry in the registry says its session and how far it wrote its records at its last export (below, "Devices", `Entry::wrote`); an older Sioul's claims say how far it wrote (round and number, `lease::Claim::wrote`), and whether it closed (`closed`: Sioul quit, or a phone put it away, after one last exchange). Each device keeps the round and number of the last record it read from each other (`Memory::read_n`, `share::heard`). A line that does not read is never passed over in silence: the problem is said (`share-other-line`) and when it was found is kept (`Memory::broken`). A file this device can no longer trust (the doses' record or their records, broken or lost) can be rebuilt (`share::rebuild`): its entries are forgotten here, so nothing of it is taken out elsewhere, and read again from every device's records, this one's too, at the next exchange. Files the window writes too are written under a lock beside them (`sioul_core::filelock`, `.<name>.lock`, which hidden names keep out of the sharing). The window's writers of the files format 2 divides (the configuration, its words and routines, the months of time, the money's ledger and its splits, the lighter days) read, change and write them holding that lock, read again once it is held, as the sharing and the switch do: a change written in between is never lost. Those that keep a whole state in memory (the lighter days, the day's choices, the money's and the Porch's states) merge on saving: what they changed goes in, what changed under them since they read it stays (`filelock::save_merged`).

## Devices
Each device says how it is, in the folder, for the doses: `<folder>/devices/<id>.device`, written by that device only, sealed like the claims (bound to "device" and its id: an entry copied under another device's name does not open), its size changed at each writing (`pad_for`), never read past 64 KiB. Code: `crates/sioul-sync/src/devices.rs` (the entry, sealed, read, written), `crates/sioul-app/src/devices.rs` (the sessions).
- **Who**: the sharing's own id (`here.toml`: a UUID made once per machine, never the host name, which can change, or be another machine's too), its name (a computer's host name; a phone's model, said "the phone" by the others), its kind (`phone`, `computer`), the Sioul that wrote it: its version and the commit it was built from (`version`, `commit`: `sioul_core::build`, [building.md](building.md#which-build)), and the format of what travels that it reads (`format`, above, "The format of what travels"; none from an older Sioul).
- **The builds that wrote to the folder**: each device's entry says its own, so the folder records which builds write to it. Settings ▸ Your folder and sharing says this device's ("This device: Sioul 0.0.3 (eff8661abcde).") and, under each other device, the one its entry says ("Sioul 0.0.3 (eff8661abcde)."), calmly when older than this one ("…, older than this device's (0.0.3 (…)): updating it there, when you can, keeps both alike."; versions compared number by number, two commits of one version never). An older Sioul's entry, with its version alone or neither, reads all the same (`devices::tests::an_entry_says_its_build_and_an_older_one_still_reads`).
- **Its session**: `started` (its last start), `closed` (its last clean close; 0 never), `working` (up from the start to the last step of a clean close; required: an entry without it is no entry, never one read as closed). On a computer, a session is the window's life: up when Sioul starts; down as the last step of a clean close, after a final export: the window closed, or SIGTERM, SIGINT, SIGHUP, which quit Qt as the window's close does (`cpp/signals.cpp`), both ending in `share::closing`. On a phone, a session is the time on the screen: down when Sioul goes to the background, after its export (Android may end a process in the background without a word), up when it comes back. A reminder handled while no session is open (a dose's alarm, "Taken" pressed on its notification) is a session of its own: up, what it records, its export, down, then the sync app asked to look, so that the lowered entry goes up, not the raised one. Only when the last session of the process ends does it go down, and only once an export read everything that process recorded of the doses since (a mark, a record, a reminder: `devices::recorded`): a final export that failed leaves it up, and the others in doubt, rather than sure of what they lack. A crash leaves it up; a session that finds it up owes an export before its own close.
- **Its sharing**: `imported` (when it last read the others), `exported` (when its files were last read for an export: everything captured there before is in its records, up to `wrote`, its round and number then), `doses` (it shares the health part), `notifications` (a phone: whether Android lets Sioul hold other apps' notifications there, as it last looked at each writing; unsaid on a computer and from an older Sioul: a computer's What reaches you counts a phone's messages unless it says no, an unsaid one as holding them), `left` (it stopped sharing: it counts no more).
- **Written** at each start and clean close, and after each exchange, whoever runs it (the window, a dose's alarm, a phone's background service), an exchange never touching the session. Kept privately too, in `share/device.toml` (never shared), changed under a lock and read just before: two processes (the window's, a background service's) never write an older session back over the other's.
- **Read** by `health::know`: each device's entry, when it was seen changing (on this device's clock), how far its clock is ahead at least (its export's date less this device's clock when seen: a lower bound, the largest of the last sixty kept), and whether everything it wrote up to its export is read here (`Heard::complete`). An entry that no longer reads is none: that device is known by its claims, as an older Sioul is, never as it last said. So is an entry saying it closed while its claim, renewed open more than a minute after that close (both on its own clock), says it runs: an older Sioul put back there, which updates no entry. Kept here: `share/peers.json` (never shared), with what you said: a device off, or forgotten, until it says anything newer.
- **What it costs**: one small file per device, rewritten at each exchange (a minute while Sioul runs), as the claims are.

## Leases: one computer at a time
Some work must happen on one computer only: reminding a dose (twice, it could be taken twice), numbering invoices (twice, one number twice), the gathered notification of sites. `sioul_sync::lease` keeps them apart through the same folder: each computer writes its own claim on a part, sealed like the records (`<folder>/leases/<part>/<computer>.lease`, one writer per file), renewed each minute while Sioul runs, alive five minutes after. Every computer reads the claims the same way:
- **following you** (medicines, the gathered notification): the live claim of the computer used most recently keeps it;
- **staying put** (invoices): the claim taken on purpose last ("Make invoices on this device"), else the oldest.
A computer acts only once it has kept a part for a minute and a half, time for the others' claims to come through the sync. Invoices also want the other live computers heard from within three minutes: a stopped sync could hide a claim, so they wait rather than risk a number twice. Sharing off, this computer keeps everything.

## Do-not-disturb's switch and list
Do-not-disturb on every device ([do-not-disturb.md](do-not-disturb.md)) adds two files, and needs no merge of the sharing's own:
- **The switch** (`state/do-not-disturb.toml`, settings): a table per device, written by that device alone and shared whole (`whole = ["device.*"]`), its last press stamped with a hybrid clock (never before a press already known there). Every device reads every table the same way: the latest press wins; two presses that did not know of each other, less than a minute apart, "on" wins. Each table also says what holds on its device and whether its system is silenced, for the others' status line.
- **Who may reach you during do-not-disturb** (`config/dnd-people.toml`, senders): one entry per person (`[[person]]`, by its `id`), names, numbers and addresses kept in the sharing itself, since the address books of two devices may not be in step.
- Neither is ever written over when it does not read; an older Sioul keeps their records waiting, unknown to it, writing neither and taking nothing out (`known_part`).

## Sealed
- Each record is encrypted on the computer with XChaCha20-Poly1305, bound to its computer, round, number and clock. The folder and its server see which computer wrote, when and how much, never what: not the entries' names (they hold addresses), not their values.
- The key is made from a passphrase with Argon2id (64 MiB, 3 passes, a salt in `<folder>/seal.toml`), typed once on each computer (twice on the first) and kept in its keyring. `seal.toml` also holds a value sealed with the key: a wrong passphrase is told at once. At least 12 characters.
- Lost, the passphrase cannot be found again: stop sharing everywhere, start again with a new folder; each device keeps its own files. **Stop sharing** asks first, saying what stops and what stays (`share-stop-ask`).
- **Yours alone on the disk** (Unix): what the sharing writes is 0600, the folders it makes 0700, as the calls' logs are (`share::private_dirs`, `new_private`, `make_private`): the records, the sealed files and the seal in the folder; its memory, the copies before sharing, the versions it keeps; the files it brings in from your other devices (settings, lists, notes, papers) and the conflict copies beside them. A file there already that others could read becomes yours alone the next time the sharing writes it (written beside then renamed, it takes the new file's mode; appended to or copied, its mode is set); nothing sweeps your folders, and a folder there already keeps its mode. A sync app carrying the folder must then run as you, as the usual ones do (Nextcloud's client, Dropbox, Syncthing started by your session); one running under another account of the computer would not read the records. A phone's shared storage and Windows have no such modes (`share::tests::what_the_sharing_writes_is_yours_alone`).

## Notes
- The notes travel with their folder's own sync, or, their part switched on, through the log, file by file (Notes and papers in the vault).
- **A link keeps its file's name** (`sioul:note/admin/lease.md`, relative to the notes folder), so it holds on every computer, wherever the folder is.
- **A linked note not on this device yet** (its sync not done, or the file removed elsewhere) shows faded, with its name and "Not on this device yet: it may still be syncing (Nextcloud, Dropbox)."
- A note renamed in Sioul updates its links (tasks' LINK lines, `links.toml`); the other computer gets the new file from the folder, the new links from CalDAV and the log.
- **One writer at a time**: the window and an agent (`sioul mcp`) read a note again, change it and write it holding one lock, in Sioul's state folder and never in the notes folder (`notes::with_lock`, `.notes.lock`): a tie written into its front matter, a note saved, renamed, or made from a title (a free name found and taken together, `notes::create`). `links.toml` is changed under its own lock (`LocalLinks::change`, `.links.toml.lock`), the one the sharing takes to write it, read again once held. Two writers at once lose nothing (`links::tests::two_writers_at_once_lose_nothing`, the MCP's `writers_at_once_lose_nothing`).

## Why plain files and a log
The sharing carries Sioul's own files, TOML and JSON as they are on each device, through a folder any sync app carries (or Sioul itself, with a Nextcloud), as sealed records in a log. Three other ways were weighed and set aside:
- **A database** (SQLite on each device with a search index, then a MySQL server carrying the same records, a table of `(computer, round, seq, clock, sealed)`): a server needs its port open to the Internet with TLS, a user limited to one table, and on shared hosting each device's address let in by hand, all of it the person's to run; it would have brought one place at once and transactions, which only invoice numbers need, and those take turns through leases instead (below, "Leases: one computer at a time"). Plain files stay readable in any editor, the notes folder in Obsidian or Nextcloud Notes, and need no server of Sioul's or the person's. SQLite and a search index wait until plain files are too slow ([architecture.md](architecture.md), "Storage").
- **Git inside the synced folder**: git rewrites its index, references and packs in place and takes lock files, and two devices pushing to one repository through a file sync end with conflicted references: the very problems of sync apps the log avoids, each device writing only files of its own, which only grow. Git merges lines, where Sioul's data merges entry by entry (a setting, a medicine, a session). The history people want from it is the local one (above, "Earlier versions").
- **Whole-folder archives** of notes and papers, sealed per part: every change would send every PDF again, hundreds of megabytes over a phone's connection, and a phone's sync app (eDrive) would download the whole archive each time. Notes and papers go file by file instead, each content sealed once (above, "Notes and papers in the vault").

## Not built
- **What the server still learns** (review F19, left for later): the size of each sealed file (gzip's size and 40 bytes a MiB), so a known document may be recognised by its size, and that two files hold the same content; the computers' notes to each other (`<computer>.toml`) are plain and unsigned, so a forged one could keep old rounds from being removed, or show one computer more. Sizes padded to steps and notes sealed like the claims would close it.
- **Listing a big notes folder** (review F23, in part): every file's size and time are looked at in each exchange; looking only into the folders whose time changed would spare a phone's battery.
- **One series of invoice numbers per computer** ("2026-A-014"): built instead is one computer numbering them at a time, the others waiting their turn ([accounting.md](accounting.md), "Invoices on one device"). French rules accept several series only when the way the activity runs justifies them (BOI-TVA-DECLA-30-20-20-10), your accountant's call.
- **Drafts in the server's Drafts folder** (IMAP), for other mail clients to see: drafts travel by the log for now.
- **The Porch's marks in IMAP METADATA** (RFC 5464) where the server has it: by the log for now.
- **Phones other than Android's**: the format is plain (JSON lines, XChaCha20-Poly1305, Argon2id, HKDF for the sealed files); Sioul for Android reads and writes it ([android.md](android.md)), no iPhone app does yet.
