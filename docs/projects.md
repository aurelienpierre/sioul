# Projects, time and invoices

A project is any matter you follow, one list for all of them (`sioul-projects.toml` in your notes folder, [notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md)): the umbrella over its tasks (REFID), events, mail (its routes), notes, time and budget lines. Projects were once "cases" beside "projects"; they are one thing, with one name, and "for a client" is a choice in its form. What was written under the old name still reads ([notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md#its-first-name)). Made, renamed, given its routes and taken out from the Projects page (its form, "Its mail"); taken out, its tasks, notes, mail and time stay where they are. The Porch's settings only choose which projects have a lane there (Porch ⚙, "Projects shown here"). A project **for a client** (`kind = "project"`) has a client, an hourly rate and the budget its invoices are expected in, and its time can be billed.

```toml
[[project]]
id = "lumen"
title = "Studio Lumen — website"
kind = "project"
client = "Studio Lumen"        # or "sioul:contact/<UID>": its client profile, else the card's postal address
rate = 60.0                    # else [invoice] rate
budget = "work"                # invoices expected there until paid
```

## A project's page (Projects, Ctrl+8)
- **One line of time**: what is coming first (dates asked of its open tasks, events), then what happened, newest first (tasks done, mail by its routes or tied by hand, notes of its record or tied to it, time noted, invoices). Mail comes by its routes on sender and subject (matched again each time), by being tied to the project (when it arrived, or by hand), and by conversation: the whole of a conversation one of its messages belongs to ([notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md#routes)). Each message is checked as the Porch checks it, each time the page is shown (`porch::Gate`, which reads the file and asks `porch::admission`, the function the Porch's own lanes use): what the Porch sets aside (a blocked sender, forged, a borrowed name, spam no route protects, hostile) never shows, whether a route, a tie Sioul made or a conversation brings it, and a conversation is followed through the messages let in only, so a forged reply joins nothing; mail nothing authenticates comes by no route on its sender's address or domain, and says "whose address could not be verified"; a message you tied by hand stays, as you tied it. The agents' `list_projects` reads the same page.
- **Its tasks** as a board, a list or a calendar: the Tasks page, filtered to the project.
- **"Note time"**: a meeting, a call, work away from the timer; for a project alone or a task.
- **"Make the invoice"**, when billable time waits: a draft of that time, issued in the same click, or refused with what is missing ("Invoices", below).

## Time (Ctrl+9)
A week, a month or a year: a bar per day (per month over a year) stacked by project, each project's hours and what is left to bill, in hours and in money at its rate, then each stretch of time, newest first.
- **Billed or not, task by task**: a task's form says "Billed": as its project says (work for a client is billed), its time is billed, or not billed (`X-SIOUL-BILLABLE` in the task, kept by CalDAV servers; greyed on Google Tasks).
- **As a spreadsheet**: the export button on the Time page writes a project's billable time as CSV: what (a line per task, or per note), hours, hourly rate, amount, then the total; this week, last week, this month, last month, all time, or from one day to another. In French, `;` between fields and a decimal comma, as French spreadsheets read them. Nothing else goes in: the full invoice is the invoice's (below). Time comes from the focus timer (a task's time counts for its project: the first of its projects that is for a client, else its first project) and from what is noted by hand (`$XDG_DATA_HOME/sioul/time/<month>.toml`, sessions with `project`, `unbilled`, `invoice`: the number that billed it, a mark made again from the issued records before each issue, "Invoices" below). Any stretch not billed, timed or noted by hand, changes with a click: its task, its project, its day, from when to when, what it was (`qml/TimeDialog.qml`; moved to another month, it is written there first); its right click takes it out. Billed time stays as it was billed.

## Invoices
Invoices, credit notes and deposit invoices you issue (`crates/sioul-core/src/invoice/`: `record.rs`, `life.rs`, `series.rs`, `totals.rs`, `presets.rs`, `ids.rs`, `html.rs`; client profiles in `clients.rs`). The invoicing's phase 1 (November 2026) built the model and its life, and its phase 4 the books made from it ("The books", below), described here. Later: the issued file, a PDF/A-3 carrying its Factur-X XML, kept beside its record with its hash (phase 2); the window's Money ▸ Invoices, the editor and Settings ▸ Invoices by country (phase 3). What the design rests on: [research/invoicing.md](research/invoicing.md), findings 76 to 80 of [research.md](research.md).

- **One record, in EN 16931's terms** (`record.rs`, `format = 2`): each field is one of the European standard's business terms (BT-xx), so that one record prints its page in any language and becomes an electronic invoice without translation. Its kind is UNTDID 1001's code (BT-3): 380 an invoice, 381 a credit note, 386 a deposit invoice. It holds its series and number, its issue day, the service's day or period (`period_from`, `period_to`: the first and last days of the time billed, the days given, else the day of issue; service-public.gouv.fr, sheet F31808, and ann. II CGI art. 242 nonies A I-10°), its due day and terms, its currency (ISO 4217) and language, its project, its client as chosen (`client_ref`), the issuer frozen as it was at issue and the client as the draft held it, its lines, its totals, the invoice it corrects (BG-3), the deposit invoices it deducts, the client's purchase order (BT-13), its notes with their subject codes (UNTDID 4451) and the late-payment rate it printed (in basis points); `file`, `file_sha256` and `record_sha256` wait for phase 2's kept file. Its payments and the day it was sent are not in it: they are its life, kept apart ("Its life, outside the record", below).
- **Where it stands is read, never typed** (`Invoice::status`, `left`, `credited`, `left_to_credit`): draft, issued, sent, partly paid, paid, credited, from its number, the day it was sent, its payments and the credit notes that cite it. A credit note has a status of its own, "credits invoice 2026-005" (`Status::CreditNote`): it is never paid nor unpaid. Partly paid needs a payment: an invoice credited in part and not paid is issued (or sent), with less left to pay. An invoice whose deposits cover it all, nothing left due, reads paid. Nothing is "overdue": what is unpaid is "not paid yet".
- **Lines** (BG-25): a description and details; a quantity in thousandths of a UN/ECE unit (Recommendation 20: `HUR`, `MIN`, `DAY`, `C62`, `MON`, `KMT`, `LS`); a unit price in cents for `base` units (BT-149); a discount; a VAT category (UNTDID 5305: `S`, `Z`, `E`, `AE`, `K`, `G`, `O`) and its rate in basis points; an exemption's reason; goods or a service; the amount; the time it bills, by session. **Lines from time** come as before, one per task (or per note, for time noted without a task), in the order first worked on, as minutes at the project's hourly price with a base of 60, so that 25 minutes at €60 an hour make €25.00 exactly, as format 1 computed it. **The time billed is what the issued records list** (`life::billed`, `with_billing`): a session an invoice lists, and no credit note freed since, is billed by it whatever its own mark says; the sessions' `invoice` marks are a cache, made again from the records before each issue (`life::reconcile_marks`), so that a crash between a record and its marks never lets the same time be billed twice.
- **Totals** (`totals.rs`), computed once in integer cents as EN 16931 checks them (BR-CO-10 to 17): each line to the cent, half away from zero (Factur-X §7.1.8); per category and rate its base and its tax; then without tax, the tax, with tax, the deposits deducted (BT-113) and the amount due (BT-115). No float in a sum: quantities are thousandths, rates basis points; a project's rate, still a float in `sioul-projects.toml`, becomes cents at that boundary.
- **Drafts** in `$XDG_DATA_HOME/sioul/invoices/drafts/<id>.toml`, beside the records, so that they travel with them: a draft has no number, and its id is when it was made and six letters (`20261116-143005-k3x9qa`), never a number: its file's name, letters, digits and dashes only, never what the file says (`draft_id_ok`). It changes freely (`life::change_draft`, which the editor of phase 3 calls): `life::refresh` takes the issuer as the settings say it, each line's tax where it is due ("Where VAT is due", below), the legal lines the preset prints for this client, the late-payment rate, the due day and the totals, and, when nothing says the service's day (no time billed, no day given), the day of issue, shown on the draft and printed (`period_default`; issued, the issue's own day); your own notes stay. A draft is made from a project's time not billed yet (`life::from_time`), for a client (`life::for_client`), blank, as a credit note (`life::credit`), or, in the core only for now, as a deposit invoice (`life::deposit`).
- **Checked before issue** (`life::checks`): what the preset asks and the draft lacks, in plain words and in the invoice's language. Required items refuse the issue; recommended ones are said only ("The client's SIREN: asked on invoices to French businesses from September 2027."). Identifiers are checked by their keys (`ids.rs`: a SIREN and a SIRET by Luhn, La Poste's own rule too; a VAT number by its own country, the two letters it starts with, never by the issuer's: a French one by its key, another member state's, the United Kingdom's and a Swiss UID by their form, an unknown prefix taken as written, `ids::vat_valid`). « en cours d'attribution » as the SIRET, or an Other identifier saying « SIRET en cours d'attribution », stands for it while it is being given (service-public F31808), and prints « SIRET en cours d'attribution ». The checks take the issued records too: a credit note is checked against what is left to credit ("Credit notes", below). A table that issued invoices before may now be refused where its preset requires what it lacks (your SIREN, a French business client's address, your legal form: "Older tables", below): a change of behaviour, said here.
- **Issued all or nothing** (`life::issue`): checked first; refused, in words, on a device whose sharing is on but leaves out the `drafts` part, which carries the invoices ("Turn on Drafts and invoices in Sharing to issue invoices on this device, or issue them on the device that has it."), since the others' numbers may not be known there; refused when some of its time was billed meanwhile, as the records say it (the sessions' marks made again from them first). Then, under the numbering's lock: numbered, its issuer frozen as the settings say it that day and its client as the draft holds it (`life::issue` takes no client: a credit note's is the credited invoice's, copied as it was), kept as `invoices/<number>.toml` (never over another file: of two numbers that make one file name, "F:1" and "F/1", the second is refused), this device's series journal written; its time marked (a credit note's cancelled time freed); for an invoice in euros, the ledger's money, a planned line in the project's budget, tied to `sioul:invoice/<number>` and due on its due day (an invoice in another currency is expected in no budget, and no bank line in euros ever joins it); for a credit note, the credited invoice's planned line lowered by its amount instead, taken out when nothing is left of it (`budget::lower_planned`; a line already paid stays as it is); the draft taken out. A step that fails undoes those before it, a lowered line put back too (`budget::restore_lowered`). **Make the invoice** on the Projects page stays one click: a draft of the project's time, issued at once; refused, it says what is missing and leaves no draft behind (`crates/sioul-app/src/projects.rs`, `make_invoice`).
- **Numbers** (`series.rs`): a pattern and its reset make a sequence. A pattern holds `{YYYY}`, `{YY}`, `{MM}`, and one counter, `{N}` or `{N:k}` (k digits at least), the rest as written; `reset = "yearly"` (then the year is in the pattern) or `"never"`. `[invoice] pattern` when set, else the older `prefix`: empty, `{YYYY}-{N:3}` from 1 again each January (2026-001); a prefix of your own and three digits, going on from the last whatever the year (F-041, F-042). Each number is one more than the largest of the series journal, the records (those that no longer read too) and every device's claim (`series::next`). The journal keeps each sequence's last number and its day, each device in its own file, `invoices/series/<device>.toml`, which no other device writes (so the sync never makes a conflicted copy of it, and an older Sioul, which lists only the folder's top files, never takes it for an invoice), read as the largest of every device's (the single `invoices/series.toml` of phase 1's first builds is read too); written in the same step as the record, under the numbering's lock: a record deleted by hand no longer frees its number, and a number of the journal's range with no record here is said ("2026-004 has no record on this device"; when the sequence's numbers hold a month, or a year it never restarts on, by its counter, "F2026{MM}-{N:3}: number 4 has no record on this device", `series::missing`), never given again. The lease's claim carries each sequence's last number too (`sioul_sync::lease::Claim::series`, from the journals at each renewal, right after each issue, the window's as the command line's, and when the window closes, `lease::close_with`; [database.md](database.md#leases-one-computer-at-a-time)), so that a device whose records have not come yet never gives one of them again. The window refuses to number when sharing is set up but its key cannot be read (a keyring locked: the others' claims cannot be read either), as the command line does ("Your sharing folder cannot be written now…", `invoice-unchecked`). Records list in their natural order, 2026-999 before 2026-1000.
- **Credit notes** (`life::credit`): a draft citing the invoice's number and day (BG-3), with all its lines (to cancel it) or some (to correct them), each line keeping the tax it was billed with, its exemption's words too (`Line::own_tax`), should the scheme have changed since; in the invoices' series, unless `credit_pattern` gives them one of their own; no payment terms and no late-payment lines. Never beyond what is left to credit: a credit note for more than the invoice's amount less the credit notes issued for it is refused before issue, naming them (`Invoice::left_to_credit`, a required check), so that an invoice is never credited twice. Issued, it settles what it credits: an invoice credited whole reads "credited", one credited in part has the rest left to pay. It reads "credits invoice 2026-005", never paid nor unpaid: the project's page shows it with no Paid box (by the row's `kind`), and a payment on it is refused (`add_payment`, `pay_rest`). It frees for billing again the time of each line it cancels whole, and all of the invoice's time when it credits the whole invoice (a format-1 invoice credited whole: all its time). Issuing it lowers the credited invoice's planned budget line by its amount, and takes the line out when nothing is left of it (above, "Issued all or nothing"), so that the client's next transfer is never paired with a credited invoice.
- **Deposits and final invoices** (`life::deposit`, `cite_deposits`), in the core only for now: a deposit invoice (386) bills an amount up front, excluding tax; a final invoice cites the deposit invoices it deducts, by number, day and amount with tax, and its amount due is what is left. No command, agent's tool or window makes either yet: they come with the editor's "Deposit…" (phase 3).
- **Payments**: each `{date, amount, means, movement}`, `movement` the address of the bank line's movement it came from, empty when said by hand; never more than is left to pay (`life::add_payment` refuses it, in words), never on a credit note. **Paid**, on the Projects page or the invoice's card in the agenda, writes a payment of what is left, by transfer, dated today (`life::pay_rest`), and its budget line follows ("Budget", below); **Undo** takes that payment back; **Not paid** takes back the payments said by hand (a format-1 record's own `paid` too), never those the bank's matching wrote (`life::unpay`). **From the bank**: a bank line that the matching joins to the planned lines of invoices, when an export is taken in, pays them (`movements::acts::resolve`, `life::take_bank_line`): one invoice, or several when one transfer pays them together, split across them, the oldest first, each up to what it has left, by transfer, on the bank's day, with the movement's address, and what remains is said as paid beyond what they asked; a payment said by hand that no bank line backed yet takes this line in as its evidence (the bank's day and movement), never gaining a second payment; nothing is written on a credit note, on an invoice with nothing left to pay, nor on an invoice in another currency than the bank line's, which is then not kept as joined to it either. Once per movement. The window says it in the status line with Undo, which takes those payments back and keeps the bank line apart from the invoices' line from then on (`movements::acts::not_same`), and nothing asks to confirm it ([accounting.md](accounting.md#payments-on-your-invoices)). Payments add up: one smaller than what is left makes the invoice partly paid.
- **Its life, outside the record** (`invoice::lives`): an issued record is never written again, so that its file (and phase 2's kept PDF, with their hashes) stays as it was issued. What happens to it later is written in `invoices/life/<device>.toml`, each device's own file, which no other device writes: its payments (`[[payment]]`: id, invoice, date, amount, means, movement), a bank line taken in as a hand payment's evidence (`[[evidence]]`: the payment, the movement, the bank's day), the day it was sent (`[[sent]]`), and removals (`[[removed]]`: an Undo, a "Not paid"), each naming by its id the entry it takes back, whichever device wrote it. Reading merges every device's file by union, a removal winning over what it removes (`lives::Lives::load`; `all_in` gives each record its life): payments said on two devices before they exchange both stay, and since each file has one writer, the sync never makes a conflicted copy of a record or of its life. A second file holding a number another holds (a sync app's conflicted copy, a copy made by hand) is left aside and said in the log: one record per number, the file its number names (`records_in`). Life writers take the device, the sharing's own id (`Numbering::device`, `add_payment`, `take_back`, `pay_rest`, `unpay`, `take_bank_line`).
- **Format-1 records** (before November 2026, no `format` key) read as issued invoices of kind 380 with time lines (`record::V1`): their SIRET an identifier, their VAT line the franchise's mention (category `E`), or no VAT (`O`) without one. They are never written: their own `paid` reads as a payment from before, of their whole amount (`older:<number>`), which "Not paid" or an Undo takes back with a removal entry, and an older Sioul ticking Paid changes only that word; their payments and the day they were sent go into the life files as any record's. They print as they always did, in the window's language. A record that no longer reads still holds its number, and is never written over.
- **Its own language and currency**: an invoice keeps its language (`en` or `fr`: the client profile's, else `[invoice] language`, else the window's) and its currency (the profile's, the settings', the preset's, else euros), prints in them whatever the window speaks, and shows its amounts in its currency in every view (the project's page, the agenda's dated card, `sioul invoices`, the agents), where a euro sign showed before whatever the currency.
- **The page and the PDF**: the record's page (`html.rs`) prints the issuer with its mark and identifiers, the client with its identifiers and delivery address, the nature of the operation, the date or period of the service, each line's quantity, unit and unit price, the VAT per rate whenever the invoice carries VAT (liable, or a credit note of an invoice from when you were, whatever your scheme on its day), the totals, the deposits deducted, the legal lines and how to pay. The window prints it to an A4 PDF through Qt's text document and PDF writer, in `[invoice] folder`, else `~/Documents/Invoices` (Factures in French); "Print again" renders the record again, until phase 2 keeps the file issued.
- **Budget**: the amount due of an invoice in euros, the ledger's money, is expected in the project's budget, a planned line on the due day (the terms: the client profile's, the settings', the preset's, else 30 days), tied to `sioul:invoice/<number>` (an invoice in another currency is expected in no budget, since the ledger does not convert yet); a credit note lowers the credited invoice's line, or takes it out ("Credit notes", above); ticking "Paid" dates it today and makes it real, with "Undo" for ten seconds; unticked, it is planned again on the day it had, its due day when that is not known (`budget::unsettle_line`), where it was in the ledger, so that Paid then not paid leaves the budget as it was.
- **From the command line** (`crates/sioul-cli/src/invoices.rs`): `sioul invoices [list] [--client <words>] [--year <year>] [--status <status>]` (drafts first, then the invoices, newest first, each with where it stands; a record that no longer reads, and a number with no record here, said), `show <number or draft>` (its lines, totals and payments, its client and whether it is billed as a business, a person or a public body, the service's day or period, and for a draft what it still lacks), `export [--year <year>]` (every invoice as CSV, in French with `;` and decimal commas, each text cell through the time spreadsheet's guard, `timereport::csv_cell`, so that a client's name never runs as a formula) or `export --html <number>` (its page to print), `draft <project>` (a draft of the project's time not billed yet; nothing numbered), `issue <draft>` (you at the keyboard, under the window's rules: one device numbers at a time, never one whose sharing leaves invoices out or whose sharing key cannot be read; with sharing on, only while this computer's window holds a live, settled claim on invoices, else "Open Sioul on this computer first…" (`invoice-cli-open-sioul`), since a claim of the command line's own would lapse unseen when it ends; right after issuing, it renews that claim with the journal's last numbers, `claim_numbers`; the draft's client issued as the draft holds it), `credit <number> [--line <n>]…` (a draft credit note, all lines or those given, never beyond what is left to credit), `pay <number> [--day <day>] [--amount <amount>] [--means transfer|card|cash|cheque|direct-debit|other] [--received <amount>]` (a payment received; what is left when no amount is given, never more; for an invoice in another currency than the books', `--received` says what reached the person in the books' currency), `received <number> <amount> [--day <day>]` (the same, said afterwards: "The books", below).
- **Agents**: `invoices` reads drafts and invoices, `draft_invoice` writes drafts only, when you ask; neither issues, sends nor records a payment; `books` reads the books ([mcp.md](mcp.md)).

### Countries: presets as data
- **One file per country**, `crates/sioul-core/data/invoice-presets/*.toml`, built into Sioul (`presets.rs`): France (`fr`), Germany (`de`), Belgium (`be`), the United Kingdom (`uk`), Switzerland (`ch`), and `elsewhere` for any country (no legal claims: every identifier and line your own). Spain (`es`) and Italy (`it`) are listed as not supported, each with why (certified, chained invoicing software, VERI\*FACTU; the state's exchange system, SdI): issuing under them is refused with that sentence, rather than a template offered that would be illegal there. No country has a code path of its own.
- **What a preset says**: its labels in English and French; its legal forms (and the mark a form prints after the name), its income regimes; the identifier kinds its invoices carry and how each is checked; what is required or recommended before issue (of the issuer or the client; for business, consumer or public clients; where the client is, at home, in another EU member state or abroad; for goods or services; under which scheme and legal form; from or until a day), with the sentence said when it is missing; its VAT schemes, the franchise's category, reason code and dated mention, its dated rates, exempt lines; where a line's VAT is due (`[[tax.place]]`, below); its legal lines with their EN 16931 subject codes, each with its source in the file's comments; its late-payment terms, its payment terms, its activities, thresholds and contribution rates, each dated, and the rules of its declarations of turnover and of its declaration of services to businesses in other member states (France's `[declaration]` and `[des]`), which the books read ("The books", below).
- **Three settings apart**: the legal form (France: an entrepreneur individuel, micro-entrepreneur included, or EURL, SASU, SARL, SAS, SA; the other presets: a sole trader or a company), the income regime for the books (France: micro or réel), and the VAT scheme, dated (`none`, not registered; `franchise`, the small-business exemption with its mention; `liable`), since none implies another: an EI micro-entrepreneur liable for VAT prints « EI » and VAT per rate; a company under the franchise prints the franchise's mention, its form and its capital. Sioul prints « EI » after a French entrepreneur individuel's name (C. com. R526-27), whatever the VAT, unless the name says it already, at its start, at its end, or in words (`life::marked_name`). A French company's capital ("SASU au capital de 1 000 €") and its RCS city are required; an EI registered as a trader (activity `goods-bic` or `services-bic`) is advised to give its RCS city. A VAT scheme the preset does not offer (`none` under France, where every business is under the franchise or liable) is refused by a required check, never taken as said.
- **Where VAT is due, as data** (`life::placed`, at each `refresh`; `default_tax` for a new line): the preset's `[[tax.place]]` rules, each for some VAT schemes, client zones (domestic, another EU member state, abroad), client kinds and natures (goods or a service), give a line its category, its reason code and its words; the first that applies wins (the review of phase 1, F4). France: a service to a business or a public body in another member state, under the franchise or liable, `AE` with `VATEX-EU-AE` and "Autoliquidation" ("Reverse charge" on an invoice in English), both VAT numbers required; liable, goods to such a client, `K` with `VATEX-EU-IC` and "Exonération, art. 262 ter I du CGI", both VAT numbers required (research-fr §10.4); a service to a business or a public body outside the EU, under either scheme, `O` with "TVA non applicable – article 259-1 du CGI" (the practice the research found, §10.3); liable, goods exported outside the EU, `G`, whose words the research does not give: a required check asks the line's own. Germany and Belgium: a service to a business in another member state takes the reverse charge under either scheme, "Reverse charge" ("Autoliquidation" on an invoice in French), Germany asking both VAT numbers under the small-business exemption too (Directive 2006/112/EC art. 226(3)-(4)); liable, their intra-EU deliveries of goods, services to a business outside the EU and exports are placed too, as are, in the United Kingdom and Switzerland, a liable issuer's services to a foreign business and exports. Where the research gives no words for a placement, a required check says why and asks the line's own. With no rule: not registered, `O`; the franchise, `E` with its mention (France: "TVA non applicable, article 293 B du CGI", reason code `VATEX-FR-FRANCHISE`), and no VAT ever shown (art. 293 E CGI); liable, `S` at the preset's standard rate (a service to a consumer abroad too). A line's own tax (`Line::own_tax`, with its exemption's words and reason code: yours, or a credit note's, kept from the invoice it credits) stays as set. The client's zone comes from its country ("Clients", below): an address without a country is domestic, and a business with no address at all is asked its country by a required check, in the presets that place VAT by it. The rates shipped (France 20, 10, 5.5 and 2.1 %; Germany 19 and 7 %; Belgium 21, 12 and 6 %; the United Kingdom 20, 5 and 0 %; Switzerland 8.1, 2.6 and 3.8 %) were not part of the research, but for Switzerland's 8.1 %: they are the rates as known in mid-2026, each file says so, and they are to be checked.
- **France's lines**: for business clients only, "Escompte pour paiement anticipé : néant.", the late-payment penalties at the rate kept with the invoice (by default the ECB's main refinancing rate in force on 1 January or 1 July, plus 10 points: 12.40 % for an invoice of the second half of 2026), and the €40 recovery indemnity (C. com. L441-9, L441-10, D441-5); none of them for a consumer, nor on a credit note, nor for a public body, whose late payments follow the Code de la commande publique, which the research did not read (your own lines there). A late-payment rate of your own below three times the legal interest rate, and terms over 60 days to a business, are said before issue. Two late-payment rates never: when your payment details speak of late fees (penalties, an indemnity, recovery costs) and Sioul prints its own lines, a required check asks you to keep one, your words out of the details (and your rate as `penalty_rate`, when it differs), or Sioul's lines off (`[[invoice.line]]` `penalties` and `indemnity`, `off = true`). Liable for VAT with that option, "Option pour le paiement de la taxe d'après les débits"; your professional insurance, when you give it.

### The books
What the law asks a business to keep and to say of the money it receives, made from the payments of the invoices it issued and, for what no invoice describes, from the work budgets that declare ([accounting.md](accounting.md#the-declaration)): the receipts book, the declarations of turnover, the VAT summary, the thresholds watched and the declaration of services to businesses in other member states (`crates/sioul-core/src/books/`: `mod.rs`, `declarations.rs`, `vat.rs`, `thresholds.rs`, `des.rs`; the window's part in `crates/sioul-app/src/books.rs` and `ledger.rs`, the command line's in `crates/sioul-cli/src/books.rs`). Built in the invoicing's phase 4 (November 2026). Money counts as the law counts it: received, on its day (finding 79). Rates, thresholds and the rules of declarations are each preset's dated data (`presets.rs`: `Contribution`, `Threshold`, `DeclarationRule`, `DesRule`), so that a release updates them, and each is overridable in `[invoice]` (below). The books prepare and estimate: nothing is declared, filed or paid from Sioul. Nothing says "late": a day to declare by is a day, before or after it. What they rest on: findings 79 and 80 of [research.md](research.md), §6 to §8 and §10 of [research/invoicing.md](research/invoicing.md).

- **Payments read in one place** (`books::received_of`): each issued record's own payments, as `invoice::all_in` gives them their life ("Its life, outside the record", above), each signed: what an invoice or a deposit invoice received, positive; a credit note's entries, money given back, negative (refunds). The receipts book, the VAT summary and the franchise's count read payments through it alone, never the life files, and a payment's turnover excluding VAT through `invoice::life::received_without_tax` (its share of the record's total without tax). An entry whose day does not read is left out.
- **The receipts book** (`books::rows`, `book`; C. com. L123-28; CGI art. 50-0, 102 ter and 286; BOI-BIC-DECLA-30-30 §20–30): one row per payment received, in the order of the days, each on the day it was received: the client (the invoice's, never the bank's words), the nature of what was sold (goods, services or both, as the invoice's lines say), the invoice's number, the means of payment in words (bank transfer, card, cash, cheque, direct debit, other; "not said" when the payment says none), cash said apart, what was received, and the turnover excluding VAT, divided as the record's lines are by activity ("Activity and nature", below). A refund paid back on a credit note is a row of its own, negative, on its day: it lowers the totals of its own period, never the period of the payment it gives back. Totals of each quarter the days asked touch (from the quarter's first day until the last day asked) and of each year (from its 1 January), VAT included, excluding it, sales and services apart: "Fourth quarter 2026: €2,080 received."; under VAT, "Fourth quarter 2026: €600 received with VAT, €500 excluding it."; a total stopped before its period's end is named until its last day ("Fourth quarter 2026, until 31 October 2026: €2,080 received.", `Total::until`).
- **Activity and nature** (`books::line_activity`, `Split`; the review of phase 4, P4-13): each line of a record is declared in an activity, apart from its nature. Its nature (goods or services) says when its VAT is due and where; its activity says its contribution rates and the threshold it counts toward, as sales or as services. The micro regime's sales are goods, food sold to take away or eaten on the spot, and lodging (C. com. L123-28, after CGI art. 50-0; service-public F32353), so a meal served or a night's lodging is a service for VAT and a sale for URSSAF and the thresholds. A line's activity is its own (`activity` on the line, `goods-bic`, `services-bic`, `bnc` or `cipav`), else the issuer's when it has one only, else the first activity said of its nature, else the first said. A record's payments divide by its lines' activities, each part its share of the record's amount without VAT (`Split::divide`); a receipt no invoice describes goes to the first activity said.
- **Every euro once, with the budgets that declare** (`receipts::declares`; the review of phase 4, P4-3 and P4-12): a receipt no invoice describes still comes from such a budget's movements, as the receipts register read them (`receipts::register_rows`: at what the client paid, on the day it was paid at its processor; its client the movement's words, its means the processor's name, a transfer, or "written by hand"). Every euro of such a movement counts once (`Moved`): a movement an invoice's payment came from (its `movement`) stands for that payment, up to its amount (a carried payment of the same amount first, as in a processor's payout); what it brought beyond the payments it stood for (an overpayment, a payout's other payments) is a receipt of its own in its budget, noted "beyond invoice 2026-031", no invoice describing it; a payment said by hand whose amount a movement tied to the same invoice shows is that movement, and a format-1 record's own "paid", which had no day, takes the movement's day; a movement the matching tied to an invoice with no payment written stays, with the invoice's client and nature. A receipt no invoice describes is cash when its words say so ("cash", « espèces », « liquide »: `cash_said`), an invoice's payment by its means. Under VAT, the amount excluding VAT of a receipt no invoice describes is not known: its row has none, and the totals read "at least".
- **In another currency** than the books' (the preset's: euros in France, Germany and Belgium, pounds in the United Kingdom, francs in Switzerland; else the settings' `currency`; else euros): a payment counts at what reached the person in the books' currency, said once (`books::book_payment`, `[[booked]]` in this device's life file, from `sioul invoices received` or `pay --received`; the last said counts, whichever device said it), its turnover excluding VAT the same share of it. Once said, the movement of that amount in a budget that declares, within 14 days of the payment's day (`BOOKED_DAYS`; the one tied to its invoice first), is that payment and counts no more (P4-3). Until it is said, its row shows the amount in its own currency and counts in no total, declaration nor threshold, and wherever an amount would be said, what waits is said instead (P4-9): the declaration, "From 1 October 2026 to 31 December 2026: $1,000 received: say what it came to in euros; to declare by 1 February 2027." (never "declare €0"), or, beside euros, "$1,000 received: say what it came to in euros (sioul invoices received)."; the Porch's row "at least" its euros, the sentence under it; the Overview's line the same; the book "One payment in USD is not counted…". Sioul converts nothing at a rate of its own.
- **As a spreadsheet and as a page** (`books::csv`, `html`): CSV with its header in the person's language: the day (2026-10-05), the client, the nature, the invoice, the means, a cash column, what was received, the amount excluding VAT when some row bears VAT, goods and services apart when the book holds both, and a note (an amount in another currency, a gross not known yet); each quarter's total after its last day's rows, the quarters without rows after, the years last; amounts with the language's decimal mark, and `;` between cells where it is a comma ("Jour;Client;Nature;Facture;Moyen de paiement;Espèces;Encaissé;…"); every text cell through the spreadsheet's guard (`timereport::csv_cell`), so that a client's name never runs as a formula. The page, "Receipts book of Camille Exemple, 1 October 2026 to 31 December 2026" (`[invoice] name`, else "your business"), says the totals, then the rows and the totals; the window prints it to PDF, as it prints invoices.
- **Declarations of turnover** (`declarations.rs`), where the preset asks them under the income regime (`Settings::declares`; France's micro regime, to URSSAF: the preset's `[declaration]`; none under the réel, none in the other presets): per period, each quarter or each month (`[invoice] declare`; unsaid, as a budget that declares says, else the preset's first, the quarter), the turnover received excluding VAT, one line per activity said (`[invoice] activity`), zero lines too. Each payment's parts go to their lines' activities ("Activity and nature", above), a receipt no invoice describes to the first activity said. The contributions are estimated at the rates of the day each payment was received (`Settings::rate_on`: a rate of the person's own that holds that day, the latest; else the preset's for the regime, the latest in force): France's from 1 January 2026 (autoentrepreneur.urssaf.fr), social contributions of 12.3 % for sales of goods (BIC), 21.2 % for commercial or craft services (BIC), 25.6 % for other services (BNC) and 23.2 % for a regulated liberal profession (CIPAV); the versement libératoire, when chosen (`vl`), 1 %, 1.7 %, 2.2 % and 2.2 %; the training contribution (CFP) on the whole, at the trade's rate, 0.1 % for commerce, 0.3 % for a craft, 0.2 % for a liberal profession. The day to declare by is the last day of the month after the period (`months_after`), moved to the next working day when it falls on a weekend (`next_working`: 31 January 2027, a Sunday, gives Monday 1 February); the declaration opens the day after its period. What it says (`declarations::sentences`): "From 1 October 2026 to 31 December 2026: €6,940 received excluding VAT, to declare by 1 February 2027.", "Other services, liberal (BNC): €6,940; contributions about €1,776.64 (25.6%). Income tax paid with them (versement libératoire): about €152.68 (2.2%).", the CFP's line, "Contributions estimated: €1,943.20."; a zero period, "Nothing received from 1 July 2026 to 30 September 2026: declare €0 by 2 November 2026." Periods before the books begin say nothing (`books::begin`: the day the activity started; unsaid, the first thing Sioul knows of, the first invoice issued, the first day a budget that declares runs from (`since`), its first receipt; while Sioul knows nothing, no period at all; the review of phase 4, P4-17): a person who says `regime = "micro"` in November is not told to declare €0 for quarters already declared elsewhere. Turnover in no activity said is counted, without contributions, and said; the CFP waits for the trade (`trade`; unsaid, the activity's when it tells one: sales of goods, commerce; BNC or CIPAV, a liberal profession; BIC services, which may be commercial or craft, tell nothing), and says so; payments in another currency not said yet are left out, and said ("In another currency", above); a receipt whose amount is not known makes it "at least". Nothing below zero is declared (`Declaration::declared`): an activity with more given back than received in the period declares €0 ("Other services, liberal (BNC): −€500, more given back than received: €0 declared."), no contribution estimated on it, and the head says what is not taken off ("From 1 October 2026 to 31 December 2026: €0 to declare by 1 February 2027; €500 given back beyond what was received is not taken off: Sioul carries nothing over to another period."): URSSAF's form takes no amount below zero, and Sioul's sources say nothing of refunds, so no rule of carrying over is guessed (P4-17). An estimate: URSSAF counts, Sioul prepares.
- **The first declaration** (`declarations::schedule`): with the day the activity started (`started`), nothing is declared before it, and the first declaration gathers the start's period and the ones after it, as many as the preset's `[declaration] first_periods` says (the review of phase 4, P4-1). France's: monthly, the start month and the three after, due the last day of the fourth month after the start month (a start in January, due 31 May; in March, 31 July; in October, the last day of February, 28 or 29); quarterly, the start quarter and the next (a start in the first quarter, due 31 July; on 12 April 2026, 31 October 2026). URSSAF's tables, justice.fr's page on declaring a micro-entrepreneur's turnover (a start on 6 March 2026, first declared by 31 July 2026; a quarterly start on 12 April 2026, by 31 October 2026) and propulsebyca.fr's monthly table all give it (finding 80). A monthly start on 15 January 2026 declares 15 January to 30 April by 1 June (31 May is a Sunday). Its key is its days (`2026-01-15..2026-04-30`), the others' their period's (`2026-Q4`, `2026-11`), the same on every device; a first declaration of one period, begun on its first day, is a period as any.
- **The VAT summary** (`vat.rs`), for an issuer liable to VAT, of the invoices issued liable (`issuer.scheme`) only: per period, each month (`vat_period = "quarter"`: each quarter), and per category and rate, the base and the VAT as each becomes due: on each payment received, its share, for services and for deposit invoices; on the invoice's day for goods (the law's delivery, which Sioul knows by the invoice), and for services too under the option for debits (`options = ["debits"]`), as each record printed it when it was issued (its issuer's copy, `issuer.options`), never as the settings say today, so that a summary already made never moves (the review of phase 4, P4-8); a deposit counts once (`vat::parts`; P4-2, P4-7): a deposit invoice that a final invoice cites pays for that invoice's lines, so it takes their nature and activity, its amount spread over them, and the final invoice holds only what its deposits did not; a deposit no final invoice cites yet keeps its own line's, as it was chosen when it was issued. Lines whose VAT is due elsewhere or not at all (the reverse charge `AE`, an intra-EU delivery `K`, an export `G`, outside the scope `O`, exempt `E`, zero-rated `Z`) are said with their base and no VAT ("Reverse charge (the client owes the VAT): €800, no VAT here."). A credit note takes its goods' VAT off on its day and its services' on the days its refund is paid back: VAT comes off only where it was due. A credit note on services of an invoice paid beyond what it asks once credited, its refund not written, is named and not counted until the refund is; one for what the client never paid needs no refund, lowers what is due and counts nothing (P4-10). An invoice in another currency than the books' is named and not counted. "October 2026: €140 of VAT collected, on €1,500 excluding VAT.", then "Sioul prepares your VAT return; it files nothing.": the return's period and day are not in Sioul's sources, so none is said. It counts as French law makes VAT due (research-fr §8.2); when VAT becomes due in the other presets' countries was not researched, and their liable issuers' summaries count the same way.
- **Thresholds** (`thresholds.rs`), each preset's, dated, each overridable (`[[invoice.threshold]]`): the VAT franchise's while the franchise holds (France's €37,500 and, higher, €41,250 for services, €85,000 and €93,500 for goods), the micro regime's ceilings under it (France's €83,600 for services and €203,100 for goods from 2026; €77,700 and €188,700 from 2023 to 2025, €72,600 and €176,200 in 2022, service-public.gouv.fr A16230, so that a second year above is seen in 2026 too; the review of phase 4, P4-6). Only the thresholds a preset marks `watched` are watched (P4-16): France's, whose counting is researched. Germany's small-business thresholds (€25,000 and €100,000) stay in its preset, not watched until research says how § 19 UStG counts the turnover, a founding year and what to say; the United Kingdom's and Switzerland's registration thresholds are not watched either. A threshold is watched when it names no activity or one of a nature the person sells (the activities said; none said, what the invoices sell; else services). **The franchise's count** is the calendar year's turnover excluding VAT of operations located in the country (BOI-TVA-DECLA-40-10-10 §180), each part sales or services by its line's activity ("Activity and nature", above): an invoice's goods on its day, its services on each of the receipts book's rows of that invoice, their share, so that a bank line tied to the invoice counts even when no payment was written on it (P4-14); a deposit's on its payments, as what it pays for, the final invoice only what its deposits did not (P4-2, P4-7); lines whose VAT is due elsewhere or outside its scope (`AE`, `K`, `G`, `O`) left out, so that services to a business in another member state never count, and a format-1 record (before November 2026, no category said, only its VAT mention in words) counted as located in France; a credit note's goods taken off on its day, its services on its refund's days; and the book's rows that no invoice in the books' currency describes (a budget's receipts, a payment in another currency at what reached the person), as the book has them. **The micro ceilings' count** is the turnover received in the calendar year excluding VAT, the receipts book's. France's goods thresholds count the whole turnover (`counts = "all"`: a mixed activity keeps its whole within €85,000 and €203,100, its services within €37,500 and €83,600; BOI-TVA-DECLA-40-10-10 §160, SP F32353), the others what their activities sell. **The first year**, when the activity started that year, each threshold the preset marks `prorated` is its amount × the days of activity / 365, to the euro, never above the whole (BOFiP's figures: a start on 12 June 2026, the higher thresholds €52,001 for goods and €22,942 for services, the base ones €47,274 and €20,856; URSSAF's: a start on 1 March 2026, the services' ceiling €70,087), and says so: "Prorated to your days of activity since 12 June 2026: €37,500 for a whole year." Where each stands (`thresholds::sentence`): "€30,100 counted this year against the VAT franchise's threshold for services, €37,500."

Past it, VAT applies from 1 January 2027."); on crossing it, to know (the franchise's: "VAT applies from 1 January 2027", the tax scheme to change then; a ceiling: "Your turnover passed the micro regime's ceiling", the regime staying this year, or, above it the year before too, "The micro regime ends on 1 January 2028", the real regime from then); on crossing a higher threshold, to do ("VAT applies from today, 14 October 2026": the tax scheme to switch and, where the preset says so (`corrective`), "invoices to businesses since then get a corrective invoice"; BOI-TVA-DECLA-40-10-20 §160 and §170). ## Budgets at their pace
A budget's verdict no longer compares a projection made only of what is scheduled with its target, which said "short" on the 3rd of every month. What is known in advance (presets, planned lines) counts in full; the rest, what simply happens, is weighed against the part of the period gone: ten days into thirty, a third of what the period still needs should have come in (or no more than a third of what it may spend should be spent). Within the one tolerance of every verdict, 5 % of what the period moves, it is on track ([accounting.md](accounting.md), "On track, without assuming money flows evenly").

The end of the period is estimated:
- with fewer than three past periods: the difference of today carried to the end (no extrapolation of three days into a month);
- with three or more: what is known, plus the median past period's unscheduled movements for the part left; the worst and best past periods give a range (the 10th and 90th percentiles from ten periods on).

A budget opens on its ledger (lines and recurring movements of the period, newest first), its balance by day, week, month or year (planned movements dotted), and "Add a movement": once, or each month or year (`[[preset]]`, written in place, comments kept).
