# Sites: secure mailboxes, chats, a dating site

Some mail never leaves its website: a bank's secure mailbox, the hospital's, the tax office's. Chats live in the browser too (Element, WhatsApp, Discord), and so does a dating site. Sites (Ctrl+4) keeps them pinned, logged in once, and turns their notifications into something that waits for you.

Sites are a stopgap: Sioul's encrypted mail exists so that such letters can come back to mail ([design.md](design.md#portals)).

## What a site is
Not an account: it holds no sign-in of Sioul's (the site keeps its own, in its profile). `[[site]]` in the configuration; files older than 4 October 2026 kept sites as `[[account]]` of kind "portal", read as sites and moved to `[[site]]` the first time Sioul starts (`config::migrate_sites_at_start`, a copy of the file kept as `config-before-sites.toml`, made once: a move that fails at each start never writes over it):

```toml
[[site]]
id = "element"
name = "Element"
url = "https://app.element.io"
site = "chat"            # its type, how it behaves: "mailbox" (the default: a secure mailbox, a client area), "chat", "video", "social", "dating", "other"
categories = ["Amis"]    # your own categories, any words ("Banque", "Santé"): the list filters by them
area = "leisure"         # what it is for: "work", "admin", "leisure", joined by "+"; unsaid, by its type (docs/areas.md)
microphone = true        # for calls; unsaid, chats, video calls and dating sites have them, no other site
camera = true
screen = true            # sharing the screen
realtime = false         # its notifications at once
muted = false            # its sounds silenced
background = true        # kept open to hear from it: chats by default
announced_by = []        # the domains whose mail announces it; the site's own domain by default
```

## The list
- **Your order**, kept in the configuration: a site's ⋮ ▸ Move up, Move down. Once your devices share each site apart (format 2, [database.md](database.md#the-format-of-what-travels)), a move also writes it as `site_order` (the sites' ids, in order), which reaches your other devices with the sites; a site it does not name comes after, in the file's order. Before that, the list travels whole, its order with it. The sort button above the list groups them **by type** instead (secure mailboxes and client areas, chats, video calls, social networks, dating, other), your order kept within each.
- **Filtered** (the funnel above the list): by what a site is for (work, your admin, leisure), by its type, by one of your categories, each with "any"; chosen again, a choice is undone. The filters in use are said under the title, with one button to show every site again.
- **Its own icon**: asked of the site itself, never of an icon service that would learn which sites you keep (`sioul_sync::favicon`: the icon its page names, the larger the better, else `/favicon.ico`), kept in `~/.cache/sioul/favicons/` and asked again after a week; its type's icon until then, and for a site that gives none.
- **The sites for these hours** first; the others fold under one line, "Other hours: 3" ([areas.md](areas.md)).
- **Changed in place**, from its menu: ⋮ for the site open, or a right click on any site of the list: name, address (https only), type and categories; its kind of thing; what it is for (work, admin, leisure, any of them together); the microphone, the camera, sharing the screen; the devices of calls; silenced, kept open; its place in your order; a link; Remove takes it out of Sioul (its sign-in stays in the profile until cleared). Sites are made, changed and removed here only, never in Accounts.
- **Tied to other things**: ⋮ ▸ Link to… ties a site to a task, a note, a contact, a project, as a correspondent is (`sioul:site/<id>`, kept by the other thing, or in `links.toml`); a link to it opens the site. The link picker finds sites by name and address.

## Presets
"Add a site" offers the sites people usually keep (about 400 on 4 October 2026), by country and, where it matters, by state or province (Québec in Canada; American utilities by state), and for everyone: offices (taxes, health insurance, family allowances, jobs, retirement, studies, papers and residence), banks, electricity and gas, phone and internet, chats, video calls, social networks (LinkedIn for work; Facebook, Instagram, Reddit, X, Bluesky, Mastodon… for leisure; Nextdoor for both), dating and friendship. Filtered by type or category, and found by a word ("ameli", "banque"); one click fills the name, the sign-in address, its type, its categories in your language, what it is for, and the domains whose mail announces it; or the fields are filled by hand. Your country is your language's (French in Canada shows Canada), and can be changed. Left out on purpose: services without a web version (Signal, Viber, Session, Hinge, Bumble now), retired ones (Skype).

"Usual sites ▾", beside "Pin a site", opens them as a menu: each country, then its groups (offices, banks, energy, phone and internet; chats, video calls, social networks, dating for everyone), then the sites; a country's states or provinces in a menu of their own ("By state", "By province"), each with its groups, or its sites at once when it has one group. Each country, state and group opens with "All of them (N)", which pins all of that group not pinned yet at once (a country's own, not its states'); a site already pinned is ticked and left as it is. Clicking a group's name opens it rather than pinning it all, so that nothing is pinned by a slip. Sites already pinned are known by their address (host and path), so two services on one host (canada.ca's accounts, a network and its messages) stay two.

The list is a plain JSON file anyone can update without building Sioul: `presets/sites.json` in the sources, `{ "tags": { "health": { "name": { "en": "Health", "fr": "Santé" } } }, "countries": { "CA": { "sites": […], "regions": { "QC": { "sites": […] } } } }, "international": […] }`, each site `{ "name", "url", "type", "tags", "area", "announced_by", "about", "calls" }`; a country may say what its regions are called in a menu (`"regions_name": { "en": "By state", "fr": "Par État" }`). Of the copy built in, one in `$XDG_DATA_DIRS/sioul/presets/sites.json` and yours in `~/.local/share/sioul/presets/sites.json`, the one with the latest `checked` date is used. `tools/check-presets.py` asks every address as a browser would and says, for each: ok; moved (it answers from another address: worth updating); blocked (it refuses robots: check by hand); broken (not found, no such host: fix it). It exits with 1 when one is broken, so it can run on a schedule; `--only FR`, `--json`.

## The browser
- **Qt WebEngine** (Chromium), started before the application (`cpp/webengine.cpp`).
- **One persistent profile** for every site (`sioul-sites`): cookies kept, so logins last; files cached on disk (512 MB at most).
- **A site's view** is made the first time it opens and kept for the session; a chat's at start, to receive its notifications. It goes only closed as a browser closes a tab: as Sioul quits, or when the site is taken out (below, "Closing").
- **Permissions**: notifications granted to every site before it asks, and kept (`persistentPermissionsPolicy: StoreOnDisk`, `profile.queryPermission(…).grant()`): Sioul catches them and gathers them (below), so no site asks for them again (Proton did, at each visit, while nothing was kept). The microphone, the camera and sharing the screen (you choose a screen or a window, or nothing) as each site's switches say: on for chats, video calls and dating sites, off for the others until you turn them on; a site asking while off is told in the status line, with where to turn it on; a yes kept from before is forgotten as soon as its switch is off. Geolocation never.
- **Sharing the screen** needs the view's `settings.screenCaptureEnabled`, set on every site's view and pop-up: Qt WebEngine keeps it off ("Disabled by default"), and then refuses every capture after the choice (Qt 6.11, `src/core/media_capture_devices_dispatcher.cpp`): you chose a screen, the call got `AbortError: Invalid state` (until 6 October 2026). The site's switch and your choice still decide. Checked by `tools/check-sites.py share`: a local page asks for the screen three times while Weston, headless, gives Xwayland a screen nobody sees; capture off, refused after the choice; on, a video track; the site's switch off, refused at once, the status line saying where to turn it on.
- **The devices of calls**: the camera, the microphone and the speaker chosen, given to every call and changed during one (below, "The devices of calls").
- **Colours**: every site and pop-up is shown in the screen's own colours, from its ICC profile, on X11 (Wayland and macOS convert by themselves), and "Calmer colours" (the Sites page's ⚙) softens loud colours on every computer: a shader over the view, through a lookup table made for each screen ([colour.md](colour.md)).
- **Pop-ups** (sign-in windows, call windows) open in a window of their own. Closed, by you or by the page once a sign-in is done, the window goes at once and its page closes behind it as a browser closes a tab.
- **Downloads**: nothing a site sends is saved before you say where, in a card over the site (below, "Downloads").
- The user agent leaves out Qt's name: some chats refuse browsers they do not know.

## On Android
Qt WebEngine has no Android version. Android's Sites page (`qml/android/SitesPage.qml`) is the computer's list, menu and forms, shared with `SitesPage.qml` (`SitesList.qml`, `SiteRow.qml`, `SiteMenu.qml`, `SiteEditDialog.qml`, `SiteAddDialog.qml`): a site added from the usual sites or by hand, changed, removed, its type and what it is for, the filter, the order, each site's icon and its news. What only Qt WebEngine carries is left out there: kept open, the call switches and the devices of calls, the page's ⚙; Choose a login is in the site's screen's menu instead (below, "A login from Bitwarden"). A site opens in a screen of Sioul's own around the system's web view (`android/package/src/com/aurelienpierre/sioul/SiteScreen.java`, its rules in `SiteWeb.java`), or in the browser. Phase 1 of the design of 19 November 2026; [android.md](android.md#sites) says what Android gives and what it does not.
- **Where it opens** is Rust's (`crates/sioul-app/src/siteview.rs`): Java says which web view the device has, its package and version (`WebView.getCurrentWebViewPackage`, verb `site-webview`); a site opens in Sioul from Chromium 120 on (`siteview::OLDEST`, December 2023: older engines are no longer mended and the sites people keep refuse them; Android 9's last web view, 138, passes), else in the browser, as an address that is not https does. The engine's own version decides, read from its user agent ("Chrome/138…", `WebSettings.getDefaultUserAgent`), the package's numbering being its maker's (the review's V4). A web view that draws pages inside Sioul's own process (`WebViewCompat.isMultiProcessEnabled` false, androidx.webkit's `MULTI_PROCESS`; Android does so on low-memory devices) sends sites to the browser too, since a page's crash there would end Sioul (`Opens::OneProcess`, the review's V1); the screen asks again before making its view. These two answers are asked on Android's main thread, three seconds at most, when a site opens or the card shows, and kept only when whole (V3); the list reads what was found, and loads nothing. Each site's line in the list says which, and why (`opens_line`). Settings ▸ Devices, this device's card, says the web view's version and what it cannot carry, in one line (`card_line`): a site's news needs a script before the page's own and a channel back (androidx.webkit's `DOCUMENT_START_SCRIPT`, `WEB_MESSAGE_LISTENER`), a site silenced `MUTE_AUDIO`, darker pages `ALGORITHMIC_DARKENING`, each asked of the web view (`WebViewFeature.isFeatureSupported`, once per process).
- **The screen**, one site at a time (another replaces it: `FLAG_ACTIVITY_CLEAR_TOP` over a live screen above the window; see "A login from Bitwarden" for one under it), over the window, in Sioul's task (one entry in Recents) and its process. A bar always in sight: back, the site's name and the host of the page shown, reload, a menu (what the site asked for and did not get, Fill from Bitwarden and Choose a login… where a Bitwarden account is set, Open in the browser, Share the page's address, Find in the page, Close this window for a pop-up, Close). The host is the committed page's (recorded by `doUpdateVisitedHistory` alone; `onPageCommitVisible`, which comes later and may name an older navigation, only draws the bar again: review of 22 November 2026, finding 1), never a navigation only asked for, in its ASCII form, cut on the left so that its end shows (the review of the extensions' sign-in, D1 and D2); "…" before a page has committed, "about:blank" for a blank page committed. The bar's two lines are always true (`SiteWeb.bar`, review N2 and N3): while the page shown is on the site's own domain, the site's name in bold over its host; on any other host (a redirect, a sign-in provider, a link wrapper, a tab or a pop-up, a blank page), that host in bold first and "from" the site's name under it, so that Sioul never lends the site's name to another domain's page. Back goes back in the page in front, then closes a pop-up, then the screen. Whatever brings Sioul up as its icon does (the icon, a notice, a dose's reminder, a share, the home screen's card, the Pause tile: each starts the launcher's intent with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_RESET_TASK_IF_NEEDED`) closes the screen first (`android:finishOnTaskLaunch`, which Android applies with that flag alone), so that the window shows at once on what was tapped and takes it as it becomes active (`backHere`); the site stays signed in, and Recents, which brings the task back without that flag, keeps it as it was (the review of 19 November 2026, L1). While the window asks for a fill, the screen is under it, the task's root, which Android spares: it stays, and comes back once the window has its answer (finding 11 of the review of 22 November 2026). A rotation, a split screen or a keyboard attached keeps the page (`configChanges`); the bar and the page keep out of the system's bars and above the keyboard (the window's insets from Android 11, `adjustResize` before). Its words are Rust's, in Sioul's language (`siteview::words`), its colours Sioul's.
- **One store for every site**: the web view's own, in the window's process (`app_webview/` in the app's private folder), as a computer has one profile; cookies written as the screen is left (`CookieManager.flush`); third-party cookies refused, unlike a computer's Qt WebEngine (the review's S2, the coordinator's choice): with one store for every site, what sites embed would follow the person from one to another; a sign-in through a provider moves the page itself and keeps its cookies. The page shown is kept in the screen's state and loaded again when Android made the screen anew after ending the process. Never in a backup (`allowBackup="false"`), never in a transfer to a new device (`res/xml/sioul_data_extraction_rules.xml`). The extensions' sign-in page never shares it: it runs in a process of its own whose web view keeps its own folders (`app_webview_signin/`), emptied at each sign-in, so that a sign-in never signs a site out and neither sees the other's cookies ([android.md](android.md#an-extensions-sign-in)).
- **Where a page goes** (`SiteWeb.where`): an https page loads, and a blank one; in a frame, a page-made (`blob:`) or inline (`data:`) one too. A link tapped (a gesture, not a server's redirect) to another domain than both the site's and the page's (`sioul_core::sites::domain_of`, the same in Java) opens in the browser; so does a new tab's first page (a window opened without a size, `isDialog` false: a link with `target="_blank"`) on another domain than the site's and its opener's, redirects included, and the empty tab goes (review N1); a sign-in moving through other sites by redirects and forms stays, and so does every https page of a pop-up the site opened with a size (`isDialog` true, as sign-ins open theirs). A tab with a page of its own is as the site's page. `mailto:` and `tel:` at a tap go to their apps; anything else is refused (http, files, content, intents, other schemes), an http link tapped opening in the browser.
- **What a page may do**: an upload through Android's file chooser (`onShowFileChooser`, several files where the page takes several), what it hands back checked before the page has it (`SiteWeb.chosen`, `mayUpload`; Android's reference for `FileChooserParams`: the result "must be treated as untrusted"): the chooser's types from the field's (an extension such as ".pdf" taken as its type, `acceptTypes`, where the web view's own intent would open nothing; the review's F5), said when no app lets one choose; another app's document (`content:`) only, never one of Sioul's own providers (its package's name or one under it, a user's number before `@` taken off) nor a `file:` path, which the web view would read as Sioul; only the answer to the request waiting (one request code each); a file to save or a folder (the chooser's other modes) not answered; a download asked about first, every time, a tap's included (the review's F4, and the owner's word of 19 November 2026): the question names the file (its name can be changed), its size when the site says it, the host it comes from, a line of caution for a program (`isProgram`), and where it goes, among the places Rust lists (`siteview::PLACES`) that the screen can save to (`SiteDownload.PLACES`): Downloads, or a folder chosen in Android's own "Save as" (`ACTION_CREATE_DOCUMENT`); Cancel saves nothing; one question at a time, while no other file is chosen or fetched, three files waiting at most, the others not saved and said once; then fetched by Sioul itself (`SiteDownload.java`, the review's F3 and F2), never through Android's download service, which would keep the site's cookies in its own database and send them on at each redirect: the web view's cookies for that address, sent to its host alone; a redirect followed on the same host only (`sameHost`, five at most); the answer checked before a byte is written (`verdict`: status 200, and no page where the web view had a file of another type unless the site calls it an attachment), 1 GiB at most with 200 MiB left free (`roomFor`); a line under the bar follows it (its share, else what came so far), with Stop, then Open once saved, or why not, with Open in the browser where the browser can save it; a file a form made (an address the screen saw sent a form's body in the last five minutes: `shouldInterceptRequest`, which answers nothing itself) or the page made itself (`blob:`, `data:`) cannot be asked for again, and the line says so at once; a window opened at a tap (`onCreateWindow` with a gesture; none without) shows over the page, one at a time, and goes at the page's `window.close()`; the camera, the microphone and the location are refused, the menu saying that the site asked (phase 2 asks you, site by site); full screen is not offered.
- **Safety**: https only, mixed content never; no file or content access from a page; no JavaScript interface, and one script call into a page, the fill's (below); the screen not exported, without an intent filter, its one extra checked before anything (`SiteWeb.Opening.of`: an https address with a host, an id and a name without control characters, a domain, colours, the words it shows); certificate errors refused (Android's default, not overridden); a page's console never logged, and no address nor cookie in a log line; the web view's own user agent, saying it is one ("; wv"), without the device's model and build (`SiteWeb.userAgent`, the review's S1): a site that refuses browsers inside apps goes through Open in the browser; the header that names Sioul's package to every site (`X-Requested-With`) stays, since the web view no longer lets it be removed (androidx.webkit's `setRequestedWithHeaderOriginAllowList` "no longer does anything"); the web view's diagnostics off (`android.webkit.WebView.MetricsOptOut`); the service workers' own settings without file or content access either (`ServiceWorkerController`, S3); an HTTP sign-in of the server's own refused and said, with Open in the browser, and a request for protected videos refused and named in the menu's line (S4). Safe Browsing is the web view's default and stays on: where the device has its maker's services, the addresses visited are checked against the maker's list; a device without them has no such check.
- **A login from Bitwarden** (21 November 2026): where a Bitwarden account is set on this device (`siteview::asked`'s `vault`), the screen's menu has **Fill from Bitwarden** and **Choose a login…**; without one, neither shows (the account is set in Accounts, not from a site). The screen asks Rust (`SiteScreen.nativeFill`, `crates/sioul-app/src/sitefill.rs`), in the window's process, with the address its web view committed for the page in front (the screen's own record from `doUpdateVisitedHistory`, never a value a page's script gave), https only. The vault open and one login for the site, made for its very host or chosen there last (the computer's rule, below in "Which login", and the same memory of the login chosen last, kept on each device), Rust builds the whole call at once: the computer's script, one constant (`sites::FILL_SCRIPT`), inside a guard that lets it run only in a document at the origin asked, its four values (the origin, the user name, the password, the code) quoted by Rust as JSON, the line separators and `<` escaped as well (`sites::fill_call`), so that nothing in a password can change it. The computer's Sites page takes the same whole call (`sioul.bitwardenFill`), since it has the same gap between asking for the login and running the script. Anything else goes through the window: the screen brings it forward in Sioul's task (`FLAG_ACTIVITY_REORDER_TO_FRONT` on the launcher's own activity, `singleTop`: the same window, never a second), which shows its own unlock card (the master password and a code: no security key on a phone) or its chooser (`SiteFill.qml`, made with the window on Android: the cards of a computer's Sites page and of the accounts' forms), then brings the site back (verb `site-fill-back`, `SiteScreen.comeBack`) by a start from the window's own activity, on its main thread, with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_REORDER_TO_FRONT` alone: until Android 10, that flag moves an activity only for a start from an activity of its task (review of 22 November 2026, finding 4). The window's activity is known from Android's activity callbacks (`SiteScreen.WindowWatch`; Qt's own `QtNative.activity()` is not public in Qt 6.11); the application's context, with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_NEW_TASK`, is the fallback. The login chosen waits in Rust as a one-use ticket, the address asked and the login's id for two minutes, never its password; the site, back in front, takes it with the address it shows then (`onResume`), and Rust reads the password from the vault only then, for the same scheme, host and port alone (`sitefill::same_origin`), while the vault is still open: else the screen says the page changed, the vault was locked meanwhile, or the login waited too long. Cancel brings the site back with nothing filled, nothing said. A card never outlives its request (finding 8): a screen that goes while its fill waits (closed, or replaced by another site), or one that finds no window's activity, tells Rust (verb `forget`), which drops the request and any login chosen for it and tells the window, whose card closes; a request also waits ten minutes from the tap, after which the card closes by itself and the site comes back, saying that no login was chosen in time. The master password and the code from another app fit in those ten minutes; the login chosen then waits two for its page. The first fill of a session flips to the window and back; later fills of a site with one login do not. A screen Android made again after ending Sioul loads Rust first (`DoseAlarms.load`, as the receivers do); the vault is locked then, and the request waits for the window, which reads it once it has started (`sitefill::started`, `site_fill_waiting`). A site opened is decided from the live screen of the process (`SiteScreen.live`, set as a screen is made and let go as it ends) and whether it is under the window (`isUnderWindow`: from bringing the window forward until it resumes), never from a fill waiting (review of 22 November 2026, finding 3): a live screen over the window is cleared (`FLAG_ACTIVITY_CLEAR_TOP`); one under the window is closed, and the new one opens over the window, since clearing down to it would close the window too; with none known, nothing is cleared: after Android ended Sioul while the window asked, a record of the screen may wait under the window, and clearing down to it would close the window and leave the site alone. Such a record, made again as its task's root, closes itself.
- **The one script call into a page** (the review of 19 November 2026 counted "no `evaluateJavascript`" among what holds; the fill adds exactly one, `SiteScreen.run`, and `SiteScreenCheck` counts them in all of Sioul's Java). Why it is safe: it goes one way, into the page, and nothing comes back (its result is not read; no JavaScript interface, no message listener or channel anywhere in Sioul's Java); the web view runs it in the main frame of the page shown, never in a frame of another site; it runs only while the page it was asked for is still in front at the scheme, host and port asked, checked again in Java just before (`SiteWeb.sameOrigin`, on the screen's record and on the web view's own `getUrl()`) as in Rust when the ticket was taken; and since both are copies the web view hands after the fact, the call checks again inside the page: its first statement compares the document's own `location.origin`, which no page can redefine, with the origin asked, and returns without touching anything when they differ (`sites::fill_call`; review of 22 November 2026, finding 1: a pop-up's opener could send it elsewhere while the window was in front). Its whole text is Rust's, the computer's script with values quoted there, so neither a page nor a password chooses what runs; the script is strict (`'use strict'`, finding 6), so a page's function it calls reads neither its caller nor its arguments. A page can do with it only what it could do with the password typed by hand: read the fields it lands in, which is why the address is checked twice. The password is in that call alone: never in an intent, a saved state, the log, the clipboard or a field kept by the screen. What is overwritten before it is freed: the login taken out of the vault (`sites::Login`), the call once quoted into Rust's answer, that answer as Java gives it back (`sioul_site_fill_free`), and main.cpp's UTF-16 copy. What is not: the texts made on the way (the quoting, the call growing as it is written, the zero added for C), Java's strings, which live until collected and which nothing here can shorten, and the web view's own copies (finding 9). While the vault is open, the password is in memory anyway.
- **Remove this site** forgets its sign-in on this device (`sites::remove`, `siteview::forget_line`, verb `site-forget`, `SiteWeb.forget`, on Android's main thread; the review's K1): where the web view can delete a site's data alone (androidx.webkit's `DELETE_BROWSING_DATA`, `WebStorageCompat.deleteBrowsingDataForSite`: cookies, cache and the pages' storage of the site's registrable domain), all of it, and nothing is said; else its cookies for its address and its domain, each set expired with its own attributes where the web view gives them (`GET_COOKIE_INFO`), and its address's storage (`WebStorage.deleteOrigin`), and a line says that its cache and what it kept under its other addresses stay until Sioul's storage is cleared in Android's settings. The question says so before ("and its sign-in on this device"). A site removed on another device and gone with the settings is not forgotten here; nothing in Sioul's Java ever empties every site's store: the extensions' sign-in screen alone empties one, its own, in its process apart (`SiteScreenCheck`, `SignInCheck`).
- **A web view that does not load after all** (turned off or updated between the list and the tap): the screen opens the site in the browser and closes, asking nothing more of the web view (`madeView`: the cookie manager is asked only once a view was made, and its failure is caught), so Sioul keeps running (the review's L5).
- **A drawing process that ends** (a page's crash, or Android taking its memory back: `onRenderProcessGone`): the screen's web views go; in front, the screen closes, said in one line; behind another app, nothing is said over it, and the page is made again when the screen comes back (the review's L2). Sioul stays.
- **A page loaded again** (after that, or in a screen Android made again after ending Sioul): the page kept, only when it is on the site's own domain, else the site's own address (`SiteWeb.again`), and a line under the bar says Android had closed it (L3): a form's page comes back empty, by a plain GET.
- **A page's dialogs** (`alert`, `confirm`, `prompt`, leaving with `onbeforeunload`) are Sioul's own (`pageDialog`): the page's host as their title, OK (and Cancel where the page asks), and Close this site always; one at a time, a second within a second of the last refused at once, so that no page holds the screen. A refused navigation is said once a page, as "encrypted pages only" for http and "no other application from a page" for any other address (N5, R2).
- **What "kept on this device alone" does not cover** (the review's K3): the page shown is kept in the screen's saved state, which Android holds in its memory and never in a backup (an address may hold a token); Android 16's transfer to another platform needs that platform's app named in the transfer rules, and Sioul names none (read, not tried); a site that keeps its sign-in in the page's session storage asks again each time its screen closes, since the screen's views go with it. A page's own picture is not shown in Recents (`setRecentsScreenshotEnabled(false)`, Android 13 and later), as a health card's is not; on Android 11 to 14 the system's bars take Sioul's colour, from 15 they are clear over the screen.
- **A notice about a site**: `sioul:site/<id>#<key>` is among the addresses an Android notice may open (`ReminderOpener.isSiteAddress`, `eventalarms::is_notice_address`): the window takes it as it comes up and opens the site's screen.

## The devices of calls
You choose them in a site's menu ▸ Devices for calls…, or, during a call, from the camera button in the row above the site, which shows while that site or a pop-up it opened has a call. Both list the camera, the microphone and the speaker from the system's own list (Qt Multimedia's: the same on Linux, Windows and macOS), read from a site's first call or the first time the list shows, since Qt Multimedia, started for it, loads FFmpeg and asks the graphics drivers what they decode; "The system's own" when unsaid (`[calls]` in the configuration). The choice is the same for every site, and a change applies at once to every call open in Sioul and to the next ones. The research behind it: [research/calls.md](research/calls.md).

- **Why a script.** Qt WebEngine 6.11 has no way to choose a site's devices or to change them under a call: it gives the device the page asked for, else the first of Chromium's list (`src/core/media_capture_devices_dispatcher.cpp`, `getDefaultDevices`), and a permission is only granted or denied. So the choice is made inside the page, by `crates/sioul-app/src/calls.js`, run in every page and frame of every site's view and pop-up, in the page's own world, before the page's scripts (`webFixesFor` in `SitesPage.qml`; Rust gives the file, `calls_script`).
- **A call keeps its tracks; Sioul changes what feeds them.** For each camera or microphone track, the page gets a relay: Chromium's `MediaStreamTrackGenerator`, fed from the real device's track through a `MediaStreamTrackProcessor`. Its label, settings, abilities, constraints, `stop`, `clone` and `muted` answer as the real device's; copies made by `MediaStream.clone()` are dressed too, and an `ImageCapture` is made on the real track. A change asks for a new real track with the call's own settings (those the page asked for, with what `applyConstraints` changed since; looser if the device cannot meet them, echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain kept), feeds the relay from it, and stops the old one. The site's mute button, its voice meter (Web Audio binds its source to the track it was given), its effects and hanging up keep working; swapping tracks with `replaceTrack` broke all four in the lab. Hanging up closes every real device: once the page has stopped every copy of a relay, its device stops.
- **Without the relay**, should a later Chromium drop `MediaStreamTrackGenerator` from pages, the script swaps the call's tracks in its senders (`replaceTrack`) and its streams instead, and what the page does to its old track (`stop`, `enabled`) reaches the new one; a voice meter or an effect may then stop. The check below would fail first.
- **Which device.** At a call's start, the device chosen in Sioul, found by its name (the browser's device ids are each site's own), asked for as `ideal`, so that a call still starts when it cannot open. Before a site was first allowed a device, the browser gives no names: the call is moved to the chosen device right after that first grant, before the page gets its tracks (until 8 October 2026, a site's first call ignored the choice). During a call, a device the page asks for by its id (its own menu) is given: the latest choice wins. With "The system's own", the page's own choice stands; on Linux the call then uses Chromium's "default" device, which PulseAudio and PipeWire move when the system's default changes (Chromium opens it with no device name and without `PA_STREAM_DONT_MOVE`: `media/audio/pulse/pulse_util.cc`).
- **The speaker**: given with `setSinkId` to every media element and audio context the page plays through, those playing by themselves (an `autoplay` element given a `srcObject`, which never calls `play()`: missed until 8 October 2026) included. An element the page sent elsewhere itself keeps that until the next change in Sioul.
- **The old callback forms** `navigator.getUserMedia` and `navigator.webkitGetUserMedia`, still in Chromium 140, go the same way.
- **A change reaches every frame**: `setCallDevice` dispatches a `sioul-devices` event in each frame of each view and pop-up (`everyFrame`: `mainFrame`, its `children`, `runJavaScript` in world 0), since a call may be inside a frame of another site, which the view's own `runJavaScript` never reaches. Each view and pop-up is also given its scripts again (`holder.fixes`, `SitePopup.webFixes`), so that a page loaded after a change starts with the new names (until 8 October 2026, a reloaded page started with the old ones).
- **Only sites allowed a device get the names**, in the script or in the event: a site allowed no microphone and no camera gets an empty choice. Those allowed one can read the names from their browser anyway.
- **What the script tells Sioul**, through `console.debug` kept before the page runs, each line marked `sioul:<token>:`, the token a secret of the run (`callToken`, from Qt's `Math.random`, which draws from `QRandomGenerator::global()`): "call" and "end" (every device closed, or the page gone: `pagehide`), which show and hide the call button (`calls`, `calling`; a pop-up counts its own); "changed"; "kept", with only the device's kind and the error's name, which the line above the site turns into a sentence naming the site and the device by the names Sioul has, never words from the page.
- **Costs**, measured in the lab (Qt WebEngine 6.11.2, Chromium's fake devices, 640 × 480 at 20 fps): about 4 % of one core more in the page's process during a call; on a page whose main thread is busy 30 % to 60 % of the time, about 25 ms more sound delay, and in one run in five at 60 %, half the video frames. The relay runs on the page's main thread: in Qt WebEngine 6.11 a track cannot be sent to a worker.
- **Limits**: a page can tell a relay from a camera (its constructor's name); a page that opens its device another way (another frame's `getUserMedia`) keeps it, unseen; the site's own menu may still name the old device after a change in Sioul; on a site's first call, the default device may open for an instant before the chosen one. Android's Sites use Android's WebView, which chooses devices itself: none of this applies there.
- **Checked** by `tools/check-sites.py devices`, in bubblewrap with a `/dev` of its own (no camera, no sound card), no user's runtime folder (no sound server) and a network of its own, on Chromium's fake devices (`--use-fake-device-for-media-stream=device-count=3 --allow-loopback-in-peer-connection`): no real camera or microphone can be opened. Four test sites: a loopback call in the page (through a canvas effect, with a voice meter), one in a frame of another host, one in a pop-up, and a mailbox allowed no device. The calls start on the camera and microphone chosen before Sioul started, before any grant; follow a change of camera (fake camera 1 is grey: the previews and the effect's remote picture turn grey), of speaker (every element's `sinkId`) and of microphone (the sound still received, the meter reading); the page's own camera button blacks the picture and brings it back; a camera that is not there is said in the line above the site while the calls keep theirs; the mailbox hears nothing; after hanging up every track has ended and no site has a call; the page reloaded after one more change starts its new call on the camera chosen last. 26 checks, all passing on 9 October 2026.

## Downloads
Asked for on 19 November 2026: a file a site offered was saved straight into the system's Downloads folder, with no question. Now a card asks where each file goes, and it can go to Sioul's own Notes, Papers or Letters. On a computer; the phone's sites have their own screen and its own question (`SiteDownload.java`). Code: `sioul_core::downloads` (the rules), `sioul_core::filelock::place_new` and its `Pending` (a file placed whole under a free name), `crates/sioul-app/src/downloads.rs` (the held folders, the card's words, the places, the notices), `qml/SiteDownloads.qml` (the requests, one after the other) and `qml/DownloadCard.qml` (the card). Reviewed on 20 November 2026 (42 findings, all settled); the review's probe is `tools/web-downloads-probe/` (below).

- **Accepted at once, into Sioul's own folder.** Qt WebEngine fetches a file whether its request is answered or not: held unanswered (the first build), the whole file waited, hidden, in the system's Downloads folder for as long as the card did. So each request is accepted inside the profile's `downloadRequested`, into a folder made for it in Sioul's cache (`downloads::hold`: `~/.cache/sioul/downloads/<time>-<process>-<n>`, mode 0700), under its name made one plain file name; the card then decides only where it goes from there, and Cancel deletes what came. **The engine's own instant**: Chromium starts every download by itself as a hidden file of the system's Downloads folder (`.org.chromium.Chromium.XXXXXX`, as the desktop's settings name that folder), a few milliseconds before or after it asks Sioul, and hands that file over when the request is accepted. Measured with `tools/web-downloads-probe/` on Qt WebEngine 6.11.2 (20 November 2026): 5 to 16 ms with the first 64 KB when the bytes come at a network's pace; 0.2 to 0.5 s, and the whole file, when 30 MB come from the same machine. No setting of Qt moves it: not the profile's `downloadPath` (set as the profile is made, tried), not the profile's builder, not the request's own folder; only the desktop's folder settings for the whole program would, which Sioul does not change. The released Sioul went through the same instant (it accepted at once into Downloads itself). Sioul never sweeps the system's Downloads folder (other browsers name their hidden files the same way); after a crash or a power cut in that instant, such a hidden file may stay there.
- **The request's view is read once**, when it is offered (`SitesPage.onDownloadRequested`: the page's address, its site, its pop-up), and never again: Qt WebEngine 6.11.2 crashes on `download.view` once the page is gone (the review saw it three times). Its address, name, type and size are kept with it (`SiteDownloads`' entry).
- **Bounds**: at most three files of one site wait for an answer; more are refused at once (cancelled, never fetched), said once (`download-too-many`). A file waiting unanswered past 1 GiB, or when less than 200 MiB would stay free on the cache's disk (`downloads::room`), is let go and said (`download-too-big`). A site that had a file refused this session, or offers more while one is asked, gets a flat link in its card: **Refuse every file from this site until it is closed**: those waiting go, the next are cancelled at once until the site is closed (taken out of Sioul) or reloaded by you, said once (`download-refused-site`).
- **At start**, once this Sioul is the profile's (a second one hands over first), the whole held folder goes (renamed aside at once, removed off the window's thread), with the hidden files a day old that a stopped placing left in the papers' folder and where scans arrive (`filelock::sweep_parts`). **At quit**, every request under way is cancelled and its folder removed (`SitesPage.closeAll`).
- **The card** (`DownloadCard.qml`, a modal card headed in two sections, as the settings' cards): **The file**, its name in a field (as `downloads::file_name` made it: `clean_name`, and a type's extension when the site gave none; its stem selected), its size when the site says it and the host it comes from, one line saying it waits in Sioul's own folder until a place is chosen and that Cancel deletes it (`download-waits`), and for a program a line of caution (`is_program`: by its extension, `links::is_program` and Android's installers, or by the type the site says); **Where**, five places, each with a line saying where, what follows, or why it cannot take this file. The places follow the name typed (asked again of `downloadOffer` a moment after it stops changing). Save and Cancel. The keyboard: Tab goes through it; the arrows move the choice among the places that can take the file; Enter in the field is Save, Enter on a place chooses it and saves there, Enter on Cancel cancels; Escape and a phone's back key are Cancel; the system's "Save as" window closed without a folder gives the keys back to the card. Every control is named for a screen reader (each place's line is its description); a problem under the places is an alert, announced. Words from the site are drawn as plain text, cut to 60 characters in their middle by Rust (`shortened`). At a phone's width it takes the window less its margins, Save and Cancel always in view.
- **Which place takes what** (`downloads::refusal`, asked by the card, at Save and again at the placing): Downloads and a folder chosen take anything, but a folder in Sioul's own data, state, configuration or cache folders (`download-folder-own`). Notes take what Notes show (below) and never a name Sioul reads as one of its own lists (`is_sioul_own`: any `.toml`, a list's copy, a move's mark), nor a text larger than 2 MiB (`notes::TEXT_NOTE_BYTES`; known from the size the site says, else once it came). Papers never a program, nor such a name. Letters scans alone (PDFs and pictures), never a program. Each needs its folder on this device.
- **Pop-ups**: a file offered from a pop-up is asked over that pop-up (the card is drawn in its window, which is raised); the pop-up gone before the answer, the question comes back to Sioul's window, over the Sites page, and Sioul's window is shown and raised for it.
- **The place chosen last for a site** is offered first (`downloads::Memory`, `downloads.toml` in Sioul's state folder, this device's own, keyed by the site's id, else by its whole host); Downloads the first time, and whenever the place kept cannot take this file. "A folder I choose…" keeps its folder too, where the system's window opens next time.
- **One after the other**: the oldest file not answered is asked first; one offered while the Sites page is not in view (or the window is in the tray), and no pop-up of its own shows, waits there, the status line saying so (`download-waiting`); the page leaving view while one is asked closes its card, and the question comes back with the page. A request Qt itself cancels or cuts while it waits is said as failed.
- **Save** (`downloads::start`): the place checked for this name and kept for the site; for "A folder I choose…", the system's "Save as" window first (QtQuick.Dialogs' `FileDialog`, `SaveFile`, without its own question of a name taken where it can: such a name gets a number instead); its answer goes to the file it was opened for, and the window closes if that file goes meanwhile.
- **Come whole and answered** (`downloads::done`, off the window's thread): checked by the antivirus when this computer has one (`antivirus::scan`, the one call a mail's attachment gets; a threat found deletes it, said as for an attachment; without an antivirus, or for a file larger than it scans, it is placed unchecked, with no question: the attachments' question before opening is not asked here. The status line and the notice then add that it was not checked, and why (`download-unchecked-too-big`, `download-unchecked-none`), and "Checking … with the antivirus…" is said only when `antivirus::present` finds one to ask: never on a phone, nor on Linux or macOS without ClamAV's programs; always on Windows, where only a scan says whether an antivirus answers AMSI), then placed by `filelock::place_new`: written beside its place under a short hidden name (`.sioul-<process>-<n>.part`, moved when on the same file system, else copied with its permissions and, on Windows, its zone mark), synced through a handle that may write (Windows refuses to flush one opened to read), then named by a hard link that fails when the name is taken; where links are not made (FAT, exFAT, some shares, a phone's shared storage), by creating the name new, which fails when it is taken too, and copying the bytes in; where the hidden file cannot be made at all (a sandbox's portal folder), by creating the final name new and writing it directly. Never a rename over a name; an error other than "no links here" (no room, a quota) fails the placing. A name taken gets a number, each place its own way: "statement (2).pdf" in Downloads, a chosen folder and Letters, "statement-2.pdf" in Papers, "statement 2.pdf" in Notes. The placed file is marked as come from the Internet (`mark_from_outside`: the Mark of the Web on Windows, the quarantine on macOS through the system's `xattr`), as an attachment saved is.
  - **Downloads**: `config::downloads_dir`.
  - **A folder I choose…**: the folder the system's window gave.
  - **Notes**: where new notes go (`notes_folder`, "notes" unless set), in the notes folder; written beside first, the notes' lock taken for the naming alone (`Pending::prepare`, then `name` under `notes::with_lock`); "#", "^", "[" and "]" become "-", as in a new note's name.
  - **Papers**: by the wallet's own way in (`papers::file_download`: `keep_checked`, then a paper made at once, yours, with a title and a type guessed from its name; what Sioul guesses counts until you correct it). Its form opens to correct it only while the Sites page is still in view and no other card or form is open; closing the form keeps the paper. Otherwise its notice opens the paper.
  - **Letters**: by the letters' own way in (`letters::add_one`), where scans arrive; read twenty seconds later as a scanner's file is (`letters::tick`, at most 100 pages: `ocr::PAGES_READ`). The Letters page opens on it only while the Sites page is still in view; it waits there for the letter two minutes at most, and not once you leave the page.
  - **A place that refuses the file** once it came (full, closed, the wrong type, a text too large): the file stays held and its card asks again, saying why (`download_refused`), so that another place is chosen; Cancel deletes it. Nothing is lost that a site would not send twice.
- **Said**: while it comes, the status line (`download-coming`); once there, the status line and one notice (`notice::Kind::Minor`, to know), about an opaque id (`sioul:download/<held folder's name>`: the shown notices' list keeps no file's name), whose click opens what this session kept for it (`downloads::opened`): in a system's folder, **Open** (never for a program, nor for a name without an extension, which a desktop would open by what it finds inside: their notice opens the folder) and **Show in its folder**, both `file:` addresses the window opens (`main.qml`'s `openFile`, which opens a program's folder, never the program); in Notes, the note; in Papers, the paper (`sioul:paper/…`); in Letters, the letter. Nothing is opened by itself, and no download takes the window to another page by itself, but Letters' as above.
- **Failed or cut** (`downloads::failed`): its held folder taken away; said in the status line and by a notice like a file placed, by Qt's reason (`interruptReason`): the disk full, the disk, the connection, the site, or cut short; the request cancelled in Chromium too, where a cut one would stay to be taken up again.

## Closing
Some sites keep a login only in the open page. Discord, as reported publicly (no real account was used here), takes its token out of its storage (localStorage) once the page has loaded and writes it back as the page goes: a reload keeps you logged in, a page destroyed without going logs you out. A browser lets a page go when it closes the tab or quits: it runs the page's `beforeunload`, then its `pagehide` and `unload`. Qt WebEngine does not when a view is destroyed, and quitting Sioul destroyed them: Discord asked to log in at every start (reported on 6 October 2026), while Chromium kept it on the same computer.
- **As Sioul quits**, once its windows are gone, every site's page is closed as a browser closes its tabs (`WebEngineView.RequestClose`), the pop-ups' too; a page's "Leave the site?" is answered yes. Then Qt WebEngine shuts down with the application and writes what the sites keep (`SitesPage.qml`, `closeAll`; `cpp/webengine.cpp`, `sioul_close_sites`, called once `exec()` returns in `lib.rs`). The pages close in a few milliseconds (7 to 10 ms measured); Sioul waits two seconds at most, out of sight, its window gone (Chromium gives a page that does not answer half a second for each step).
- **Every way of quitting** comes there: the window's close (its button, Alt+F4, the taskbar's Close); a logout or a shutdown (on X11, the session manager's "Die" is Qt's quit, which closes the windows; Plasma on Wayland closes them); SIGTERM, SIGINT (Ctrl+C) and SIGHUP, which ended Sioul on the spot with nothing written, and now quit it as its window does (`cpp/signals.cpp`: a pipe read by Qt's event loop; a second signal ends it at once, and so do thirty seconds).
- **While Sioul runs**: a site taken out has its page closed so before its view goes (its login stays in the profile, whole); a pop-up closed, by you or by its page, closes its page behind it; a site's view, once made, stays for the session, so unticking "Keep it open" on a chat never opened no longer destroys its page.
- **What cannot be kept**: Sioul killed (SIGKILL, a crash, the power cut) loses what the pages hold in memory, and what they wrote last: Chromium writes localStorage to the disk some seconds after a page writes it, then less often (a test killed 35 s after the load lost its last 30 s), cookies every thirty seconds. Its web processes killed with it may too: systemd stopping the unit Plasma starts Sioul in (`app-sioul@….service`, `KillMode=control-group`), as when the session ends without a logout, signals every process of it at once; in two tests the token written back at `beforeunload` was kept, those of `pagehide` and `unload` once.
- **Checked** by `tools/check-sites.py quit`: a local page stands in for Discord (three tokens taken out of localStorage, each written back in one of the three handlers; a value written once; one written four times a second, in localStorage and in a cookie; a cookie from the server), Sioul run twice offscreen on a demo profile. Before: the window closed or SIGTERM, no handler ran, the three tokens lost; SIGTERM killed Sioul in 0.1 s. Now: the window closed, SIGTERM 1, 3 or 35 s after the load, a pop-up closed and the site taken out while Sioul runs: every token, value and cookie found, the last writes within a second and a half of the close; Sioul gone 0.3 to 0.9 s after the close (2.5 s on a machine loaded by six builds).

## Notifications at your pace
Every site's notifications are accepted, then caught by Sioul, never shown by the site itself:
- from the site in front of you: nothing more;
- from a site in real time (its checkbox, always in view; or real time for everything): a desktop notification at once, a notice to know ([notifications.md](notifications.md)): about the site and the web notice's own key (`sioul:site/<id>#<tag>`), the site's cached icon on it, at normal urgency, gone after the minutes set; the same tag (one conversation) takes the last one's place; a click opens the site, then calls the web notice's own `click()`, kept by the Sites page (`webNotices`: Qt gives the object to JavaScript, which would collect it), so that the site opens the conversation, the site alone when it is gone; its `closed` takes Sioul's away for a call alone (some sites close a message's own after a few seconds); kept by the Sites page only when Sioul told it, an hour at most, and dropped once the site is opened; a notice without a tag keyed by its site alone, the next in its place; every message kept for the Porch and the next gathering, told or not;
- **a call** ("incoming call", "vous appelle"…): at once, unless the site is silenced, a notice to do: it stays until the site withdraws it, three minutes at most (`CALL_RINGS`); a missed call waits like the rest;
- else it waits, under "<site> has news" in the Porch, in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/site-notices.toml` (a notification told twice is kept once; the newest two hundred). Opening the site clears its news.
- **Gathered**: at set times (Settings ▸ Reminders, 9:00, 13:00 and 18:00 unless you set others; three a day helped most in a field trial, Fitz et al. 2019), one notification says which sites have news since the last time it rang, "WhatsApp (3) · Discord (1)", with Open the Porch: each site's notification is named once (`sites::Notice::told`, written only once set: an older Sioul passes the key over), and stays on the Porch until the site is opened. Only the sites of those hours, on the computer you are at (`sioul_sync::lease`, as the medicines).
- A site whose area does not fit the hours ([areas.md](areas.md)) keeps its notifications, real time and calls included, until its hours come. While you sleep, every site keeps them: no gathered notification, no real time, no call ([health.md](health.md), "Do not disturb").

A message whose sender belongs to a site's `announced_by` ("you have a new message in your secure space") gets a button above its headers: "Open <site>".

## Security keys and one-time codes
- **A security key** (WebAuthn, FIDO2: a YubiKey for GitHub, Proton Mail, Google…): when a site asks, Sioul's dialog says which of the key's accounts, asks its PIN (or a new one when the key has none or asks to change it, with how many tries are left), says to touch it again, and says in plain words why it failed (not registered, PIN blocked, timeout…), with Retry. A plain touch shows nothing: the key blinks and the page waits (Qt 6.11 says nothing then). Passkeys kept on the key work; passkeys in a phone or in the system do not on Linux and macOS (Qt WebEngine offers none); on Windows, Windows' own dialog takes all of it.
- **A fix for Qt 6.10–6.11**: sites that ask `PublicKeyCredential.getClientCapabilities()` waited forever for an answer that never came (QTBUG-149575); a one-line script, in every page before its own, takes that question away, and the site asks the key directly.
- **Linux**: works with Qt WebEngine built with udev (Fedora's is) and systemd's rules for keys (no udev rule to add). The Flatpak needs `--device=all` to reach `/dev/hidraw*`.
- **One-time codes**: "Fill the login" on a page asking for a code writes the code the vault's secret gives now, only on the site its login belongs to, only when you click.

## Bitwarden
Logins are filled from Bitwarden, read by Sioul itself (`sioul_sync::bitwarden`): nothing to install, on every system. The account (its e-mail, and its server when it is not bitwarden.com: bitwarden.eu, or your own, Vaultwarden too, always over HTTPS) goes in the password manager's card at the top of Accounts ▸ Your accounts (`VaultCard.qml`, the core's `settings::for_view("bitwarden")`), its one place: the card holds the two fields while no account is set, and its settings once one is (Add an account lists no Bitwarden); it is kept in `[bitwarden]` of the configuration, shared with the other settings. One vault for the whole app: the Sites page fills logins from it (on a phone, the site's screen: below, "On a phone"), and every password field of the account forms offers "From Bitwarden…" (`VaultFill.qml`), which names the login that filled it. A password so taken is the vault's: one keeper per page (`VaultHeld.qml`) keeps every field the vault ever filled until the field is destroyed, whatever the hand does to it, and the value it gave until the next release. At each release (the vault locks, by Lock or its time without use: the signal `bitwarden_locked`; the vault read as not open; Add an account or the page left; the account added; the password dialog closed) it purges each such field: emptied when it still holds the vault's value, else left with the person's own text, and either way set by code twice, since `QQuickTextInput::setText` clears the field's undo and redo only when the text changes (the same code in Qt 6.9.0, 6.9.3, 6.10.0, 6.10.2 and 6.11.0). Until then it veils them (`PasswordField.veiled`: neither the eye nor "Show passwords as you type" shows them unless the vault is open). A field the vault never filled is left alone. Tested on the real page by `tst_vaulthold.qml` (emptied by hand, typed over, undone and redone, with the eye on and off, then Undo and Redo after each release). The choosers drop their last list as the vault locks. Only the account's own password dialog remembers the login chosen for its mail server; the forms remember nothing, so that a host the Sites page fills never inherits their choice. The Sites page's ⚙ says nothing of it; a fill asked without an account says where it goes, with a button that opens the card. The key settings a server gives are held to Bitwarden's own bounds, so a server cannot weaken them. "Fill the login" asks for the master password once per session; it derives the master key as Bitwarden's clients do (PBKDF2-SHA256 or Argon2id, as the account says), logs in with its hash, opens the user key (HKDF-stretched master key; AES-256-CBC checked by HMAC-SHA256 before anything is decrypted), an organisation's key through the RSA key, an item's own key when it has one, and newer accounts' COSE messages (XChaCha20-Poly1305, AES-256-GCM; XAES-256-GCM not yet). The password and the keys are dropped; the logins stay in memory, wiped when the vault closes. Read only: nothing is ever written to the vault. Bitwarden refuses clients too far behind its own version ("Please update your app to continue using Bitwarden": a declared 2024.12.0 was refused in October 2026), so Sioul declares itself Bitwarden's desktop app at the server's own version, read from its `/config` once a session (2026.9.0 when the server does not say).
- **The security key alone** (Bitwarden's "log in with passkey", for a key whose passkey is used for encryption: Settings ▸ Security ▸ Master password in Bitwarden's web vault): "Open with my security key", above the password; what opened the vault last time is proposed first. Sioul asks Bitwarden for a login's options (`accounts/webauthn/assertion-options`, asked of no account: the key says whose it is), then asks the key at once, as for a second step (below), for the passkey (its PIN in Sioul's dialog) and its secret (WebAuthn PRF, salted with SHA-256 of "passwordless-login", as Bitwarden's apps salt it). The answer waits in the page until Sioul takes it from there; the secret never goes in an address, which would carry it out. Bitwarden checks the key's signature (`grant_type=webauthn`) and gives back an RSA key sealed by the secret and the user key sealed by that RSA key (`WebAuthnPrfOption`): the secret, stretched as a master key is (HKDF "enc" and "mac"), opens the RSA key, which opens the user key (RSA-OAEP-SHA1); the rest is as with the password. No second step and no new-device code: Bitwarden counts the key's PIN as a second factor. A passkey of another Bitwarden account than the one set in Accounts is refused (the access token names its e-mail). When the key gives no secret, when its encryption is off, or when Bitwarden does not know the key, the dialog says so and what to do in Bitwarden.
- **A second step**, the account's own first, in the order Bitwarden's apps propose them, any other one choice away:
  - *a security key* (a YubiKey or any FIDO2 key, as Bitwarden registers them now), asked at once when its step comes. A key signs only for the page it is asked from, and Bitwarden takes signatures for its vault's address only (`ServerDomain` and `Origins`): so the vault's own light security-key page (`webauthn-connector.html`, nothing shown unasked) is loaded, held unseen in the dialog (a one-point view, given the focus: a page asks a key only while it has it), and Sioul's script (`bitwarden-key.js`) asks the key there with Bitwarden's options as Bitwarden's page passes them (the AppID extension of keys registered as U2F included), then writes the answer word for word as that page writes it (`clientDataJson`, the extension results): the step's code. The PIN, when the key has one, comes in Sioul's own dialog, above; a plain touch shows nothing, the key blinks. "Stop" drops the page; "Use the security key" asks again. No separate window: one would cover the PIN's dialog;
  - *a YubiKey's code* (YubiKey OTP: it types it), *an authenticator app*, *e-mail* (the code is sent when this step is chosen, "Send the code again" beside it), *a recovery code*.
  - Which steps come is the server's choice: a security key registered as FIDO2 is offered to every account; "YubiKey OTP" and Duo only while the account has Premium (Bitwarden hides them from every app otherwise, `TwoFactorProvider.RequiresPremium`). When no key is offered, the dialog says so and why.
  - "Remember this device" is kept in the system keyring, so the next sessions skip the step. Duo cannot be taken here. A new device's code, mailed by Bitwarden, is asked the same way. This computer's identifier is kept (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/bitwarden-device`), so it is a new device once.
  - **The key's page is the window's** (`SitesWeb.qml`): on a computer, the sites' one web profile and Bitwarden's page that asks a key are held by the window, made the first time a site's view or a key asks for them, so that the vault's dialog asks the key the same way from Sites, from Accounts' card, and from "From Bitwarden…" in the account forms (`VaultUnlock.keys`, the window's `vaultKeys`). A phone has no Qt WebEngine: there, a security key as the only step makes the dialog say so instead of naming Duo, and point to an authenticator app or e-mail codes as a second step in Bitwarden, or the password typed; the card opens the vault by password and code.
- **Locked again** (3 November 2026): the password manager's card in Accounts shows the vault as it is on this device, "Bitwarden is open." with **Lock**, or "Bitwarden is locked." with **Unlock** (`settings::Kind::Vault`, SettingRow.qml); without an account set, one line and a button to add one; **Lock Bitwarden** (`bitwarden.lock_after`, minutes: never, 5, 15, 30, 60, 240; never unless you choose) locks it once unused that long, as Bitwarden's own apps do: opening it, a site's or a login's lookup and a fill count as use (`sites::Vault::touch`), and the window's minute locks it (`sites::bitwarden_lock_if_idle`, from `refresh_mode`, the status line saying so). Locked, its logins leave memory; the next fill asks to open it again. An older Sioul skips the setting's line. Tested: `sites::tests::the_vault_locks_after_its_time_without_use`, `settings::tests::the_vault_s_card_holds_its_account`, `tst_vaultrow.qml` and `tst_vaultcard.qml` (the card's three states, the forms' "From Bitwarden…"), on a stand-in vault: nothing asks Bitwarden.
- **Bitwarden's server is asked off the window's thread**: the unlock, a passkey's options and its answer, the e-mail's code (`sites::bitwarden_later`). Each call gives the dialog a ticket at once, and its answer comes with the signal `bitwarden_answered(ticket, answer)`; the dialog waits for its own ticket only, says "Waiting for Bitwarden…" meanwhile, greys Open, and forgets the answer once closed (the vault opens all the same). The window stays free while the server answers. Checked by `tests/qml/tst_vault.qml` on a stand-in, and in the runner (`SIOUL_GRAB_STEPS=bitwarden-wait`, no account set, so no server is asked).
- **On a phone** (21 November 2026): the site's screen fills a login from the same vault, held by the window's process ([On Android](#on-android), "A login from Bitwarden"). Its two lines, Fill from Bitwarden and Choose a login…, are in the screen's menu; the unlock card and the chooser are the window's, which comes forward for them and sends the site back once answered. A security key cannot be asked there: the vault opens with the master password and a code.
- **Which login**: the site's only one is filled at once when it is made for the page's own host at its port (the same host, or the same once one leading "www." is taken off either; the port written, else the scheme's own), or when it was chosen there last; several, none, one made only for the page's rough domain, or another chosen there last open the chooser (`LoginChooser.qml`); ⋮ ▸ "Choose a login…" opens it any time. The rough domain is not enough (review of 22 November 2026, finding 2): where people register under a longer suffix (`asso.fr`, `nhs.uk`) or a host gives each customer a name (`github.io`), its last two labels are two people's sites. A login made for another host of the domain (`www.` and `login.` of one name) costs one chooser, once: chosen there, it is remembered for that host. The public suffix list would read registrable domains for the fill, the bar and the links alike; it is a later decision. The login chosen last for a site comes first (kept in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/bitwarden-chosen.json`, the site's host → Bitwarden's item id). The chooser holds names, user names and sites only: a password leaves the vault for the one login chosen. A login not made for the page's own host says which host it is for, in the warm colour ("for bank.example.net"), so a look-alike or a sister site shows. Opened for a site, the chooser's first line names the host the login will be filled in ("Fills the page on forum.example.net"): on a phone the window covers the site and its bar (finding 7).
- **The search** (`bitwarden::Logins::find`): two fields, **Site** and **User name**, each optional, both matching when both are filled, each with a button at its end that empties it. Until 6 October 2026 one field searched names, user names and sites at once: "gmail" found every login with a Gmail address rather than Gmail's own.
  - **Site** matches the login's sites, never its user name, best first: the same host; the same registrable domain (`sites::domain_of`; an address in numbers, 192.168.1.1, is its own); a host whose labels, or their parts between hyphens, begin with the words typed ("ameli": ameli.fr and assure.ameli.fr, not camelia.com; "agricole": credit-agricole.fr; "ameli.f", on the way to "ameli.fr", already); then the login's name, its words beginning with the words typed, or for an address typed, the domain's own name as a whole word ("ameli" for ameli.fr; "x" for x.com, not "Xbox"). A login's sites are its web addresses ("https://", "http://" or none): an app's ("androidapp://…") and a match pattern ("^https?://…") are no site.
  - **User name** matches the user name only, holding every word typed.
  - Case and accents aside (`text::fold_char`). Neither field reads a password, a note or a one-time code's secret: the search reads an index made as the vault opens, each login's name, user name, sites and their domains, folded, and nothing else.
  - **The order**: the login chosen last for the page; then by how its site matched (host, domain, label, name); the page's own (its host, then its domain); the user name typed whole before a part of one; the name. Fifty are listed, the others counted ("12 more logins: narrow the search"); with both fields filled and nothing found, the chooser says to empty one.
  - **Opened for a site**: Site holds its domain (the page's host first among its logins), or the domain of the login chosen there last when that one was made for another domain (a site that signs in with Google). Changed for a sign-in on another domain, or emptied to search by user name alone.
  - **Opened for a mail account** (Accounts ▸ Password… ▸ From Bitwarden…): User name holds the address; Site stays empty, unless a login was chosen for that server before (its domain then). A mail server's domain is seldom where its provider's login is kept (imap.gmail.com and accounts.google.com, mail.ecloud.global and murena.io, imap.mail.me.com and icloud.com, ssl0.ovh.net and ovh.com), and with both fields matching it would hide the very login looked for; the server's own logins still come first. A whole mail address in User name also finds the logins made for its domain, whatever their user name (`mail_domain`): a provider's login kept under a customer number or another address than the account's, missed before 10 November 2026; those whose user name is the address come first. Another domain is no warning there: the password goes to the account's own server, which refuses a wrong one.
  - **Keys**: Tab goes from one field to the other (the buttons that empty them are no Tab stop); the arrows move in the list; Enter takes the highlighted login, the first unless moved, after searching what was just typed; Escape closes. Searched as you type, once typing pauses for 150 ms. Side by side, the two fields stack on a phone (under 400 px of dialog).
  - **Speed**: the index is made once, as the vault opens (58 ms for 20,000 made-up logins), and only the fifty kept are sorted: a search of those 20,000 took 1 to 5.5 ms on a desktop computer busy with builds (`cargo test -p sioul-sync a_large_vault_is_searched_quickly -- --ignored --nocapture`). Up to 5,000 logins the search runs on the window's thread, so the chooser opens filled; above, off it (`bitwarden_search` answers {"later": n}, then the signal `bitwarden_found(n, …)`): an older answer is dropped for a newer one, and Enter pressed before the answer takes its first login once it comes.
- **The form**: the login is written into the page's password field and the name field before it, as typing would (the values travel as JSON, so nothing in them can change the script); on a page asking for a one-time code, the code the vault's secret gives now (RFC 6238: base32, `otpauth://` with its digits, period and SHA-1/256/512, Steam's five characters).
- **Checked** against Bitwarden's own key-derivation test vectors (PBKDF2, Argon2id, HKDF), RFC 6238's codes, a changed byte refused before decryption, and a COSE message; against the live cloud with a made-up account (refused for its password, not its version: `cargo test -p sioul-sync the_cloud_takes_the_version -- --ignored`); a passkey's keys made as Bitwarden's web vault makes them, opened by its secret and refused for another key's; the cloud's passkey options (`the_cloud_asks_a_passkey`, ignored: it asks Bitwarden); offscreen, Bitwarden's page held unseen in the dialog, focused as a key needs, the key asked at once, for both ways; a refusal and an answer without secret taken back to the dialog; the search on a made-up vault of twelve logins (`cargo test -p sioul-sync bitwarden`): by host then domain, label starts (ameli, not camelia), names when no site matches, user names alone, both fields together, "gmail" as a site finding none of the Gmail addresses, case and accents, passwords and one-time secrets never matched; offscreen, the chooser on a stand-in backend (Qt's test runner): what the fields hold as it opens for a site and for an account, the pause before searching, Tab, Enter right after typing, the arrows, Escape, emptying a field, the status line, the warning, a large vault's answers coming later and out of order, Enter before them, side by side at 1000 px and stacked at 412 px in English and French. The window's own steps on a made-up vault (`SIOUL_GRAB_STEPS=bitwarden-choose` with `SIOUL_TEST_VAULT`, in a test build only) follow the two fields (a site's logins, a user name alone, both fields, the last chosen first) but have not run with them yet. Not yet with a real key and vault: Qt WebEngine's support of the PRF extension is the first thing a real key shows.
