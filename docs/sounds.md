# Sounds to focus or to rest by

The status line has a sound button, never on by itself: noise **to focus** (white, pink, brown), **nature to rest by** (waves on a beach, rain, wind in the trees, crickets at night, a distant storm, birds at dawn), both made by Sioul, and **your own recordings**. One at a time, looping without a seam, fading in and out over five seconds.

## What the research says
- **Noise helps attention a little, for some.** White or pink noise gave a small benefit on attention tasks for people with ADHD or many ADHD traits (g = 0.25) and a small cost for others (g = −0.21) across 13 studies (Nigg et al. 2024, *JAACAP*, doi:10.1016/j.jaac.2023.12.014). No study tested brown noise. Hence "to focus", and offered, not pushed.
- **Nature sounds calm better.** Recovery after stress tended to be faster with water and birdsong than with noise (Alvarsson, Wiens & Nilsson 2010, doi:10.3390/ijerph7031036); across studies, natural sounds lowered stress and annoyance and improved mood, water most for mood (Buxton et al. 2021, *PNAS*, doi:10.1073/pnas.2013097118). Hence "to rest", with recordings.

## How it works
- **Noise is made here** (`sioul_core::sounds`): a minute of white noise, of pink noise (Paul Kellett's filter, −3 dB per octave) and of brown noise (a leaky integration, −6 dB per octave), quiet (about −20 dBFS), its last two seconds faded into its first with equal power, so the loop has no seam. Written once as WAV in `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/sioul/sounds/`. Nothing downloaded, nothing to license.
- **Nature is made here too** (`sioul_core::sounds::nature`), stereo at 22 kHz (what these sounds hold lies below 10 kHz; a minute is 5 MB), seeded the same every time, after Andy Farnell's *Designing Sound* (2010): noise shaped by slow envelopes and filters, and events at random.
  - **Waves**, on a gently sloping beach: a wave swells in every 6 to 11 seconds, somewhere along the beach (both ears hear it, from where it comes in). It swells over 1.8 to 3.2 seconds from nothing, rounds over without a corner nor a crash (noise low-passed under 870 Hz at its top, never bright), and slides back as slowly, about two to three seconds, the wash running up and back for 2.5 to 4 seconds in a soft, dark fizz of foam (400 Hz to 2 kHz). Under the waves, the surf of the rest of the beach (pink noise from 140 Hz to 1.8 kHz with foam above it), soft and steady, drifting a tenth over eight seconds, partly shared by both ears. Two minutes.
  - **Rain**: a hiss (pink noise between 450 Hz and 6.5 kHz, its level wandering), about 160 small drops a second (short decaying tones, 2.4 to 7 kHz) and five larger ones on leaves and stones. One minute.
  - **Wind in the trees**: soft and dark, from 60 Hz to 4.5 kHz, in three layers following gusts that rise and fall slowly over 15 and 6 seconds and rest between: the branches' low roar (60 to 350 Hz, nearly the same in both ears), the air's whoosh (250 Hz to 1.3 kHz, in part) and the leaves (1.5 to 4.5 kHz, louder in the gusts, each ear its own). A minute and a half.
  - **Crickets at night**: six field crickets (4.1 to 4.9 kHz, three or four pulses a chirp, each its rhythm and place between the ears, near or far), a tree cricket's soft trill at 2.8 kHz, the night's air under them. One minute.
  - **A distant storm**: thunder every 8 to 28 seconds, each strike rolling in two to six bursts within seconds, low (brown noise under 110 to 330 Hz) and brighter at its start the nearer it is (up to 2.9 kHz), the same in both ears from where it is; bursts that overlap roll on louder without piling up into one clap; thunder too far to make out rolling on under 220 Hz; and soft, steady rain (pink noise from 250 Hz to 2.5 kHz, drifting a fifth over 15 seconds, a soft drop about once a second), well under the thunder. Three minutes.
  - **Birds at dawn**: six birds after common birds of European woods and gardens, without being any one of them, each where it sits: one with an accelerating run of down-strokes, a second phrase and a flourish (a chaffinch's song), one with "tea-cher" pairs (a great tit's), one with thin varied phrases of high hooks (a robin's), one with low fluty warbled notes and a soft second harmonic, then a quiet twitter (a blackbird's), a far one trilling fast in sections (a wren's), a dove cooing low ("coo, COO-coo, coo-coo", near 500 Hz: a fuller second harmonic, breath around the pitch, a slight roughness, soft edges, each coo lifting a little then sinking). Each syllable is a whistle moving as recorded syllables do: a hook (fast, then slower; often an octave in a few hundredths of a second), a stroke, or a wobble (a warble, a buzz). Each bird has two or three songs and sings one three to six times, a little different each time, before the next; the far ones sound darker; the wood sings more, then less, over tens of seconds. Under them, a faint, steady, low air, and the wood's short echo (Schroeder's reverberation, damped, each ear its own). A minute and a half.
  - Quiet (about −22 dBFS; the waves −26 and the wind −29, backgrounds that stay light), the end faded into the start over four seconds. Never clipped nor rounded off: a smooth limiter holds the rare loudest moments (a thunderclap, a near bird) under −6 dBFS, its gain dipping within 5 ms and coming back over 0.4 s, so the waveform keeps its shape and gains no harmonics. Checked by tests: the seam, the level, where each sound's energy lies (the crickets near 4.5 kHz, the birds between 2 and 5 kHz, the waves and the thunder low), the two ears partly alike, and by looking at their spectrograms; listening is yours.
  - The files of an older recipe are taken away when the new ones are made.

## Tuned against recordings
The waves, the wind, the storm and the birds were measured beside CC0 recordings on 13 November 2026: five beaches, six winds in trees, six distant storms, six dawn choruses or gardens (Freesound, listed in `tools/sounds/references.tsv`, chosen without voices, traffic or planes). `tools/sounds/features.py` measures a recording and Sioul's sound alike, each brought to one level: the spectrum in third octaves, how the level moves in three bands (its spread, and its modulation from 0.03 to 16 Hz), how sharp the clicks are, how alike the two ears are, how tonal the sound is, chirps and claps. `compare.py` puts Sioul's beside the recordings' range and median. The recordings stay outside Sioul; only the recipes changed.

| Sound | What differed (before) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Waves | too bright (centre 1.9 kHz, recordings' median 1.1); ears unrelated (0, recordings 0.5 to 0.75); one slow swell every 6.5 to 11.5 s, 19 to 23 dB deep (recordings about 10) | centre 1.4 kHz; ears 0.6 to 0.7; a break every 2.5 to 6.5 s, 7 to 12 dB |
| Wind | a narrow band sliding up and down, no lows (63 Hz 19 dB under the recordings); ears unrelated | broadband, every third octave within 3.2 dB of the recordings' median; lows nearly the same in both ears (0.84), leaves each ear's own (0.2) |
| Storm | bright steady rain (3 to 6 kHz 12 dB over the median), smooth thunder under 110 Hz; ears unrelated | every third octave within 2.6 dB of the median; thunder rolling in bursts, the same in both ears (0.96) |
| Birds | new | brightness, spread, ears, tonality, notes a second (0.9, recordings 0.4 to 2.1) and pitch within the recordings' range; every third octave within 8 dB of their median |

Listening is still yours: measures say what a sound holds, not whether it calms. A first tuning, the same day, also matched the recordings' fast flicker (their level moving 1 to 16 times a second) and their clicks, with noise whose level jumped every few hundredths of a second and with tiny clicks (drops, pebbles, twigs): listened to, every sound carried "radio static", too loud and never steady, and the birds, matched only in their statistics (slow arched glides at random pitches), sounded nothing like birds. In the recordings that flicker comes from real water and leaves, and from the previews' MP3 coding; made from noise, it is static. So the beds are now steady and quieter, the flicker and the clicks are gone, the waves roll in slower and softer (the second listen found them violent, their rolls short), the dove breathes (its pure whistle sounded synthetic), the waves' surf is much quieter and the waves and the wind play quieter, the wind darker and calmer (the third listen found them too loud and too present for sounds to focus or rest by), the waves swell and slide back as on a gentle slope, never crashing (the fourth found them breaking too fast and too harsh), the soft clipping that rounded off thunder's peaks is replaced by the limiter above, and the birds sing syllables shaped as close spectrograms of the recordings show them. The table above describes that first tuning's measures; the spectra, the ears and the slow movement held, and the birds' notes a second (1.1; recordings 0.4 to 2.1) and middle pitch (3.1 kHz; recordings 2.2 to 4.6 kHz) lie within the recordings' range.
- **Recordings are yours**: any sound in a `sounds` folder of your notes (Ogg, Opus, FLAC or WAV loop exactly; MP3 adds a short gap at the loop, as its encoder pads the ends).
- Played with Qt Multimedia's `MediaPlayer` (`loops: MediaPlayer.Infinite`, seamless since Qt 6.5.1 with FFmpeg).

## ASMR and ambience channels, without advertising
Not inside Sioul, and not by any trick: YouTube's terms forbid blocking its advertising, playing it in a background or audio-only player, and caching it (Terms of Service, 15 December 2023; Developer Policies, September 2026); Invidious and Piped stream it outside those terms and take creators' income away. What is lawful:
1. **YouTube Premium** on your own account, in a site pinned in Sioul's Sites page, or in YouTube's own app.
2. **What creators sell or give**: Bandcamp purchases (yours to play on your devices), a Patreon member's private audio feed, their podcasts. The files go in the `sounds` folder.
3. **Internet radio** through Radio Browser (free and open; ambient and nature stations exist; their streams may carry advertising).
