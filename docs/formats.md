# Formats: where each kind of data lives, and the way out

Interoperability is the ground of Sioul. It connects the standard protocols and keeps the standard files: IMAP and SMTP for mail, CalDAV and CardDAV for calendars, tasks and contacts, WebDAV for files, Markdown for notes, and PDF, images and audio as the files they are. It has no private file format, so everything stays readable and writable by the other programs that handle those formats. What Sioul adds is the management of these things and the links between them, gathered in one application that runs on each system (Linux, Windows, macOS, Android; iOS not yet). Its extensions turn closed services into standard calendars and address books, which a person can share with people who do not use Sioul, and which those people read and write with their own programs. So the admin kept with Sioul stays usable without Sioul.

This note checks that claim against the code, one kind of data at a time, and says plainly where it does not hold yet. The same for people using Sioul, in plain words: the guide's [Your admin stays usable without Sioul](https://sioul.aurelienpierre.com/guide/without-sioul.html). What each server and program does with what Sioul writes, and how far each was tried: [compatibility.md](compatibility.md).

Read from the code on `main` at aca237ca (26 November 2026). Each line cites where it was checked (`file:line`, from the repository's root, or a section of another note). "Tried" means a test or a recorded use; everything else is read from the code, or from the format's own specification.

## The folders

Sioul keeps its own files in four folders (`crates/sioul-core/src/config.rs:3246-3283`: the XDG variables when set, else Linux's places, and the system's own folders on Windows and macOS through the `directories` crate; Android sets the four variables under the app's own storage, `android/main.cpp:293-302`):

| Folder | Linux | Windows | macOS | Android |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| configuration | `~/.config/sioul` | `%APPDATA%\sioul` | `~/Library/Application Support/sioul` | `files/config/sioul`, in the app's private storage |
| data | `~/.local/share/sioul` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\sioul` | `~/Library/Application Support/sioul` | `files/data/sioul` |
| state | `~/.local/state/sioul` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\state\sioul` | `~/Library/Application Support/state/sioul` | `files/state/sioul` |
| cache | `~/.cache/sioul` | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\sioul` (the data's folder) | `~/Library/Caches/sioul` | the app's cache folder, which Android may empty |

On Linux and macOS, Sioul makes these four folders readable by their owner alone at each start (`config.rs:3285-3310`). Below, `<config>`, `<data>`, `<state>` and `<cache>` name them.

The person's own folders are outside them, chosen in the window:
- **the notes folder** (`case_store`, [notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md#where-it-is-set-and-when-there-is-none)). At the move of Sioul's lists out of it, the papers' and letters' folders may sit neither in the notes folder nor in Sioul's own records, state or dated copies, nor hold them, and the notes folder may neither hold Sioul's records folder nor sit inside it (`crates/sioul-core/src/storage.rs:680-704`, `check_folders`; [notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md#the-move-and-what-makes-each-step-safe-to-stop), step 1); a notes folder elsewhere under Sioul's folders is not refused. On Android, a notes folder never chosen is `~/Notes`, which is `files/Notes` in the app's private storage (`config.rs:1853-1855`, `android/main.cpp:297`);
- **the papers' and the letters' folders**, `Papers` and `Letters` in the documents folder unless others are chosen (`config.rs:1881-1889`);
- **the issued invoices' PDFs**, `~/Documents/Invoices` (`Factures` in French) unless another folder is chosen (`config.rs:1281`, `crates/sioul-core/src/invoice/mod.rs:50-59`);
- **the sharing folder**, one a sync app carries, or one Sioul keeps in step with a Nextcloud itself ([database.md](database.md#kept-in-step-by-sioul-itself)).

## On the disk today

On `main`, Sioul seals nothing it keeps on a device, with three exceptions: the texts' archive and the replies to the phone's notifications, sealed line by line with a key made from the sharing key (`crates/sioul-sync/src/textseal.rs:4-23`), and the secret part of an OpenPGP key kept on the device, encrypted with a passphrase the system keyring keeps, as OpenPGP keys are (`crates/sioul-core/src/pgp.rs:6-12`). Passwords and tokens are in the system's keyring, never in a file. The only ciphers in the code are the sharing's (`crates/sioul-sync/src/share.rs`, `blobs.rs`), the texts' (`textseal.rs`) and Bitwarden's client (`bitwarden.rs`). Everything else below is a plain file.

**Not on `main`: the sealed store** (branch `store`, decided, not built yet). It will seal every file of Sioul's own on the device (its configuration, lists and records, its copies of mailboxes, agendas and address books, the clients' files), and give them back as standard files by a command (`sioul store export`, `sioul store unseal`), which is planned and not built. Notes, papers and letters stay out of it, plain on the file system, for Obsidian, Nextcloud Notes and the system; a person who chooses to place one of those folders inside Sioul's own folders may, as a trade of their own. Everything below describes `main` as it is.

## A change made outside Sioul

Another program's change to an item on a CalDAV or CardDAV server comes to Sioul at the next sync. When both changed the same item, the server's version is kept and Sioul's is set aside among the earlier versions, to put back from the window (compatibility.md, the `If-Match` line). Sioul rewrites only the lines it edits (`lines.rs`).

**A calendar an extension fills** (Planity, Skolengo) is an ordinary calendar of the person's own on their CalDAV server, chosen by them ([extensions.md](extensions.md#where-an-agenda-goes-a-calendar-of-the-persons-own)). Its sharing is the server's: someone without Sioul subscribes to it, reads it and writes in it with any CalDAV program. What happens to their change is the agenda's door's rule, at each refresh (when Sioul starts, then every three hours while its window runs, and in a phone's background step: `crates/sioul-app/src/extensions.rs:16-21`):
- **An event the extension wrote, changed by someone**: kept as they left it, as long as the service's version of that event does not change: its hash, `X-SIOUL-EXTENSION-HASH`, written on the event, is compared with the service's (`door.rs:275-288`), and a program that keeps unknown lines (RFC 5545 §3.8.8.2) keeps it. Once the service changes that event, it is written again whole from the service, and the change is lost (`door.rs:285-286`); its description says so: "From Skolengo, through Sioul: a change made here is replaced when Skolengo changes this event." ([extensions.md](extensions.md#where-an-agenda-goes-a-calendar-of-the-persons-own)). If their program dropped the hash line, the device that wrote the event compares with its own note of the hash; another device running the extension, which has no note of it, writes the event again at its next refresh (`door.rs:282`).
- **Deleted by someone**: it comes back at the next refresh while the service still has it (`door.rs:289-296`).
- **Dropped by the service**, within the days the service said it read: taken out of the calendar, whatever was changed on it (`door.rs:300-311`). A day the service did not answer changes nothing.
- **Made into a meeting** (someone added guests): Sioul no longer changes or removes it, since a meeting's server would tell every guest (`crates/sioul-core/src/agenda.rs:1212-1218`).
- **Events others add to that calendar**: never read as the extension's, never changed, never removed: an event is the extension's by the form of its UID alone (`door.rs:209-225`).

**An address book an extension fills** works the same way, more gently: Sioul only adds to a card what it lacks when the service changes that person, never removes a line, replaces only a name it wrote itself that nobody changed since (`crates/sioul-core/src/extensions/cards.rs:44-48`), never deletes a card; a card it made and someone deleted comes back while the service still has that person ([extensions.md](extensions.md#where-contacts-go-an-address-book-of-the-persons-own)).

**Writing back to the service is not built.** Version 1 of the extensions' interface has two calls, `sign-in` and `refresh` ([extensions.md](extensions.md#the-calls)), and an extension brings agendas and contacts only (`crates/sioul-core/src/extensions/mod.rs:44-48`): nothing carries a change back to Planity or Skolengo. The Planity extension says itself that it reads only ("A Planity Pro salon agenda, read-only, for Sioul": `planity/src/lib.rs:3` in the `sioul-extensions` repository). Two-way editing is deferred.

## Systems

Built by GitHub on each push to `main` that changes the code: Linux, Windows and macOS, with their tests (`.github/workflows/build.yml:61-73`); Android's APK is built (`.github/workflows/android.yml`), its tests not run there. In daily use on Linux and on one Android phone; Windows and macOS not yet run by a person ([architecture.md](architecture.md#platforms); the guide's [Works with](https://sioul.aurelienpierre.com/guide/compatibility.html#systems)). iOS: not yet; the sharing's format is plain enough for another phone's app to read it, and none does yet ([database.md](database.md#not-built)).

## Where it does not hold yet

Each point below is a place where something of a person's admin is reachable only through Sioul today, with what it would take to make it hold.

- **Texts kept only in Sioul's archive.** On every device that shares them, the texts' archive is sealed line by line (`textseal.rs:4-10`) and only grows: a text deleted on the phone stays only there, readable by Sioul alone. To hold: a command that writes the archive out as a standard file (such as the XML that SMS backup apps write and put back on a phone), or the store's planned export covering texts.
- **The sharing folder on its server.** What travels only by the sealed sharing (Sioul's lists, health, time, settings, drafts, invoices' records, calendars and books kept on a device only, texts) has no copy on any server that another program reads: the sharing folder opens only in Sioul, with the passphrase. Each device keeps plain files (texts aside), so the way out holds while one device is there. To hold without a device: a command that opens the folder with the passphrase and writes each device's files out plain (`sioul share export --to DIR`), not built.
- **Notes and papers that travel by the sealed sharing.** A device whose notes folder no sync app carries (a phone's, with eDrive) sends its notes and papers through the sealed sharing: on the server they are sealed pieces, which Nextcloud Notes or another device's Obsidian cannot open. Carried by a sync app, they are plain files on the server. To hold: Sioul writing the notes folder to a WebDAV server as plain files, as Nextcloud's client does (Files and Notes are "not yet": compatibility.md, "Finding servers").
- **Drafts.** Kept in Sioul's own TOML files and carried by the sealed sharing; another mail program never sees them. To hold: drafts written to the server's Drafts folder by IMAP APPEND ([database.md](database.md#not-built)).
- **On Android, Sioul's folders are the app's own.** Its configuration, data and state are in the app's private storage (`android/main.cpp:293-302`), which other apps cannot open, and with `allowBackup="false"` ([android-basics.md](android-basics.md#where-files-live-on-the-phone)); a notes folder never chosen is there too (`config.rs:1853-1855`), and so are the papers' and letters' folders that follow it ([notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md#the-places), "Defaults"), and the invoices' PDFs unless another folder is chosen (`~/Documents/Invoices`, or `Factures` by the language, is under `files/` there: `invoice/mod.rs:50-58`). The command line's ways out above (`sioul invoices export`, `sioul movements --json`…) do not exist on a phone: Android's package builds the window alone. Mail, calendars and contacts stay on their servers; Sioul's own lists, health, time and texts on a phone alone are reachable only through Sioul there. To hold: the notes folder in the phone's shared storage by default (Documents), asked once; and a way to write Sioul's own files out to the shared storage.
- **Filters and rules** are Sioul's own, in plain TOML: no other program runs them. Writing mail filters as Sieve (RFC 5228) on servers that take it (ManageSieve, RFC 5804) would let them run without Sioul; not built.
- **When the sealed store lands**, every file of Sioul's own will be sealed on the device; mutt, notmuch, khal, khard and vdirsyncer would then read the servers, or an export. Its way out as standard files (`sioul store export`, `sioul store unseal`) must be built with it for the claim above to hold.
