# Tasks, notes, links and focus

A plan written in prose is a wall: every line is there at once, and the next step has to be found again each time. Sioul turns the plan into tasks that wait for each other, shows the one step to take now with the reason, and keeps every task tied to what it needs: its notes, the mail it came from, the people, the drafts, the project. The research behind each choice is in [research.md](research.md), findings 6 to 20.

## Calm first, for tasks
1. **One next step, picked for you, with its reason.** "Now" shows one step, why it comes now ("its date is 30 October: three weeks left"; "it frees two other steps"), and the one after it. Equal steps are not shown as a choice to make: Sioul picks, and says it picked. Two other choices wait behind one click.
2. **Nothing is ever overdue.** A day to start that passed says nothing: the task is simply free. The date asked from outside (DUE) is said as time left; once passed, as "Date asked: 1 October", never in red, never counted. The plan always starts from today.
3. **"Not now" is honoured without argument.** It puts the step's whole stream off until tomorrow (the tasks tied to it by what they wait for or by being steps of the same task), so what comes next is free to start, not its own next step; streams are ranked by how soon their dates come. A new day starts clean.
4. **Optional stays optional.** A task tagged `joy` (only if you want to) is offered in a fold, never proposed as the next step; one tagged `someday` (when you say so) is parked at the end of the list. Neither takes room in the plan.
5. **Starting is the hard part, so it is helped.** "Start" offers two minutes first; "What makes it hard?" gives one matching help per answer: its details opened (its notes and messages), a step added, two minutes offered under the card ("Just two minutes", which starts the time only when pressed), a lighter day. Only "Start" starts the time: an answer records or offers, never starts.
6. **Time made visible, without pressure.** The focus window stays on top while you work elsewhere: a disc drains in a neutral colour, with no ticking and no sound. Two minutes before the end it says it is time to find a stopping point; at the end, "Keep going" or "Stop here, it counts". Stopping offers one line, "next time, start by…", shown when the task comes back.
7. **How much a day holds is yours to say.** "How is today?": clear, haze or fog. Haze keeps less for today; fog shows only small steps, and one step is a full day. Nothing is inferred from what you do.
8. **Finishing says what it changed.** "Done. This frees: Send the registered letter." Once, in the status line.
9. **Nothing counts what you did not do.** No streaks, no postponement counters, no score. "Done this week" shows what got done.
10. **The plan proposes; your dates stay yours.** The days the plan gives each task are computed each time, from today, and never written into the tasks. Only what you set (a day to start, a date asked, an order) is stored, and the times you pin tasks to, kept as events in a calendar ("Pinned to a time", below). The plan is made again when Sioul starts, every twelve hours, at midnight, and at once whenever something it depends on changes: a task made, changed, done, dropped, deleted or put off ("Not now"), an estimate, time noted, an event made, moved, deleted, accepted or declined, a meal, a nap or the night changed for a day or as usual, your hours and days off, the day's weather, "Done for today" and work now, what your other devices or a sync bring, the capacity settings. Each change asks for it, off the window's thread, several in a row making it once (`backend::coalesced`); the day is laid again with it. It reads the calendars once for the next four weeks (`plan::HORIZON_DAYS`), an event whose deletion waits for "Undo" already left out, and makes its settings in one place (`plan::settings_from`), as an agent's server makes them, so that the plan an agent reads is yours. A task's card takes the task's own fields from its file, one read, and only the plan's numbers (its days, the minutes it is laid for) from the plan as last made (`work::PlanShown`), never making it again on the window's thread. That plan counts as current until anything is written after the read it was made from, by Sioul, another program (`sioul mcp`, `sioul`) or the sync: this process's count of writes and the calendars' folder times (`timeline::Stamp`; each item is written beside its place, then renamed in, which changes its folder's time). Its save carries the task as the card read it and writes only the fields the card changed since, over the file as it is now (`TaskEdit::changed_over`): a checklist item ticked elsewhere while the card was open stays ticked (review of 1880bce, M1). A task not done by its planned day moves on, and what waits for it follows.
11. **"Done for today" closes the day, early or not.** It opens the end of the work day first ([reviews.md](reviews.md)): what was said of the morning, how the day felt, its mix, a note, each optional; **Close the work day** closes it with no answer as well as with, and nothing asks whether to stop. "Undo" waits in the status line. At the end of the day's last hours the status line offers it, with one quiet notice, once; before sleep, **Close the whole day** looks back over the whole day. Today's room goes to zero and the plan flows on into the next days with room, never above a day's room; nothing is written into the tasks. A screen, read in ten seconds, says when work comes back, what got done or worked on (only if something was), that everything else has its place, the first step when work comes back (the line left when a session stopped, else the plan's first step, one tap to say it your way), a date asked before then if there is one (with "Ask for more time", or leave it), and what still gets through. No count of what was not done, no history of early stops, no "are you sure". The next working day opens on that step, never on yesterday. Then quiet time until work comes back ([porch.md](porch.md)): "Rest of the day", only what is yours; a thought noted then waits, out of sight, for work to come back. Findings 21 to 23 of [research.md](research.md).
12. **Offices have hours.** A task marked "Needs an open office" is proposed only while offices are open (Tasks ⚙, Monday to Friday 9:00–17:00 by default) and planned only on those days; on a Saturday, the next step is one you can do.

## In the standards
Tasks are CalDAV tasks (VTODO, RFC 5545 §3.6.2) in task lists on your calendar server, so any client and any device sees them. What ties them together is written into them, in RFC 9253's terms where it exists, under Sioul's own names where a phone's account manager cannot send the standard ones back (27 October 2026: [compatibility.md](compatibility.md), "A phone's ical4j"); both forms are read:

| What | Written as |
|---|---|
| A task | a VTODO in a task list (a calendar whose `supported-calendar-component-set` holds VTODO) |
| A step of a bigger task | `RELATED-TO;RELTYPE=PARENT:<UID>`, in the step |
| A checklist: what to bring, what to check | a GitHub task list in its notes (`DESCRIPTION`): `- [ ] item`, `- [x] item`, and `- [-] item` for not needed this time (the Obsidian Tasks plugin's "dropped"; a convention, not GFM); a line ending with a colon just before items names their group; indented lines are an item's detail ("Checklists", below) |
| Its place among its bigger task's steps | `X-APPLE-SORT-ORDER:<n>`, the smaller first, as Nextcloud Tasks, Tasks.org ("My order") and Apple Reminders write it. Sioul writes it on each step it makes, in Nextcloud's scale (seconds since 1 January 2001): a new step after the others, a step moved between its neighbours (one file), or the steps renumbered 1000 apart when its neighbours touch (`tasks::reorder`). Unsaid, a step's place is the seconds from 2001 to its creation, as Nextcloud counts it. |
| Waits for another task | `X-SIOUL-RELATED-TO;RELTYPE=DEPENDS-ON:<UID>`, in the one that waits. With a gap ("the answer comes within two weeks"): `X-SIOUL-RELATED-TO;RELTYPE=FINISHTOSTART;GAP=P14D:<UID>` in the one that comes first, as RFC 9253 §4 places temporal links. RFC 9253's own `RELATED-TO` with these types is read too, `NEXT` too, and a gap in weeks (`P2W`, as ical4j writes it back) |
| Can start from | `DTSTART` (a date, or a time in UTC); kept by Sioul too in `X-SIOUL-START` when it is not before `DUE` (the same day), which a phone's account manager drops, and in its place when the two are of different value types (a day to start, a time due), which a CalDAV server refuses: never a `DUE` before `DTSTART` nor of another type (`tasks::dates_lines`). `X-SIOUL-START` is read only where `DTSTART` is gone, and never after `DUE` |
| The date asked | `DUE`: a date (`VALUE=DATE`), or with an hour a time in UTC ("A date asked and its hour", below) |
| How long it takes | `ESTIMATED-DURATION:PT15M` (draft-ietf-calext-ical-tasks) |
| Who does it, what it is about | `CATEGORIES`: `you`, `proxy` (someone with you or for you), `joy`, `someday`, and topics |
| Its kind: a call, writing, online (a form), going out, reading, thinking, making, or one of yours (Tasks ⚙: renamed, taken away, added) | `X-SIOUL-CONCEPT;VALUE=URI:tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/task-type/call` (RFC 9253 §7.3's `CONCEPT`, a tag URI, RFC 4151, read too); other applications' concepts are left as they are |
| Needs an open office | `X-SIOUL-CONCEPT;VALUE=URI:tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/needs/office-hours`; the hours it follows are read each time, never copied into it ("Offices' hours", below) |
| Its project | `X-SIOUL-REFID:<project id>` (RFC 9253's `REFID`, read too; [notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md)) |
| Its notes, the mail it came from, its drafts, an event | `X-SIOUL-LINK;LINKREL=describedby` (a note), `via` (what it was made from), `related`; `VALUE=URI` for a note, a message or a draft, `VALUE=UID` for an event (RFC 9253's `LINK`, read too); a relation that is a URI (`tag:…`) in `X-SIOUL-REL`, quoted, beside `LINKREL=related` |
| The people and offices involved | `CONTACT;ALTREP="sioul:contact/<UID>":<name>` |
| Order among equals | `PRIORITY` (1 first) |
| Time kept before and after it, in minutes: getting there, getting ready, coming back (events too) | `X-SIOUL-BEFORE:20`, `X-SIOUL-AFTER:15` |
| What it costs and what it gives back, 0 to 10 each as you rate them, the rated ones only (events too): thinking, feelings, anxiety, body and senses | `X-SIOUL-COST:COGNITIVE=3;EMOTIONAL=5;ANXIETY=7;BODY=2`, `X-SIOUL-GAIN:6` |
| How it was, said after it, optional: one rating a day, the newest five kept ([capacity.md](capacity.md)) | `X-SIOUL-FELT-COST;X-SIOUL-ON=20261006:ANXIETY=3`, `X-SIOUL-FELT-GAIN;X-SIOUL-ON=20261006:6` |
| Its first estimate, written by Sioul when it first has one, never changed | `X-SIOUL-ESTIMATE-FIRST:PT30M` |
| Pinned to a time ("Do at…", a step dragged in the agenda's Day or Week) | a time block: an event in a calendar ("Pinned to a time", below); the task links to its current block only: `X-SIOUL-LINK;LINKREL=related;X-SIOUL-REL="tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/rel/time-block";VALUE=UID:<the event's UID>` |
| A time given to a step for one day by a drag, before time blocks | `X-SIOUL-AT:20261006T123000Z` (UTC): read for compatibility, never written now; one for today or later becomes a block once, the line taken out. Not `DTSTART`: RFC 5545 §3.8.2.3 wants `DUE` of `DTSTART`'s value type and later than it, and servers that check (Sabre: Nextcloud) refuse a task whose start has a time while its date asked is a date, or has passed; nor a line of the task's own: other applications never show it |
| Light, heavy or what gives back: chosen, or computed from its costs once one is rated (none for the usual) | `X-SIOUL-ENERGY:HEAVY` |
| Comes back | `RRULE:FREQ=…`; done, it moves to its next turn after today, as tasks.org does |
| Done | `STATUS:COMPLETED`, `COMPLETED`, `PERCENT-COMPLETE:100` |
| The supplies it plans to use, and, done, what it used ([supplies.md](supplies.md)) | `X-SIOUL-SUPPLY;X-SIOUL-ID=<supply id>:<quantity>`, a line a supply; `X-SIOUL-SUPPLY-USED;X-SIOUL-ID=<supply id>:<quantity>`, written when Sioul finishes it (what it planned, unless put right) and taken off when it is open again; a task to resupply, `X-SIOUL-RESUPPLY:<supply id>` |
| Where a task a project's route made came from ([notes-folder.md](notes-folder.md#routes-that-make-tasks)) | `X-SIOUL-ORIGIN:mid:<Message-ID>` (a message without one, `sioul:mail/<account>/<digest>`); its UID made from the project and the message, so that two devices make one task; tied to its message in `links.toml` (`how = "made"`), nothing of the mail's text copied |
| A call-back task, made by the calls' rules ([porch.md](porch.md), "Calls missed, and calls of yours that did not reach them") | `X-SIOUL-CALL-BACK:sioul:contact/<UID>` (whom it is for), `X-SIOUL-CALLS:<start in ms>-in,<start>-out` (its calls, each its start and way); once a call in which you spoke closed it, as "What closes by itself" does (below), `X-SIOUL-CALLED:<that call>`, kept when you open it again, after which no call closes it again |

- **Editing keeps the rest**: tasks are changed line by line, as contacts and events are; what Sioul does not edit (alarms, another application's fields) comes back exactly; a form saved unchanged writes nothing.
- **New lists** are created on the server with MKCALENDAR (RFC 4791 §5.3.1), new address books with an extended MKCOL (RFC 5689), at the next sync; until then they wait here, and a sync never deletes them. A list in the account `local` stays on this device.
- **Things are named by addresses**: `mid:<Message-ID>` for a message (RFC 2392); `sioul:task/<UID>`, `sioul:event/<UID>`, `sioul:contact/<UID>`, `sioul:note/<path>`, `sioul:draft/<id>`, `sioul:project/<id>` (`sioul:case/<id>` as written before), `sioul:budget/<budget>/<line>`.
- **Each link lives in the thing that makes it**: a task or an event (above), a note (a Markdown link, or its front matter: `task:`, `event:`, `mail:`, `links:`), a draft (its `links`), a budget line (its `links`). What none of them can hold (a message is never changed) goes in `$XDG_DATA_HOME/sioul/links.toml`. Links are read both ways: every thing shows what it points at and what points at it.
- **Time spent** is kept in plain files, one per month (`$XDG_DATA_HOME/sioul/time/2026-10.toml`), not in CalDAV: it is your record, not the task's. Each record says how its minutes were known (`kind`): measured by the timer, typed by hand, or measured then corrected by hand; one written before that was kept reads as unknown. The session running now is kept too, so closing the window loses nothing.

## The plan
- **Order**: Kahn's algorithm over the open tasks; a step waits for what its bigger task waits for, and a bigger task ends with its last step. Tasks that wait for each other in a loop are found (Tarjan's strongly connected components) and said: "These wait for each other: A, B. One of them has to go first."
- **The next step**, among the tasks free to start: the one started; then the one whose latest start comes first (its date, or a later task's, minus the work in between); then your PRIORITY; then the one that frees the most others; then the smaller.
- **Days**: each open task goes into the first days with room, in that order, never before today, never before what it waits for (plus its gap). Room is your hours ([areas.md](areas.md)), each kind for its own tasks: work hours for work, admin hours for your admin; admin without hours of its own goes to work hours, work without hours to admin hours; leisure has no hours, so what is only for leisure takes no room and waits for none. Within that room, what a day holds: each day is filled to 85 % of what it holds for you on each cost and in total, learned from your own days ([capacity.md](capacity.md); "as now" until your days say more, so that nothing changes at first). The events in them are taken out, with a pause of five minutes before and after each, and each step leaves a pause of five minutes after it; not an event cancelled, one marked free (`TRANSP:TRANSPARENT`: a holiday, "working from home"), nor an invitation you declined (your own `PARTSTAT=DECLINED`, by your accounts' addresses), which hold no time and still show in the agenda (`Occurrence::holds_time`, `plan::event_spans`; the same for the day's layout, meals moved past events, and what a day's events cost, `capacity::held_by_events`). A step up to an hour is never cut; a longer one is cut in parts of a quarter of an hour at least. Margins are not pauses: a task's room is its length plus the time kept before and after it. A task with margins is never cut (going there twice is not the same step): the plan lays it whole on the first day whose room holds it, or on a day of its own when no day's room does (`plan.rs`, `whole`, `alone`); the day lays it in one piece, its margins at both ends, in the first gap that holds it, or says it does not fit (`dayview.rs`). An event's margins are taken out before its pauses. No hours set at all: Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00, for everything. Today's room, and what today holds, start now and are scaled by the weather (haze 60 %, fog 30 %). Each task is laid at its corrected length, your estimate times how long tasks like it take you, and each day keeps free time for steps running long and half an hour for a slot of time for you ([capacity.md](capacity.md)). Days off and today once closed have no room; a task that needs an office goes only on days offices open. A task whose plan ends after its date says so once, calmly, with what would help: doing it sooner, making it smaller, handing it over.
- **Streams**: tasks tied by what they wait for or by being steps of the same task form a stream (union-find); "Not now" sets a whole stream aside for the day, and the next step comes from another stream.
- **Steps in order**: a bigger task's steps come together in the plan, where the best placed of them would go, and among themselves in their sequence (`X-APPLE-SORT-ORDER`, above). The sequence is soft: the first step that can be offered now is; a later one is free, never waiting, and ticking it first is not out of order. A step under way comes first; on a day that takes no heavy step, a heavy one comes after the others. A real wait stays a wait, written by you (`DEPENDS-ON`, `FINISHTOSTART`), never by the sequence: Tasks.org's provider would turn waits into steps on a phone ([compatibility.md](compatibility.md)). Two steps of one task are never "equal, picked": their order says which. A step without an estimate counts five minutes (`plan::STEP_MINUTES`), not a task's usual default; its length is never corrected by your past ratios, and a finished step teaches the ratios nothing, since the estimates of parts sum to more than the whole's (Forsyth & Burt 2008, [research/time-estimation.md](research/time-estimation.md)). Short steps of one task that follow each other in the plan (under a quarter of an hour, without margins, not pinned) are one run: each laid at its own length rather than a quarter of an hour, back to back, with one pause after the run (`Planned::in_run`, `run_next`); the day lays them so too. The List and the task's details show the steps in their sequence, those done in their place.
- **Waiting on others**: a task done starts the clock of what waits for it with a gap; the next one waits until then, and says until when.
- **Consistent constraints**: the next step is never a task that waits for one still open, nor one whose day is ahead, even when it was started too soon (it stays in "Started", and says what it waits for); never a call to an office that is closed now.
- **An office's hours**: a task that needs an open office follows, the most specific first, the hours typed on it ("mo-fr 09:00-12:00, 14:00-17:00", `X-SIOUL-OFFICE-HOURS`: old tasks, an office with no card), else those on the card of the first office it involves whose card has some, else offices' usual hours (`[[office_hours]]`, Monday to Friday 9:00–17:00). It is then proposed only while that office is open, planned on its days, never on a public holiday, and the note says when it opens next ("Offices' hours", below).
- **What it is for**: work, your admin, leisure, any of them together (`X-SIOUL-AREA`), else as its tags say; Now, the calm view, shows what fits the hours now ([areas.md](areas.md#calm-views-and-full-views)); The day, List, Board, Gantt and Search, the full views, show every task whatever the hour (`work::replan_now`'s `full_filter`, without `Filter::quiet`, its lists left out by your word alone, `agendas::hidden_by_hand`; the agenda filter there turns lists against "shown unless kept hidden", `agendas::turned_by_hand`, never the hours; The day draws today's layout, laid with every task: `work::today_laid_out`). Asleep, the cover (the status line's sentence and **Show anyway**) lies over Now alone; the tabs stay in reach, and the line under them notes a thought for later, the one field for it. A task made on the Tasks page stays in view until you leave the page, whatever the hours (since 3 November 2026: one made in quiet time vanished at once); while the hours would hide it, its row says when it comes back, "Out of these hours: back tomorrow at 09:00." (`taskview::Filter::fresh`, the next frame of the coming week whose hours keep it, `quiet::back_for`).

## Offices' hours
Designed on 20 November 2026 (what a person sees: the guide's [Contacts](../website/docs/guide/contacts.md#opening-hours)): an office's opening hours are written once, on its card, beside the number to call, and every task that involves the office follows them (`opening.rs`).

- **On the card**: lines of Sioul's own, vCard having no property for hours, each value in OpenStreetMap's `opening_hours` syntax, the part Sioul can count: weekly ranges, `24/7` and `PH off`. `X-SIOUL-OPENING-HOURS;X-SIOUL-SOURCE=typed;X-SIOUL-CHECKED=2026-11-20:Mo-Fr 09:00-12:00\,14:00-17:00\; PH off`; `X-SIOUL-PHONE-HOURS` (same parameters) only when it answers the phone at other hours; `X-SIOUL-HOURS-NOTE:<text>`, the free line for what Sioul cannot count (seasons, week parity, "by appointment"), shown, never computed. The source is `typed`, `service-public.gouv.fr` or `openstreetmap.org`; the day is when it was typed or checked; `X-SIOUL-HOLIDAYS=open`, a parameter of Sioul's own, says the office is open on public holidays (the form's tick; set by `24/7` and by a source's hours of their own on `PH`); a line without it is closed on them, as before. Written only through the contacts' writer (`contacts::with_hours`): the first line of each name replaced where it was, every other line's content as it was (the writer folds lines at 75 octets, as it always has); a second line of the same name (two cards merged, another program's) is read apart (`CardHours::extra`) and its text goes to the free line at the person's next save, never dropped unseen. A save whose hours count the same as the card's keeps the card's line as it is, a part Sioul cannot count included, and writes nothing when nothing else changed. At most 1,000 characters of hours and 2,000 of the free line are read (`opening::MOST_HOURS`, `MOST_NOTE`); beyond, nothing counted, shown cut. A value read back unescaped (another program's) reads the same.
- **Reading a value** (`opening::parse`): rules split at `;` outside quotes; each overrides the days it names, as the syntax says ("Mo-Fr 09:00-17:00; We 09:00-12:00"); `off` or `closed` with no hours closes the days it names, with hours cuts those hours out ("Mo-Fr 08:00-18:00; Mo-Fr 12:00-14:00 off": 8:00–12:00 and 14:00–18:00), as the specification says ("they will cut off the times they match"); `open` changes nothing; a comma before days adds a rule; hours without days are every day; `24/7`; ranges that touch are joined. Days in two letters, in English (`mo`…`su`) or French (`lu`, `ma`, `me`, `je`, `ve`, `sa`, `di`), or named in full in either language (`monday`, `lundi`, `mon`); hours as `9`, `9:30`, `9h30`, each range starting at or after the one before ("9-12, 1-5" is not counted, never read as the night). "Di" is Sunday in French and Tuesday in German: a value where it stands beside English days with no day only French has is not counted (`DOUBT_DI`, said in the form). **Public holidays**: a `PH` rule that ends with `off` or `closed` closes them, whatever shares its list or follows it; `PH` with hours opens them (the rule shown in the free line when it names `PH` alone); `PH` in a list of days is taken out and the days counted (`Sa,PH off`, `Mo-Fr,PH 09:00-17:00`). A rule that selects by what Sioul does not count (a month, a week number, a year, Easter, school holidays, the sun's hours, `Mo[1]`) goes to `rest` as written, and so does a sentence with no day nor hour in it; a quoted comment goes to `rest` and its rule's hours count; a fallback (`||`) goes to `rest`. A rule of weekdays and hours Sioul still cannot count (an open end `+`, a range that goes backwards, "and" between two ranges) leaves the whole value uncounted, kept whole, never half read; so does a value that leaves no hours at all ("closed", "24/7 off"), which a lookup keeps as the free line.
- **Which hours a task follows** (`opening::hours_for`, the one function the plan, the day, Now and the task's panel ask): its own; else the first office it involves (`CONTACT;ALTREP="sioul:contact/<UID>"`) whose card has hours Sioul counts, the phone's for a call when the card has them, else the opening hours (a card with phone hours only is followed by a call, never by an outing); else the usual ones. The cards come from the contacts each time the tasks are read (`opening::Cards::of`, in `quiet::Situation::now` and from it the plan's settings): changing a card plans its tasks again (`work::show_work`), cards that arrive from the sharing too (`changes::remade`).
- **Public holidays**: an office is closed on the days of the subscribed calendars of public holidays (Settings ▸ Hours), whether they count as your days off or not (`opening::public_holidays`), unless its hours say it is open then (`Hours::holidays_off`: the value's own `PH` rule first, then `X-SIOUL-HOLIDAYS=open`); usual hours are closed on them. Now skips them when it says when an office opens, the day lays nothing then, the plan puts nothing then. With no calendar of public holidays subscribed, the card says Sioul knows none and where to add one. The holidays are those of the person's calendars and the hours are read on the device's clock: an office in another country or time zone is typed as it falls on that clock (the guide says so).
- **Ticked by itself**: a call or an outing that is not ticked "Needs an open office" is ticked when an office whose card has the hours it would follow comes to it, by a tie or a save of its form, or when a task already tied to such an office becomes a call or an outing in its form (`opening::ticks`; `links::Loaded::holding`, `work::save`, `work::create`); and when a card gets hours Sioul counts for the first time by the person's act on this device (its form saved, a lookup picked), its open calls and outings not ticked yet are ticked (`opening::to_tick`, `work::tick_for_office`, from `offices::card_saved`). Never on a card's arrival by sync: the tick travels with the task (`offices::tests::a_card_arriving_by_sync_ticks_nothing`). Never again for an office already there, so an untick holds; an unticked task's panel says it does not follow its office's hours, with **Follow them**.
- **Call back…** on the card: the task "Call ‹name›", a call, the office attached, in the usual list, ticked when the card is an organisation's or has hours, else a plain call; a second press within a second makes nothing (`offices::call_back`). The task's panel says which hours it follows and whose ("Tax office: open until 12:00", "open all day" for `24/7`, `taskview::office_view`), read again each minute while it shows, opens the card, and calls its number as the card does.
- **Looked up** (`hourslookup.rs`, `sioul_sync::hours`): only on the person's press, and only for a card that says it is an organisation's (`Contact::card` "org": `KIND:org`, its name its organisation's, Apple's `X-ABShowAs:COMPANY`) or that already holds hours or a free line (`hourslookup::Asked::of`, the gate in the core); on any other card, the link "This is an office, a shop or a practice" marks it through the contacts' writer (`contacts::as_office`: `KIND:org` on a vCard 4.0 card, else its name written in `ORG` when that is empty, else `X-ABShowAs:COMPANY`, every other line kept), said with Undo as the contacts' other changes are: the card as it was kept thirty days with them (`duplicates::apply`, kind `office`; Duplicates' "Done lately"), put back by the status line's Undo or from there (`duplicates::put_back`), a card changed since set aside first. The dialog first shows the words that go and to whom, and what goes along (Sioul's name and version, the window's language to the map). One request to each source, both at once (the administration's directory for an address in France: a five-digit postal code with "France" after it, or its line last and the person's region France, or no address and the region France; never a name made only of small words; the map's search service, `[map] search`, OpenStreetMap's Nominatim by default, waiting its turn with the contacts' map), over https only, redirects included; each press numbered, its answer alone filling the list. The list's pick writes the hours with their source and the day, and the holidays' parameter when the source opens them; a source with no hours leaves the card's; what it cannot count is added to the free line; "kept on the card" is said only when something was written; a number or an address the card lacks is offered (the address built from its parts, `addressdetails=1`: street, postal code and town), never written by itself. In the demo, the answers are the tests' invented ones, read by the same code, and said to be examples. Sources and their shapes: [research.md](research.md), finding 86.
- **The map's search service** (`mapsearch.rs`): one setting, `[map] search` (Contacts ⚙, a shared setting that travels with the others), for the contacts' map, an event's map and the lookup: unset, OpenStreetMap's Nominatim; an `https://` address; `none`, which stops both. After a 403 or a 429 from it, it is asked nothing more until the next local day, for every use, kept in `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/map-search-wait` (the day and the address that refused; another address chosen meanwhile is asked), said once by what met it. Sioul is never switched from afar: this setting is the answer to Nominatim's rule that an application can be moved off it without an update.
- **An older Sioul** keeps the card's lines (its writer changes only its own fields) and reads a task as before: ticked, with the usual hours. A task's format does not change.
- **Not built** (phase 3 of the design): "Call back when open" from the calls' list; the hours in the agents' tools and the command line; a card in a read-only address book copied into one of yours (it says only that its hours cannot be written there).

## Pinned to a time
A task is pinned to a time by a **time block**: an event in a calendar, tied to the task both ways (`sioul_core::blocks`; the window's side, `crates/sioul-app/src/blocks.rs`).

- **Why an event.** An event shows in every calendar application, a phone's too, moves there like any other, and is a standard object. The alternatives were worse: a line of the task's own (`X-SIOUL-AT`, written for drags until blocks) is invisible to other applications; a time on the task's `DTSTART` breaks when its `DUE` is a date (RFC 5545 §3.8.2.3 wants the same value type, and Sabre, Nextcloud's server, refuses the task with a 415), and would change what "can start from" means.
- **The block**: `SUMMARY`, the task's title, kept in step (the task renamed in Sioul, at once; elsewhere, at the next re-plan; the block renamed in Sioul's event form renames the task); `DTSTART` and `DTEND` in UTC; busy, `TRANSP:OPAQUE`; `X-SIOUL-TASK:<the task's UID>`, which Sioul reads, and `X-SIOUL-LINK;LINKREL=related;X-SIOUL-REL="tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/rel/block-of";VALUE=UID:<the task's UID>` (RFC 9253 §8.2's LINK under Sioul's own name; LINKREL takes a registered relation or a URI: the URI, a tag URI (RFC 4151) as the task kinds, goes in `X-SIOUL-REL`, which a phone's account manager keeps quoted); for a repeating task, the turn it pins, `X-SIOUL-TURN:2026-10-09` (its date asked, else its day to start, when pinned). No `VALARM` unless Tasks ⚙ asks for one ("An alarm in each block"): Sioul reminds of a block as of any event ([reminders.md](reminders.md)), and a phone with its own calendar would remind twice; asked, it comes five minutes before (`TRIGGER:-PT5M`), in the blocks made or moved from then on.
- **Its length**: what the plan lays for the task (its corrected estimate, less the time spent), its margins aside, by whole five minutes, a quarter of an hour at least; "Do at…" changes it; a step dragged in the day keeps its card's length. The task's margins are kept around the block, never inside it.
- **Its calendar** (Tasks ⚙, "Time blocks go into"): unsaid, « Planned tasks » (« Tâches planifiées »), made at first use on the account of the task's list (`MKCALENDAR` at the next sync), its folder and its address `planned-tasks` on every device, so that two devices make one calendar; for a list kept on this device, or on an account Sioul makes no calendar on (Google), it is kept on this device. Or any calendar that can be written and holds events; a Google calendar adds its default reminders to every event it receives, a block too ([google.md](google.md)), and blocks there are not tried against Google yet.
- **Pinning**: "Do at…" in a task's details (a day, a time, a length), a step dragged in the agenda's Day or Week, or the block itself dragged in the agenda or in any calendar application. The task's block is moved, or made; the task linked to it and to it alone (`blocks::task_pinned`); "Undo" puts both files back unless something changed them since.
- **Seen**, quietly (the accent colour, never a warning's): in the task's details, near its title, a pin and its block's day and time in words ("Pinned: today, 14:00–14:45"; "Pinned: Thursday 8 October, 10:00–10:30"); a click opens the block in the agenda, its menu (right click, a long press, or ⋯) offers "Move…" ("Do at…" again) and "Let the plan place it". A row of the list and a card of the board show a small pin and its time ("14:00", another day "Thu 8 Oct 10:00"); Now's card the pin and when, when the next step is pinned; the day a pin on its card. A block over, the task not done, shows no pin: the task is back in the plan (Breeze's `pin` icon, bundled, `tools/bundle-icons.py`).
- **What the plan does** (`plan.rs`, `dayview.rs`): a task is pinned to its first block still to come, open, of its turn (`blocks::Blocks::of`); its other blocks wait their turn and hold nothing. A block of a task known here is never an event to the plan, the day or the record (`blocks::without_blocks`): its time, its margins and a pause around it leave its day's room once (`Settings::with_pins`); the task is laid on that day before the others, its block's minutes counted as its own (its load, a heavy step, the free time kept), whatever the room, the budgets or what it waits for; the free time around it filled as usual, everything after it re-planned at once. Longer than its block, the rest goes on from the next day. The day lays it in its block, its margins around it, its time in the accent colour. Now puts a step pinned more than a quarter of an hour ahead after the others. Meals move past a block as past any event (as the Health page moves them). Blocks are read in the plan's four weeks of events, and in the calendars made or chosen for them as far as the plan goes.
- **Elsewhere**: a block moved in another application moves the task at the next sync; deleted there, the task is no longer pinned. The task's link to it goes the next time Sioul pins it or leaves it to the plan: nothing is written in a task in the background, where another device's sync could race it into a conflict.
- **Over, the task not done**: it pins nothing; the task returns to the plan; the block stays as it was, with no "missed" and no count.
- **Done or dropped** (here, or found so at a re-plan): a block not begun is deleted, its time freed; one under way ends then (when it was done; dropped, now); one over stays, the record of when the work was done, and the day shows the task done there. Deleted: its blocks to come go with it, under one "Undo" (`mail::schedule_removals`).
- **"Let the plan place it"**: its blocks not begun deleted after ten seconds, one under way ending now, the task's link taken out at once; "Undo" puts everything back.
- **A repeating task**: a block pins one turn. A turn done here closes its blocks as above; one done elsewhere moves the task's dates on, so its block pins nothing, and goes when it is still to come. Its dates changed in Sioul's form: the block follows the new turn.
- **Set right at each re-plan** (`blocks::upkeep`), only what differs, so that nothing is written twice. A block whose task is not known here (not synced yet, deleted elsewhere) is an event like any other, and left alone.
- **`X-SIOUL-AT`**: read for compatibility (the plan and the day lay such a step today, as before), never written. One for today or later, on an open task, becomes a block once, and the line goes from the task.

## An appointment and its steps
A CalDAV file holds one kind of thing (RFC 4791 §4.1), so an appointment with steps is two files, tied both ways (`pairs`): the event stays a plain event (its guests, its place, its alarms), and a task, its title yours, holds the steps and the list. The task's link is `X-SIOUL-LINK;LINKREL=related;X-SIOUL-REL="tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/rel/appointment";VALUE=URI:sioul:event/<UID>`, the event's `X-SIOUL-LINK;LINKREL=related;X-SIOUL-REL="tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/rel/preparation";VALUE=URI:sioul:task/<UID>` (both forms read, RFC 9253's `LINK;LINKREL="tag:…"` too) (`VALUE=URI`: RFC 9253 §2 wants a `UID` value in the same collection, and the pair crosses a calendar and a task list). An event that cannot be written, or a meeting with others (a guest who is not you, or an organizer who is not you beside a guest: its server would tell them of any change, RFC 6638), keeps no line: the task's is enough, and the pair is found from either end (`pairs::preparation_of`). The links read as "Its appointment" and "Its steps and list" wherever ties are shown.
- **Tied** (`pairs::attach`): from the task's form ("Its appointment… (an event's title)"), the event's details ("Add steps and a list" makes a task titled as the event; "Use “Prepare: …” as its steps" ties a task already made from it, offered when one is tied to it and none prepares it), `sioul tasks attach`, the MCP's `attach_to_event`; "Make it an appointment" on a task with steps keeps the task and ties it to the new event (`pairs::appointment_for`, the MCP's `task_to_event` too, or with `keep_task`). Refused for a task done or one you only read, a task of a Google Tasks list (Google keeps no link: the tie would be one device's), a time block, another appointment's leg, an event another open task prepares already. Its DUE becomes the moment the appointment starts when it has none or a later one (`pairs::writable_due`; the appointment's day for one of a whole day, and nothing for one whose start cannot be read): an hour means "done by", and the plan places the task before it. Never a DUE a server refuses (Sabre, so Murena, RFC 5545 §3.8.2.3): the start day when the task starts on a later day, nothing when its start has a time at or after the appointment; the date it replaced comes back in the start's form (`pairs::put_back`: as kept without a start, its bare day beside a start date and never before it, none beside a start time). A link written before 7 November 2026 says the day it wrote (`X-SIOUL-DUE`), and keeps its date and that rule. A date of its own the tie never replaced (on or before the appointment's day) stays its own: nothing is written on the link, and it never follows. The moment written stays on its link (`X-SIOUL-DUE-AT`, in UTC; `X-SIOUL-DUE` for a day), and the date it replaced (`X-SIOUL-DUE-WAS`): the DUE follows the appointment while it says that moment exactly (another hour you gave it, or another day, is yours), always the earlier of the appointment's time and that date of its own, which stays on the link once it came back, so that a later move before it is followed again; a detach puts back the date it replaced, and tied again (the same appointment, or another after the first went) the day Sioul wrote stays Sioul's to change; tied again as it is, nothing is written. It follows only an appointment this device saw move since it last looked (`pairs::Seen`, `$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/pairs-seen.toml`, this device's own): a device that has the task's new date before the moved event never writes the old one back, and two devices that both saw the move write the same lines, which the sync takes for one (`checklist::merge_calendar`: they differ by their stamps alone). A write that fails is not tried again at once (`upkeep` says what it wrote), but at the next pass: the moved day is noted once each of its tasks was written or needed nothing. The day is the appointment's in this device's time zone: travelling, an appointment near midnight may change day, and its task's date with it.
- **Its steps**: one with a start of its own goes by its start. Otherwise the nearest date of its own up the chain decides (the step's, a bigger step's, the tied task's own, never the day the tie wrote, `pairs::own_date`): before the appointment's day, the step is free now, to be done by then; on that day or after, or none, the step waits for the day, as a follow-up cannot be done before the appointment. A candidate rule, open to correction.
- **The appointment gone** (deleted, cancelled, or not here yet: not synced): the task and its steps wait for a date (`Planned::parked`), offered nowhere, laid nowhere, said "Its appointment is not here: it waits for a date."; another appointment tied, they come back in their order. An appointment of an account switched off is still here (`pairs::appointments_here`). Two tasks tied to one appointment on two devices: the one the event's line names prepares it, else the oldest.
- **Changed into another kind**: an appointment made a task, or a task made an appointment, carries no end of a pair (`pairs::is_pair_link`): the task that prepared an appointment made a task is untied from it by the same rule as the ×, its DUE back (`pairs::untied_text`, in `appointments::retarget`).
- **Untied** (`pairs::untie`, one rule wherever it is asked: × beside the appointment in the task's form, "Undo this link" in a Related list (`links::Loaded::untie`), `sioul tasks attach <task> none`, `detach_from_event`): the task's link out, its DUE back to the date it replaced when it still says the day the tie wrote, and the event's line out when the event takes lines (never a meeting with others, never a calendar you only read). An event that keeps its line, or is not here, leaves the task a mark (`X-SIOUL-UNTIED:<the event's UID>`), so that the stale line never ties it back, nor shows the pair in a Related list (`pairs::line_ties`); tied again, the mark goes. No tie of any kind is written into a meeting with others (`links::Loaded::tie` writes it elsewhere).
- Each change is said with Undo in the window, and by the MCP's line.
- **Its date follows** by the one rule of what follows an appointment (`moves`, below): the task's link keeps the instant it was set for (`X-SIOUL-AT=<the appointment's start, UTC>`, written at each tie and each follow; a link written before it stands by the day it wrote). Tied again to the same appointment, nothing of the task changes, whatever this device's copy of the event says. Tied to another, the old event's line naming the task goes in the same change, where it may be written. A task moved into a Google Tasks list loses its tie (said first among what that list does not keep, "its tie to its appointment"), untied by the same rule.
- **What closes by itself** (`pairs::closing`, after each pass of the follow machinery): once the appointment is over (its end passed, and, for a calendar or a list that syncs, the appointment's calendar, the task's list and each step's alike, a pull begun after it went through whole, so that a move or a change made elsewhere has come; for one of this device's own that the sharing carries, its last exchange gone through whole), the open steps that are plainly its preparation are set aside (`CANCELLED`, never `COMPLETED`: Sioul never says you did them; marked `X-SIOUL-SET-ASIDE;END=<the appointment's end, UTC>:<its UID>`); a task with steps whose steps are all closed then closes, done when every step was done (no mark: done is never set aside), else set aside the same way, and never while it has a time block not begun (its block's time to come is kept; a block beyond the events read is found by its own file). Nothing is said, nothing counted. Stamped with the appointment's end, so two devices write the same bytes. **Never** a step that comes after it, one with a start, a date of its own on or after the appointment's day, or a time block (follow-ups by the plan's rule), one made, or made a step, once the appointment was over (its CREATED, or its place among the steps, `X-APPLE-SORT-ORDER`, after the end: a follow-up, never a preparation forgotten), one with open steps of its own, or one that carries that appointment's mark while open (brought back, whatever reopened it: Sioul, an agent, another program; a mark of another appointment it was tied to before holds nothing), each read from its file as it is; never for an appointment that ended before this device first ran this (`moves-seen.toml`'s `closing_from`, written once, and only from a clock at or after the build's commit time: the past at the install is no preparation forgotten), that repeats, is cancelled, lasts the whole day, is a meeting with others (whose time may have moved in a message not answered yet) or is not here.
- **Moved later, it gives back what it set aside**: an appointment whose end moves after the end its marks keep, and after now, reopens in the same pass what it set aside (`NEEDS-ACTION`, the mark taken out), stamped with the set-aside's own `END`, so two devices write the same bytes; a step only while its task is open or comes back with it (never an open step under a task done since); the task tied to it with its date following the appointment in the same write, not one pull late. It sets them aside again only once it is over again. Its files are read only when the appointment, as read, ends later than the mark's end. Never on its deletion: an appointment over, then deleted, happened.
- **The way back**: the appointment's card lists what it set aside under "Set aside after it", each with **Bring back** (open again, the mark kept as `;BROUGHT-BACK=TRUE`, never set aside again by that appointment, which another one it is tied to since may; a step's task set aside with it comes back too), with Undo. The task list with **Done too** lists the same, however old, each said "Set aside …, its appointment over" (without its date when its mark has no `END`) with **Bring back**; a set-aside row ticked open is brought back the same way, its task with it: the way back needs no card, the appointment deleted since. An older Sioul keeps the mark and sees a dropped step.
- **The reminder to leave**, with what to bring (part F's task half), came with part 7 of the unified agenda (29 October 2026): one per outing, at the usual time before its earliest leg, naming the leg and up to three unticked things of the preparation's list (`reminders::leaving`; [reminders.md](reminders.md), "Leaving").
- **Not yet**: a step dated by its appointment's day (`X-SIOUL-ANCHOR;GAP=P1D:BEFORE` on a step, the part of E6 cut: only its side is built).

## One time of a repeating event
A task can come before one time of a repeating event, as it does before a one-time appointment. The task's link names that time by a parameter, `X-SIOUL-OCCURRENCE`: `X-SIOUL-LINK;LINKREL=related;X-SIOUL-REL="tag:aurelienpierre.com,2026:sioul/rel/appointment";X-SIOUL-DUE-AT=20261110T100000Z;X-SIOUL-AT=20261110T100000Z;X-SIOUL-OCCURRENCE=20261110T100000Z;VALUE=URI:sioul:event/<UID>`. The value is the time's RECURRENCE-ID in one form (`agenda::Turn::rid`): its first start in UTC, or its day (`20261110`) for a whole day, whatever the file's own form. RFC 5545 §3.8.4.4 keeps that value when the time moves, so the tie follows a time moved. No standard names one time in a tie: RELATED-TO and RFC 9253 name a UID, and an unaware client must read an unknown RELTYPE as a parent (RFC 5545 §3.2.15), so the tie stays on Sioul's own link. A parameter, not a form of the address: an older Sioul reads the same address, a tie to the series, and keeps the line.
- **The event's file is not written**: the task's line alone holds the tie, found from either end, as for an event that cannot be written. So a meeting with guests is only read, and a calendar you only read can have tasks before its events. Several tasks may come before one time (`pairs::before_turn`), and "This appointment has its steps already" is left to ties to a whole event.
- **Tied** (`pairs::attach_turn`): the task form's field offers each repeating event by its next three times (`links::World::search_events`), each row saying "Tue 10 Nov 11:00" and its agenda; a row ties that time. The field searches every agenda, a person's too. An agenda shown for its availability alone is searched only when its titles are shown, and the mirrors of your busy times never are. The same search, in the same order (`links::Found::rank`: what comes before what was, then titles starting with the words, whatever the limit of 40 cuts), serves Link to…, whose row names the event once, by its next time, since a plain link names the whole of it. Named no time (`pairs::attach`, `sioul tasks attach` without `--on`, `attach_to_event` without `occurrence`), a repeating event ties its next time; a task tied to it already stays as it is, and a series with no time to come ties as a whole. "Add steps and a list" on an event's card ties the time that card shows. A file holding changed times alone, with no series (an invitation to some times of someone else's meeting, the series kept on its organizer's side), is an event too, counted as repeating: its times are offered by their RECURRENCE-ID and tied one by one (`agenda::event_ref`; before 10 November 2026 it was no event, and neither picker found it).
- **What shows**: the task reads "Before: Team meeting, Tue 10 Nov 11:00" (`pairs::turn_line`), and its rows the same (`taskview::due_text`). The card of that time lists it under **To do before**, that time alone, a changed time too (`pairs::AppointmentView::to_do_before`, by `agenda::Occurrence::rid`).
- **Its date** is that time's start, as for a one-time appointment above, and follows it by the one rule (`moves`), that time being followed on its own under `<UID>#<its name>` in `moves-seen.toml`. A date you gave it holds, and the tie stays. Its steps are free now: the rule that holds a step to its appointment's day is a follow-up's, and these come before.
- **Passed, the task not done**: it stays open. Its line turns to the past, "Was before: …"; no mark, and nothing counts (`Task::tie_passed`: no latest start in the plan, no time budget, no "date asked" line). One button, **Next one: Thu 12 Nov**, moves it, by the same rule as tying. It never moves by itself: it is about that meeting.
- **Cancelled, or timed away** (a time left out or marked cancelled, or a series given other times, so that its name matches nothing): the task keeps its date, its line says "That time is no longer in its agenda", **Next one** is offered, and nothing is deleted. The whole series deleted or cancelled parks it as any appointment gone.
- **A task done** keeps its line as a record, with nothing to move.
- **"Each time" is not built.** Repeating tasks come back through `tasks::set_status`, which works on a task's own text and its own RRULE, and knows no event. One live task showing the next time would need a rule copied from the event, which drifts when the event changes, or every way of closing a task (the window, the command line, the MCP, a bigger task closing with its steps) reading the event first; and a background pass reopening it when its time passes. That is a lifecycle of its own.
- **Google Tasks** keeps no link and discards the hour of a due (its API: "the time portion of the timestamp is discarded"). A task of such a list is never tied, and its form offers neither (`capabilities::task_fields_lost`, `due_hour`).
- A tie to a whole repeating event, made before one time could be named, stays as it was: it shows on each time of it, and its date does not follow.

## A date asked and its hour
A date asked (`DUE`) is a day, or a day and an hour. The form gives it an hour beside its day, **No hour** by default and the button back to it: a day alone never gets an invented hour (Apple Reminders assumes 9:00). An hour means "done by", never a slot: it holds no time, and draws no block.
- **Shown and counted back from it**: "By 30 October, 16:00: three weeks left" on the board, the list and Now (`taskview::due_words`); at its hour among the dates asked of you (`dated`, a small mark at the head of its day, as any date); in the reminder, a few working days before as ever, "Asked for Thursday 8 October, 16:00", which holds until that hour.
- **The plan honours it**: an hour makes the day it falls on the day to be done by only if that day's hours for the task hold it before that hour, else the last day before it with hours for the task (`plan::done_by`): that day orders the plan's latest start and says whether the plan is tight. The day lays a step with an hour first, the earliest hour first, in the gaps before it (`dayview::lay_on`). With no room before it, the step is laid where it fits, and the plan says it is tight.
- **What a server refuses is never written** (`tasks::dates_lines`, RFC 5545 §3.8.2.3, Sabre, so Murena): DUE and DTSTART share a value type and DUE is not before DTSTART. An hour on a date beside a day to start takes DTSTART out, and the start is kept by Sioul in `X-SIOUL-START`, no hour invented for it either. The form says a date asked before its start, or at the very moment of it, as it says its other errors, and writes nothing (`tasks::dates_refusal`, `work::save`). A start and a date asked on one day stand: the day holds both. Other writers (a drag, an agent, an import) go through the same writer, which moves the start rather than ever writing a refused file.

## Getting there and coming back (legs)
A leg is a ride or a walk around an appointment: an event of its own, in your calendar, that moves with the appointment (`anchors`). It says what it is in its own file, since the appointment may be one you cannot write:
`RELATED-TO;RELTYPE=PARENT:<the appointment's UID>` and `X-SIOUL-ANCHOR;GAP=PT25M;TIMETABLE=TRUE;WAS=20261029T130000Z:BEFORE` (`BEFORE` or `AFTER`; `GAP` from the leg's end to the appointment's start, or from its end to the leg's start; `WAS` the appointment's start, or end, it was last set for; `TIMETABLE` its time comes from a timetable), `X-SIOUL-UNCONFIRMED:<UTC>` once a timetable leg was moved with its appointment, `X-SIOUL-CANCELLED-WITH:<UID>` on a leg cancelled with it. Other programs see a plain event and keep the lines.
- **Made**: "Add a ride or a walk…" in the appointment's card (the event form, its times before the appointment, before or after it, from a timetable or not), or the MCP's `add_event` with `anchor`. In the appointment's calendar when it can be written and is no Google calendar (which may not keep the lines), else the calendar for new events; in the appointment's projects (for agents' consent too). Never for an appointment that repeats or lasts the whole day. Untied by its card's ×: an event of its own again.
- **One follow machinery** (`moves`, for the legs and the tied tasks' dates alike): this device keeps the instants it saw each followed appointment at (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/moves-seen.toml`, version 2; the older `pairs-seen.toml` is no longer read nor written), and each doubt by the instant its follower was set for (a follower written later has its own). A follower consistent with the appointment needs nothing; one set for the instant this device saw it at, the appointment elsewhere now, follows (it moved, seen here); one set for an instant this device never saw (another device wrote it first, the appointment's copy not here yet) waits, writing nothing, until a pull of the appointment's calendar begun after the doubt goes through whole (`vdir::State::pulled`; for a calendar of this device's own that the sharing carries, part "lists", the sharing's last exchange gone through whole, so that a leg whose file came before its appointment's waits for it; at once for a calendar that neither syncs nor is carried), after which the copy here is the current one. A move is noted only once each follower was written or needed nothing. Each follow reads the follower's file as it is now. Travel is not a move: instants are absolute.
- **How a leg follows** (`anchors::upkept`): a leg already over stays as it was; a leg before follows the start, a leg after the end, by as much in absolute time (its length and distance kept; across a change of the clocks too); a timetable leg is then marked unconfirmed, said plainly: "Moved with the appointment; the timetable was not checked." A leg is where its anchor set it within the minute its `GAP` is written to (whole minutes), so a leg whose times carry seconds follows too. A leg moved by hand elsewhere (anywhere but its own `WAS − GAP`) keeps its time, its side and distance taken again from the appointment as it is, its doubt gone, once a pull begun after went through (the appointment may have moved with it, its copy not here yet); both at once (two devices, offline), the hand wins. The appointment cancelled, its legs to come are cancelled with a mark, and come back with it. Nothing for an appointment gone, repeating or of a whole day, nor a leg of a calendar you only read or a meeting with others. The stamps written are the appointment's, so two devices write the same bytes; running twice writes nothing.
- **Gestures** (`anchors::gesture`): a drag of the appointment, its form, or the MCP's `update_event` move its legs in the same change, through `Change::write` (which takes back what it wrote when a file fails), under one Undo: "Moved with it: Bus 24 (timetable not checked)." The MCP's `legs: "stay"` keeps them where they are. A leg dragged by hand is set from its appointment at once. Deleting the appointment takes its legs to come under the same Undo (those over stay); the MCP's `legs: "keep"` keeps them.
- **Read** (`anchors::mark`, inside `agenda::occurrences`, so the plan, the day, quiet time, pauses, health and the reminders all read it): while an appointment has legs, its time kept before and after comes from them (from the earliest leg's start, to the latest leg's end, each where it will be once followed), its own margins back without legs. A leg counts only within three hours of the appointment, or of a leg of it that counts (`anchors::LEG_REACH`): a train the evening before stands as an event of its own, busy itself, the night between free, told its own reminders. "Leave at" is the first leg's start. A leg and its appointment, or two legs of one, are one outing: never said to clash (`overlaps`), while another event within it still is said too close. One reminder for the outing, the appointment's, counted from its first leg, with "(timetable not checked)" when a leg was moved by arithmetic; a leg's own "before" and "working day before" are not told while its appointment is here (its own alarms still come).
- **Drawn**: in the Day and the Week a quiet line in the appointment's lead, in its column, taking none; named on the appointment's line in What comes and a phone's week list ("Leave at 13:10 · Bus 24 · Next: …"); not in the Month. Show's "Each ride or walk as an event of its own" draws them as events. The appointment's card: **Getting there** (its legs before it), **Coming back** (its legs after it, then the steps that come after it); a leg's card: "Before Doctor (14:00) · moves with it", its doubt, × to untie it.
- **A leg that is a step's time** (an event with `X-SIOUL-TASK` and its `block-of` link too; the step's `time-block` link back, `anchors::leg_for_step`): the leg form's **Its step** (one of the preparation's open steps with no time of its own), or the MCP's `add_event` with `anchor` and `step`; the leg and the step in one change. Never deleted, ended or retitled by the blocks' upkeep, nor taken out with its step; the plan counts it as the step's time. Offered only once every other device that counts says its Sioul keeps such a leg (`devices::Entry::legs`, `share::legs_as_steps`; with no sharing set up, no other Sioul is known): an older Sioul (679f96a) would take such a leg out once its step is done, and give it the step's title. Until then the form says why, and the MCP refuses `step`. The answer is found by the sharing's own job after each exchange (`share::legs_as_steps`), kept in a small file of this device's (`anchors::steps_offered`), which the leg form and the MCP read, never the folder on the window's thread; with no sharing set up, the window says yes at its start.
- **A step after its appointment** (`X-SIOUL-ANCHOR:AFTER` on a step; its menu's "After the appointment"): laid forward from the appointment's end and its coming back, said under Coming back, never dropped once the appointment is over. Other steps come before it, laid back to back so the last ends when leaving begins.

## Composed tasks
A task with steps is a composed task: nothing says it but its steps (`composed`). What changes what a thing is goes through `composed`, the same for the window, `sioul tasks parent` and the MCP (`update_task`'s `parent`, `convert_item`), each change worked out as the files it writes, refused with its reason, then written once, said with Undo (`agents::say`, whose Undo puts back only files still as they were left):
- **A step of another** (`demote`; the panel's "A step of…", a list's menu "Make it a step of…"): never of itself nor of one of its own steps; never a task of a list you only read, nor one done. A step stays in its bigger task's list: from another list it moves there with its own steps through the move journal (`vdir::move_item`), what that list would not keep asked first (`capabilities::task_losses`). Not into the GitHub list, written by its sync alone: steps of an issue stay in your own list. Its waits, projects, blocks and links stay; it goes after the other steps (`next_in_sequence`). Google Tasks keeps one level of steps: a step of a step there, or a task with steps made a step there, is refused.
- **A task of its own** (`promote`): its PARENT line goes, and a CHILD line its bigger task holds for it (another program's way); its place stays.
- **An item made a step** (`item_to_step`) or **a task** (`item_to_task`, tied to what held it by a `related` link): its words the title, its detail the notes, its tick kept (done: done; not needed: dropped), the item taken out of the list.
- **A step folded into the list** (`step_to_item`): an item at the end of the bigger task's list, its notes its detail, its tick kept; the step and its time blocks to come taken out. A step holding more than words and a tick (an estimate, a wait or what waits for it, steps, a block, dates, a kind, links, people, tags, margins, costs, a place, projects of its own beyond its bigger task's, its "How was it?" ratings, what it is for, its billing, what it takes, time noted for it) says what would go first (`beyond_words`), and goes on when asked again.
- **Breaking a step down** opens the step with its line to add steps: depth has no limit in the files; the window shows two levels at once.
- **Closing with its last step** (`closing_with`, D16): a bigger task without an estimate of its own, without an open wait, not coming back, that can be written, is done once none of its steps is open and one of them is done (all dropped, nothing was done); further up too. Only a step done for good closes it (`done_for_good`, its written status): a repeating step's turn done comes back open, and closes nothing. An agent closes only the bigger tasks it may change, never one kept from it, nor names it. The window says "Done. “Going to the doctor” is done too." with Undo, which reopens the step and the bigger tasks; the command line and the MCP say it in their answer. A bigger task with an estimate of its own keeps its own tick, five minutes in the plan.
- **In the task views** (composed tasks §7.2): **Now** shows one card, its reason first when it is a step ("Second step of “Going to the doctor”." and, its task preparing an appointment still to come, "It comes before Bus 24 at 13:10.": the earliest leg, else the appointment; never for a step after it), and "Then" its next step; no count of what is left. **List, Board and the Gantt** fold a task with steps into one row, card or bar ("Going to the doctor · Thu 29 Oct 14:00 · next: Brush teeth": its appointment's time when it prepares one, then its next step), its arrow showing its steps in order under it; the fold is the default, the tasks opened kept in `tasks-view.toml` (`opened`), never in the task; a search opens them all; **Done too** still lists what an appointment set aside under a fold. The Board puts a folded task in the column of its step furthest on (started, else free to start, else waiting), lists its done steps only once it is done, and moves it by its steps, never by a drag of its own. Agents and `sioul tasks` see every row. **The details** put the appointment at the top, with "Leave at 13:10 · Bus 24" under it, then the steps in order, then the notes with their checklist; Edit turns the steps into drag handles.
- **Not yet**: a top from outside (a GitHub issue) closed there does not offer to let go of its steps; they stay open as ordinary tasks (D21).

## Checklists
A task's or an event's notes may hold a checklist (`checklist`): GitHub task list lines, read wherever they are in the notes, outside fenced code. Not `STYLED-DESCRIPTION` (RFC 9073), which asks `DESCRIPTION` to be marked derived, nor a property of Sioul's own: one truth, readable everywhere.
- **Read, and ticked where it is read**: drawn in the task's details and the event's (`description::description_html_tickable`), each box a link, `sioul-check:<its line>`; a tap toggles it (to do or not needed → done → to do), the box's menu (right click, long press) sets done, to do or not needed. A tick changes one character of the notes, and their `DESCRIPTION` line alone is written (`checklist::ticked`, `tasks::apply`, `agenda::apply`). The line's item is checked again before it is ticked: a list changed meanwhile is said, never ticked by a guess.
- **Only your own**: a task of a list you write to, an event of a calendar you write to that is no meeting with others (a guest who is not you, or an organizer who is not you beside a guest). An invitation's list, or one of a meeting you organise, is not ticked here: its server would send the change to every guest. Its boxes show, for reading.
- **Written**: by hand in the notes, or with "Add a list to tick" under the notes field (the task's form, the event's dialog), which adds `- [ ] `. An agent writes it with `set_checklist`, in Sioul's form (`checklist::written`): the free text, a blank line, the items of no group, then each group, its name and a colon on a line of its own; an item written again without a state keeps its tick.
- **Merged**: changed on two devices by their ticks alone (`checklist::merge_calendar`: every component's lines alike but for the stamps and the notes' marks), the server's version and yours merge at the sync, each item at the stronger of its marks, done before not needed before to do, and the merge goes back to the server at the next sync. Anything else changed on both sides is the usual conflict: yours set aside ([database.md](database.md)).
- **Google Tasks** keeps notes of 8,192 characters at most (`capabilities::GOOGLE_NOTES`): a longer change is said and not written (the window's form, the MCP's).
- **Not yet**: a list that cannot be written where it is (a list you only read, an invitation) is not moved into a note tied to it; the agenda's card and the leaving reminder do not show it yet (composed tasks, parts D and F).

## Places
- **Where you stopped**: one line, left whenever something interrupts you (a pause to move taken, a meal's or the night's notice and its **Options…**, the pause to move's notice and its **Where I stopped…**, the end of the day, a timer stopped, or **New ▸ Where I stopped…** at any time), shown again at the top of the Porch and of this page, and in the focus window, until "Done" (`sioul_core::stopped`, `StoppedCard.qml`; carried to your other devices). Stopping then costs no fear of forgetting where you were: a cue to resume cuts the time to get back into a task (Trafton et al. 2003; Leroy & Glomb 2018). A timer stopped without a word keeps the line left at its pause.
- **The time running, in the system's notifications**: while a session runs, one notification says its task and since when ("Since 14:02, 25 min chosen"; paused, "Paused, 12 min so far"), with **Pause** (**Go on**) and **Stop**, the focus window's own buttons; a click on it opens the task. It follows the session from wherever it changes (the window, the notification, another device, `sioul focus`), after each reading of the tasks and at the window's minute (`timenote.rs`). On a Linux desktop, the desktop's (`sioul_sync::notify::ongoing`, on a D-Bus connection of its own, which hears its buttons): resident, without sound, at normal urgency, for Plasma shows a low one nowhere by default; where the server keeps what it shows (Plasma's history, GNOME's tray), it goes there after its popup, buttons alive, elsewhere it never expires; taken away when Sioul quits. A session bus that does not give its connection (a stall), or loses it, does not lose it for the session: its connection is made again, five seconds apart, what changed meanwhile shown then (review of battery part C, L4). A notification server that answers late never gets it twice: while its Notify is not answered, none other is sent, its late answer gives its number, and what changed meanwhile goes in its place; closed by that number (the fourth review's L1; `notify::tests::a_lasting_notice_answered_late_is_never_shown_twice`); taken away before that answer comes, it is closed once it comes, half a minute at most (`CLOSE_LATE`; the fifth review's L4; `notify::tests::reminders_keep_their_word`). On Windows and macOS, none yet: `notify::ongoing` does nothing there, and the focus window alone shows the time. On a phone, Android's, with a chronometer, its buttons working without the window ([android.md](android.md), "The time running").
- **Now**: the next step and why, its estimate and where you stopped, "Start", "Done", "Not now", "What makes it hard?"; after that, the next one in a line; folded: two other choices, what is started, what you might do if you want, what got done this week.
- **A timer on any task, at any hour** (time for every area, the owner's word of 18 November 2026): **Start** on Now, on a task's card, and in a row's menu in List, Board and Gantt (`taskStartMenu`, the card's durations), whatever the task is for; `work::focus_start` asks nothing of the hours or of the area. Its time counts in the Time page and in the plan's ratio as work's does ([capacity.md](capacity.md)).
- **List**: every open task, a bigger task followed by its steps, in the plan's order, grouped by project or by list; a search; done tasks on request; the optional ones last. "With a date asked" (`by = "deadline"`, 8 November 2026): only the tasks with a date asked, one group, the nearest first (a whole day before its hours), each on its own row, the done ones of the last two weeks after them on request (`taskview::by_deadline`).
- **Board**: free to start, started, waiting, done (two weeks). Cards move by dragging, with a mouse or a touchpad (`acceptedDevices`): to "Started", to "Done", back to "Free"; on a touch screen a drag scrolls the board. "Waiting" is decided by what each task waits for, and each card says what. More than three started: one line asks whether to finish or park one.
- **Gantt** (named Calendar until the unified agenda's part 5; `task-mode-timeline`, "timeline" in the code and `sioul tasks timeline`), on demand: each open task on its days, the date asked as a small diamond, days without room shaded, one project or all.
- **A task, open on the right, its details first**, from any view: a click (list, board, calendar, the day), "Details" in its menu (right click, or a long press on a touch screen: lists and the calendar), "Details" on Now's card. Nothing starts: start, done, not now; what matters in words; its tags, steps (ticked there), what it waits for and frees; its fields in words, only those said (`TaskPanel.facts`); its steps in their order (with a mouse, dragged in its form; **Move up** and **Move down** in a step's menu, a right click or a long press; Alt+↑ and Alt+↓ on a step), its notes, their Markdown drawn safely (`description::description_html`, `Description.qml`: HTML as words, no picture drawn, links to the web, an address or a number only, opened outside Sioul once their address is shown, task lists as boxes; an event's notes the same); everything tied to it, and "Write an email" (a draft to the people it involves, linked both ways) or "Make a note". "Edit" at the top turns the details into its form, "Details" back: its steps and one more in a line, what it waits for (removable, one more found by its title, with "Then wait": a gap in days or weeks for the next one added, written as `sioul tasks import` writes it, `tasks::set_gap`; each wait shows its gap, `taskview::gap_words`), and under "More" every field (dates, length, margins, the costs' tiles, what it takes, project, billed, kind, area, office hours, how it comes back, list, notes in Markdown), each saved as it changes; a change made right after another goes on from it (`change()` keeps what it saved until the task is read again). Done, its details offer "How was it?" (below).
- **A new task opens in full**: New ▸ A task, "New task" beside the line at the top of the page, and Add ▾ ▸ A task on any card open the panel on a new task's form (`newTaskForm`, `TaskPanel.startNew`), every field unfolded, the title focused. From a card it starts with what that card gives, as a task made from it always did: a message's subject, the message linked (`via`), its sender and its project; for an event, "Prepare: …", its day as the date asked, the event linked, its projects; anything else, its title and a tie (a contact involved, a project, a note describing it, anything related). The fields are kept in the form until the title is given, by Enter or by leaving the field in any way (another field, Close, Escape, Back); the task is then made with all of them (`save_task` with no UID), in the list the form shows: the usual one (Tasks ⚙, "New tasks go into"), else the first made for tasks, unless another is chosen there. The panel then goes on as the task's form. Closed without a title, nothing is made; a task made by mistake is deleted like any other, "Undo" offered. Until the task is read back (the plan is computed again on a thread, a moment), the form keeps what is changed and saves it then. Asleep, on Now, Now shows anyway, as its "Show anyway" does. Why the title makes it: a task file needs a title, and making it at once keeps every later field saved as it changes, as for any task; nothing waits in a draft that a closed window could lose.
- **How was it?** A task done, whichever way (a list, its panel, the focus window: the backend's `task_done` signal), the status line offers "How was it?" while it says the task is done; a done task's details offer it too (`HowWasIt.qml`, `FeltRatings.qml`). The same tiles, what was foreseen shown as a thin mark on each gauge and nothing faint proposed; only what is tapped is kept as felt (`set_felt`: today's rating written alone, from the file as it is), never copied from the forecast, whose bias it would bring back (research criteria 6 and 7). Ignored, nothing changes; never asked twice, never counted.
- **One line makes a task**, from any place on the page: "Call the tax office tomorrow ~15m #taxes {30/10}". At the end of the line, in French or English: the day to start ("tomorrow", "vendredi", "next week", "dans 3 jours", "30/10", "30 octobre"), `~` how long, `#` a project or a tag, `{…}` the date asked; "@day" anywhere, and "@call", "@write", "@online", "@out", "@read", "@think", "@make" (or "@appel", "@écrire", "@enligne", "@dehors", "@lire", "@réfléchir", "@faire") for its kind. Without one, a first word that says it plainly gives the kind ("Call…", "Remplir…"); "Ask…" says nothing, as it could be a call or a message. What was understood shows as chips before Enter. Enter makes it, in the usual list, and opens it; in quiet time it is noted for when work comes back instead (its day to start set then), out of sight.
- **One kind, one category**: two choices at the top of the page show only those tasks in every view, Now included ("the next call"), Search aside, which starts on them and keeps its own ("Search", below); the plan stays whole, so a call that waits for a message still waits. The choices (and the list's grouping, done tasks, one project) are kept between sessions (`$XDG_STATE_HOME/sioul/tasks-view.toml`); searches are not.
- **The agenda filter, for lists**: "Agendas" beside them lists the task lists as the agenda's filter lists its calendars, the same filter, as the research asks of every view, the Gantt included ([client.md](client.md#several-agendas); research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 17): a list it leaves out (a work list outside work hours, one turned off by hand) has its tasks in no view, Now included, the plan whole (`taskview::Filter::lists_off`). A list kept read only, or for its availability, is never written: its tasks open for reading.
- **A child's list** ([client.md](client.md#childrens-events)): its tasks carry who takes each (`X-SIOUL-TAKEN-BY`); one sits in your plan and Now only when you take it (`Task::elsewhere`, read in `tasks::all`, left out of the plan's open tasks), one someone else takes, or nobody yet, stays on the child's page with its holder's name, never in your plan (research/agendas.md §6, recommendation 7, after Ciciolla and Luthar 2019: feeling solely responsible is what weighs).
- **Kinds and categories are yours** (Tasks ⚙): a kind is renamed in place, taken away (tasks keep it) or added (`[[tasks.kind]]`, its id written in the task's concept, "@" and its name in one line); a category is renamed on every task that has it (a name already used merges them) or, after a second word, taken off them all.
- **Notes**: the notes folder, read as Obsidian reads a vault: `[[wikilinks]]` (found by name anywhere, the same folder first, then the shortest path, then aliases), Markdown links, `#tags`, front matter, links back. Nextcloud Notes' folder works as well: its categories are folders, and its `.txt` notes (its default) are read and written as Markdown like `.md` ones, keeping their extension (`notes::kind_of_file`, `notes::text_stem`); new notes are `.md`. Search first, by name and by text; then, by folder, the notes changed lately and the tree of folders, folded until opened, or one dated list; one type or all ("Notes: the search, the outline, the made day", below). A note opens read, its links followed with a click wherever they lead; editing is one switch away. Unticked `- [ ]` lines are one click from becoming tasks, linked to their note.
- **Pictures, PDFs and sounds are notes too**: listed beside the Markdown files (`png`, `jpg`, `gif`, `webp`, `svg`; `pdf`; `ogg`, `opus`, `mp3`, `m4a`, `wav`, `flac`…), opened in place (a picture fitted to the page, a PDF's pages with QtPdf, a sound with play, pause and where it is), and tied like any note (`sioul:note/<path>`). `![[scan.png]]` shows the picture inside a note, at most as wide as the page; any other embed is a link. "Record an audio memo" records into `<notes>/memos/` as Opus, the microphone open only while it records, and opens it as a note.
- **Renaming and deleting notes** (right click): a new name takes the notes that name it along (`[[name]]`, `![[name.png]]`, Markdown links by path, `sioul:note/` addresses), and the tasks and ties that link to it; deleting moves it into the vault's `.trash`, as Obsidian does, "Undo" offered.
- **Lists, calendars and address books** (Tasks ⚙, Agenda ⚙, Contacts ⚙): renamed in place, here and then on the server (`PROPPATCH`); an empty one deleted, here and then on the server, unless something was put in it there meanwhile (`DELETE`); one holding anything stays.
- **Everything made from everything**, linked both ways: a task, an event or a note from a message; a note for an event (dated, its guests listed), then sent to the guests; a task to prepare an event; a task from a note's line; an email from a task. Every message, event, contact, task and note shows what it is tied to.
- **Links by hand**: every card ends with "Add ▾" (a task, an event, a note, a message, or a sender into the contacts), "Link to…" and "Ties" (the chain of ties, below), which finds anything by a few words of its title, of one kind or all (`World::search`). Right click on a task, an event, a note, a contact, a message, a Porch line or a budget line offers the same; right click on a tie undoes it. A tie is written where it can be held (`Loaded::tie`): in the task when one end is a task (so the server and your other devices carry it), else in an event whose calendar can be written and that is no meeting with others (`Loaded::writable_event`, `agenda::meeting_with_others`; a note made for an event too, `Loaded::tie_into_event`), else in a note's front matter (`links:`), else in `links.toml`. Undoing takes it out wherever it is written, but from a meeting with others, which is never written: a tie an older build left there stays, and that is said ("This tie is written in a meeting with others…", `Loaded::kept_in_meeting`); a step, a wait, a guest or a link in a note's text is changed where it is, and that is said.

## Notes: the search, the outline, the made day

20 November 2026, on the owner's request of that day: "search into files content and titles, and the outline of the docs from headings in the right sidebar on desktop/tablet or at the top of the file on phone, into a collapsible section", and browsing "by date created/modified, type". Code: `sioul_core::notes` (`Vault::find`, `outline`, `front_made`, `Births`, `dated`), `crates/sioul-app/src/notesview.rs` (the window's lists and questions), `NotesPage.qml`, `NoteOutline.qml`. Tests: `notes::find_tests` (`notes_find_tests.rs`), `notesview::tests`, `tst_notesfind.qml`, `tst_notespage.qml`.

- **One search** (`Vault::find`): a note is found when its title, path and tags hold every word typed (`text::fold`: case and accents aside), or, for a text note (`.md`, `.txt`), when its title, path, tags and text hold them together. Found by the name first, then by the text, each the latest changed first. One found by its text carries its line: the first line that holds every word its name does not, else the first that holds the first of them, on one line, cut to 90 characters around the first word (`line_holding`, `cut_line`); the window draws it as plain text. A text is read from its file, at most `TEXT_NOTE_BYTES` (`search_text`, the bytes that are no UTF-8 as "�"); one that does not read now is found by its name alone, and nothing is asked. PDFs and sounds are not searched inside: when the type searched holds one, the list says in one quiet line that they are found by their names only (`names_only`). Type narrows what is found (`Kinds`).
- **Read again at each search, never kept**: the window asks the names at once, on its thread (`notesview::search`), then, after 300 ms without typing, the texts on a thread of its own (`search_texts`). The notes are read whole at each load already (`Vault::open`, at each change): keeping their texts would hold them twice while two loads overlap, a phone's memory with them. Measured on a made-up vault (`notes_search_measure`, an ignored test, release build, a computer's SSD, files in the system's cache): 3,000 notes, 12.0 MB of text, 0.5 to 45 KB each, half of them with accented words. `Vault::open`, the load made at each change, 228 ms; the names alone, 2 ms; the texts read again, 57 ms when one note holds the word, 78 ms when every note does (85 ms for two words); the texts kept in memory, 14 and 34 ms, holding 18.3 MB; the outline of a 45 KB note, 0.6 ms; a dated list, 1.5 ms. Reading the files is most of the cost now that ASCII text is lowered at once (`folded`; before it, folding took 130 ms of the 158): keeping the texts would save some 45 ms after a 300 ms pause, for 18 MB held, twice while a load overlaps another. A phone, several times slower, should take a few tenths of a second for such a folder, under "still looking" (not measured on a phone).
- **Each answer to its question** (`notesview::Gate`): words typed, Show or Type changed ask a new question, numbered; each answer carries its number and a ticket taken when it begins, and fills the list only while its question is the last asked and no answer begun after it is shown. The texts' reading stops as soon as another question is asked. While words are typed and the texts not read yet, the answer says `looking`, and the list says it is still looking. The notes read again (any change) list again for the question asked now, without a new number; with words typed, the whole answer comes once the texts are read, the names-only list never shown in between (the list does not flicker).
- **Show and Type** (`notesview::Show`, `notes::Kinds`): one line of two boxes under the search, bound to the column's width, their names shown when the line has room (`choices.named`), each box named for a screen reader. ## Notes: a long note opens at once

21 November 2026, on the owner's word of that day: "The rendering of long .md notes is terribly long in the GUI, that shouldn't happen with Rust + Qt C++". - **Where the time went**: Rust reads a note and makes its HTML in milliseconds. Qt's text document shapes and lays out every line of a rich text before it shows the first, on the window's thread: 0.87 s for a note of 208 KB set into one `TextEdit`, and 0.4 to 0.7 s again at each width change, each new set. A text appended in slices is laid out whole at each slice, so that does not help.
- **The note as a list of its blocks**: Rust cuts the note where Markdown does, from the events `markdown_html` writes (`shown_events`, one function for both, every rule on HTML written in a note, pictures from elsewhere and tick boxes with it): a heading, a paragraph, a code block, a quote, a table, a rule, a block of HTML; a list at the top is cut into its items, each with what it holds (its nested lists), in a list of its own that keeps its number (`<ol start="4">`). The blocks joined, each item's list said once, are `markdown_html`'s, to the byte (the tests compare them on a corpus). The page shows them in a `ListView` (`reuseItems`, `cacheBuffer` 600): only those in sight and a little around are laid out, each delegate one `TextEdit`, nothing else at work in it, used again for the next block scrolled to. A block's text depends on the width only when it holds a picture (`fitted`).
- **The same to the eye**: Qt leaves between two blocks of one document the larger of the margins that meet, none at a document's edges. Each block gets that space under it (`gapBelow`): above, a heading's own (18, 16, 14 points for the first three levels), none for a table nor for an item of a list drawn tight, else 12; under, none for an item its own words or a nested list end, else 12. Rust says the elements a block's first and last lines are drawn in (`kind`, `end`). Qt gives a list's bottom margin to its last item, before a list nested in it: an item followed by another of its list is drawn without it (`blockText`). Measured on Qt 6.11 (every pair of kinds); `tst_notesblocks.qml` holds each block's first line where the whole note drew it, to half a point. Left as they are: a few pairs Qt lays out its own way (a sixth-level heading or a rule right after a table, a rule, a sixth-level heading or a nested list: Qt puts no space there; a code block right before a table).
- **The editor takes the text when Edit is pressed**, and lets it go when reading again: while reading, no item holds the whole text. It still lays the whole text out when Edit is pressed (0.2 to 0.6 s for 208 KB); not in this part.
- **A change elsewhere leaves the note alone**: at each change of the notes folder the page asks the stamp of the note open (`noteStamp`: its file read and hashed, nothing else), and reads the note again only when it differs from the one its blocks were made from (`shownStamp`): a sync, a tie, the plan made again set nothing. The button that sends a note to an event's guests follows the ties as listed beside the note (`RelatedList`), which follow every tie made or undone, the note's text unchanged.
- **What it costs**: a selection made with the mouse stays within one block. Ctrl+A in the reading view selects the whole note (its ground in the selection's colour) and Ctrl+C then copies its Markdown text (`copyText`); a click or another key ends it; Edit gives the whole text as before. The scroll bar's size is the list's estimate until the blocks were seen (`ListView` knows the height of what it has laid out only). A note whose text could not be saved stays in the editor when Read is pressed, the status line saying why (before, Read went back to reading and the typed text was read over).
- **Measured** (21 November 2026, a computer, Qt 6.11, offscreen, the page with an invented note of 617 KB, 2,306 blocks, 369 headings): Rust makes its HTML in 4 ms and its blocks in 5 ms. Before, the first frame came after 3.2 s, a change elsewhere in the folder froze the window 1.0 s, a width change 5.2 s. Now, the first frame after 0.21 s (0.07 s of it without the outline's 369 lines: those are now the larger part), a change elsewhere 5 ms, a width change 12 ms, a jump to a heading 8 ms, a scroll a screen at a time 10 ms a step on average. ## The chain of ties
From any thing, **Ties** (on its card, in its menu as "Its ties", "See them all" under the things it is tied to, or Ctrl+G) opens a page of its own, "Ties of …": the chain of ties from that thing, both ways, whoever wrote each tie (findings 67 to 69; [research/link-graph.md](research/link-graph.md)).
- **What it follows**: every tie Sioul knows, read from both ends of one index built in memory (`ties::TieIndex`): a task's links, steps, waits, contacts and project; an event's guests, stand-in, its child's agenda and who takes it; notes' links and the projects whose files hold them; drafts; budget lines; `links.toml`; a payment's line, mail, letter, invoice, contract and budget, as the budgets page derives it; papers, letters, contracts, invoices and persons; a message's reply, its conversation, its sender's card and a project's routes. The index is never written anywhere; it is made again when what it reads changes (`changes`).
- **What it shows**: up to four steps, the strongest chains first: a thing's score is the product of its ties' strengths by kind (by hand, made from, an appointment, a reply, a step, a wait, its notes, a payment's sure source 1; a probable source, a contract, whom a paper or letter is for, a stand-in, who takes a child's event, instalments 0.8; in the project, a contact or guest, a sender, a mention, a budget, a site's person, a person's agenda, a conversation 0.6; a project's routes alone, a client or a party by name 0.4), times 0.75 per step after the first; leaving a thing with many ties, each weighs less (√(4/d)). Things under 0.1 are left out; five under each thing, 24 in all on a computer and 12 on a phone (`chain::Weights`).
- **Collections stop it**: a project, a contact, a person, a budget and a site are shown with how many things they hold, and not gone through, unless one is where the chain starts. Inside one, one of each kind comes first (a letter, a task, a note, a message, a payment, then the others), then the rest by date, nearest the entry point's first, and two messages from one sender at most before the others. Equal scores elsewhere come nearest in time first, then by kind, title and address: the same files give the same picture.
- **"+N"** on each thing counts its ties not shown (not the one it hangs from, nor things shown elsewhere), its name saying what they are ("12 more: 9 messages, 3 tasks"); "+" shows eight more by the same score, moving nothing already shown; "−" folds them back. Time noted on a task is a count on it, never a thing of its own.
- **What it never follows**: mail the Porch set aside is shown when a tie made by hand reaches it, in the Porch's words, and not gone through; a tie computed from a message's headers passes only through mail the Porch admits; an event another shares for its availability alone shows as "Busy" and is not gone through; the web, files and programs are ends. A health thing on the page keeps Sioul's picture out of the phone's overview of apps (`RecentsGuard`).
- **Two views of one walk**: the drawing, a layered tree read from the left, one column per step, the tie's words on each edge and ties between things already shown as thin curves, zoomed with Ctrl and the wheel or a pinch, "Fit" for the whole; and the outline, the same things in the same order, indented by step. A computer opens on the drawing, a phone on the outline. Each thing shows its family's shape (time and doing a rounded square, messages a wide pill, people a circle, money a diamond, records a folded page, projects a hexagon), its kind's icon and its kind in words.
- **Keys**, in both views: ↓ ↑ move, → presses "+" or steps in, ← folds or steps out, Enter opens, Home goes to the entry point, the menu key (Shift+F10) or a right click or a long press opens the thing's menu (Open, Its ties, "+" or "−", undo the tie to the thing above), typing finds a title; Escape goes back. A card opens beside the page on a computer and over it on a phone; Back comes back to the same picture. Each opening starts afresh.
- **Made in Rust, drawn in QML**: `chain::walk` gives one `ChainView` as JSON (`sioul.askChain`, answered by `chainReady`, on a worker thread); the Related panel reads the same index once it is made. Its cost goes to the log (`sioul-perf: ties: …`). `sioul links <address> --chain` prints the same chain as an outline; agents' `links` tool walks it with their consent at every step ([mcp.md](mcp.md)).

### Importing
`sioul tasks import` reads a TOML file of tasks, offices and drafts, for agents and scripts. Each entry has a key; UIDs are the file's `prefix` and the key, so importing again changes what the file made, line by line, instead of adding it twice. What you added since (a link, a tag, a tick) stays.

```toml
list = "account/plan"      # where new tasks go (else the first list made for tasks)
book = "account/admin"     # where new contacts go
prefix = "plan-"

[[contact]]
key = "office"
name = "Tax office"
phones = ["+33 3 00 00 00 00"]
address = "1 rue de l'Exemple, 75001 Paris"

[[draft]]                  # kept in Sioul; nothing is sent
key = "ask"
account = "mail-account-id"
to = ["office@example.org"]
subject = "Late returns"
body = "…"

[[task]]
key = "send"
title = "Send the registered letter"
parent = "returns"         # a key, or "uid:<UID>"
after = ["gather"]         # waits for
after_gap = { letter = 14 }  # waits 14 days after "letter" is done
start = "2026-10-15"       # can start from
due = "2026-10-30"         # the date asked
estimate = 20
priority = 3
tags = ["you", "admin"]
projects = ["taxes"]       # its projects' ids ("cases", as files written before say, reads too)
note = ["admin/letters.md"]                   # notes of the notes folder
links = [{ uri = "mid:abc@example.org", rel = "via" }]
contacts = ["office"]
drafts = ["ask"]
done = "2026-10-01"        # already done, on that day

[[event]]                  # an appointment: an event, never a task
key = "dentist"
title = "Dentist"
start = "2026-10-23T14:00" # a time, or a day for a whole day
end = "2026-10-23T14:45"   # unsaid: an hour after a time
location = "1 rue de l'Exemple, 75001 Paris"
calendar = "account/personal"  # else the file's `calendar`, else Agenda ⚙'s
```

**A task or an appointment is the file's to say, never guessed from its times** (21 October 2026): a task can start at a time and still be a task. An appointment is an `[[event]]`. A `[[task]]` with what only an appointment has (`end`, `all_day`, `calendar`, `attendees`, `organizer`, or `kind = "appointment"`) stops the import and asks which it is; any other field a task does not read is said, and left out. A key moved from `[[task]]` to `[[event]]` makes the event, with the same UID, and takes the task out with its time blocks, in one step of the move journal (`vdir::replace_items`: never both, never neither). A key in both stops the import. The MCP's `add_task` and `add_event` say the same to agents: ask the person when they did not say which.
