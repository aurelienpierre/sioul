# Sharing availability: what servers do alone, and what a mirror costs

**The question**: Sioul shares a person's availability today in one way per server and one share at a time: on Google a busy-only share of their calendars with one address, elsewhere a "Busy times" calendar of blocks that Sioul derives and keeps up to date itself. A calendar kept by Sioul is only as fresh as the last Sioul that ran: an appointment booked by cal.com, or typed into the phone's own agenda, reaches it only when a Sioul device syncs and runs its upkeep. What can servers share without Sioul running, where must a mirror stay, who keeps it then, and how should several ways of sharing, for several audiences, be shown in one place?

Researched on 5 November 2026; built the same day ([client.md](../client.md#sharing-your-availability); findings 53 and 66 of [research.md](../research.md)). Builds on [agendas.md](agendas.md) (4 November 2026), whose §3.3, §4.3 and §6 (recommendations 14 and 15) are not repeated.

**How evidence is graded**: **standard** (an RFC or an IETF draft); **code** (a server's or a library's source, read); **vendor** (an app's or a server's help pages, not tested here); **issue** (a bug tracker's report: one or a few users' observation); **qualitative**, **survey**, **experiment** for studies; **measured** (Sioul's own figures, in its docs); **inference** (a design reading, mine, marked so). "(unverified)" marks what was not confirmed at a primary source.

## Summary of findings
- **Servers already compute busy times from the live calendars, with nothing running elsewhere**, but each reaches only one audience:
  - Google's per-person free/busy share and its public free/busy;
  - Nextcloud's free/busy lookup between users of one server, fed by working hours stored as VAVAILABILITY;
  - Fastmail's free/busy URL;
  - booking pages on the person's own server (Nextcloud Appointments, Google appointment schedules).
  None of these goes stale (§2, §3).
- **A mirror stays needed** for a person on another server, for a busy-only public link on Nextcloud, iCloud or plain CalDAV, for agendas spread over several accounts, for words per agenda and for Sioul's own margins. It goes stale silently while no Sioul device runs. Today only a Sioul window keeps it, from its plan's run, and it may write an old copy over a newer one (§1, §3).
- **Sioul's phone already wakes every 5 to 15 minutes** for its own steps, and the measured cost there is the radio's wakes, not the bytes. A check of the source calendars inside those steps would add no wake. A mirror kept by WorkManager alone would lag by hours, by a day in the restricted bucket (§4.1).
- **A reader's own refresh can be hours** (Google "From URL" up to 12 hours or more; a Nextcloud subscription daily or weekly), however often Sioul writes (§4.2).
- **Nextcloud counts every calendar in a person's home as their busy time**, those shared with them and Sioul's own calendar of blocks included, unless marked transparent (sabre's code). Both should be marked (§5.3).
- **Free/busy is not neutral.** Its shape reveals health rhythms and absence. EU case law counts data that reveal a sensitive category by deduction as sensitive. A standing view gives far more than one exchange of proposals (§6).

## 1. What Sioul had before (read in the code, 5 November 2026)

- **One availability at a time** (`[agenda.availability]`, `sioul_core::availability::Availability`): one account, the agendas picked, one recipient (`with`) or a link, a word per agenda, the calendar Sioul made, whether the share was made. `share_availability` refuses a second one ("availability-already").
- **On Google**: each picked calendar of that Google account gets an ACL rule `user:<address>` with role `freeBusyReader` (`google_availability`); nothing is copied, and Google computes the busy times from the live calendars. Calendars of other accounts cannot take part (`availability-google-own`).
- **Elsewhere (Nextcloud, any CalDAV with sharing)**: `vdir::create` makes a local calendar "Busy times", which the next sync makes on the server (MKCALENDAR); `availability_upkeep` fills it with the blocks of the picked agendas for the next two months (`now − 1 day` to `now + 61 days`), runs from the plan's run in the window's process (`work.rs`, beside the children's upkeep), and shares it once its server holds it (read-only share to one principal of the server, or Nextcloud's public link). Each block is `SUMMARY:Busy` (or the agenda's word), `TRANSP:OPAQUE`, `CLASS:PUBLIC`, UTC times, a UID derived from start, end and word, so that two devices write the same bytes.
- **What counts as busy** (`availability::blocks`): an event of a picked agenda, not cancelled, not transparent, not declined, not all day; blocks with the same word that touch or overlap merged. The margins kept around an event (`X-SIOUL-BEFORE`, `X-SIOUL-AFTER`) and the plan's time blocks for tasks are **not** counted; working hours play no part.
- **Who keeps it**: only a Sioul window that runs its plan. The phone's background service (`StepService`, `steps.rs`) exchanges Sioul's sealed folder, fetches mail and keeps do-not-disturb in step; it does not sync calendars and does not run the plan, so it never updates the blocks ([android.md](../android.md), "Sioul keeps your devices in step").
- **What the upkeep does not check** (inference from the code): it writes from this device's copy of the calendars as it stands, without asking whether that copy was pulled recently or whole. A device opened after days offline writes its old blocks (removing those another device added for events it has not pulled yet) until its own sync brings the news and the next plan run corrects them; on the server, two devices' writes to the same derived items can meet as 412 conflicts, whose losing side lands among "earlier versions" ([client.md](../client.md), "Storage and sync").
- **What the person sees**: one line ("Your busy times are shared with Sam."), Stop, and nothing about how fresh the blocks are.

## 2. What servers share by themselves

Each way below is computed by the server from the live calendars, so it needs no Sioul running, unless said otherwise. Read on 5 November 2026.

### 2.1 The standards
- **CalDAV free-busy query** (RFC 4791 §7.10): a REPORT on a calendar that returns a VFREEBUSY of its opaque events; its own privilege, `CALDAV:read-free-busy` (§6.1.1), which servers "MUST allow … to be granted without the DAV:read privilege": the standard busy-only share of one calendar. Standard. (Already in [agendas.md](agendas.md) §4.3.)
- **Scheduling free-busy** (RFC 6638 §5): an organizer POSTs a VFREEBUSY request to their own scheduling Outbox, and the server answers for each attendee it hosts. It is a lookup made while inviting, not a standing view, and it reaches only users of the same server: the cross-server protocol that would have carried it, iSchedule, never left the draft stage (draft-desruisseaux-ischedule, expired; standard, unfinished; not read in full). Standard.
  - What sabre/dav, Nextcloud's CalDAV library, puts in the answer (`lib/CalDAV/Schedule/Plugin.php`, `getFreeBusyForEmail`, master, read 5 November 2026): every calendar of the user's calendar home, **calendars shared with them included** (the only filter is `schedule-calendar-transp`: "transparent" calendars are skipped), then the VAVAILABILITY of the Inbox's `calendar-availability` property merged by `FreeBusyGenerator::setVAvailability`. Code. **Reading**: on Nextcloud, a calendar someone shares with the person counts as the person's busy time in this lookup unless the person marks it "Never show me as busy" (transparent). Whether Nextcloud keeps that mark per sharee was not checked.
- **VAVAILABILITY** (RFC 7953): repeating available periods; inside a VAVAILABILITY's span, time outside its AVAILABLE components counts as BUSY-UNAVAILABLE (§3.1); PRIORITY orders overlapping ones, 0 or absent the lowest (§4); stored as `CALDAV:calendar-availability` on the owner's scheduling Inbox and used "when an iTIP free-busy request is targeted at the calendar user who owns the Inbox" (§7.2.4). Its wish for "different periods of available time depending on which user is making the request" (§1) is stated as a goal only: no mechanism is defined. Standard. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7953
- **A published VFREEBUSY** (RFC 5545 §3.6.4, METHOD:PUBLISH) at a URL named in a vCard's FBURL (RFC 6350 §6.9.1) is the oldest server-independent way; Fastmail's free/busy URL (§2.4) is one. Standard.
- **JMAP Calendars** (draft-ietf-jmap-calendars-33, 14 October 2026, work in progress) defines `Principal/getAvailability`, which "calculates the availability of the Principal for scheduling within a requested time period" as busy periods (confirmed, tentative, unavailable), each event's details given only with `showDetails`, never for a "private" event, and "secret" events left out; a calendar's right `mayReadFreeBusy` lets a user read its free-busy through that method without reading its events (§2.2, §4). Standard draft; Fastmail runs JMAP, whether it serves this method was not checked. https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-jmap-calendars

### 2.2 Nextcloud
Nextcloud user manual, Groupware ▸ Calendar (https://docs.nextcloud.com/server/latest/user_manual/en/groupware/calendar.html) and admin manual, Groupware ▸ Calendar (https://docs.nextcloud.com/server/latest/admin_manual/groupware/calendar.html), both "latest" on 5 November 2026. Vendor, unless said.
- **Free/busy between users of one server**: "Free-busy information is only available for other users on the same Nextcloud instance." Shown in the event editor's "Find a time" once attendees are added: own busy blocks in the calendar's colour, out-of-office in grey, others' busy times in their avatar's colour. "When logged-in, Nextcloud can return FreeBusy information for all users of the instance"; an admin can switch it off for the whole server (`dav disableFreeBusy`). No sharing act is needed and none can narrow it per person: every user of the server may look.
- **Working hours** ("general availability independent of scheduled events", Settings ▸ Groupware ▸ Availability) feed the free/busy view; one-time absences go "on top of your regular availability in the Absence settings section". The settings page converts its weekly slots to and from VAVAILABILITY (`@nextcloud/calendar-availability-vue`, https://npmjs.com/package/@nextcloud/calendar-availability-vue) and stores them in the Inbox's `calendar-availability` by PROPPATCH, where sabre reads them: `findScheduleInboxAvailability` and `saveScheduleInboxAvailability` in `apps/dav/src/service/CalendarService.ts` (master, read 5 November 2026; code), which removes the property rather than write an empty availability, since RFC 7953 would read that as "explicitly unavailable". **Reading**: Sioul can write the person's own hours there with one PROPPATCH, as Nextcloud's settings page does.
- **"Never show me as busy (set calendar to transparent)"**, per calendar: "no events in this calendar will be taken into account" for free/busy.
- **Appointments** (booking pages): public (listed on the profile page) or private ("only accessible to the people who receive the secret URL"); a duration, an increment, booking hours (the working hours or a schedule of their own), preparation time before, a buffer after, minimum notice, a maximum per day; "Only slots that do not conflict with existing events in your calendars will be shown to attendees"; "Every booked appointment will be written into one of your calendars"; a booking holds only once the booker clicks the confirmation link mailed to them, and meanwhile its slot can still be booked by someone else. The conflict calendars are chosen ("Additional calendars to check for conflicts", Calendar 3.1.0, February 2022; calendars shared with the user added by server PR #48621). An open report (nextcloud/calendar #8446, May 2026, Nextcloud 32.0.8, Calendar 6.2.2; issue, no maintainer answer) finds subscribed ICS calendars ignored by the conflict check (`CalendarProvider.php` asks `getCalendarsForUser()` only), and, in the reporter's tests, events without a STATUS line not blocking, and zero-length all-day events ignored. https://github.com/nextcloud/calendar/issues/8446
  - **Reading**: a private appointment page is a server-side, always-fresh "public link" to the person's free slots, for one kind of meeting; it shows free slots, not busy blocks. Whether Sioul's events (written without STATUS) block slots must be tried on a real server: if the report holds, every event Sioul or a phone writes would leave its slot bookable.
- **Calendars shared read-only or published by link** show `CLASS:CONFIDENTIAL` events as "Busy", stripped to their times, and hide `CLASS:PRIVATE` ones (server code, read 4 November: [agendas.md](agendas.md) §3.3). The class is set **per event**, by whoever writes it: an event typed into another app (a phone's agenda, a booking tool) arrives `PUBLIC` unless that app sets it. **Reading**: sharing a real calendar this way fails *open*: each event another app adds shows in full to the reader, until something marks it.
- **Federated calendar shares** (Nextcloud 32.0.0, read-only; read and write since 33.0.0; setting `enableCalendarFederation`): "Sharing a calendar with a user on another Nextcloud instance works just like sharing with a local user", by `<username>@<instance>`. Whether a federated read-only sharee gets confidential events stripped, as a local read-only sharee does, was not checked.
- **Subscriptions** (a calendar read from an ICS URL) are "cached on server and refreshed periodically"; the admin manual gives a default of one day (`calendarSubscriptionRefreshRate`, "If a server provides a refresh interval, it is respected"), the user manual "every week by default". The two vendor pages disagree.

### 2.3 Google
Vendor pages, read 5 November 2026.
- **Per person, "See only free/busy (hide details)"**: "People can only find out when you're busy on your calendar"; the person "must click the link in the email" to add it (https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37082). This is the ACL role `freeBusyReader` Sioul grants now. Live, per calendar, revocable. Whether an address without a Google account can use it is not said.
- **Public, free/busy only**: "Make available to public" with "See only free/busy (hide details)"; a "Public address in iCal format" that "only works if the calendar is public"; "Changes may take up to 4 hours to take effect" (https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37083). That the public iCal address then holds events titled "Busy" is said by third-party help pages only (a school's help centre, a 2015 university guide; unverified at Google).
- **Appointment schedules**: "A personal Google Account or Workspace account"; made on a computer only; "Check calendars for availability" picks the calendars whose busy times block slots; bookings go into the calendar chosen; buffers, a daily maximum, a booking window, "a 4-hour minimum lead time" by default; "Some appointment schedule features require an eligible Google Workspace or Google One subscription" (https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/10729749). Only the calendars of that Google account (and co-hosts') are listed as checkable.
- **No API for appointment schedules**: the Calendar API v3 has eight resources (Acl, CalendarList, Calendars, Channels, Colors, Events, Freebusy, Settings) and none for booking pages (https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/v3/reference). Sioul can link to Google's page, not make one.

### 2.4 Fastmail, Apple, Proton
- **Fastmail**: each calendar can be published by a read-only URL, either "Full event details" or "Free/busy information", which "only reveals the times you are busy, but does not contain any information about the events themselves"; "URLs are not guessable", and Reset URL changes it (https://www.fastmail.help/hc/en-us/articles/360060590793, vendor). Server-side and live; per calendar, not per person. Sharing with users of the same Fastmail account has its own guide, not retrieved.
- **Apple iCloud**: a calendar is shared with iCloud users (view or edit) or made public (read-only, full details); there is no busy-only share ([agendas.md](agendas.md) §3.3; https://support.apple.com/en-us/105124, vendor). Apple Calendar's availability window depends on the account ("a calendar service that tracks availability"); community reports and two vendors (Kerio, BusyMac) say it shows nothing for iCloud or CalDAV accounts (https://support.kerioconnect.gfi.com/article/115683-user-availability-free-busy-functionality-is-not-showing-correctly-in-apple-calendar-on-macos-big-sur-and-later; https://support.busymac.com/help/70616; issue-grade). Publishing an iCloud calendar is done in Apple's apps, not over CalDAV (inference: no CalDAV method for it is documented).
- **Proton**: links in "Limited view" ("shows when you're busy but doesn't show any event details") or "Full view", and shares with Proton users; participants' busy slots shown when inviting inside an organisation ([agendas.md](agendas.md) §3.3, vendor). Proton offers no CalDAV, so Sioul cannot reach its calendars at all.

### 2.5 Booking tools
- **cal.com** reads busy times from the calendars selected "to check for conflicts" and writes bookings into one destination calendar (third-party mirrors of its docs, https://mintlify.com/calcom/cal.com/features/calendars; vendor-derived). On Google it uses "the Google Calendar Free/Busy API" and states that "no specific event details, titles, locations … is accessed or stored" (https://cal.com/help/privacy-and-compliance/google-calendar-privacy.md, vendor); its Google busy times are cached, the cache kept by Google's push notifications (release 4.8, https://cal.com/it/blog/v-4-8, vendor). On CalDAV it asks a calendar-query REPORT with a time range, reading the events themselves, and writes a booking by PUT (Shahbaz 2023, cal.com's blog, https://cal.com/nl/blog/the-intricacies-and-challenges-of-implementing-a-caldav-supporting-system-for-cal, vendor); an issue reports recurring busy events from a Nextcloud not blocking slots (calcom/cal.com #1588, issue). Its **ICS Feed** app links read-only ICS feeds "for usage via getAvailability" (PR calcom/cal.com #13337; API `save-an-ics-feed`, `readOnly` true by default, https://cal.com/docs/api-reference/v2/calendars/save-an-ics-feed.md; vendor). **Reading**: cal.com can read busy blocks from a busy-only link (Fastmail's, Google's public free/busy, or a calendar of blocks) without ever holding a password to the person's calendars; it then still needs a calendar to write bookings into, or it only mails them.
- **Calendly** connects "Google Calendar, Office 365 or Outlook.com, and Exchange"; one calendar on the free plan, "up to six calendars per user" on paid ones; each new event goes into one calendar (https://calendly.com/help/connect-your-calendar-to-calendly, vendor). No CalDAV and no ICS feed: a community post says new iCloud connections stopped on 20 August 2024 (community, unverified at Calendly). **Reading**: Calendly sees a Nextcloud person's busy times only if they are copied into a Google or Microsoft calendar.
- **Nextcloud Appointments and Google appointment schedules** (above) are booking tools inside the person's own server: they read the live calendars and write the booking there, with nothing else to trust.

## 3. Which ways need Sioul running, ranked by how brittle they are

"Brittle" here means: what can go wrong without anyone noticing, and in which direction. A way that goes stale shows the person **free when busy** (a booking lands on an appointment, a co-parent plans on a time already taken) or **busy when free**; a way that fails *open* shows **details** the person meant to keep. The ranking is mine (inference), from §2 and §1.

| Rank | Way | Needs Sioul running? | Fresh within | Fails how | Reaches |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Google per-person `freeBusyReader` on the person's calendars | no | live (Google's own) | stays as shared until taken back; the reader needs Google Calendar | one Google user per rule |
| 1 | Nextcloud free/busy lookup between users of one server ("Find a time"), with working hours as VAVAILABILITY | no | live | cannot be narrowed: every user of the server may look; calendars shared *with* the person count as theirs unless made transparent (§2.1) | users of that server, while inviting |
| 1 | Fastmail free/busy URL; Google public free/busy | no | live at the server (Google: "up to 4 hours" for the setting itself) | public to anyone holding the link; Google's may be found by search when the calendar is "searchable" | anyone, any client |
| 2 | Booking page on the person's own server (Nextcloud Appointments, Google appointment schedules) | no | live | a slot stays bookable until the booker confirms (Nextcloud); reported gaps in which events block (Nextcloud #8446: no STATUS, subscriptions) | anyone with the link, for one kind of meeting |
| 2 | External booking tool reading the person's calendars directly (cal.com over Google, CalDAV; Calendly over Google, Microsoft) | no | cached by push (Google), or read per query (CalDAV) | the tool holds a login to every calendar of the account, details included, on CalDAV; recurring events reported missed (cal.com #1588) | the booking tool's visitors |
| 3 | A real calendar shared read-only or by link, events marked `CLASS:CONFIDENTIAL` (Nextcloud) | only to mark the events | live | **fails open**: an event another app adds arrives PUBLIC and shows in full until marked | Nextcloud users, federated ones (32+), link holders |
| 4 | **A mirror calendar of busy blocks** kept by a Sioul device (today's "Busy times"), shared to a person, a group, a federated user or by link | **yes** | as fresh as the last Sioul device that pulled every source and wrote it, plus the reader's own refresh (a Nextcloud subscription: daily or weekly; Google "From URL": "up to 12 hours", 24 to 48 reported) | **stale silently**: an appointment booked by cal.com or typed into the phone's agenda is missing until a Sioul device runs; a device with an old copy can write old blocks over new ones (§1) | anyone the server can share with |
| 4 | Fantastical's mirroring (prior art): "Events are created on device and sent directly to your destination calendar" | yes (Fantastical) | while a device runs it | the same as Sioul's; a disabled rule leaves its copies "in place but they will not be updated" | the destination calendar's readers |
| 5 | A cloud mirror service (Reclaim, OneCal, CalendarBridge: "Sync as free/busy") | no Sioul; the vendor's servers run it | the vendor's | a third party reads every source calendar in full | Google, Microsoft (iCloud only indirectly, by reviews) |

Sources: §2; Fantastical's help, https://flexibits.com/fantastical/help/calendar-mirroring (vendor); Reclaim's page, https://reclaim.ai/features/calendar-sync (vendor; its update rhythm "real-time" by the vendor, "on a schedule" by a review); Google's subscription delay, quoted from Google's settings page by third-party help pages (https://support.noterro.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004516803-Appointments-Not-Syncing-with-External-Calendar; https://help.timetastic.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/203174832; unverified at Google).

**Reading**:
- The server-side ways (ranks 1 and 2) never go stale and never need Sioul; each reaches one audience only: Google's people, one Nextcloud's users, a link's holders, a booking page's visitors. Sioul's work there is to **set them up and take them back**, never to keep them.
- A mirror stays needed when: the recipient is on another server and the server cannot share busy times across (every CalDAV server but Google and Fastmail); a public busy-only link on Nextcloud, iCloud or plain CalDAV; the agendas that count live in several accounts (a Google work calendar and a Nextcloud personal one); a word per agenda ("Work") for a partner; the margins kept around events (`X-SIOUL-BEFORE`, `X-SIOUL-AFTER`), which no server knows; Calendly for a person whose calendars are not on Google or Microsoft.
- The `CLASS:CONFIDENTIAL` way (rank 3) costs no upkeep, but its failure is a leak, not a stale block; for availability shared with someone who should not see details, a stale block is the lesser harm. It fits a calendar only the person and Sioul write, never one a phone's agenda or a booking tool also writes.

## 4. Where a mirror stays: who keeps it, how stale it may get, how Sioul says so

### 4.1 What the phone can do in the background
- **WorkManager** repeats work at 15 minutes at the shortest ("The minimum repeat interval that can be defined is 15 minutes (same as the JobScheduler API)"), at a time that "depends on the constraints … and on the optimizations performed by the system" (https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/persistent/getting-started/define-work; vendor). Under Doze, "regular jobs and inexact alarms are deferred" to its maintenance windows; by standby bucket, jobs get "up to 10 minutes in a rolling 24 hour period" in *rare* and "once per day for up to 10 minutes" in *restricted*, which also has network "Disabled"; in Doze, while-idle alarms are "Limited to 7 per hour" for the compatibility quota (https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/power/power-details; vendor). AOSP's alarm service gives exact allow-while-idle alarms of apps that hold the exact-alarm permission a rolling quota of 72 an hour, and the compatibility quota 7 an hour (AlarmManagerService, `DEFAULT_ALLOW_WHILE_IDLE_QUOTA`, `DEFAULT_ALLOW_WHILE_IDLE_COMPAT_QUOTA`, as mirrored in /e/OS's tree: https://gitlab.e.foundation/e/os/android_frameworks_base/-/commit/5cef33d2889f94bf8b4ec2cb91395fd67d2072f1; code, read through a downstream mirror). **Reading**: a mirror kept by WorkManager alone would be hours stale on a phone left still, and a day stale in the restricted bucket.
- **Sioul's phone already wakes** for other reasons: a foreground service (`StepService`, type `specialUse`) steps every 2 minutes while another device is in use, 5 while the person is awake, 15 while they sleep, by exact allow-while-idle alarms, each step one burst of network (pull, sends, mail together) ([android.md](../android.md), "Sioul keeps your devices in step"; measured). Its cost, measured on the night of 10 to 11 October 2026 (/e/OS 4, Android 12, mobile data, 14 h 46 on the battery): Sioul 119 mAh, 4 % of a 2,946 mAh battery, of which the radio 91 mAh, the processor 16, the wake lock 1.4; "the cost left is the radio: how often a step wakes the modem, not how much it sends"; about 0.2 mAh of radio a step (measured, one phone, one night).
- **Reading** (inference from those figures): a check of the source calendars **inside steps that already run** adds no wake and no radio tail; it adds bytes (one sync-collection REPORT per source calendar, RFC 6578, a few hundred bytes when nothing changed) and, only when something changed, the processor time to read two months of events and write the changed blocks. A check in a wake of its own would cost about what a step costs, 0.2 mAh of radio each: every 15 minutes, some 20 mAh a day, under 1 % of that battery. Neither figure was measured for calendars.
- **What already reaches the server without Sioul**: an event typed into the phone's own agenda reaches its CalDAV server through DAVx⁵, which Android runs "when local data has been changed (normally very quickly, but in some cases, it may take a few seconds or even minutes)"; its periodic sync cannot be shorter than 15 minutes, "a moderate sync interval (like one hour)" advised (https://manual.davx5.com/settings.html; vendor). A booking tool writes on the server directly. So the server holds the truth within minutes; only the mirror lags.
- **Push instead of polling**: WebDAV-Push, a draft of DAVx⁵'s authors, lets a Nextcloud with the "DAV Push" app (`nc_ext_dav_push`, the only server so far) wake a client through UnifiedPush or Google's FCM when a collection changes; "experimental" in DAVx⁵'s manual (https://manual.davx5.com/webdav_push.html; https://github.com/bitfireAT/nc_ext_dav_push; vendor). A later way to wake the phone only when a source calendar changes; not for now.

### 4.2 How stale it may get
The age of a block a reader sees is the sum of (inference):
1. the time until a Sioul device looks at the source calendars after the change (today: until a window runs its plan; with phone steps: 5 to 15 minutes, or never while no device runs Sioul);
2. that device's write and its sync to the server (seconds);
3. the reader's own refresh: a share inside one Nextcloud, at the reader's next sync; a subscription in another Nextcloud, daily or weekly by default (§2.2); Google's "From URL", "up to 12 hours", 24 to 48 reported (§3); cal.com's ICS Feed, not documented.

Step 3 is beyond Sioul: a busy-only link read by Google Calendar is hours late however often Sioul writes. Step 1 is the only one that can be unbounded: an appointment another application writes is missing from the blocks until a Sioul device runs.

**Two devices**: both compute the same bytes from the same calendars (derived UIDs), so they agree when both are up to date. A device that has not pulled the newest events computes an older set and, as written today, writes it (§1). The rule that prevents it: **write only from a view pulled whole since the last write** (every source calendar's sync token newer than the one the last write was made from), and say which device wrote last in Sioul's own sealed records between devices, not on the server (inference; Sioul's devices already exchange their entries: [database.md](../database.md#devices)).

### 4.3 Saying how fresh it is
- **To the person**: what a mirror's freshness says about the person's own devices (when they run) is the person's to see, and only theirs. Each share's line in Agenda ⚙ can say it plainly when it matters: nothing while fresh, "Last brought up to date yesterday at 18:10, on the computer." once older than a day, said once at the next admin window, never as an alarm (findings 2 and 5 of [research.md](../research.md)).
- **To the reader**: a time of last update would tell them when the person's devices run, a rhythm (§6). A date alone tells little: the calendar's name or description can say "Busy times, as of 5 November", changed at most once a day (inference).
- **Beyond what is known**: when no device runs for weeks, the far days of a two-month window stay as last written, then pass. A mirror read by a booking tool should end with one block "Not known yet" from its horizon on, so that the tool offers nothing it cannot vouch for (fails closed); a mirror read by a person needs no such block, the date in its description being enough (inference).

## 5. Several ways for several agendas

### 5.1 What availability is made of
Across the standards and the apps read, an availability is four things (inference, from §2 and the apps below):
1. **Which agendas count as busy**: Nextcloud's per-calendar "Never show me as busy" and Appointments' "Additional calendars to check for conflicts"; Google's "Check calendars for availability"; cal.com's and Calendly's calendars "to check for conflicts" (up to six on Calendly); CalDAV's `schedule-calendar-transp` (RFC 6638).
2. **Which hours are offered**: VAVAILABILITY (RFC 7953); Nextcloud's working hours and each appointment's booking hours; cal.com's named availability schedules with date overrides, which "updates every event that pulls from that availability schedule" (https://cal.com/help/availabilities/date-overrides, https://cal.com/blog/what-are-date-overrides-and-how-do-i-enable-them; vendor).
3. **The margins**: preparation time and a buffer after (Nextcloud Appointments; Google's buffer time; cal.com's "buffers before or after a meeting", which a cal.com post describes as travel time between venues: https://cal.com/help/event-types/display-issues, https://next.cal.com/blog/mastering-event-level-time-limits-what-lies-beyond-buffer-times; vendor). Sioul keeps its own margins on events (`X-SIOUL-BEFORE`, `X-SIOUL-AFTER`, [tasks.md](../tasks.md)), which no server reads.
4. **What a block says**: "Busy" (Google, Fastmail, Proton, Nextcloud confidential), "Blocked Time" or the title (Fantastical), or a word (Sioul's per-agenda word, after Thayer et al. 2012: [agendas.md](agendas.md) §2.3).

And a share is: **an audience** (one person, a group, anyone with a link, a booking tool's visitors), **a way** (one of §3), and the **server** it lives on.

### 5.2 How apps present it
Vendor documentation, as read above and on 4 November ([agendas.md](agendas.md) §3.3).
- **Google**: sharing is per calendar, in each calendar's settings (people, public, link); working hours and appointment schedules live elsewhere; nothing gathers "what I share with whom" across calendars.
- **Nextcloud**: sharing per calendar (people, groups, federated users, link), transparency per calendar, working hours and absences in Settings ▸ Groupware, Appointments in the Calendar app: four places.
- **Fastmail, Proton**: per calendar, a busy-only link beside the full one.
- **Fantastical**: one rule per source and destination pair, no chaining.
- **cal.com**: the closest to one object: named availability schedules, conflict calendars, a destination calendar, per event type its buffers and limits.

**Reading**: nobody shows a person, in one list, everything they have given out of their time and to whom. Sioul can, since it makes and takes back each share.

### 5.3 What goes wrong
- **Details leak**:
  - Google: "New events follow the calendar's default settings. If the default is 'public,' anyone can find your new event", and a public calendar "may appear in search results" when its site is searchable (https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/37083; vendor).
  - Nextcloud: confidential events fail open, as above (§2.2).
  - A school help page warns that a calendar shared busy-only with everyone can still show details to people given another level (help.sites.schoolstatus.com, third-party).
  - Fantastical can mirror titles and places.
- **Busy times go stale**:
  - Subscriptions: Google "From URL" in hours; Nextcloud daily or weekly.
  - Booking pages: Nextcloud's unconfirmed slots; cal.com's recurring CalDAV events (issue #1588); Nextcloud Appointments ignoring subscriptions and events without STATUS (issue #8446).
  - Fantastical's disabled rules leave copies in place, "not … updated".
  - Calendly users report showing free when busy (https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/availability-issues-free-when-i-should-be-busy-455; community).
- **Someone else's busy time counted as the person's** (inference from sabre's code, §2.1): on Nextcloud, a calendar another parent or a partner shares with the person, for its busy times, counts in the person's own free/busy unless marked transparent. A colleague's "Find a time" then shows the person busy whenever the other parent is.
- **The mirror counted again** (inference, same code): Sioul's calendar of blocks sits in the person's calendar home, opaque as a calendar today. On Nextcloud it re-enters the person's own free/busy, margins and agendas they had marked "Never show me as busy" included. Shared with a user of the same server, it also enters *that* user's free/busy, so the reader looks busy to their colleagues whenever the person is. The calendar of blocks should be marked transparent as a whole (`schedule-calendar-transp`), its events staying opaque for their readers. Whether a mark set by the owner holds for the sharees on Nextcloud was not checked.

## 6. What free/busy tells, and what Sioul already promises

### 6.1 What busy blocks reveal
- **Inference from what says nothing explicitly.** In distributed meeting scheduling, agents that only propose and accept or refuse times still let the others infer their private schedules; privacy loss can be measured, and it trades against how fast a time is found (Wallace RJ, Freuder EC (2005). Constraint-based reasoning and privacy/efficiency tradeoffs in multi-agent problem solving. *Artificial Intelligence* 161(1–2):209–227. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2004.10.006; simulation, abstract read). **Reading**: a standing busy-only view gives far more than one exchange of proposals; whoever reads it for weeks learns the person's week.
- **Rhythms and health.** A block every Tuesday at 17:00 for fifty minutes, three mornings a week at a clinic's hours, a cycle every three weeks: their shape can say therapy, dialysis or treatment without a title (inference). In EU law, data "liable to disclose indirectly" a special category, through "an intellectual operation involving comparison or deduction", falls under GDPR Article 9 (CJEU, judgment of 1 August 2022, *OT v Vyriausioji tarnybinės etikos komisija*, C-184/20, ECLI:EU:C:2022:601; legal text, read through commentaries: https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/special-category-data-by-inference-cjeu-significantly-expands-the-scope-of-article-9-gdpr/). The person sharing their own calendar falls under the household exemption; the reading is about care, not compliance (inference).
- **Absence.** Crime follows the convergence of a motivated offender, a suitable target and the absence of a capable guardian (Cohen LE, Felson M (1979). Social change and crime rate trends: a routine activity approach. *American Sociological Review* 44(4):588–608. https://doi.org/10.2307/2094589; theory with time-series data). **Reading**: a public link that shows a week of travel tells an empty home; all-day events, which Sioul does not count (§1), carry most of it.
- **The devices' rhythm.** When a mirror is updated tells when the person's devices run, roughly when they wake and work (§4.3; inference).
- **Being watched.** "A shared calendar may set the expectation that co-parents have the right to monitor each other's movements" (Relationships Australia Victoria 2024; [agendas.md](agendas.md) §2.2), and 26 of 30 found a bare "busy" suspect (Thayer et al. 2012). Busy-only sharing is narrower, never neutral.
- **Who may look without being asked.** On Nextcloud, every user of the server can see the person's free/busy when inviting them, and no setting narrows it to some users (§2.2): on a family or association server, that is the whole membership.

### 6.2 What Sioul's rules already promise
- Someone else's availability shows to the person alone, as bands without titles; never written, re-shared, exported, nor given to an agent (finding 52; `Consent::keep_private`).
- The person's own availability: busy blocks only, never what fills them; one word per agenda at most; nothing shared until the person presses; revocable (finding 53).
- The calendar of blocks is no agenda of the person's: not shown, reminded, planned around or met in a conflict (`availability::blocks_place`).
- Agents never make, remove or share an agenda, nor share availability: the MCP has no tool for it ([client.md](../client.md#sharing-your-availability)).
- A child's health stays in the child's agenda; a stand-in in a work agenda says "Away" and is confidential (finding 54).

### 6.3 What follows for sharing (inference)
- **The narrowest audience by default**: one person before a group, a group before a link; a public link only by an explicit choice, saying who can open it.
- **Coarser for wider audiences**: blocks rounded outward to the quarter hour and gaps under a quarter hour merged, so that a fifty-minute session reads as an hour among others; a public link with a shorter horizon (two weeks) than a person's share.
- **Health never named**: an event of a health kind counts as busy with the plain word, never an agenda's word, whatever the audience.
- **No device times**: freshness shown to the reader as a date at most.
- **Say what the server shows without being asked**: on a Nextcloud, that its users see the person's busy times when inviting them.

## 7. What only real servers can settle
Nothing below was tried; Sioul's tests run on stand-ins only ([compatibility.md](../compatibility.md#sharing-and-agendas-on-their-servers)).
1. **Nextcloud free/busy**:
   - whether calendars shared with the person count in their free/busy, as sabre's code reads;
   - whether a transparent mark set by an owner holds for a sharee, and one set by a sharee for themselves;
   - whether a PROPPATCH of `calendar-availability` from Sioul shows in Settings ▸ Groupware ▸ Availability and in "Find a time".
2. **Nextcloud Appointments**: whether events Sioul writes (no STATUS line) block slots (issue #8446); whether a calendar of blocks, or a calendar shared with the person, can be picked among the conflict calendars.
3. **Nextcloud federation** (32 and later): whether a federated read-only sharee gets confidential events stripped, as a local one does; how often the remote server pulls a federated calendar.
4. **Google**:
   - what the public "free/busy" iCal address holds (titles "Busy"?);
   - whether a `freeBusyReader` rule for an address without a Google account does anything;
   - how long a public ACL change takes (the help says up to 4 hours).
5. **Readers' refresh**: how often Nextcloud (another instance), Google "From URL", Thunderbird, DAVx⁵/ICSx⁵ and cal.com's ICS Feed fetch a published calendar of blocks; whether any honours `REFRESH-INTERVAL` (RFC 7986) or `X-PUBLISHED-TTL` in Nextcloud's export.
6. **The phone**: the battery cost of a calendar check inside the existing steps, measured as on 10 to 11 October (`dumpsys batterystats`), with nothing changed and with a change an hour.
7. **Two devices**: a phone offline for a day, then opened, while the computer keeps the blocks; whether the rule "write only from a view pulled whole since the last write" holds, and whether any 412 conflict on the blocks reaches "earlier versions".
