# Money movements, fees, the ledger and the mail about money

**The question**: a payment processor's mail states what the payer paid ("X paid you €52"), and the bank later receives less, after fees, often batched with other payments. How should a Sioul money movement keep what was paid, the fees and what was received; which of those a French declaration needs; how a movement should be made, changed and removed by the person, by an agent and from the command line; and does a four-tab page (home, budgets and accounts, the full ledger, the history of money mail) hold against the research on financial attention, mental accounting and Sioul's own budget findings?

Researched on 5 November 2026, building on [budgets.md](budgets.md) (4 November: the literature on budgets, thirteen apps, matching) and findings 38 to 45 of [the findings](../research.md), which are not redone here. Findings 62 to 65 come from it; what was built from it is in [accounting.md](../accounting.md) ("Fees", "Payouts", "Movements tab", "From mail", "The declaration").

**How evidence is graded**, as in budgets.md: **meta** (meta-analysis or systematic review), **RCT** (randomised, field or lab), **study** (observational or quasi-experimental, its kind said), **qualitative**, **grey** (reports by organisations), **theory**, **review** (a narrative review). Legal and official texts: **law** (a code's article, BOFiP, a directive), **official** (an administration's guidance page: URSSAF, impots.gouv, service-public). For processors and apps: **vendor** (the maker's own page, read), **snippet** (known only from a search summary, because the page refused automated reading or was not opened: unverified). **Inference** marks my own reasoning, where no source says it.

## 1. What Sioul has today, as it bears on fees (read in the code, 5 November 2026)

- **A movement is derived, never stored whole** (`crates/sioul-core/src/movements.rs`): each reading makes it again from the budget file's lines and presets, the bank's lines (`sioul-bank.toml`), contracts, letters, the mail and the store of corrections (`sioul-movements.toml`). It has one signed amount (`Sum`: exact, about, or a range), one currency, one day and an expected day, one place (a budget, parts, a transfer between own accounts, nowhere, not placed yet), sources with keys, links, a state among five (planned, announced, not final yet, done, not seen) and what it is "probably together" with. **It has no fee, no gross and no net**, and no created or modified date: what it is made from has them, or not.
- **PayPal's and Stripe's exports are read net of fees** (`bank::read_csv`: "Net of fees first (PayPal, Stripe), else the amount"); the gross ("Avant commission", "Gross", Stripe's "Amount") and the fee columns are recognised by the tests' sample files but dropped.
- **The mail's amount is the one the message states** (`payments::detect`): for a processor's "you received" mail, the gross. A money mail and a bank line are candidates for one another within 5 % (a euro at least) for card payments, exactly for debits and transfers, and "a quarter either way" in another currency (`matching.rs`; accounting.md, "Matching"). A PayPal payment of €52 that reaches PayPal's balance as €50.14 (at 2.90 % plus €0.35) falls inside 5 %; the payout to the bank days later, batching several payments, matches none of them.
- **"Probably together"** already knows one client's transfer paying two or three invoices to the cent, one order charged in parts, a deferred-debit card's monthly sum, and instalments. It does not know a payout settling many processor payments minus their fees.
- **The mail about money shown on the budgets page** comes from the mail the Porch gathers (`budget::mail_lines` over `Backend::gather`), not from every message ever kept. Each message is detected again at each reading. The per-folder mail cache (`mailcache.rs`, each message parsed once, ever, its columns stamped by the words they depend on) holds no money column today.
- **Issued invoices** are records of their own (`invoice.rs`, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/sioul/invoices/<number>.toml`): number, date, due day, client, lines, paid or not. They are a movement's source (`sioul:invoice/<number>`).
- **Agents and the command line read money only** (`budgets`, `invoices`, `contracts` tools; `sioul budgets`); nothing writes a movement except the window's forms (`MovementDialog.qml`, `PaymentForm.qml`, the row menus in `MovementActions.qml`).
- **The page** (`BudgetsPage.qml`) is one long scroll: each account to its next income, the chart, what the hour leaves out, each budget in words, What is coming, reserves, bank accounts, contracts, the mail about money.

## 2. French declarations: gross or net of fees?

### 2.1 Micro-entrepreneur, BNC and BIC
- **The tax base is gross.** Micro-BNC: "le bénéfice imposable … est égal au montant brut des recettes annuelles diminué d'un abattement forfaitaire de 34 %", at least €305 (CGI art. 102 ter; **law**, read in a commented copy updated February 2026, advizexperts.fr, and Légifrance's English translation by snippet). Micro-BIC applies its allowance (71 % or 50 %) to the turnover excluding VAT (CGI art. 50-0; **law**, known, not re-read today). The allowance is the flat stand-in for every expense: no real expense is deducted.
- **What counts as a receipt** under micro-BNC: "toutes les recettes … effectivement encaissées"; "l'ensemble des honoraires perçus"; reimbursements of expenses received from clients are receipts; *débours* (sums paid in the client's name and passed on at cost) and sums that only transit are not; commissions or rebates the taxpayer grants to clients cannot be deducted (BOI-BNC-DECLA-20-10, §40–§80, published 19 August 2026; **law**, BOFiP, read). It does not name payment processors.
- **URSSAF's declaration is the cash received, gross, excluding VAT.** URSSAF's practical sheet "Déterminer mon chiffre d'affaires" says only money collected in the period counts, that "aucune charge ou dépense ne peut être déduite du chiffre d'affaires" (débours at cost aside), that platform commissions are not deductible, and that a refund made before the declaration lowers it while a later one needs the earlier declaration corrected (autoentrepreneur.urssaf.fr; **official, snippet**: the site reset every automated connection on 5 November 2026, so the page was not read). Specialist sites give the same worked example: a €1,000 mission through Malt at 10 % is declared €1,000, not the €900 received (superindep.fr, shine.fr; **grey**, secondary).
- **When a sum is received**: when the person can freely dispose of it; for a transfer, when it is credited to the account (BOI-BNC-BASE-20-10-10, §10 and §20; **law**). **And when a third party collects for the person, at that third party's receipt**: "il est imposable à raison des honoraires versés par la clientèle à ce tiers, dès leur encaissement par ce dernier", even if the third party pays out only in a later year (§80, citing CE, 29 July 1983, no. 24526; **law**, BOFiP, read). *Inference*: a payment received on PayPal, Stripe, Malt or a marketplace wallet is received on the day the processor credits it to the person's balance, not on the day of the payout to the bank. A payout in early January of December's sales belongs to December's receipts.
- **The receipts register** of a micro-entrepreneur, chronological, shows the amount and the origin of each receipt ("identité du client ou de la société mandataire"), the means of payment, and the references of the supporting documents (invoice numbers); totals every three months (Service-public, "Obligations comptables du micro-entrepreneur", F23266, checked 7 September 2026, citing code de commerce art. R123-203 to D123-208-01; **official**, read). *Inference*: the register needs each gross receipt with its own day, client and invoice, never the payout that batched them.
- **Platforms withhold contributions from 2027.** Under art. 6 of the LFSS for 2024 (loi no. 2023-1250 of 26 December 2023), platforms that put micro-entrepreneurs in touch with clients will declare their turnover to URSSAF and pay their contributions for them: volunteer platforms from 2026 (sources disagree: April or 1 October 2026), all concerned platforms from 1 January 2027; the person then receives the sum net of contributions as well as of commissions (bpifrance-creation.fr, weblex.fr, shine.fr; **grey, snippet**; the law itself and URSSAF's page not read). *Inference*: a payout can lack two different things, the platform's fee (an expense) and contributions withheld (paid for the person, and declared by the platform), and Sioul must not treat them alike.

### 2.2 Under the real regime (BNC déclaration contrôlée, BIC réel)
- **BNC réel** keeps cash accounting: receipts received in the year, gross, and the expenses the profession needed are deducted as such (CGI art. 93, **law**, known; the same encashment rules of BOI-BNC-BASE-20-10-10 apply). A processor's fee is then an expense of the year it was charged. *Inference, unsourced on the exact line*: on form 2035 it goes among financial or other external costs; the line chosen is the accountant's.
- **BIC réel** uses accrual: income when the claim is acquired, normally on the invoice, and fees as charges; in the French chart of accounts, bank and similar services are account 627 (**standard**, the PCG, from general knowledge, not re-read today).
- Either way, the fee is not netted against the receipt: the receipt is gross, the fee is a separate expense. A movement that kept only the net would make the income too small and the expense invisible, and the error would cancel only in the profit, never in the turnover that thresholds and contributions read.

### 2.3 VAT
- **The taxable amount is everything the supplier receives or is to receive from the customer** for the supply (Directive 2006/112/EC art. 73; CGI art. 266-1-a; **law**, known). Directive art. 79 lists what is excluded (price reductions, discounts, sums received in the customer's name as repayment of expenditure); a fee retained by a payment intermediary is not among them.
- **The CJEU held that the amount retained by a third party does not lower it**: where a finance company paid the retailer less than the price the customer paid, the retailer's taxable amount was the full price (Primback, C-34/99, 15 May 2001; **law**, read in a case summary only, swarb.co.uk). *Inference*: VAT, when it applies, is due on the gross price, never on the payout.
- **The fees themselves**: payment and transfer services are exempt from VAT (Directive art. 135(1)(d); **law**, known), so a PayPal or Stripe processing fee normally carries no VAT to recover; a platform's marketplace commission (Malt, Etsy) is a taxable service and its invoice may carry VAT. *Not verified per processor today*: the design must read the fee invoice's VAT where one is given, not assume.
- A French micro-entrepreneur under the franchise (art. 293 B) charges no VAT; Sioul's invoices already print that line (`InvoiceSettings::vat`).

### 2.4 What platforms report about the person
- **DAC7** (Directive (EU) 2021/514; in France CGI art. 1649 ter A): platforms report each seller's "consideration" per quarter, defined *net* of the fees, commissions and taxes they withheld or charged, and report those fees, commissions and taxes separately, per quarter (OECD Model Rules, 2020, and HMRC's manual IEIM901741; **official**, read in summaries, the Official Journal's text did not load). So what a platform tells the tax office about the person is the net plus the fees, from which the gross follows; what the person declares to URSSAF is the gross. *Inference*: keeping gross, fees and net per payment is what lets the person's figures be checked against a platform's report.

### 2.5 So a movement keeps three amounts, and two days
- **Paid** (gross: what the payer paid, what the mail says, what the invoice says, the turnover to declare and the VAT base), **fees and deductions** (each with its kind: a processor's fee, a platform's commission, contributions withheld, a currency conversion's cost), **received** (net: what reached the balance or the bank, what the budgets and the account's stretch count).
- **The day it was paid** (the processor's credit, the day that counts for the declaration) and **the day it reached the bank** (the payout's line, the day the account's balance moves). For a card payment or a transfer straight to the bank they are one day.
- Under micro, only the gross is declared and the fees count for nothing fiscally, but they still matter for the budgets (money that never arrives) and for a person comparing regimes; under the real regime they are an expense. Strength: the gross base rests on law (CGI 102 ter, BOFiP) and URSSAF's own wording (read as a snippet); the day at the third party's receipt rests on BOFiP §80 (read), applied to processors by my inference.

## 3. Payment processors: where the gross, the fee and the net appear

Read on 5 November 2026. PayPal's help centre and community, Malt's help centre and URSSAF refused automated reading; what comes from search summaries is marked **snippet**.

### 3.1 PayPal (personal and business)
- **The mail** to the payee states what the payer sent (the gross). One seller reported an eBay sale's mail at £78 while £75 reached the balance (PayPal Community thread "Amount from eBay sale in email from PayPal is different", **snippet**: the forum refused connections). *Not verified*: whether the "you received a payment" mail ever states the fee and the net; the design must read them when it does and never assume it does.
- **The activity download** (CSV) gives, per row: Gross ("the localized gross amount of the transaction, including fees"), Fee, Net ("typically the gross minus any fees"), the running Balance, the Balance Impact (Debit, Credit, Memo), the Type (a T-code's description), the Transaction ID (17 characters), the **Reference Txn ID** ("the encrypted transaction ID of the parent transaction") and the Invoice Number (PayPal Developer, "Activity Download report"; **vendor**, read). Fees are "not amortized across multiple transactions": each fee sits on its payment's row.
- **Row kinds** by T-code: payments received (T0000 general, T0006 Express Checkout, T0007 web checkout, T0011 mobile), withdrawals to a bank (T0400 "General withdrawal — bank account", T0403 manual), currency conversions (T0200, T0201 user-initiated, T0202 to cover a negative balance), refunds (T1107 payment refund, T1109 fee refund), chargebacks (T1201, reversal T1202), holds (T1500 to T1503), fees on their own rows (T0106 chargeback fee, T0107 payment fee, T0114 dispute fee…) (PayPal Developer, "Transaction event codes"; **vendor**, read).
- **Rates in France**: most commercial receipts 2.90 % plus €0.35; **on a refund, "les frais que vous avez payés à l'origine pour recevoir le paiement ne vous sont pas remboursés"**; dispute resolution €14 (€28 at high volume), a bank chargeback €16; receiving in another currency, 3.0 % above the base rate (PayPal, "Tarification PayPal pour les marchands", France, updated 4 November 2026; **vendor**, read).
- **The bank line** is the withdrawal (T0400), one sum for every net received since the last one, labelled with PayPal's name; the bank's line and the PayPal withdrawal row carry the same amount, a day or a few apart.
- **Matching**: mail → PayPal row by the Transaction ID when the mail shows it (*inference*: PayPal mails usually quote it; not verified today), else by gross, day and payer; PayPal row → withdrawal by the balance (the nets since the last withdrawal add up to it); withdrawal → bank line exactly, as a move between the person's own accounts. Sioul already treats PayPal's withdrawals as such moves when both exports cover the day (accounting.md, "Bank accounts", step 3).

### 3.2 Stripe
- **Each balance transaction** has gross, fee, net, its `reporting_category` (charge, refund, dispute, fee…), `available_on`, the `charge_created` day, the `customer_facing_amount` and currency (what the customer saw, in their currency), the `invoice_number`, a `withheld_tax` already inside the fee, and **`automatic_payout_id`**, the payout that carried it to the bank; the itemised payout reconciliation report lists every charge, refund, dispute and fee inside each automatic payout; each payout has a **`trace_id`**, "a unique identifier generated by bank … sometimes referred to as a Reference number", and a `payout_statement_descriptor` shown, cut, on the bank statement (Stripe Docs, "Payout reconciliation report"; **vendor**, read). Instant payouts cannot be traced to their transactions by Stripe itself.
- **Refunds**: the original charge's processing, Connect and currency-conversion fees are not returned (Stripe Support, "Understanding fees for refunded payments"; **vendor**, snippet).
- **The balance history CSV** Sioul reads today (`id, Type, Source, Amount, Fee, Net, Currency, Created, Available On, Description`) holds gross ("Amount"), fee and net per row; Sioul keeps the net.
- **Matching**: payout → bank line by the trace id or the amount and the expected arrival day; each charge → its payout by `automatic_payout_id`; mail → charge by amount, day and customer.

### 3.3 Wise
- **Receiving**: "receiving domestic payments for the majority of currencies is free"; USD domestic wires carry a fee, Swift receipts carry fees in every currency, and correspondent banks may take their own on the way (Wise Help, "What are the fees and limits for receiving money"; **vendor**, read; the amount, 4.14 or 6.11 USD, differs between pages, **snippet**). An older help page says the fee is taken off what arrives ("if you're sent 1,000 USD, we'll take off 4.14 USD", **snippet**).
- **Statements**: the balance statement exists as CSV, PDF, XLSX, CAMT.053, MT940 and QIF, in two shapes: **COMPACT**, one line per transaction, and **FLAT**, "accounting statements where transaction fees are on a separate line" (Wise API, "Balance statement"; **vendor**, snippet). The CSV's columns, by third-party importers: TransferWise ID, Date, Amount, Currency, Description, Payment Reference, Running Balance, Exchange From, Exchange To, Exchange Rate, Payer Name, Payee Name, … **Total fees** (ledger-autosync's Wise plugin, Debian sources; **review**). One file per currency.
- *Inference*: Wise is an account in its own right; a conversion is a move between two of its currency balances with a fee, and a transfer out to the bank a move between own accounts.

### 3.4 Revolut (personal and Business)
- **Personal statement CSV**: Type, Product, Started Date, Completed Date, Description, Amount, **Fee**, Currency, State, Balance (revolut-toolkit README and a converter's page; **review**). Started and completed dates differ for card payments, which moves a payment across a month's end (Revolut Community thread; **snippet**).
- **Business statement CSV**: Date started (UTC), Date completed (UTC), ID, Type, Description, Reference, Payer, Card number, Orig currency, Orig amount, Payment currency, Amount, **Fee**, Balance, Account (an importer's requirements; **review**).
- **Acquiring** (Revolut Business accepting cards and Revolut Pay): settlement the next day; whether the fee is taken from each payment or billed apart was **not found** (only launch coverage, 2020; **grey**).

### 3.5 SumUp
- The payout report lists the card payments already paid out, with the payout's date and each one's fee; a monthly fee invoice totals the fees and needs no payment, "as it has already been deducted from your payouts"; the transaction report lists every payment, cash and refunds included (SumUp Help, "My reports" and "Download or send payout reports"; **vendor, snippet**: the article's body did not load). Payouts are daily or weekly sums.

### 3.6 Lydia and Sumeria
- Between private users, payments and pots are free (finance-heros.fr; **review**); a professional's collection by card or QR code carries a commission (1.5 % plus €0.10, or 2.5 % with €0.10 minimum, by two sources that disagree; meilleurtaux.com and Sumeria's March 2026 tariff via moneyvox.fr; **snippet**). No export format was found.

### 3.7 Malt
- Malt holds an invoicing mandate and issues, at the end of each project, three invoices: the service invoice in the freelancer's name to the client, the client's service fee, and **Malt's commission invoice to the freelancer**; Malt "déduit automatiquement ses frais de service du montant total que vous recevez" (Malt Help, "Comment fonctionnent la facturation et le paiement", "Comment fonctionne la facturation client sur Malt"; **vendor, snippet**: help.malt.com answered 403). Rates by secondary sources: 10 % excl. VAT, 5 % after some months with the same client (**grey**, disagreeing).
- *Inference*: here the mail carries everything: the gross (the service invoice), the fee (the commission invoice, with its VAT) and the net (gross minus the commission invoice's total), and the bank line is the net.

### 3.8 Marketplaces common in France
- **Vinted**: a private seller pays no commission; the buyer pays the buyer protection fee; the money waits in the Vinted wallet until the seller asks for a transfer to their IBAN (Vinted, "Protection acheteurs" help page, **vendor, snippet**; transfer delays from fripio.app, **review**). The bank line is a batch of the wallet's balance.
- **Etsy**: a 6.5 % transaction fee on the order's total, plus a payment processing fee that depends on the bank account's country, taken "prior to deposit"; deposits weekly by default, or daily, every two weeks or monthly (Etsy Help, "What are the Fees and Taxes for Selling on Etsy?", **vendor, snippet**; the French rate not found).
- **Leboncoin, eBay, Amazon, Airbnb**: not read today. They work the same way by every account I could find: the platform collects the gross, keeps its commission and pays out the rest in batches.
- **DAC7 statements**: platforms report each seller's sales and fees to the tax office (section 2.4) and must also give the seller the same information. *Not re-read today*: that the deadline is 31 January (Directive 2021/514, Annex V). The design treats such a statement as money mail worth catching.

### 3.9 What holds for every processor
- **The gross is told first**: in the payer's or the payee's mail, the invoice, the platform's order.
- **The fee is told on the processor's own record**: the export's row (PayPal, Stripe, Revolut, Wise), a separate fee row (Wise FLAT, PayPal's fee T-codes), a fee invoice (Malt, SumUp's monthly invoice), or nowhere but the difference.
- **The net reaches the processor's balance first, then the bank in batches**: PayPal's withdrawals, Stripe's and SumUp's payouts, Etsy's deposits, Vinted's transfers. The bank line names the processor, not the payer.
- **Refunds keep the fees** at PayPal France and Stripe; disputes and chargebacks add fees of their own; currency conversion adds a margin above the base rate (3 % at PayPal France).
- **So one income makes up to three records**: the gross told (mail, invoice), the net on the processor's balance (its export), and a share of one batch line at the bank. Matching the mail to the bank line directly, as Sioul does now within 5 %, is right for a single card payment and wrong for a payout.

## 4. How ledger apps model fees, payouts, splits and transfers

### 4.1 What each does
- **hledger** (plain-text, double entry). Its documented PayPal CSV rules make **three postings per PayPal payment**: the net into `assets:online:paypal`, the gross out of the other party's account (income or expense), and, "if there's a fee, … a third posting for the money taken by paypal" into `expenses:banking:paypal`. The worked entry: a $10.00 donation, $9.41 into PayPal, $0.59 of fees (hledger 1.19 manual, "CSV format", PayPal example; **vendor**, read). The withdrawal to the bank is a separate transfer between two asset accounts.
- **GnuCash** (double entry). Every transaction has at least two splits and may have many; the guide's own example of a split transaction is a pay slip: the gross salary out of Income, each deduction into its expense account, the net into the current account (GnuCash Tutorial and Concepts Guide, "Simple vs. Split Transactions"; **vendor**, snippet). A split has a value (in the transaction's currency) and an amount (in its account's commodity), so a currency conversion is one transaction across two currencies; each split has its reconcile mark (n, c, y). Each transaction keeps **the date posted and the date entered** (GnuCash API docs, `Transaction.h`, and piecash's object model; **vendor**, snippet). Users ask on its list how to record PayPal fees and are pointed to multi-split transactions (gnucash-user, April 2016, "Paypal transaction split confusion"; **qualitative**, a mailing list).
- **Beancount** (double entry). A transaction is a list of postings that must sum to zero; one posting's amount may be left out and is computed (Beancount Language Syntax, Blais; **vendor**, known, not re-read today). Fees are one more posting, as in hledger.
- **Firefly III** (double entry underneath, shown as withdrawals, deposits and transfers). A split transaction is a "transaction group" of several "journals", each with a source and a destination account. A split carries a budget, a category, a subscription, tags, notes, an internal reference, an external id and URL, a foreign amount and currency, SEPA fields (mandate, creditor, end-to-end id), **seven dates** (date, interest, book, process, due, payment, invoice) and attachments; **no fee field** (Firefly III architecture page, **vendor, snippet**; the TransactionSplit model as the generated Rust client documents it, docs.rs `firefly-iii`, **review**).
- **Actual Budget and YNAB** (single entry per account, envelope budgets). A transaction can be split across categories, and a transfer is a pair of transactions between two of one's accounts; YNAB allows a transfer as one line of a split (YNAB, "Split transactions"; Actual docs, as read on 4 November for budgets.md; **vendor**). Neither has a fee field nor a payout object: a PayPal payment net of fees is entered as its net, or as a split with a fee line, by hand (*inference* from their documentation; no PayPal guide found at YNAB).
- **HomeBank** (single entry): splits and internal transfers, no fee field (**vendor**, known from budgets.md).
- **Accountants' tools** describe the same pattern for PayPal: record the gross invoice amount, the fee as its own line, the net as the deposit, so that "the full invoice amount still matches" (FinSync, "How to account for PayPal fees"; **grey**, snippet); Sage's community advice for SumUp is to show the money SumUp deducted as part of income, then the fee as an expense (Sage community; **qualitative**, snippet).

### 4.2 What this gives Sioul
- **Every ledger that models fees properly makes them a part of one transaction**, beside the gross and the net, never a transaction of their own and never folded into the net. The tools that have no fee concept (YNAB, Actual, HomeBank, Firefly's UI) leave the person to split by hand, which is the work Sioul must not ask for.
- **Payouts are transfers between the person's own accounts** in every double-entry tool (the processor's balance is an account). Without the processor's export, the bank's payout line is the only record; no tool found ties it back to the payments it settles except Stripe's own reconciliation report and the processors' payout reports.
- **Dates**: GnuCash's entered date and Firefly's seven dates show that a money record carries several days. For Sioul three matter: the day it was paid (the processor's credit: the declaration's day, section 2.1), the day it moved the account (the bank's), and the day Sioul was told (made); plus when it was last changed.
- **Single or double entry.** Double entry's balance check (every transaction sums to zero) is what catches a lost fee, and its accounts are what makes a payout a transfer. Its words (debit, credit, accounts of income and expense) are what the budgets research says not to show (findings 43; Alenazi & Sas 2023; Fernandes et al. 2014). *Inference*: Sioul can keep the check without the words: a movement holds gross, fees and net, and must satisfy gross − fees = net (money in) or gross + fees = what left (money out); a transfer has two sides of one sum (it does already); a payout is a transfer whose incoming side is the sum of the payments it settles. That is double entry restricted to the few accounts a person has, shown as single rows.

## 5. The four tabs, checked against the research

The design asked for on 5 November 2026: **home** (overview and what is coming, the only one filtered by the hour's areas), **budgets and accounts** (no area filter), **the full ledger** of money movements (search; filters by account, budget, date, in or out, amount, area), **the history of money mail** (search; filters by account, budget, tied to a movement or not, date, in or out, amount, area).

### 5.1 What the literature adds to budgets.md
- **Information avoidance is general, and grows with what the information may say.** People avoid information that may be painful even when it is useful and free: in finance, in health, about themselves (Golman, Hagmann & Loewenstein 2017, **review**, *Journal of Economic Literature*). Investors looked at their accounts less after falls (Karlsson, Loewenstein & Seppi 2009; Sicherman et al. 2016, **study**), and Icelanders logged in more after payday and less as spending and overdrafts grew (Olafsson & Pagel 2017, **study**, already in finding 39).
- **Easy access helps when it comes unasked or cheaply.** After moving from a computer to a phone app, aggregator users logged in more often and cut discretionary spending (Levi & Benartzi 2020, **study**, working paper, abstract and press read: −15.7 % spending over a few months, per press; unverified magnitude); an app that lowered the cost of looking cut insufficient-funds fees (Carlin, Olafsson & Pagel 2023, **study**, in budgets.md).
- **Checking recent movements is what people do most with a bank on a phone**: 94 % of US mobile-banking users had checked a balance or recent transactions in the past year, ahead of transfers between their own accounts (58 %) (Federal Reserve Board 2016, *Consumers and Mobile Financial Services 2016*, **survey**, about 2,500 people, executive summary read).
- **Recording progress helps reach a goal**: across 138 randomised studies (N = 19,951), prompting people to monitor their progress raised goal attainment (d = 0.40), more when the progress was physically recorded or reported (Harkin et al. 2016, **meta**, abstract read; mostly health goals).
- **How losses are framed changes how they hurt.** Thaler's hedonic editing proposes that people feel better when gains are told apart, losses together, and **a small loss with the larger gain it comes with** (cancellation) (Thaler 1985, **theory**); people's own judgments supported separating gains but, against the theory, also preferred losses apart (Thaler & Johnson 1990, **RCT**, lab judgments; read in summary). Evaluating outcomes more often makes losses loom larger and risk feel worse (myopic loss aversion: Benartzi & Thaler 1995, **theory** with market data; Gneezy & Potters 1997, **RCT**, lab). Seeing each payment vividly raises the "pain of paying" (Prelec & Loewenstein 1998, **theory** and survey).
- **Finding things again**: in personal files, people navigate far more than they search (56–68 % of retrievals by folders against 4–15 % by search, and better search engines changed little; Bergman et al. 2008, **study**, two questionnaire studies); in mail, 345 long-term users' 85,000 refinding actions showed that filing in folders did not improve finding, while search and threads did, and that scrolling was the most frequent way of finding (Whittaker et al. 2011, **study**, field log study; abstract read).

### 5.2 Home: overview and what is coming, filtered by the hour's areas
- **Holds.** It is the page the research asks for: the facts that reach the person unasked, once, in words (findings 38, 39, 40), the worry answered first by time (each account to its next income), what is coming in date order (finding 42), and the shortfall said whatever the filter (finding 39; rule 7 of budgets.md §6). Being the first tab, it is what opens.
- **Each budget in words belongs here, filtered**, since its sentences ("Paid: rent. Still to come: phone…", the period's end) are overview, not definitions (finding 43).
- **Change suggested**: none of substance. Fees appear here only inside an income's row ("€52 from A, €50.14 reaches the account"), never as a line of their own: a small loss integrated with the larger gain it comes with (Thaler 1985's cancellation, **theory**), and no new number to worry about.

### 5.3 Budgets and accounts, unfiltered
- **Holds.** This tab is where budgets, accounts, reserves and contracts are made and changed: a definition is not news, and hiding one by the hour made a bank line impossible to place on 4 November (accounting.md, "Taking in an export"). Every budget shows, at every hour.
- **Change suggested**: group the budgets under their areas' headings (work, admin, leisure) rather than one list, the hour's own areas first. Budgets are the person's labelled mental accounts (Thaler 1999; finding 41), and the area is one of their labels; grouping keeps the label without the filter. *Inference*: since the tab is reached on purpose, showing work budgets at leisure hours is a pull, not a cue pushed into the evening (finding 21's concern is cues that reach the person).
- Accounts here say their balance, their last export's day and their processor's way of paying out ("PayPal: what you receive waits on PayPal until you move it to My bank"); the facts about an account that matter now (a shortfall) stay on Home.

### 5.4 The full ledger
- **Holds, as a place to look things up, not to start from.** Every movement in one list is what people use a bank app for most (Federal Reserve 2016) and what a declaration, a dispute or a correction needs. Its risks are the ostrich effect (a list of every loss, visited least when it matters: Golman et al. 2017; Olafsson & Pagel 2017) and narrow bracketing (each payment a loss of its own: Benartzi & Thaler 1995). Both argue that nothing essential may live only in the ledger, which the four-tab plan already ensures by keeping Home first and unasked facts on the Porch.
- **Changes suggested**:
  1. *Open on the latest movements, newest first, grouped by day, with the search field on top and filters folded away*: scrolling the recent past is the commonest way of finding (Whittaker et al. 2011), and people navigate more than they search (Bergman et al. 2008). The filters show as words over the list ("Work · money in · October"), each removable, never a form to fill first.
  2. *One row per movement, its fees inside it*: "Client A · paid €520 · €504.62 arrived on PayPal · fees €15.38". A payout is one row too, between your accounts, saying what it carried ("Payout of 4 payments"), opened to show them. No fee rows of their own, no column of fees (cancellation, Thaler 1985; and the ledger apps' own model, §4).
  3. *No running total, no totals in red, no count of "unmatched"*: a sum shows only when the filters ask a question that has one ("Money in, work, October: €2,340 paid, €2,281 arrived") (finding 43; Bazley et al. 2021).
  4. *The area filter starts as the hours say*, the same ticks as Home, changed in one tap, because the ledger is a list like Mail's and Mail starts as the hours say (areas.md, "A page's filter by area"; as asked on 4 November: "the same rules as emails"); *a typed search reaches every area*, each row naming its area, since a person looking up the energy bill at night came for it (*inference*; no study). The version first asked for (the area as an ordinary filter, all ticked) is the alternative; the difference matters only outside work hours.
  5. *A declaration's view*: filtered to work money in and a declaration's period, the ledger says the turnover to declare, the gross, with the day it was paid at the processor ("Turnover received in October, to declare by 30 November: €2,340"), and exports the receipts register (date, client, amount, means of payment, invoice: Service-public F23266). This is where the gross matters (section 2) and the "nightmare" of taxes for freelancers is met (Hsieh et al. 2025, finding 44; finding 31's rule: carry the small business's admin to the figures for the accountant).
- **Filter by amount**: keep, folded with the others; no evidence either way.

### 5.5 The history of money mail
- **Holds, with care.** No budgeting app ties the mail that told of a payment to its bank line (budgets.md §3); PocketSmith's attachment inbox, receipts waiting for their transaction, is the nearest, and it is a to-do pile. Sioul's ethos forbids that pile: automatic ties count until corrected, and nobody gets "to review" duties (finding 45's rule: no queue to review).
- **Changes suggested**:
  1. *Open on all money mail, newest first*, each row saying in words what became of it ("the same payment as Rent, 1 November"; "counted as a payment in Home"; "part of PayPal's payout of 12 November"; "not counted: you said it is no payment"; "not tied to any movement"). The filter "tied or not" exists, but is never the default, never counted, never badged.
  2. *What the row offers*: "Not the same payment", "The same as…", "A payment…", "Not a payment", "Open the message". Each act says its effect in one line and is undone in one tap, as today's row menus.
  3. *Search first, filters folded*, as in the ledger (Whittaker et al. 2011).
  4. *The tab replaces the page's present section* "The mail about money" (`BudgetsPage.qml`, the end of the scroll), so nothing is shown twice.

### 5.6 Tabs, or one page?
- *Inference, no study found*: the present page is one long scroll of nine sections; four tabs put the worry's answer alone at the top and move what one looks up behind a tap, which is progressive disclosure. On a phone each tab is one pane within 412 points, the tab bar at the top as the Settings page's tabs are (`main.qml`, accounts in tabs). Four short labels fit: Overview, Budgets, Movements, From mail (Aperçu, Budgets, Mouvements, Courriels), the last being the present section's own title (`mail-section`). The word "ledger" is an accounting word (finding 43); "movements" is the bank's own French word ("mouvements").

## 6. The history of money mail

### 6.1 What Sioul read, and the gap (code, 5 November 2026)
The gap was closed the same day by phase 3 (8eb9736): every message kept on the device is read once by a shared reader into the money index, and From mail is the history of money mail, Sent included, closed Porch or not ([accounting.md](../accounting.md), "From mail"). As it was before:
- The budgets page's "From mail" section is built from `Backend::gather`, the Porch's own items: the messages the Porch has not been closed on, and, for an account never closed, its first fourteen days (`porch::gather`, `DEFAULT_SYNC_DAYS = 14`). **Once the person closes the Porch on a message, it leaves the money section**; a mail about money from March is no longer listed in November, unless it was recorded as a line (its `mid:` kept in the line's links) or joined to a movement (its link kept in the store). So there is no history today: the request names a real gap.
- Each listed message is detected again on each reading (`budget::mail_lines` → `payments::detect` over the subject and the first 2,500 characters, plus `payments::invoice_of`, which opens attached PDFs and XML).
- The per-folder mail cache (`mailcache.rs`) reads every message once, ever, and keeps a line of its headers per message, with one settings-dependent column (a list's one-time code) under a stamp of the words it depends on. It keeps no money column.

### 6.2 Which mail counts
From the processors and the declarations (sections 2 and 3), and from what `payments.rs` already knows:
- **Money in**: a payment received (a client's transfer notice, PayPal's "you received", Stripe's payment notification, Lydia, Wise and Revolut notices); a payout or withdrawal notice (Stripe's "your payout is on its way", SumUp's payout, Etsy's deposit, Vinted's transfer); a refund to the person.
- **Money out**: a receipt, an order confirmation, a debit notice (with its SEPA mandate), a bill, a reminder of a bill, a tax or contribution notice (URSSAF's, the tax office's).
- **Invoices in and out**: an invoice received (often a PDF, Factur-X or UBL from September 2026: budgets.md §5.2); an invoice the person sent (Sent folder, with an invoice attached, or Sioul's own issued invoices, `invoice.rs`); a platform's invoices issued in the person's name (Malt's mandate) and its **commission invoice** (the fee).
- **Fee documents**: SumUp's monthly fee invoice, Malt's commission invoice, a bank's statement of charges.
- **Statements**: a platform's yearly DAC7 statement of sales and fees (section 2.4), a processor's monthly statement.
- **Paper letters** with an amount (`letters.rs`) belong to the same history: they are mail by another way, and are already movement sources (`sioul:letter/<id>`).
- **Not**: newsletters, marketing with prices, shipping notices, account activity reports, mandates without amounts (already `payments.not_payments`); and never mail set aside as forged or spam (as today).

### 6.3 Found once, kept as an index, never read twice
*Inference from the code; no outside source*:
- **A money index beside the mail cache**, one small file per folder (`$XDG_CACHE_HOME/sioul/money-index/<account>/<folder>.tsv`), keyed like the cache by the message file's unchanging part, with what `payments::detect` and `invoice_of` found: kind, amount, currency, fee and net when the mail states them, party, references, due day, instalments. It is filled as the cache is: a message new to it is read once, its line appended under the file's lock, so the window, the phone's background step and `sioul mcp` never add it twice. Its first line stamps the payment words and the mail rules it depends on; when they change, only that index is rebuilt, in the background, from the messages, never the mail cache.
- **A prefilter keeps the cost down**: a message is opened for detection only if its headers already in the mail cache say money is possible (a sender known for money, a money word in the subject, an attachment named like an invoice, a processor's domain); the others get a "nothing" line at no cost beyond the cache's. *Not measured*: on the 20,000-message profile, the share of messages the prefilter keeps and the first build's time; a builder should measure both (android.md's timing practice).
- **The history is then a query**: every message whose index line says money, its ties read from the store (`[[link]]`, `[[apart]]`) and the budget file's lines, sorted newest first, paged by 60 rows as Mail's list is.
- **Sent mail counts**: an invoice the person sent is found in Sent like any other message; an issued invoice of Sioul's own is a row too, from its record.

### 6.4 Corrections that teach, with nothing to validate
- **Sioul's rule** (finding 45): an automatic tie counts until the person corrects it, and a correction teaches; no queue to review. The spam filter shows the pattern at its strongest: the person's own word, given in passing, is the best training there is (finding 35; Cormack 2008).
- **What each correction teaches**, by inference:
  - *"Not the same payment"* (exists: `[[apart]]`): never these two again; and the pair's names stop counting as one payee's (`[[name]]` removed when it came from them).
  - *"The same as…"* (exists): the tie, and the payee's bank name (`[[name]]`, exists); for a processor's mail tied to a payout, that this account's payouts from that processor carry that label (a processor rule, learned, section 7 of the design).
  - *"Not a payment"* (exists: `money.toml`'s `ignored`): that message; and, by the same sender, mail of the same shape (its subject with the digits and amounts taken out) is no longer counted either, said once on the row ("Mail like this from X is no longer counted. Count it again"), undone in one tap. No question asked.
  - *"A payment…"* on a mail Sioul did not count (exists: `PaymentForm.qml`): the amount, the party and the kind the person gave teach the shape the other way: the next mail of that shape from that sender is counted.
  - *A fee changed by hand*: the processor's fee for that account, until an export says otherwise.
- **Nothing is ever shown as waiting**: no badge, no count of mail not tied to anything, no "to review" filter by default. The filter "not tied to a movement" exists for a person who goes looking, as the ledger's filters do.

## 7. Recommendations, each with its rule

1. **A movement keeps what was paid, its fees and what arrived.** *Rule*: gross, fees (each with its kind and source) and net, with gross − fees = net for money in and gross + fees = what left for money out; the budgets and the account's stretch count the net, the declarations the gross. *Evidence*: CGI art. 102 ter and BOFiP (law), URSSAF's wording (official, snippet), DAC7's net-plus-fees reporting (official, summaries), every double-entry ledger (vendor).
2. **Two days, not one.** *Rule*: the day it was paid (at the processor) and the day it reached the bank; a receipt counts for the declaration on the first. *Evidence*: BOI-BNC-BASE-20-10-10 §80 (law, read), applied to processors by inference.
3. **Fees come from the most exact record there is.** *Rule*: the processor's export row, else a fee invoice, else the mail's own fee line, else the payout less the gross it settles; the published rate only as "about" for what is still expected, never for a declaration. *Evidence*: section 3 (vendor).
4. **A payout is a move between the person's own accounts that settles several payments.** *Rule*: with the processor's export, a transfer like any other; without it, the bank's payout line settles the processor's payments told since the previous payout, oldest first, as far as its amount goes with fees within the processor's bounds; what remains waits "on PayPal". *Evidence*: Stripe's reconciliation report, SumUp's payout report (vendor); oldest-first is inference.
5. **Fees are shown inside their payment's row, never as rows or totals of their own.** *Rule*: "paid €52 · €50.14 arrived · fees €1.86". *Evidence*: Thaler 1985 (theory, mixed support); finding 43.
6. **Home stays first and filtered by the hour; Budgets lists everything, grouped by area.** *Evidence*: findings 38–43; budgets.md §6 rule 7; the 4 November bug.
7. **The ledger is for looking things up**: newest first, search on top, filters as words, no running totals, a sum only when the filters ask a question that has one; its area filter starts as the hours say, a typed search reaches every area; a declaration's view gives the turnover to declare and the receipts register. *Evidence*: Federal Reserve 2016 (survey); Whittaker et al. 2011, Bergman et al. 2008 (studies); Golman et al. 2017 (review); Service-public F23266 (official).
8. **The mail history is every money mail ever kept, found once and indexed**, each row saying what became of it, corrected in one tap, never counted or badged. *Evidence*: the code (section 6.1); finding 45's rule; finding 35's (own word as training).
9. **Every act on a movement is the same in the window, the agents' tools and the command line**, each said with its effect and undone in one tap. *Evidence*: Sioul's own rule for agents' writes (the MCP's instructions: each change said in the window, with Undo); no outside source.

## 8. Strength of the evidence, and what only a trial can settle
- **Strongest**: that the declared turnover is the gross (CGI art. 102 ter, BOFiP, URSSAF's wording); that a sum collected by a third party is received when the third party receives it (BOFiP §80, read); the processors' own records of gross, fee, net and payout (PayPal's and Stripe's documentation, read); PayPal France keeping its fees on refunds (its fee page, updated 4 November 2026, read).
- **Weakest**: that the payment mails of PayPal, Stripe, Wise and Revolut do or do not state the fee (not verified: their help centres refused automated reading); Malt's, SumUp's, Lydia's and the marketplaces' rules (snippets); Revolut Business's acquiring fees (not found); the tab structure (no study compares tabs with one page for money; progressive disclosure is a design principle, not evidence); the hedonic-editing argument for fees inside rows (theory, mixed support); the ledger's hour-started area filter (inference).
- **Only a trial with real servers and real mail can settle**: which processors' mails state fees and in which words (a corpus of the person's own processor mail, read by the detector); how often a payout's amount equals a prefix of the told payments within the processor's fee bounds (a test on real PayPal, Stripe and SumUp payouts); the money index's first build time on a large mailbox, on the computer and on the phone; whether the "Not a payment" shape rule misfires on senders that mix payments and marketing (PayPal does).
